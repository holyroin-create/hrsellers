Since March 31, 2026, a reseller's unlabeled unit arriving at FBA is defective inventory, and a brand owner's is fine. Two barcode systems, one change date, and opposite consequences depending on which side of Brand Registry you sit — announced at Accelerate on September 17, 2025 and in force for every inbound shipment since the end of March. This is probably the single largest structural change of 2026 for resellers, and it received far less coverage than the fee increase.

## The four identifiers, briefly

Sellers confuse these constantly and the distinctions matter for what follows.

| Identifier | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **GTIN** | The umbrella term for global trade item numbers |
| **UPC** | A 12-digit GTIN format, standard in the US |
| **EAN** | A 13-digit GTIN format |
| **ASIN** | Amazon's catalog identifier for a product page |
| **FNSKU** | Amazon's internal barcode linking a **physical unit** to **your seller account**. Not a universal product identifier |

The flow: you buy a UPC from GS1, use it to create a listing, Amazon assigns an ASIN, and for FBA Amazon generates an FNSKU for your inventory.

## What changed on March 31, 2026

Amazon **eliminated commingled inventory** on that date, and the consequences split by seller type.

**Brand owners in Brand Registry** with a Brand Representative role can use their GS1 manufacturer barcodes — the UPCs printed on their packaging — as the primary tracking identifier. **No FNSKU stickers.** the industry as having spent roughly **$600 million a year on re-stickering** purely to avoid commingling, and that expense as gone for qualifying brand owners.

**Resellers** get the opposite. FNSKU labels are **mandatory on all inventory, no exceptions**, even where the product already carries a compliant GS1 barcode. Inventory received after March 31 without a proper FNSKU is **classified as defective.**

GS1 UK's own note frames the rationale: Amazon is pursuing unit-level traceability so every item can be linked back to the exact seller who supplied it, and manufacturer barcodes alone are no longer accepted for reseller inbound shipments.

## The compounding problem for resellers

Put two 2026 changes side by side.

**January 1:** Amazon ended US FBA prep and labeling services — see the [prep guide](/guides/amazon-stopped-prepping/).

**March 31:** FNSKU labels became mandatory on every reseller unit.

**The same seller now has more labeling to do and no service to do it.** Reported third-party prep costs of $0.35 to $2.00 per unit apply to that labour, and the alternative is doing it yourself.

For a wholesale or reseller business at any volume, that is a new per-unit cost line that did not exist in 2025, on top of the [fee increases](/guides/fba-vs-fbm-breakeven/). It is also a reason the [Brand Registry](/guides/brand-registry-trademark/) question changed shape: registry now removes a per-unit operating cost rather than only unlocking marketing tools.

## Why cheap UPCs fail

Reporting is consistent: **Amazon verifies barcodes against the GS1 global database**, and third-party resold UPCs fail that verification.

The mechanism: if the brand or company associated with a GTIN does not match your seller account information, the code is treated as invalid. Consequences include listing suppression, ASIN creation blocks and GTIN verification issues.

**This is the mistake that is expensive to unwind**, because listings built on invalid codes may have to be rebuilt, and rebuilt listings lose their review history and rank.

**Reported GS1 costs differ by route**, and the difference matters:

| Route | Reported cost | Renewal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single GTIN from GS1 US | $30 per GTIN | No renewal requirement |
| GS1 prefix (multiple GTINs) | Roughly $250 a year | Annual — and a lapsed prefix will trigger Amazon listing issues |

That renewal point is worth noting. A prefix that lapses in the GS1 database causes listing problems on products that were fine yesterday, and the cause is not obvious from inside Seller Central.

**Each variation needs its own GTIN.** Size, color, flavour, quantity, bundle configuration. A product in four colors and three sizes is twelve UPCs — which changes the arithmetic between the single-GTIN and prefix routes considerably.

## GTIN exemptions

Amazon offers exemptions allowing listings without a UPC, reported as available for private label, handmade and custom products that genuinely lack barcodes, and for parts, accessories and self-assembled bundles.

Two cautions from reporting:

**You still need FNSKU labels.** An exemption removes the listing requirement, not the FBA unit-labeling requirement.

**Exemptions can be revoked.** Reported as category-specific and potentially withdrawn if a brand grows past a small-scale threshold. One source's conclusion is that most sellers find buying GS1 UPCs simpler than maintaining exemption status — and if your brand or category already uses GS1 barcodes, you will not qualify anyway.

## Label mechanics

The details that cause receiving problems:

- **Size:** at 1 × 2 inches (25 × 51 mm)
- **Color:** black ink on white, non-reflective
- **Placement:** the FNSKU goes **over** the manufacturer barcode, covering it entirely. Two scannable barcodes on one unit is a receiving error
- **Scannability:** every unit entering a fulfillment center needs a scannable barcode, or Amazon cannot receive it — leading to relabeling fees, delays or rejection

Reporting names missing or wrong barcodes as **the leading reason FBA shipments are held at receiving or destroyed.**

## What to do

**Establish which side of the change you are on.** Brand Registry brand owner with a Brand Representative role, or reseller. The answer determines your labeling obligation and your per-unit cost.

**If you are a reseller, cost FNSKU labeling into your unit economics** — either your labour or a prep center's per-unit rate.

**If you are a brand owner not yet in Brand Registry**, the calculation changed. Registry now removes a recurring labeling cost per unit, which for anyone at volume can exceed the trademark cost within a year.

**Buy GTINs from GS1 directly.** Reporting is unanimous that resold codes fail verification. Choose between single GTINs and a prefix based on how many variations you actually need.

**Diarise your GS1 renewal** if you hold a prefix. A lapse produces listing problems with no obvious cause.

**Check your listing data matches your GS1 registration.** product details on the Amazon listing must match what is registered against the barcode.

**Check the FNSKU covers the manufacturer barcode completely** on a sample before a full run — particularly if a supplier or prep center is doing the labeling.
