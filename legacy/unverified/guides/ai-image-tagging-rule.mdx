A niche rule with a wide blast radius, per this month's reporting: images and A+ Content that feature **photorealistic AI-generated people** must now carry **"contains-synthetic-performer"** in the file's XMP metadata at upload. Real people stay untagged; so do AI-touched photos of real people and obviously non-photorealistic figures. The target is the synthetic human who could pass for a hired model.

## Why a metadata field deserves your attention

Because of who reads it. This lands in a season the seller press is calling enforcement-first: automated systems flagging listing issues before human review. A rule that lives in metadata is a rule made for machine checking — cheap to comply with, cheap to detect, and awkward to argue about after a takedown. If your lifestyle imagery comes from AI generation (an increasingly common cost saving), the fix belongs in the pipeline: add the XMP keyword to the export template once, and every future asset ships compliant. While you are in the images anyway, the [main image compliance guide](/guides/main-image-compliance/) covers the older rules that still generate most suppressions.
