Write "we're a small family business" in a buyer message and Amazon's filter may block it. Post-purchase messaging is the last place sellers try to build a relationship with an Amazon customer, and Amazon's position is that the relationship is theirs. The Communication Guidelines say so in plain terms.

## The two categories

**Necessary permitted messages** — communications required to complete an order or respond to a customer service inquiry. Answering a buyer's question about product details, resolving a delivery problem, arranging a return.

**Proactive permitted messages** — everything you initiate. Narrowly defined, and Amazon already handles much of the post-purchase communication sellers used to send themselves.

**The practical consequence:** if the buyer did not ask and the order does not require it, the message probably does not need to exist.

## What gets blocked

Blocked content:

| Blocked | Note |
| --- | --- |
| Persuasive or marketing language | Including sentiment such as "we're a family business" |
| Review requests with sentiment | "Please leave 5 stars" |
| External links | Any off-Amazon URL |
| Emojis | Blocked |
| Incentives | Discounts, free products, compensation of any kind |
| Phone numbers and email addresses | Unless required to complete the order, described as rare |
| Attachments | Unless necessary for fulfillment |
| Logos and branded images | Prohibited |
| **Buyer-name personalization** | Blocked in practice, per agency reports |

**The name rule surprises people**, because personalization is standard practice everywhere else. Agencies give the example directly: "Hi Sarah! Your 12-pack water bottles are on the way!" is blocked; a neutral greeting paired with product-specific detail — "Hello! Your [product] is on the way" — is the compliant alternative.

## The format rules

Amazon's requirements for a compliant proactive message:

- **Within 30 days** of the order
- **Order ID included**
- **Written in the buyer's language**
- **No prohibited elements** from the list above

## What happens when you get it wrong

Consequences escalate:

**Blocked messages** — the immediate outcome, and the useful one, because nothing reaches the customer.

**Restriction to pre-approved templates** — Amazon limits you to its own message formats.

**Account warnings, and suspension** on repeated violations.

**And the diagnostic problem** covered in the [review manipulation guide](/guides/review-manipulation-risk/): notices do not specify which message triggered them, so a template running across thousands of orders produces a flag you then have to locate yourself.

## The rule that resolves most of it

Reporting's own conclusion, and it is the right one:

**Use Request a Review for feedback. Reserve custom messages for genuine order issues. If you are unsure whether a message complies, do not send it.**

Request a Review sends Amazon's own standardized message between 5 and 30 days after delivery, with content Amazon controls and the seller cannot customize. **That lack of control is the feature** — there is nothing in it for an algorithm to flag.

**Which makes the calculation straightforward.** A custom message might convert marginally better. A blocked-message pattern costs account health, and a review-manipulation flag costs considerably more.

## Where automation fits

tools that automate messaging with templates designed to follow current rules, and recommends reviewing automated campaigns periodically against policy updates.

**Two cautions:**

**A tool does not transfer responsibility.** A template that was compliant when written and is not now is your violation.

**Volume amplifies error.** A slightly non-compliant message sent once is a blocked message; the same message on automation across a catalog is a pattern — which is what the flagging systems look for.

## What to do

**Audit any active message template** against the blocked list, particularly for buyer names, emojis and persuasive phrasing.

**Remove review-request language entirely** and use Request a Review instead — the same conclusion as the [inserts guide](/guides/product-inserts/).

**Keep proactive messages to genuine necessity.** If the buyer did not ask and the order does not require it, do not send it.

**Include the order ID and stay inside 30 days** on anything you do send.

**Review automated campaigns quarterly** against current policy rather than treating a template as permanent.

**When in doubt, do not send.** Reporting's own advice, and the cost of following it is one message that would probably have been blocked anyway.
