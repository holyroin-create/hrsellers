You have a pallet of returns and you want to sell it. Amazon Renewed is not the door. The entry requirements — $50,000 of refurbished purchase invoices in 90 days, an order defect rate under 0.8%, a warranty you fund — describe a refurbishing business, not a seller clearing stock.

## What it is

A program allowing approved sellers to offer **pre-owned, refurbished and open-box products** — mostly but not only electronics — under Amazon's condition standards.

Condition grades: **premium, excellent, good and acceptable.** Products may have minor physical flaws such as scratches or marks and typically arrive without original packaging, but must be inspected, tested and cleaned to work like new.

**Renewed Premium** is a higher tier: 90% battery capacity against 80% for standard, and a one-year warranty rather than the standard 90-day guarantee.

## The entry requirements

**Invoices.** Itemized, showing a minimum total value of **$50,000 in qualifying refurbished purchases in the previous 90 days** — or 180 days for products related to Home, Tools, Kitchen or Lawn & Garden.

Amazon's own program page specifies what those invoices must show: **authentic and unaltered**, a completed transaction, your full business name and address, the supplier's full name and contact information, purchase quantity, and **itemized brand and model information that can be validated online.** Unit prices may be redacted.

**That is a stricter version of the same document six other systems ask for** — see [the supplier invoice](/guides/the-supplier-invoice/).

**Brand-specific thresholds.** Amazon's program page names one: **$2.5 million for Apple** branded items in Consumer Electronics, in the previous 90 days. Agency guides describe comparable gates for Samsung wireless products; Amazon does not publish them, so treat them as an application-stage surprise rather than a planning number.

**Order Defect Rate at 0.8% or below**, over a trailing 90 days. is **tighter than the general 1% threshold** covered in the [ODR guide](/guides/order-defect-rate/).

**Product and packaging images.** A minimum of 8 images for factory-refurbished items without manufacturer warranty — the shipping box, internal packaging and padding, and the product from top, bottom and all four sides, with the screen on for electronics. One source reports 10 images for a sample unit generally.

**Trademark documentation or Brand Registry enrollment** if you are the manufacturer or brand owner.

**Application** is through Seller Central or by email to Amazon's Renewed application address, with a response in about 10 to 12 business days.

## Who it is for

Amazon's own page names the seller types: **distributors and resellers, manufacturers and brand owners, brand-authorized refurbishers, refurbishers, repairers, and IT Asset Disposition providers.**

**Read that list against the invoice requirement** and the shape becomes clear: the program is for businesses whose activity is refurbishment, not for sellers with a pallet of returns.

**Which answers the question most sellers arrive with.** If you have removal-order inventory or open-box units to clear, this is not the route — the [removal guide](/guides/removal-vs-hold-decision/) covers liquidation, and the [eBay guide](/guides/ebay-etsy-for-amazon-sellers/) covers the marketplace that takes used goods without an entry threshold.

## The obligations after approval

**The Amazon Renewed Guarantee.** Your company backs every refurbished product and is responsible for all claims under it. — a **90-day minimum warranty** — a limited warranty under which a customer can obtain a free refund or replacement if the product does not work properly.

**That is a real liability**, funded and administered by you, on used goods, for ninety days.

**Ongoing performance standards**:

| Metric | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Order Defect Rate | 0.8% or below |
| Renewed Quality Score | 98% or above — fewer than 2% of orders with reported defects over 13 weeks |
| 1- and 2-star reviews | Below 10% of total reviews on renewed listings |

**The last one is unusual** and worth noting: a review-composition standard, not just a defect standard. On used goods, where expectations vary, that is a demanding metric.

## The honest assessment

**The case for it:** a defined market for refurbished goods with a trust framework Amazon supplies, in categories where used inventory has real value.

**The case against it, for most readers of this site:** the entry threshold assumes you already operate at scale in refurbishment, the warranty is a genuine liability on used equipment, and the ongoing standards are tighter than the general marketplace ones — on a product category where returns and defects are structurally higher.

**Where it makes sense:** if refurbishment is your business, or if you are a brand owner wanting a controlled channel for your own returned and repaired units rather than liquidating them. That second case is the one most under-considered — a manufacturer refurbishing its own returns has the invoices, the trademark documentation and the technical capacity that the program asks for.

## What to do

**Check the invoice position first.** If you cannot produce $50,000 of itemized, validatable refurbished purchase invoices in the window, nothing else matters.

**Check your ODR against 0.8%**, not 1%. Many accounts that are comfortably healthy generally are not comfortable against this threshold.

**Cost the warranty.** Ninety days of refund-or-replacement liability on used goods, funded by you, belongs in the unit economics before the application.

**Prepare the images properly.** Eight to ten images of a sample unit and its packaging from every angle, with electronics powered on.

**If you are a brand owner, consider it for your own returns** — that is the case where you already hold most of what the program asks for.

**If you are looking to clear returns you did not manufacture, look elsewhere.** The threshold is the answer.
