Almost every private-label seller puts a card in the box. Most of those cards contain at least one sentence that as prohibited, written in good faith by someone who thought they were being helpful.

## What is allowed

An insert can:

- **Thank the buyer**
- **Explain how to use the product** — setup instructions, assembly, troubleshooting
- **Offer assistance** with problems
- ** the customer is free to report an honest experience**
- **Cross-sell your other products on Amazon**
- **Provide warranty registration**, within the policy boundaries

**That is a genuinely useful card**, and it is the version that carries no risk.

## What is not

Reported prohibitions, and each one catches a common template:

**Asking for a positive review.** Reporting quotes Amazon's own wording: directing customers to write a positive review is prohibited **even if no incentive is offered.**

**Offering any incentive.** Discounts, refunds, free products, gift cards or other compensation in exchange for a review — reported as prohibited **even when the compensation is not conditioned on the review being positive.**

**Review gating.** Directing dissatisfied customers to contact you instead of leaving a negative review. Reporting is explicit that Amazon treats this as review diversion **even where the intent is customer service.**

**Redirecting off Amazon for shopping.** Covered separately under the Seller Code of Conduct, not the review policy.

**Newsletter and email capture.** an insert asking customers to sign up for a newsletter as unlikely to convert well and **likely to violate the policy on diverting sales.**

**QR codes, social links and landing pages** used in ways that manipulate reviews or move customer service off Amazon. these as permitted in narrow cases with enforcement depending on use case and wording — which is another way of saying the risk is yours.

## The technique that separates the two

One published approach is worth stating precisely because it is the difference between a compliant insert and a suspension trigger.

**Do not put the review mention and the customer service offer in the same sentence, or adjacent to each other.**

Reporting explains why: it may seem a subtle difference, but separating them leaves the buyer to make the connection that contacting you is a better route than leaving negative feedback. **If you make that connection for them, you have broken the rule.**

**Non-compliant:** "If you have any problems, please contact us before leaving a review."

**Compliant:** a support section giving contact details for help with the product, and — separately, if at all — a neutral statement that honest feedback is welcome.

## The safest version

One source's conclusion is more conservative and worth taking seriously given what a review-manipulation flag costs.

**Remove review-request language from inserts entirely.** Use Amazon's Request a Review button — the only officially sanctioned proactive mechanism — and let the insert do setup, support and Amazon-only cross-sell.

The reasoning: this **eliminates the most common false-positive trigger** that catches good-faith sellers, because there is no review-request language anywhere for an algorithm to flag.

**And the exposure extends beyond your own card.** Reporting names supplier packaging with review requests as a trigger — meaning a factory that includes its own insert, or reuses packaging from another client, can create a flag against your account. Add "no inserts, no review language" to the [packaging specification](/guides/packaging-design-fba/) and check it at [inspection](/guides/qc-inspections/).

## What a flag actually costs

Covered in full in the [review manipulation guide](/guides/review-manipulation-risk/), and worth summarizing here because it is the reason to be conservative:

Amazon's system will flag **patterns rather than confirmed violations**, notices do not specify which reviews or messages triggered them, and false positives are documented and common in 2026.

**Which means the cost of a slightly aggressive insert is not a warning about the insert.** It is a notice you have to diagnose yourself, on an account that may be suspended while you do — see the [suspension guide](/guides/account-suspension-appeal/).

## What to do

**Audit your current insert against the prohibition list.** Most fail on the customer-service-before-review sentence.

**Separate support from feedback**, physically on the card if you keep both.

**Remove incentives entirely**, including Amazon-only discounts paired with review language.

**Remove newsletter and off-Amazon calls to action.**

**Specify no supplier inserts** in the purchase order and verify at inspection.

**Use Request a Review** as the proactive mechanism and let the insert be genuinely useful instead.

**Reprint rather than use up existing stock** if the current card is non-compliant. A reprint costs a few hundred dollars; the alternative is an unexplained flag on the account.
