The Featured Offer disappears from a listing and the first place most sellers look is Account Health. That is the wrong dashboard for almost every case. Account Health tells you whether your account is in standing; Pricing Health tells you whether this offer, at this price, is allowed to be featured — and after Amazon's removal of the seller-eligibility step, price is the gate that remains. This guide is about reading Pricing Health the way Amazon describes it and fixing the rows in an order that does not cost you margin you did not need to give up.

## Where it is and what the two tabs hold

Pricing → Pricing Health. Amazon's own description of the page is short and worth quoting in structure: an **Inactive Offers** tab, listing offers that have been deactivated because of potential pricing errors, with suggested actions to reactivate them; and a **Pricing Opportunities** tab, listing all offers that are ineligible to be a Featured Offer, together with offers that are eligible and priced within 5% of the Featured Offer price.

Since Amazon added an export, the Pricing Opportunities list can be downloaded as a file — both the ineligible offers and the close-to-featured ones. Pull it before you change anything, because the list moves as prices move and you want the starting state on record.

| Row you are looking at | What Amazon says it means | What clears it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inactive Offers | Deactivated for a potential pricing error | Confirm or correct the price; review min/max |
| Pricing Opportunities — not eligible | Total price above the Competitive External Price, or significantly above recent prices | Move the price, or decide to hold without the box |
| Pricing Opportunities — eligible, within 5% | Eligible to be featured; priced within 5% of the current Featured Offer price | A rotation-share decision, not a fix |

## The price test, in Amazon's words

The FAQ on the Pricing Health page describes when an offer becomes ineligible to be featured: when your total price is above the Competitive External Price when Amazon has one available, or otherwise when your total price is significantly higher than recent prices. Two things in that sentence do the work.

**Total price.** Item price plus shipping. An FBM offer with a $6.99 shipping line is compared at item + $6.99; a free-shipping offer is compared on the item price alone.

**Competitive External Price.** Amazon's term for the lowest price it finds for the same item at other reputable retailers. It is visible per SKU in Manage All Inventory under the reference and business prices view. When it exists, it is the test. When it does not, the test falls back to your own recent price history — which is how a listing that ran a coupon and then returned to list price can trip the second test without any competitor involved.

## The fix order

**1. Export first.** Download the Pricing Opportunities file and screenshot the Inactive Offers tab. Turn on the "Featured Offer eligible" column in Manage All Inventory (Preferences) and export that too. You now have three dated records of the starting state. Everything you do next will be judged against them.

**2. Clear Inactive Offers before anything else.** A deactivated offer is not competing at all, so this is the only row where every hour costs the whole sale. Amazon deactivates on a suspected pricing error — a price far below or far above what it expects for the item. The suggested action on the row is the fix: confirm the price if it is correct, correct it if it is not, and check the minimum and maximum you have set on the SKU, because a min/max pair that no longer brackets the market is a common reason the flag returns after you clear it.

**3. Work the ineligible rows against the Competitive External Price.** For each SKU, open the reference and business prices view and read the Competitive External Price. Then one of two things is true.

If your total price is above it, you have a decision, not a problem. Matching it restores eligibility; whether it should is a contribution-margin question. Put the Competitive External Price into the margin calculator as the sale price, with your current fee stack and cost, and read the per-unit result before you touch the listing. A price that clears eligibility and runs negative per unit is not a fix.

If no Competitive External Price is shown and the row is still ineligible, the second test fired: your price is significantly above your own recent prices. This is the post-promotion trap. The remedy is time or a step down toward the recent price; there is no competitor to match.

**4. Treat "eligible, within 5%" as a rotation question.** These SKUs are already allowed in the box. Amazon surfaces them so you can consider a small move to increase your share of the rotation. Whether a few percent of price buys enough extra share to pay for itself is not knowable from the dashboard; the [Buy Box guide](/guides/buy-box-featured-offer/) covers what is and is not published about how the rotation is weighted. Do not reprice this row on reflex.

**5. Set guardrails from your margin, not from Amazon's suggestion.** Amazon's built-in remedy for the ineligible row is the Competitive Price Rule in Automate Pricing, which you can enroll in from the Pricing Health row. Amazon describes what it does: it matches the Featured Offer price across all offers for the same ASIN and condition, compares against prices at retailers outside Amazon, and keeps adjusting after prices move elsewhere. It offers a recommended minimum and maximum based on historical trends for the Competitive Price, and lets you set your own.

Set your own. The recommended minimum is derived from where the competitive price has been, not from what the unit costs you to sell. A rule that follows an off-Amazon retailer down to Amazon's recommended floor will restore eligibility and can do it below your breakeven. The minimum you enter should be the price at which contribution margin after the full fee stack reaches the floor you can live with, and nothing in the tool will tell you that number.

## An illustrative walk-through

Illustrative figures, not quoted rates: a kitchen SKU lists at $32.99 with free shipping. It ran a $5 coupon for two weeks, the coupon ended, and the row now sits in Pricing Opportunities as not eligible. The reference-prices view shows no Competitive External Price. So the second test fired: $32.99 is significantly above the recent effective price of $27.99. The choices are a step to something between the two, or holding at $32.99 and selling without the box until the recent-price window rolls forward. If the margin at $29.99 is still acceptable per unit, the step is cheap; if the coupon was already running at breakeven, holding is the correct answer and the dashboard's amber row is the cost of having run the promotion.

Now the same SKU with a Competitive External Price of $24.50 from another retailer. Matching it restores eligibility. Whether you match depends entirely on the per-unit result at $24.50 after referral fee, fulfillment fee and cost of goods — a number the dashboard does not show and the Competitive Price Rule will not calculate for you.

## What changed in 2026, and what did not

Amazon's July 2026 announcement removes the seller-level eligibility screen from Featured Offer selection, rolling out across all stores by the end of the year. It does not touch the price tests above; Amazon says the selection criteria — competitive pricing, delivery speed, performance — are unchanged. The practical effect for this page is that the ineligible list gets cleaner: an account-level lockout that used to sit beside the price failures goes away, and what is left is price. The [news analysis](/guides/featured-offer-eligibility-gate-removed/) covers the announcement itself.

Open Pricing Health now, export both lists, and write the Competitive External Price next to your top ten SKUs before you change a single price.
