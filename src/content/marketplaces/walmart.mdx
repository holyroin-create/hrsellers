Walmart Marketplace takes a **referral fee** on each item you sell, and nothing until you do: the fees are only deducted once you complete a sale <C id="marketplace-walmart-02"/>. Walmart describes its cost structure as having zero setup, monthly, or hidden fees <C id="marketplace-walmart-03"/>. The rate depends on the category: 15% for many, including Everything Else <C id="marketplace-walmart-38"/>, and as low as 6% for Personal Computers <C id="marketplace-walmart-27"/>. The base matters as much as the rate: Walmart applies the percentage to the total sales price, which includes shipping and handling, gift wrap and other charges <C id="marketplace-walmart-01"/>.

## Which fee applies: rate, band and fee base

Three questions settle almost every Walmart fee: which category row you are in, whether the row uses a whole-price band or a portion split, and what goes into the "total sales price."

<Decision
  title="Work out your Walmart referral fee"
  rows={[
    { if: "Your category shows one percentage (Home, Toys, Pet Supplies, Books)", then: "Multiply the total sales price, including any shipping you charge, by that rate", claim: "marketplace-walmart-01" },
    { if: "You sell apparel, baby, beauty or grocery items", then: "Pick the band using the total sales price, then apply that single rate to the whole amount", claim: "marketplace-walmart-04" },
    { if: "You sell furniture, jewelry, watches, electronics accessories or compact appliances", then: "Apply the higher rate up to the threshold and the lower rate only above it", claim: "marketplace-walmart-18" },
    { if: "You sell office supplies that are printer cartridges", then: "Use 12%, not the 15% Office Products rate", claim: "marketplace-walmart-24" },
    { if: "You sell binoculars, telescopes, spotting scopes, trail monitors or night vision goggles", then: "Use 8%, not the 15% Outdoors Products and Sports rate", claim: "marketplace-walmart-26" }
  ]}
/>

Because shipping is part of the fee base <C id="marketplace-walmart-01"/>, the whole-price categories reward careful pricing near a band edge.

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): shipping pushes apparel into the 15% band">
A T-shirt priced at $18.00 with free shipping has a total sales price of $18.00, which sits in the 10% band: fee $1.80. Price the same shirt at $18.00 plus $5.00 shipping and the total sales price becomes $23.00, above $20, so the whole amount is charged at 15%: fee $3.45. The buyer pays $5.00 more and the referral fee nearly doubles. <C id="marketplace-walmart-04"/> <C id="marketplace-walmart-01"/>
</Callout>

The portion-based categories only start saving money above their thresholds. A $350 sofa in Indoor & Outdoor Furniture would pay 15% on $200 ($30.00) plus 10% on $150 ($15.00), or $45.00, compared with $52.50 at a flat 15% <C id="marketplace-walmart-18"/>. A $400 compact appliance would pay 12% on $300 ($36.00) plus 8% on $100 ($8.00), or $44.00 <C id="marketplace-walmart-05"/>.

## Common Walmart fee mistakes

| Mistake | What the fee page actually says | Effect on a hypothetical order |
|---|---|---|
| Calculating the fee on item price only | Total sales price includes shipping and handling, gift wrap and other charges <C id="marketplace-walmart-01"/> | A $20 item with $6 shipping is charged on $26, not $20 |
| Treating apparel bands like a split | Apparel uses one rate for the whole price, chosen by the band <C id="marketplace-walmart-04"/> | A $30 shirt pays 15% on all $30 ($4.50), not a blend |
| Treating furniture like a whole-price band | Furniture charges 10% only on the portion above $200 <C id="marketplace-walmart-18"/> | A $250 chair pays $35.00, not $37.50 |
| Using the parent rate for an exception | Printer cartridges are 12% inside Office Products <C id="marketplace-walmart-24"/> | $50 of cartridges: $6.00, not $7.50 |
| Budgeting for a monthly subscription | Walmart describes zero setup or monthly fees <C id="marketplace-walmart-03"/> | Fixed monthly cost of selling is $0 at the referral-fee level |

The pattern behind most of these errors is copying a rule from one category to another. Walmart's table uses three different structures, a flat rate, a whole-price band and a portion split, and sometimes an exception inside a single row, so each category needs its own calculation before a price goes live. For a seller importing a mixed catalog, it is worth building the referral fee into the landed-cost sheet per SKU rather than applying one blended percentage across everything.
