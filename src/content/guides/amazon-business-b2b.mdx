If you already sell on Amazon with a Professional account, reaching business buyers is mostly a pricing decision, not a new channel: you open B2B Central, add a business price and optional quantity discounts to listings you already have, and decide whether to list case packs or pallets. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-20"/> <C id="amazon-business-b2b-02"/> <C id="amazon-business-b2b-08"/> Amazon's pitch is that "Business customers often buy in larger quantities and return items less." <C id="amazon-business-b2b-01"/> The questions that matter are which products justify a separate business price, how deep your tiers can go before margin disappears, and whether you hold the certifications that procurement teams filter for.

## What Amazon Business is, from each side

For buyers, Amazon Business is an account type for organizations. Its buyer site invites them to "Create a free account" and promises "instant savings on millions of products with quantity discounts and case packs." <C id="amazon-business-b2b-29"/> <C id="amazon-business-b2b-30"/> It also offers purchasing controls, described as "transparent guardrails and centralized approval processes." <C id="amazon-business-b2b-31"/>

For sellers, Amazon describes the program as tools and features inside your existing account. Its own figures for the buyer base are $35 billion in annualized sales driven by Amazon Business, 8 million organizations served globally (excluding emerging markets), and 97 of the Fortune 100 shopping on it. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-23"/> <C id="amazon-business-b2b-24"/> <C id="amazon-business-b2b-25"/> These are Amazon's marketing figures for the whole program, not a forecast for any single listing.

## Business prices and quantity discounts

A business price is a second price on the same offer, visible to Amazon Business customers. Amazon says setting business prices and quantity discounts "can increase your visibility and your chances of becoming the Featured Offer." <C id="amazon-business-b2b-03"/> There are three ways to set them:

If you manage listings through the Selling Partner API, Amazon's developer changelog says the Listings Items API and the JSON listings feed support Amazon Business pricing: you set the `audience` attribute to `B2B` to create a business offer and use `quantity_discount_plan` for quantity-based pricing. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-26"/> <C id="amazon-business-b2b-27"/>

### Seeing the business offer the way business buyers do

Amazon's offers lookup returns consumer offers unless you ask for business ones; the CustomerType setting takes Consumer or Business, with Consumer as the default. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-39"/> In the business view, each tiered price carries a quantityTier, the quantity at which it becomes active, <C id="amazon-business-b2b-40"/> and the B2B_ANY_OFFER_CHANGED notification reports the quantity tier of each offer. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-50"/>

One detail in Amazon's definitions explains an odd result. When a consumer (B2C) offer wins the Buy Box at a quantity-discount tier, Amazon says that is because others do not have inventory at that quantity, not because that seller has a quantity discount. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-41"/> If a competitor without tiers wins your 50-unit tier, check stock depth before cutting price.

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: a toner cartridge with a retail price of $40.00. You set a business price of $38.00, then a quantity discount of 5% at 10 units and 8% at 25 units, applied to the business price. A buyer ordering 30 units pays 30 × ($38.00 × 0.92) = 30 × $34.96 = $1,048.80, against 30 × $40.00 = $1,200.00 at retail. The $151.20 gap is what you give up for the larger order; set it against your own landed cost, fees and the lower return rate you expect before choosing tiers. The tier levels here are invented for the arithmetic and are not Amazon recommendations.
</Callout>

## Referral fees on larger orders

Among the program's benefits, Amazon lists: "Enjoy lower referral fees on larger purchases." <C id="amazon-business-b2b-17"/> The public program page does not publish the discount schedule. Sellers can see what they actually received in a Selling Partner API report called the Referral Fee Discounts Report (`FEE_DISCOUNTS_REPORT`), which "Contains a summary of the seller's fee discounts" and is available to Amazon Business sellers. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-28"/> Its columns include a target price and the referral fee at that target price. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-43"/> Its data can be up to 24 hours old, and Amazon asks that it not be requested more than once in 24 hours. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-42"/> For standard category referral rates, see the [referral fee guide](/guides/amazon-referral-fees/).

### Planning B2B prices before you know the discount

The discount schedule is not public, but the report above returns the inputs you need to measure it for your own listings. Three steps turn it into a pricing rule:

1. **Start from the standard rate.** Set business prices and quantity tiers so each one is profitable at your category's standard referral rate. The fee reduction on larger purchases can then only add to that margin. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-17"/>
2. **Read your effective rate from the report.** The report shows the referral fee at a target price; where those target prices line up with your quantity-tier prices, fee ÷ price gives the effective rate *e* at that tier — your own figure, measured rather than assumed. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-43"/>
3. **Fund quantity tiers from what you can see.** Where you can read an effective rate for a tier, you know how much of a deeper quantity discount the lower fee pays for, and how much comes out of your margin.

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example: how much of a quantity discount the fee saving covers">
Write *P* for the business price per unit, *q* for the quantity discount you offer at a tier, *r* for the standard referral rate and *e* for the effective rate the report shows at that tier.

- Revenue given up per unit by the discount: *P* × *q*.
- Referral fee saved per unit: *P* × *r* − *P* × (1 − *q*) × *e*.
- The tier costs you nothing extra when the saving at least equals the revenue given up. Any shortfall is a real price cut, and it should earn its keep in volume.

Run the calculation per tier: a deeper discount at a tier where *e* is barely below *r* is mostly paid from your margin.
</Callout>

The report data can be up to a day old, so re-run the calculation after changing a price rather than immediately. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-42"/>

## Bulk listings, quotes and B2B tools

Amazon suggests you "encourage bulk sales by creating package hierarchies and listing products as case packs and pallets." <C id="amazon-business-b2b-08"/> For product identifiers, see [barcodes and FNSKU](/guides/gs1-barcodes-fnsku/).

The other B2B Central tools Amazon names:

- **Manage quotes** — "Review and respond to quote requests using Manage quotes." <C id="amazon-business-b2b-07"/>
- **B2B Product Opportunities** — recommendations for B2B products to add. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-09"/>
- **Case Pack Product Opportunities and Business Discounts Insights** — which products suit bulk sale, and suggested B2B prices and discounts. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-10"/>

## Measuring B2B sales separately from retail

With FBA, business orders draw on the same inventory pool as retail orders, <C id="amazon-business-b2b-18"/> so B2B results need their own numbers. Amazon's data marks them in these fields:

| Where | Field | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Orders | IsBusinessOrder | True when the buyer is a Verified Business Buyer <C id="amazon-business-b2b-33"/> |
| Orders | PurchaseOrderNumber | Only returned when the buyer entered a PO number at checkout <C id="amazon-business-b2b-34"/> |
| Sales and Traffic report | unitsOrderedB2B | Units ordered by Amazon Business customers <C id="amazon-business-b2b-35"/> |
| Sales and Traffic report | buyBoxPercentageB2B | Share of business page views where the buy box showed for your product <C id="amazon-business-b2b-37"/> |
| Sales and Traffic report | unitSessionPercentageB2B | Units bought by business customers relative to business customers who viewed <C id="amazon-business-b2b-38"/> |

*Source: Amazon Selling Partner API models, Orders API v0 and Sales and Traffic report schema, checked Nov 9, 2026.* The B2B report fields are only populated for B2B sellers. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-36"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): did the quantity tiers work?">
Inputs: for one SKU, the four weeks before adding tiers show 120 unitsOrderedB2B and a unitSessionPercentageB2B of 6%; the four weeks after show 210 units and 9%. Units rose by 90 (75%), and conversion by 3 points. Using the earlier toner example's tier economics, the question is whether those extra 90 units at tier prices earn more than the margin you gave up on business units that would have sold anyway. Compare buyBoxPercentageB2B in the same weeks: if it did not change, the gain is less likely to have come from winning the business Buy Box more often.
</Callout>

## Certifications and the business profile

Many business buyers are asked to meet sourcing targets. Amazon says certifications "can help attract Amazon Business customers, many of whom are tasked with meeting quality sourcing objectives." <C id="amazon-business-b2b-32"/> You choose from a list of accepted certifications and "upload a digital copy of the certificate or provide a business identifier number (such as DUNS)." <C id="amazon-business-b2b-13"/> Certifications "appear to Amazon Business customers throughout the shopping experience" and are listed on your profile page. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-14"/>

<Callout type="warning" title="Amazon does not issue or vet your eligibility">
"Amazon is not a certifying body, nor does Amazon provide assistance to obtain credentials." <C id="amazon-business-b2b-12"/> Sellers are expected to determine their own eligibility, so only add a certificate you actually hold and can produce.
</Callout>

## Tax-exempt buyers

For tax-exempt buyers, Amazon points to the Amazon Tax-Exemption program: "Automate tax exemptions on qualified purchases by participating in the Amazon Tax-Exemption program." <C id="amazon-business-b2b-16"/> How marketplace facilitator rules shift sales tax collection is covered in the [sales tax nexus guide](/guides/sales-tax-nexus/).

## Fulfillment: FBA, your own warehouse, or both

"You can fulfill orders directly, use Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA), or use both methods on a per-product basis." <C id="amazon-business-b2b-19"/> With FBA, B2B orders draw on "the same inventory pool used to fulfill orders from retail customers," minus anything you reserved by creating business-only offers. <C id="amazon-business-b2b-18"/> If you ship yourself, plan for receiving hours: Amazon notes that "Business customers expect deliveries during regular business hours." <C id="amazon-business-b2b-15"/>

## Where to check the rules and your own B2B numbers

Re-check Amazon's [Amazon Business seller program page](https://sell.amazon.com/programs/amazon-business) for features and steps, and the [selling plans page](https://sell.amazon.com/pricing) for the Professional plan price. The referral fee discount schedule and the Tax-Exemption program terms are published in Seller Central; to find them, search Seller Central Help using words such as business pricing or tax exemption (suggested searches, not page titles). For your own account, the `FEE_DISCOUNTS_REPORT` shows discounts applied, the [B2B product opportunities report types](https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/report-type-values-b2b-product-opportunities) show what to add, and the B2B columns of the Sales and Traffic report show what business buyers did. This page explains how the program is described publicly; your agreement with Amazon and the terms inside Seller Central govern what applies to your account.
