Keep FBA units when the storage they will incur before they sell is less than what removing them costs and loses; remove them when the months-to-sell stretch into the aged-inventory period or the October–December rate. Amazon can return, dispose of or liquidate inventory in a fulfillment center for a per-item charge, while holding costs monthly storage and, after 181 days, an aged inventory charge. <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-01"/> <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-03"/> Check the current per-item removal rate and aged inventory rate in Seller Central before running the numbers below.

## What holding costs

FBA storage is charged monthly based on the daily average volume your inventory occupies, measured in cubic feet. <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-02"/> Amazon quotes standard-size storage at $0.78 per cubic foot per month from January to September and $2.40 from October to December. <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-04"/> On top of that, aged inventory is "charged monthly for all items stored in a fulfillment center for more than 181 days." <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-03"/> Exceeding your restock limit may also increase storage costs. <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-06"/>

For customer returns, FBA Grade and Resell gives sellers a resell channel on Amazon, with Amazon inspecting each item and assigning a condition such as "Like New" or "Acceptable." <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-16"/> Amazon's ecommerce fulfillment guide lists removal, disposal and liquidation orders among the potential charges that inventory management and best practices can help you avoid. <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-05"/>

Because MCF and FBA draw from one inventory pool, slow stock can fill orders from your website or other channels without first being removed. <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-14"/> See [MCF for off-Amazon orders](/guides/mcf-off-amazon-orders/) for the fee table.

## The report columns that give you the inputs

Amazon's Selling Partner API documentation lists the fields in each FBA report, so you can see exactly which column answers which part of the decision.

| Report (SP-API report type) | What it is for | Columns to read |
|---|---|---|
| Manage Inventory Health (`GET_FBA_INVENTORY_PLANNING_DATA`) | Sales over 7, 30, 60 and 90 days, estimated storage and long-term storage fees, aged and excess units | `days-of-supply`, `estimated-storage-cost-next-month`, `recommended-removal-quantity`, `pending-removal-quantity` |
| Storage Fees (`GET_FBA_STORAGE_FEE_CHARGES_DATA`) | Estimated monthly storage fees for each ASIN | `average_quantity_on_hand`, `average_quantity_pending_removal` |
| Recommended Removal (`GET_FBA_RECOMMENDED_REMOVAL_DATA`) | Sellable items that will be 365 days old or older at the next long-term storage cleanup | `sellable-181-270-days`, `sellable-271-365-days`, `sellable-365+-days`, `sellable-removal-quantity` |
| Removal Order Detail (`GET_FBA_FULFILLMENT_REMOVAL_ORDER_DETAIL_DATA`) | All removal orders and the items in each, for a chosen period | `order-type`, `order-status`, `requested-quantity`, `shipped-quantity`, `disposed-quantity`, `removal-fee` |
| Removal Shipment Detail (`GET_FBA_FULFILLMENT_REMOVAL_SHIPMENT_DETAIL_DATA`) | Shipment tracking for removal orders | `shipment-date`, `carrier`, `tracking-number` |

*Source: Amazon Selling Partner API documentation, FBA report types, checked Nov 9, 2026.* <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-08"/> <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-25"/> <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-09"/> <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-31"/> <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-07"/> <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-24"/> <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-10"/> <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-19"/> <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-11"/> <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-22"/>

Two timing details matter. The Recommended Removal report flags 365-day units only "if the report is generated within six weeks of the cleanup event date," although it covers sellable and unsellable stock and is refreshed daily. <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-23"/> And `pending-removal-quantity` tells you which units are already on a removal order, so you do not count them twice when you plan the next one. <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-25"/>

## The decision method

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs (your own figures, chosen for illustration): 200 standard-size units, each 0.25 cu ft, margin after fees $6 per unit. The removal charge per unit is written *R*: take it from Seller Central rather than assuming one.

Case A — sells 100 units a month, starting in October: 2 months to clear. Average stock is about 150 units in month 1 (37.5 cu ft × $2.40 = $90) and 50 units in month 2 (12.5 cu ft × $2.40 = $30): $120 in total, or $0.60 per unit. That is well below the $6 margin: hold.

Case B — sells 10 units a month, starting in January: 20 months to clear. Each unit costs 0.25 × $0.78 = about $0.20 a month from January to September and 0.25 × $2.40 = $0.60 a month from October to December, so a typical unit that waits a year costs about $3.56 in storage alone, before aged inventory charges that start after 181 days. Most units would pass that point, so holding could eat much of the $6 margin. Removing costs 200 × *R*. As long as *R* is below the storage a unit would otherwise pay while it waits (about $3.56 a year here, before aged inventory charges), removal and resale elsewhere, or liquidation, deserves a serious look.
</Callout>

Check of Case B: 9 × $0.195 + 3 × $0.60 = $1.755 + $1.80 = $3.555, rounded to $3.56. <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-04"/>

## Liquidation: what actually reaches your account

In Amazon's Finances API, an FBA liquidation payment event carries two amounts: what the liquidator paid for your inventory, and the liquidation fee. Amazon's model says the seller "receives this amount minus `LiquidationFeeAmount`." <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-27"/> That event "is used only in the US marketplace." <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-28"/> For units returned to you, the removal shipment item's `FeeAmount` is "The fee that Amazon charged to the seller for the removal of the item," recorded as a negative number. <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-26"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): three exits for 200 units">
Inputs: 200 slow units. Amazon's amounts are kept as letters because they come from Seller Central and your settlement data: *R* is the removal charge per unit, *P* the amount the liquidator pays per unit and *L* the liquidation fee per unit. *D* is the disposal charge per unit. *S* is what one unit nets when you resell it elsewhere after your own shipping and handling (your estimate).

- **Return to you and resell:** 200 × (*S* − *R*).
- **Liquidate:** 200 × (*P* − *L*), with no further storage. <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-27"/>
- **Dispose:** −200 × *D*; only sensible when the units cannot be sold. <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-01"/>

Returning beats liquidating whenever *S* − *R* is larger than *P* − *L*. Put differently, the resale route has to net at least the liquidation payout plus the removal charge, after your own handling. Compare the winning figure with the holding cost from Case B above; if resale elsewhere is not realistic, liquidation is the exit that turns stock into a payout.
</Callout>

## What changes for held stock on February 1, 2027

Amazon will "remove the aged inventory surcharge and combine it with monthly storage fees into a single, age-based rate," with storage changes effective February 1, 2027. <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-29"/> <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-32"/> Amazon says that means flat or lower fees for inventory turning in under six months and higher fees for aged inventory. <C id="removal-vs-hold-decision-30"/> For the remove-or-hold math, the separate aged-inventory step in Case B is replaced by the age-based storage rate, which Amazon says will mean higher fees for aged inventory. The age bands and reports are covered in [the aged inventory surcharge and the 15-month tier](/guides/aged-inventory-15-month-tier/).

## Where to check removal and storage rates

Check the per-item removal, disposal and liquidation rates and the aged inventory rates in Seller Central, and the fee descriptions at sell.amazon.com/fulfillment-by-amazon. Re-check standard-size storage rates at sell.amazon.com/programs/supply-chain and AWD's rule on FBA inventory at sell.amazon.com/programs/warehousing. Pull the Manage Inventory Health and Recommended Removal reports for current, ASIN-level inputs, and the Removal Order Detail report after any removal.
