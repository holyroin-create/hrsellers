A customs bond is the guarantee that the importer will pay duties and meet CBP's requirements, backed by a surety or by cash. For a formal entry it is not optional: merchandise is not released from CBP custody when CBP receives the entry documentation unless a single entry or continuous bond on CBP Form 301, containing the bond conditions in 19 CFR 113.62, has been filed. <C id="customs-bonds-01" /> The bond must be executed by an approved corporate surety or secured by cash deposits or U.S. obligations. <C id="customs-bonds-01" />

The choice a seller makes is between the two forms that 19 CFR 113.62 allows: the basic importation and entry bond "may be either a single transaction or a continuous bond." <C id="customs-bonds-02" /> The amount is set by CBP under 19 CFR 113.13, which fixes a $100 floor and lists the factors CBP weighs. <C id="customs-bonds-09" /> <C id="customs-bonds-10" />

<Callout type="note" title="Scope of this page">
This page summarizes the bond regulations. It does not calculate the bond amount for your imports. Entry types and filing deadlines are covered in [Entry type 11 vs 01](/guides/entry-type-11-vs-01/).
</Callout>

## When a bond is required

Several provisions trigger a bond:

- **Formal entry release.** The 19 CFR 142.4(a) rule above. <C id="customs-bonds-01" />
- **A small-value waiver.** The port director may waive the surety or cash deposit on that bond when the merchandise value does not exceed $2,500, the entry summary is filed and estimated duties are deposited before release, and the importer has not been delinquent or otherwise remiss. The waiver does not apply to quota merchandise, merchandise that cannot be easily appraised or classified, or goods where redelivery may be in question. <C id="customs-bonds-03" />
- **Nonresident corporations.** A corporation not incorporated in the U.S. customs territory or the U.S. Virgin Islands may not enter merchandise for consumption unless it has a resident agent for service of process and files a bond on Form 301 with the 113.62 conditions, having a resident corporate surety. <C id="customs-bonds-04" /> More on foreign importers in [Who is the importer when your supplier quotes DDP or FOB](/guides/incoterms-who-is-importer/).
- **CBP's general authority.** Where a bond is not specifically required by law or regulation, the Commissioner of CBP may require bonds or other security considered necessary to protect the revenue or assure compliance. <C id="customs-bonds-05" />

## Single transaction vs continuous

| Point | Single transaction bond | Continuous bond |
|---|---|---|
| Who approves | Revenue Division or the director of the port where filed | Revenue Division |
| How many | No per-principal limit in 19 CFR 113.12 | Only one per activity for each principal |
| Application | Port director may require one | Application states the general character of the merchandise and the total ordinary customs duties |
| Effective date | Date of the transaction on CBP Form 301 | Effective date on CBP Form 301 |

*Source: eCFR, 19 CFR 113.11, 113.12 and 113.26, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="customs-bonds-06" /> <C id="customs-bonds-07" /> <C id="customs-bonds-08" /> <C id="customs-bonds-14" />

Two details matter once a continuous bond is in place. If facts in the application change significantly, a new application must be filed no later than 30 days after the new facts become known to the principal. <C id="customs-bonds-13" /> A continuous bond, its application or a rider may be filed up to 60 days before the requested effective date. <C id="customs-bonds-14" /> And because a principal may hold only one continuous bond for a particular activity, <C id="customs-bonds-07" /> raising coverage means changing that bond rather than adding a second one.

## How CBP sets the amount

Part 113 does not give a formula. It gives a floor and a list of factors.

**The floor.** The amount of any CBP bond must not be less than $100, unless a law or regulation expressly allows less, and the amount is stated as the next highest dollar. <C id="customs-bonds-09" />

**The factors.** In deciding whether a bond amount is sufficient, CBP considers: <C id="customs-bonds-10" />

1. The principal's prior record of timely payment of duties, taxes and charges.
2. The principal's prior record of complying with CBP demands for redelivery, holding unexamined merchandise intact and other enforcement requirements.
3. The value and nature of the merchandise involved.
4. The degree and type of supervision CBP will exercise.
5. The principal's prior record of honoring bond commitments, including payment of liquidated damages.
6. Any additional information in the bond application.

**Ongoing review.** CBP periodically reviews each bond on file to decide whether it is adequate to protect the revenue and ensure compliance; once notified of a deficiency, the principal has 15 days to remedy it. <C id="customs-bonds-11" /> If CBP believes accepting a transaction under a continuous bond would place the revenue in jeopardy, it may immediately require additional security. <C id="customs-bonds-12" />

## What the bond commits you to

Under the basic importation and entry bond, the principal and surety, "jointly and severally," agree among other things to deposit, within the time prescribed, any duties, taxes and charges imposed or estimated to be due. <C id="customs-bonds-16" /> The bond conditions also cover producing documents, redelivering merchandise on demand and other entry obligations, and a breach is priced as liquidated damages. <C id="customs-bonds-16" />

| Default under 19 CFR 113.62 | Liquidated damages |
|---|---|
| General default involving merchandise | Value of the merchandise involved |
| Same, if merchandise is restricted or prohibited | Three times the value of the merchandise involved |
| Defaults covered by paragraph (n)(3) | $1,000 for each default |
| Failure to deposit duties when due | Two times the unpaid duties, taxes and charges, or $1,000, whichever is greater |
| Violations under paragraphs (k) and (l) | $5,000 for each violation |

*Source: eCFR, 19 CFR 113.62, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="customs-bonds-17" /> <C id="customs-bonds-18" /> <C id="customs-bonds-19" /> <C id="customs-bonds-20" />

Late paperwork also triggers the bond: if an entry summary is not filed on time, the port director "shall make an immediate demand for liquidated damages." <C id="customs-bonds-21" />

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical values)">
An importer defaults on a deposit of estimated duties of $300. Two times the unpaid amount is $600, which is less than $1,000, so under the "whichever is greater" rule the liquidated damages claim would be $1,000. With $800 unpaid, two times is $1,600, which exceeds $1,000, so the claim would be $1,600.
</Callout>

## Ending a bond

A principal's written request to terminate a bond takes effect on the date requested if that date is at least 10 business days after CBP receives it. <C id="customs-bonds-15" /> A surety may not disavow obligations already incurred, but it may terminate its agreement to accept future obligations with reasonable notice, and 30 days is reasonable notice unless the surety shows a shorter time frame is reasonable. <C id="customs-bonds-22" /> After termination, no new customs transactions may be charged against the bond, and a new bond must be filed before further customs activity. <C id="customs-bonds-23" />

## Proposed: bonds for informal entries

Informal entries are currently bonded only in certain situations. <C id="customs-bonds-24" /> CBP's notice of proposed rulemaking "Low-Value Shipments," published October 8, 2026, would require all entry type 11 filers to obtain a basic importation and entry bond, single transaction or continuous, and would set a new $1,000 minimum for each breach of a listed bond condition. <C id="customs-bonds-25" /> <C id="customs-bonds-26" /> Comments are due on or before December 7, 2026; it is a proposal, not a final rule. <C id="customs-bonds-27" />
