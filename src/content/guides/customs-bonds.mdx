You need a customs bond to get goods released at entry: under 19 CFR 142.4, merchandise is not released when CBP receives the entry documents unless a single entry or continuous bond on Customs Form 301 has been filed. <C id="customs-bonds-01"/> The bond must be executed by an approved corporate surety or secured by cash or U.S. government obligations. <C id="customs-bonds-02"/> Informal entries need one today only in certain situations, but a pending CBP proposal would change that. <C id="customs-bonds-25"/> <C id="customs-bonds-24"/>

## How CBP decides the amount

The regulations set one hard number: the amount of any CBP bond must not be less than $100. <C id="customs-bonds-05"/> Above that floor, sufficiency is a judgment. The first factor 19 CFR 113.13 lists is your prior record of timely payment of duties, taxes and charges, and several further factors follow it. <C id="customs-bonds-06"/> For a continuous bond, that application states the duties and taxes accruing on everything you imported during the preceding calendar year. <C id="customs-bonds-08"/>

If CBP finds your bond insufficient, you have 15 days from the notice to remedy the deficiency. <C id="customs-bonds-07"/> More generally, where no bond is specifically required by law or regulation, CBP may still require bonds or other security considered necessary for the protection of the revenue. <C id="customs-bonds-04"/> Section 113.13 lists factors rather than a formula, so ask your surety to show how it arrived at the amount it proposes. <C id="customs-bonds-06"/>

<Callout type="tip" title="Where the bond comes from">
Corporate sureties must appear on Treasury Department Circular 570, which lists the corporations authorized to act as sureties on bonds. If a provider offers a bond, check that its surety is on the current Circular. <C id="customs-bonds-11"/>
</Callout>

## What you promise when you sign

CBP states that the terms and conditions for Activity Code 1 bonds are set forth in 19 CFR 113.62, the basic importation and entry bond. <C id="customs-bonds-19"/> Its conditions read like a checklist of your obligations as importer.

<Checklist title="Core conditions of the basic importation and entry bond" items={[
  "Deposit duties, taxes and charges within the time prescribed by law or regulation",
  "Redeliver conditionally released merchandise on CBP's demand in the cases 113.62(d) lists",
  "Comply with Importer Security Filing rules — or pay $5,000 per violation",
  "Pay liquidated damages set by the type of default"
]} />

Each line has a source: the duty deposit promise is in 113.62(a), the redelivery promise in 113.62(d), and the $5,000-per-violation figure for Importer Security Filing in 113.62(j). <C id="customs-bonds-12"/> <C id="customs-bonds-13"/> <C id="customs-bonds-14"/> When a default occurs, the damages scale with it. Defaults without their own amount are measured at the value of the merchandise involved, or three times that value for restricted or prohibited merchandise or alcoholic beverages. <C id="customs-bonds-15"/> Failure to deposit estimated duties brings two times the unpaid amount or $1,000, whichever is greater. <C id="customs-bonds-16"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): unpaid estimated duties">
Inputs: rule in 19 CFR 113.62(n)(4) — the greater of 2 × unpaid estimated duties or $1,000.
**Shipment A, $400 unpaid:** 2 × $400 = $800, which is below $1,000, so the claim is $1,000.
**Shipment B, $3,000 unpaid:** 2 × $3,000 = $6,000, which exceeds $1,000, so the claim is $6,000.
</Callout>

## Liquidated damages at a glance

Liquidated damages are the bond's enforcement tool: amounts the obligors agree in advance to pay when a condition is broken. The amounts depend on which condition was breached.

| Default | Liquidated damages under the bond |
|---|---|
| Importer Security Filing violation | $5,000 for each violation |
| Other defaults without their own amount | Value of the merchandise involved, or three times the value for restricted or prohibited goods or alcoholic beverages |
| Failure to deposit estimated duties | Two times the unpaid amount or $1,000, whichever is greater |
| Proposed: breach of a listed condition on type 11 and type 13 informal entries | New $1,000 minimum per breach (not yet final) |

*Source: eCFR — 19 CFR 113.62; Federal Register, Low-Value Shipments proposed rule; checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="customs-bonds-14"/> <C id="customs-bonds-15"/> <C id="customs-bonds-16"/> <C id="customs-bonds-26"/>

The practical lesson is that the cheapest bond is the one never called. Timely duty deposits and complete Importer Security Filings protect your payment record, and that record is the first factor CBP weighs when it judges whether your bond is sufficient. <C id="customs-bonds-06"/>

## Waivers, cash and nonresidents

The port director may waive the requirement for surety or cash deposit on the entry bond when the goods are valued at $2,500 or less, the entry summary is filed and estimated duties deposited before release, and the importer has not been delinquent. <C id="customs-bonds-03"/> A nonresident consignee may make entry, but any bond taken in connection with the entry must have a resident corporate surety. <C id="customs-bonds-23"/>

Cash is the alternative to a surety. Section 142.4 allows the entry bond to be secured by cash deposits or obligations of the United States instead of a corporate surety, and under the eBond proposal a bond secured by cash in lieu of surety would be transmitted to CBP by the principal itself. <C id="customs-bonds-02"/> <C id="customs-bonds-27"/> Weigh the working capital that stays locked up against the premium a surety would charge.

Under the eBond proposal, bonds secured by a surety would be transmitted by the surety or its authorized agent, and cash bonds by the principal. <C id="customs-bonds-21"/> <C id="customs-bonds-27"/> The Low-Value Shipments proposal would also set a new $1,000 minimum in liquidated damages for each breach of a listed bond condition on type 11 and type 13 informal shipments. <C id="customs-bonds-26"/> Both are still proposals; both signal that a bond is becoming a baseline cost of importing rather than a formal-entry extra.

## How to confirm what applies to you

Read 19 CFR Part 113 (bond amounts, approval and the basic importation bond conditions) and 142.4 (when a bond is required for release) on eCFR. <C id="customs-bonds-05"/> <C id="customs-bonds-01"/> Your surety's bond application will show the activity code and amount it proposes; compare that with the factors in 113.13. Sufficiency is CBP's call under 113.13, not the surety's, and nothing here substitutes for that determination. <C id="customs-bonds-06"/>
