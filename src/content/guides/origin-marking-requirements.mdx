If you import goods to sell on Amazon, Walmart, eBay, Etsy or your own store, each item must be marked with its country of origin in a conspicuous place, as legibly, indelibly and permanently as the item allows, so that the ultimate purchaser can see it. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-09"/> For goods sold at retail in their imported form, that ultimate purchaser is the retail buyer. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-05"/> Getting this wrong costs an additional duty of 10 percent of the final appraised value. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-11"/> The rules sit in 19 CFR Part 134, which implements the marking requirements and exceptions of section 304 of the Tariff Act. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-01"/>

## What "marked" means in practice

Part 134 defines country of origin as the country of manufacture, production or growth of an article of foreign origin entering the United States. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-02"/> If work is done in a second country, that country becomes the origin only if the work effects a substantial transformation. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-03"/> The [country of origin and sourcing guide](/guides/country-of-origin-sourcing/) covers how that test works.

The marking itself has three practical tests:

- **Conspicuous.** It must be capable of being easily seen with normal handling of the article or container. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-10"/> The ultimate purchaser must be able to find it easily and read it without strain. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-14"/>
- **Durable.** It must survive normal distribution and store handling. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-13"/> A label that peels off in a fulfillment center tote fails that test.
- **Correctly worded.** It must use the full English name of the country of origin. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-15"/> Abbreviations that unmistakably indicate a country, such as "Gt. Britain," are acceptable. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-16"/>

### When your packaging mentions the U.S.

Many private-label sellers print a U.S. business address on the box. If words such as "United States," "American" or "U.S.A.," or the name of a U.S. city, appear on the article or its container and may mislead the ultimate purchaser about the actual origin, a stricter rule applies. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-20"/> The country of origin must then appear in close proximity and in at least a comparable size, preceded by "Made in," "Product of" or similar words. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-21"/> A small "China" on the bottom panel next to a large "Designed in Austin, Texas" on the front is exactly the situation this rule targets.

## Who the ultimate purchaser is

The whole marking system is built around one person: the ultimate purchaser, generally the last person in the United States who will receive the article in its imported form. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-04"/> Who that is depends on what happens to the goods after import.

## Exceptions that come up for small importers

19 CFR 134.32 lists general exceptions. The ones most relevant to marketplace sellers:

| Exception | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Incapable of being marked | Articles that physically cannot carry a mark <C id="origin-marking-requirements-25"/> |
| Injury | Articles that cannot be marked before shipment without injury <C id="origin-marking-requirements-26"/> |
| Prohibitive cost | Articles that cannot be marked before shipment except at an economically prohibitive expense <C id="origin-marking-requirements-27"/> |
| Container tells the story | Articles whose marked containers will reasonably indicate their origin <C id="origin-marking-requirements-28"/> |
| Own use | Articles imported for the importer's own use and not for sale in any form <C id="origin-marking-requirements-29"/> |
| Purchaser must know | Articles whose ultimate purchaser must necessarily know the origin <C id="origin-marking-requirements-30"/> |
| Antiques | Articles produced more than 20 years before importation <C id="origin-marking-requirements-31"/> |
| U.S. goods returned | Products of the United States exported and returned <C id="origin-marking-requirements-32"/> |

*Source: eCFR — 19 CFR 134.32, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="origin-marking-requirements-25"/>

Section 134.33, the "J-list," separately excepts named articles such as works of art, cigars and cigarettes, eggs, feathers and firewood. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-33"/> Check the full list against your exact product before relying on it.

### An exception for the item is not an exception for the box

When an article is excepted, the outermost container in which it ordinarily reaches the ultimate purchaser must be marked instead. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-22"/> A "usual container" is the container in which a good will ordinarily reach its ultimate purchaser. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-23"/> Containers that are treated as imported articles in their own right under the tariff schedule must show their own country of origin, in addition to the origin of their contents. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-24"/>

## What happens when goods arrive unmarked

Articles held for inspection will not be delivered until they are marked with their country of origin. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-44"/> CBP notifies the importer on Customs Form 4647 or its electronic equivalent. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-40"/> Articles in examination packages that are not exported, destroyed or properly marked within a reasonable time, not more than 30 days, are sent to general-order storage. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-41"/>

If goods have already been released and CBP demands redelivery, you have 30 days from the date of the notice of redelivery to comply. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-42"/> Miss that and CBP demands liquidated damages under your bond equal to the entered value of the articles. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-43"/> On top of that, the 10 percent marking duty applies unless the goods are exported or destroyed under Customs supervision. <C id="origin-marking-requirements-12"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: 2,000 phone stands with a final appraised value of $6,000 arrive with no origin marking and are released before the problem is caught. The marking duty is 10% × $6,000 = $600. If a redelivery notice goes unanswered for more than 30 days, liquidated damages equal to the entered value (assume it is also $6,000) can also be demanded.
</Callout>

## Common mistakes and how to avoid them

| Mistake | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Origin printed only on the master carton | The ultimate purchaser is the retail buyer, who never sees the carton <C id="origin-marking-requirements-05"/> | Mark each unit or its retail package |
| A country code or unclear abbreviation | The rule calls for the full English country name; only abbreviations that unmistakably indicate the country are accepted <C id="origin-marking-requirements-15"/> <C id="origin-marking-requirements-16"/> | Print "Made in China" |
| U.S. address large, origin tiny and elsewhere | U.S. references that may mislead trigger the "Made in" proximity rule <C id="origin-marking-requirements-21"/> | Put the origin line right next to the address |
| Loose sticker that falls off in transit | Marking must survive normal distribution and store handling <C id="origin-marking-requirements-13"/> | Use print, sewn labels or a strong adhesive in a visible spot |
| Bundling at a prep center with no certification | Where Part 134 requires a repacking certification, it is filed at entry summary <C id="origin-marking-requirements-37"/> | Plan repacks before the goods ship |

*Source: eCFR — 19 CFR Part 134, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="origin-marking-requirements-09"/>

## How to confirm it for your goods

Read 19 CFR Part 134 on the eCFR, starting with the definitions in subpart A, the exceptions in subpart D and the methods in subpart E, and match your product, packaging and any planned repacking against the exact text. If your item may fall under the J-list or a general exception, check the specific entry rather than a similar one, because the exceptions are narrow and the container rule usually still applies.
