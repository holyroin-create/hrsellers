A state can require an out-of-state seller to register and collect its sales or use tax once sales into that state pass the state's economic threshold, and for marketplace orders that duty usually shifts to the marketplace itself. The thresholds are not uniform: California and Texas use $500,000, while Florida, Illinois and Washington use $100,000. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-01"/> <C id="sales-tax-nexus-07"/> <C id="sales-tax-nexus-12"/> <C id="sales-tax-nexus-17"/> <C id="sales-tax-nexus-21"/> The table below gives each state's rule as its own revenue department states it, then explains what changes when a marketplace facilitator is in the picture.

## Thresholds in five large states

| State | Economic nexus threshold | Measurement period | Marketplace rule | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| California | Sales of tangible personal property for delivery in CA over $500,000 (including related persons) | Preceding or current calendar year | Facilitator generally pays or collects the tax on facilitated sales (from Oct 1, 2019) | CDTFA |
| Texas | Safe harbor below $500,000 total Texas revenue | Preceding twelve calendar months | Seller using only a certifying marketplace provider needs no permit | Texas Comptroller |
| Florida | Remote sales of taxable items delivered to FL of $100,000 or more | Previous calendar year | Facilitated sales reported by the marketplace provider | Florida DOR (DR-1MP) |
| Illinois | $100,000 or more in gross receipts; 200-transaction test removed Jan 1, 2026 per Bulletin FY 2026-12 (Dec 2025) | Lookback checked quarterly | Same test applies to marketplace facilitators | Illinois DOR |
| Washington | More than $100,000 in combined gross receipts, marketplace sales included | Current or prior year | Seller need not collect on facilitated sales, with documentation | Washington DOR |

The wording differs in ways that matter. California's figure must be exceeded, Florida's is met at "$100,000 or more," and Washington's needs "more than $100,000." <C id="sales-tax-nexus-01"/> <C id="sales-tax-nexus-12"/> <C id="sales-tax-nexus-21"/> Texas frames its number as a safe harbor: remote sellers with less than $500,000 in Texas revenue over the preceding twelve months are not required to get a permit. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-07"/>

The Illinois sources don't match yet. Bulletin FY 2026-12, issued in December 2025, says the 200-transaction threshold no longer applies from January 1, 2026. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-16"/> The department's main retailers' occupation tax page, which shows no update date, still lists the older either-or test: $100,000 or more in cumulative gross receipts or 200 or more separate transactions. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-15"/> The bulletin is the dated statement of the change; if your Illinois sales are near either figure, confirm with the department.

## What a marketplace facilitator changes

A marketplace facilitator (Texas and Florida say "marketplace provider") is the platform that takes on collection for third-party sellers. Illinois defines it as a person who, under an agreement with an unrelated third-party marketplace seller, facilitates sales for that seller. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-20"/> In California, the facilitator is considered the seller and retailer for each sale through its marketplace when deciding whether it must register, and since October 1, 2019 it has generally been required to pay or collect the tax on the retail sales it facilitates; the marketplace seller is no longer liable for the tax on those transactions. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-03"/> <C id="sales-tax-nexus-04"/> <C id="sales-tax-nexus-05"/> Florida says facilitated sales will be reported by the marketplace provider. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-14"/>

That relief has edges. It covers the facilitated sales, not everything you sell:

Washington is the clearest example of a state where selling only through marketplaces does not end the conversation. Sales made through a marketplace facilitator count toward the $100,000 threshold, and marketplace sellers who meet nexus must report all sales sourced to the state and pay business and occupation (B&O) tax. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-22"/> <C id="sales-tax-nexus-25"/> <C id="sales-tax-nexus-24"/> The exemption from collecting on facilitated sales also depends on having documentation that the facilitator collects the tax. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-23"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
A seller ships $45,000 of goods to Washington buyers through a marketplace and $70,000 through its own website in one calendar year, for $115,000 combined. Because Washington includes marketplace sales, the seller is over the "more than $100,000" test. The marketplace collects on the $45,000; the seller must register, collect on the $70,000 of website sales, and report B&O tax.

The same seller has $300,000 of Texas revenue in the preceding twelve months. That is below Texas's $500,000 safe harbor, so no Texas permit is required for remote sales.
</Callout>

## The facilitator's own threshold is a separate test

Facilitators measure nexus too, and their count includes the sales they make on behalf of sellers. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-06"/> <C id="sales-tax-nexus-13"/> Florida requires a marketplace provider to register when it facilitated taxable remote sales into the state of $100,000 or more in the previous calendar year. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-13"/> Illinois Bulletin FY 2026-12 applies the same $100,000 test to remote retailers and marketplace facilitators from January 1, 2026. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-16"/> For a seller, the facilitator's registration doesn't answer the separate question of whether your own direct channels put you over a threshold.

That second question is where multi-channel sellers get caught. A brand that sells through two marketplaces and its own site can sit under every state line on each channel individually, yet cross a threshold once the channels are added together in a state like Washington that counts them all. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-22"/> Run the numbers by state, by channel, and in total.

## When collection has to start

Crossing a threshold doesn't always mean collecting the next day, and the deadlines differ.

Illinois requires remote retailers to collect and remit both state and local retailers' occupation tax at the destination rate. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-19"/> On its remote seller page, Texas lists the current single local use tax rate as 1.75 percent. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-11"/>

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What the state pages say |
|---|---|
| Leaving marketplace sales out of your Washington total | Sales through a marketplace facilitator count toward the threshold |
| Relying on the 200-transaction figure still shown on Illinois's main ROT page | Bulletin FY 2026-12 says the 200-transaction test no longer applies from January 1, 2026 |
| Assuming marketplace-only means no records in Texas | All sellers keep marketplace sales records for four years |
| Treating California's figure as per-entity | Sales by the retailer and all related persons are combined |
| Assuming no B&O tax in Washington because the marketplace collects | Marketplace sellers meeting nexus must report and pay B&O tax |

*Source: Washington DOR, Illinois DOR, Texas Comptroller and CDTFA, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="sales-tax-nexus-22"/> <C id="sales-tax-nexus-16"/> <C id="sales-tax-nexus-15"/> <C id="sales-tax-nexus-09"/> <C id="sales-tax-nexus-01"/> <C id="sales-tax-nexus-24"/>

Illinois's main retailers' occupation tax page also defines a remote retailer as an out-of-state retailer with no physical presence in Illinois who meets a tax remittance threshold — the economic test is what reaches sellers who only ship in. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-15"/> California's facilitator notice tells marketplace facilitators to include all sales of tangible merchandise for delivery in California, including sales they facilitate for marketplace sellers, when measuring their own threshold. <C id="sales-tax-nexus-06"/>

## How to confirm it for your state

Go to the revenue department page for each state you ship to: [CDTFA](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/wayfair.htm), the [Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/sales/remote-sellers.php), the [Florida Department of Revenue](https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/Documents/dr1mp.pdf), the [Illinois Department of Revenue](https://tax.illinois.gov/research/publications/bulletins/fy-2026-12.html) and the [Washington Department of Revenue](https://dor.wa.gov/taxes-rates/retail-sales-tax/marketplace-fairness-leveling-playing-field/remote-sellers). This page covers five states only; every other state sets its own test. A written ruling or guidance request to the state's revenue department settles how a threshold applies to your particular sales mix.
