Grouping products into an Amazon variation family puts their reviews in front of shoppers together. Amazon's guidance limits a family to the same product in different sizes, colors or other attributes, and the FTC has looked at the separate practice of making a review written for one product appear to be about a substantially different one. Decide which side of that line each product is on before you build or merge a family, and once it is built, check which child the reviews are actually about.

This page draws on Amazon's public seller guidance, its catalog and review-data API documentation, and the FTC's rulemaking record. Amazon's category-by-category variation policy is published in Seller Central Help; read the version for your category before you change a family.

## How a variation family is built

Amazon describes variations as sets of products related to each other by color, size, or other attributes. <C id="variations-review-pooling-01"/> A family has three parts:

  {t:"Children", d:"The buyable products related to the parent.", claim:"variations-review-pooling-03"},

In clothing, for example, Amazon describes sharing color, print and size options for a single product by attaching multiple child ASINs to a parent ASIN. <C id="variations-review-pooling-29"/> The same structure exists at the listing level: a listing can be marked as a variation parent or child of other listings by SKU, <C id="variations-review-pooling-14"/> and Amazon's product type schemas for variation family listings are requested by parentage level. <C id="variations-review-pooling-15"/> To audit a family, you can pull every listing that is a child of a given parent SKU. <C id="variations-review-pooling-16"/>

The same family is visible at catalog level, by ASIN rather than SKU. In the Catalog Items API, catalog item relationships list childAsins and parentAsins, <C id="variations-review-pooling-36"/> and a VARIATION relationship carries a variation theme naming the attribute combination that defines the family; <C id="variations-review-pooling-33"/> Amazon's schema gives COLOR_NAME/STYLE_NAME as an example theme. <C id="variations-review-pooling-34"/> A separate relationship type, PACKAGE_HIERARCHY, describes a package container and its contents. <C id="variations-review-pooling-35"/>

| What you are checking | Listing level (your SKUs) | Catalog level (ASINs) |
|---|---|---|
| Who is parent and child | parentSkus / childSkus on a VARIATION relationship | parentAsins / childAsins |
| What defines the family | Variation theme on the listing relationship | variationTheme, e.g. COLOR_NAME/STYLE_NAME |
| Pack versus variation | — | PACKAGE_HIERARCHY is a separate relationship type |

### Themes are set by category

Each category has different variation themes, <C id="variations-review-pooling-05"/> and not every category allows variations; some allow only select ones. <C id="variations-review-pooling-08"/> Amazon names Pet Supplies, Toys & Games, Sports & Outdoors, Grocery, Health & Beauty, and Clothing, Accessories & Shoes among the categories that allow them. <C id="variations-review-pooling-07"/> Theme values must stay clean: color and size variations shouldn't include any information other than the color and size. <C id="variations-review-pooling-06"/>

## What belongs in one family

Amazon gives three tests. Parent and child products should be the same type, brand, and style. <C id="variations-review-pooling-04"/> To count as a variation, the product should have a standard design and function but come in different sizes, colors, or other attributes. <C id="variations-review-pooling-09"/> And a new version of an older product should get a new listing rather than be added as a variation of the old one, because that could mislead customers. <C id="variations-review-pooling-12"/>

Duplicate detail pages are a separate matter: Amazon's DUPLICATE listing issue means the ASIN may be a duplicate of another ASIN. <C id="variations-review-pooling-17"/> Listing-level issues are covered in [why an Amazon listing disappears](/guides/listing-disappeared/).

## Why reviews pool, and why that matters

Amazon is open about the benefit. Because all versions of a product are grouped together, reviews for more popular variations can encourage a purchase, <C id="variations-review-pooling-11"/> and a good review for one color of a jacket could help sell the other colors. <C id="variations-review-pooling-10"/>

### Seeing which child the reviews are about

Pooling cuts both ways: a complaint about one size can sit beside every other size. Amazon's Customer Feedback API lets you separate them. Its item review topics operation takes a child ASIN, <C id="variations-review-pooling-37"/> and for each topic it returns the child's own metrics, the parent ASIN's metrics (null if there isn't enough data), <C id="variations-review-pooling-38"/> and childAsinMetrics for the other children with the same parent. <C id="variations-review-pooling-39"/> Those sibling metrics name the child whose reviews mention the topic most and the one whose reviews mention it least. <C id="variations-review-pooling-40"/> Metrics include numberOfMentions, occurrencePercentage and starRatingImpact, <C id="variations-review-pooling-41"/> and a separate operation gives six months of review trends. <C id="variations-review-pooling-42"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): one size dragging the family">
Inputs: a jacket family with children in S, M, L and XL. You request review topics for the M child. A negative topic "runs small" shows occurrencePercentage 6 for M and 9 at parent level, and childAsinMetrics names the S child as the one whose reviews mention it most.

- The parent figure is higher than M's own, so other children are pulling it up; the sibling metrics point to S.
- Start with the S child's listing and product, not the whole family.
- Re-check the six-month trend after the change for the S child to see whether the topic falls.
</Callout>

### The FTC's review-reuse proposal

When the FTC began its consumer-reviews rulemaking, its advance notice described review hijacking as a seller stealing or repurposing reviews of another product. <C id="variations-review-pooling-27"/> In its proposed rule, the FTC concluded that unfair or deceptive reuse or repurposing of consumer reviews is prevalent. <C id="variations-review-pooling-26"/> Proposed Sec. 465.3 sought to address using a review written for one product so it appears written for a substantially different product. <C id="variations-review-pooling-23"/>

The proposed rule defined a substantially different product as one that differs in one or more material attributes other than color, size, count, or flavor. <C id="variations-review-pooling-24"/>

The FTC did not finalize that section. Commenters raised concerns about the definition, the Commission said it would need to address them first, <C id="variations-review-pooling-30"/> and because it could not resolve them on the record, it decided not to finalize the provision. <C id="variations-review-pooling-25"/> In the codified rule the slot is visible: 16 CFR 465.3 is printed as "[Reserved]", <C id="variations-review-pooling-31"/> while the adopted sections cover fake reviews, buying positive or negative reviews, insider reviews, company-controlled review sites, review suppression and fake social media indicators. <C id="variations-review-pooling-32"/> Those adopted provisions are covered in [the FTC fake review rule](/guides/review-manipulation-risk/).

## Reviews and Vine at the parent level

The parent is also the unit for Amazon Vine. Enrollment is charged per parent product, <C id="variations-review-pooling-18"/> with no additional cost for child products. <C id="variations-review-pooling-19"/> In the US, the cost is $0 for up to 2 units, $75 for 3–10 units, and $200 for 11–30 units per parent product, <C id="variations-review-pooling-20"/> and you can offer up to 30 units. <C id="variations-review-pooling-22"/> Eligible products must have fewer than 30 reviews on their detail page. <C id="variations-review-pooling-21"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): Vine cost per variation">
Inputs: a new shirt family has 5 colors, and you enroll 25 units across the family. At the 11–30 unit tier the parent pays $200, with no charge for the children. <C id="variations-review-pooling-20"/> <C id="variations-review-pooling-19"/> Spread over 5 colors that is $40 per color ($200 ÷ 5), or $8 per unit ($200 ÷ 25).
</Callout>

Amazon's review rules apply to every family: Amazon tells sellers not to attempt to influence customer ratings, feedback, and reviews. <C id="variations-review-pooling-28"/>
