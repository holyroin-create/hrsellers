This page exists because the same question arrives in different forms — the listing vanished, sessions collapsed, the ASIN says inactive — and the answer is a checklist rather than an explanation.

Work down it. Each cause links to the guide with the detail.

## 1. Main image compliance

**Check first, because it is the most common and the least visible.**

Reporting describes suppression triggered by a background that is not exactly RGB 255, 255, 255, a product filling under 85% of the frame, prohibited text or logos, or resolution below 1,000 pixels.

**And the silent version:** a listing running fine for weeks, then caught by a periodic compliance sweep. A sudden session drop with no other explanation points here.

→ [Main image compliance](/guides/main-image-compliance/)

## 2. Barcode and GTIN verification

Amazon verifies barcodes against the GS1 database. Reporting states that where the brand or company associated with a GTIN does not match your seller account, the code is treated as invalid — producing listing suppression and ASIN creation blocks.

Also, since 31 March 2026: reseller inventory received without a proper FNSKU is reported as **classified as defective.**

→ [Barcodes and FNSKU](/guides/gs1-barcodes-fnsku/)

## 3. A restricted category or brand gate

Gating operates at category, brand and ASIN level independently. Reporting is explicit that **an individual ASIN can be blocked even after category and brand approval** — flagged for safety, compliance, authenticity or dangerous goods.

→ [Ungating and restricted categories](/guides/ungating-restricted-categories/)

## 4. A dangerous goods review

Anything containing a lithium cell, pressurised, flammable, reactive or strongly magnetic can enter review. While it does, you cannot send inventory and existing stock may be held.

→ [Hazmat and dangerous goods](/guides/hazmat-dangerous-goods/)

## 5. A compliance document request

Reporting describes Amazon requesting certificates at any time with a window of around 72 hours, and ASIN suspension as the consequence of missing it. Children's Product Certificates, General Certificates of Conformity, test reports.

→ [US product compliance](/guides/us-product-compliance/)

## 6. An intellectual property or authenticity complaint

A rights owner filed against the listing, or an inauthentic allegation was raised. These are not image or data problems and they do not resolve by editing the listing.

→ [IP complaints](/guides/ip-complaints-retractions/) · [Suspension appeals](/guides/account-suspension-appeal/)

## 7. Insurance non-compliance

Reporting describes a window of typically 30 to 60 days to submit a compliant certificate of insurance before listings are suppressed, once you cross the reported threshold of $10,000 in sales across three consecutive months.

→ [Seller insurance](/guides/seller-insurance-coi/)

## 8. Stranded inventory

Units sitting in a fulfilment centre with no active listing attached. This is usually a **symptom** of one of the causes above rather than a cause itself — but it has its own cost, because stranded inventory percentage is one of the four factors in your IPI score, and it is storing product that cannot sell.

Reporting recommends resolving stranded listings within 48 hours as a standing habit.

→ [IPI and restock limits](/guides/ipi-restock-limits/)

## 9. Capacity restrictions

Not suppression, but frequently mistaken for it: you cannot send inventory in. Below the IPI threshold, Amazon caps capacity and can restrict inbound, and ASIN-level restock limits can throttle individual products.

→ [IPI and restock limits](/guides/ipi-restock-limits/)

## What is not suppression

Three things that look similar and are not, because the fix is different.

**No Buy Box on a live listing.** The page loses its Add to Cart button and buyers must click through to all buying options. Reporting describes this as generally meaning the lowest landed price on the listing sits high against prices Amazon sees at other retailers, with the Fair Pricing Policy suppressing the box rather than the listing. → [Buy Box](/guides/buy-box-featured-offer/)

**Losing Buy Box share to another seller.** Rotation, not suppression. The listing is fine; your offer is not winning. → [Buy Box](/guides/buy-box-featured-offer/)

**A ranking decline.** Gradual, not sudden, and usually conversion or stock related. Reporting notes rank erodes before a stockout actually happens, as raising prices to conserve inventory drops conversion. → [Listing optimisation](/guides/listing-optimisation-a10/)

**The distinction that separates them:** suppression removes the listing from search entirely. If the listing is still findable, it is not suppressed, and you are looking at a different problem.

## The order to work in

1. **Open the Account Health dashboard.** Policy notifications name the cause where one has been issued. Two minutes.
2. **Check Manage Inventory** for the ASIN's status and any listing limitation flags.
3. **Look at your own listing as a customer, on mobile.** Findable or not decides which half of this page applies.
4. **Check the main image** against RGB 255 with a colour picker, and against the 85% rule.
5. **Check for stranded inventory** — it often points at the cause.
6. **Work down causes 2 to 7** in order.
7. **Fix and resubmit**, then verify the listing is findable again rather than assuming.

## Preventing most of it

Almost every cause above is a document or a specification collected in advance:

- **A verified white canvas** on every main image, checked before upload
- **GS1 barcodes bought directly**, with listing data matching the registration
- **Compliance certificates filed per SKU** so a 72-hour request is retrieval rather than research
- **An SDS or exemption sheet** on anything with a battery or chemical
- **A certificate of insurance** in place before the threshold
- **Supplier invoices** in the right form — see [the supplier invoice](/guides/the-supplier-invoice/)

Six habits, one folder per SKU. That is the whole preventive programme.
