TikTok Shop spent two years marketing the cheapest headline commission of any major US marketplace. In January the promotional rate went; in August, per recent reporting, the standard rate followed it upward. Neither move was loud. Both compound. If you priced your catalogue against 6% — or worse, against the introductory rate — you are now running a P&L that belongs to a previous quarter.

## What changed, and what the sources say

The reported sequence: the platform ended its promotional 1.8% commission in January 2026, then on **4 August 2026** raised the standard referral fee for most US non-food categories from **6% to 8%**, with beauty and personal care above that.

The honest complication — and the reason this guide exists rather than a one-line note — is that the published record currently disagrees with itself. Several substantial 2026 guides, including ones reviewed against TikTok's own US seller documentation this summer, still describe a flat 6% for most categories with select jewellery lower. Our [TikTok Shop fee page](/marketplaces/tiktok-shop/) has carried that disagreement in its contested-figures section for weeks; the weight of recent, dated reporting has now shifted to 8%.

What that conflict means operationally is simple: **treat 8% as the working figure, and confirm against your own settlement statement rather than any published table — including ours.**

## The margin math, worked

<Figure value="-20%" kind="cost" source="Worked example at a 10% pre-change net margin; checked August 2026">
  A $25 unit paying two additional points surrenders $0.50 per unit. If the unit netted $2.50 under 6%, it nets $2.00 under 8% — a fifth of the profit, gone without a single operational change.
</Figure>

Scale it: at 1,000 units a month, the same catalogue loses $500 a month to a revision most sellers will first notice as an unexplained dip in their settlement. And the referral line is only the anchor. The rest of the stack is unchanged and unchanged is not cheap: creator commissions of 10–30% that you set yourself, and Fulfilled by TikTok's flat per-unit fulfilment — reported at roughly $2.86–$4.28 depending on basket — which takes about 9% of a $40 sale but roughly a quarter of a $15 one. Cheap products did not work at 6%. They work less at 8%.

Two mechanics in the fee base deserve their own line:

| Mechanism | Reported behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| Platform-funded promos | Referral fee computed on the buyer-paid amount **plus** the platform-funded discount, excluding tax — the subsidy is reimbursed, but the fee applies to the pre-discount value |
| Returns | Referral fee reverses, but an administration fee of **20% of the referral fee, capped at $5 per SKU**, is retained — and at 8%, that 20% is a bigger number too |

## What to do — this week, in Seller Center

**Read the settlement, not the guides.** Open your most recent settlement statement and read the actual referral percentage per order. Given the conflicting published record, your own statement is the only authoritative source for your account and categories.

**Re-run the top of the catalogue at 8%.** Put your five biggest SKUs through the [margin calculator](/tools/margin-calculator/) with the new referral line plus your real FBT and creator-commission figures. Any SKU whose net falls below your floor gets a price revision or a delist decision now — before Q4 volume locks the mistake in at scale.

**Recheck co-funded promotions.** If you run platform-funded deals, remember the fee base includes the pre-discount value. A promo that penciled at 6% on the discounted price is doubly wrong at 8% on the full one.

The wider lesson is the one this site is built on: headline rates are marketing, settlement statements are accounting, and the distance between them is where margins go to die. The [full TikTok Shop fee page](/marketplaces/tiktok-shop/) carries every line — dated, sourced, and updated as the record moves.
