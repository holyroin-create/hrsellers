Seller Fulfilled Prime (SFP) lets you put Prime branding on listings for products you ship yourself. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-01"/> Before applying, decide whether your operation can promise Prime delivery speeds every day: entry requires prequalification and a 30-day trial, and Amazon keeps checking performance weekly after you are enrolled. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-05"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-08"/> The numeric thresholds and the minimum package count are not on Amazon's public program page; they sit in the Seller Fulfilled Prime program policy in Seller Central. What is public is where your own numbers come from, and this page shows which report fields and order fields to watch.

## Who can apply

You need a Professional selling account and a domestic US address as your default shipping address. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-02"/> The Professional plan costs $39.99 per month plus selling fees. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-20"/> You are not required to use a warehouse: Amazon lets you choose your own storage facilities, and says some sellers use storage space in their homes or third-party solutions. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-15"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-14"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-28"/> Made-to-order goods are not excluded either: you can offer customizable products if you can fulfill customized orders while meeting Prime promises. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-29"/>

## Getting ready before you register

Prequalification is measured on orders you have already shipped, so the work starts well before the trial. The three measures Amazon names are late shipments, valid tracking and cancellations, alongside a minimum package count. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-03"/> If you are not prequalified yet, the place to watch your progress is the business reports and Account Health dashboard. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-04"/>

Your existing shipping settings also matter. Amazon's FBM tools let you set a handling time, a transit time and a capacity limit in shipping templates, and the trial adds a separate template with one- and two-day options. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-25"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-06"/> Before the trial starts, check that your carrier pickups, cut-off times and staffing can actually meet one- and two-day promises for the products you plan to enroll, because the trial requires meeting all performance requirements for its full 30 days. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-05"/>

You choose which products to enroll, so you do not have to put your whole catalog into the trial; keep in mind that SFP's three size tiers carry different delivery-speed expectations. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-14"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-10"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-11"/>

## What you commit to after enrollment

Enrollment is not permanent. Amazon reviews performance weekly, from Sunday through Saturday, and sends email notices for any requirement you miss; ongoing failures "may lead to Prime offers being disabled and disenrollment from the program." <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-08"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-26"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-09"/> You monitor this on a dedicated Seller Fulfilled Prime performance dashboard. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-21"/> Disenrollment is not a permanent bar: if you later prequalify again, you can start a new trial to re-enroll. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-27"/>

Delivery promises depend on size. Amazon says the delivery speed offered to Prime customers varies by size tier, and SFP has three tiers: standard-size, oversize and extra large. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-10"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-11"/>

The main safety valve is volume control. The "Control Prime order volume" option sets daily limits on how many same-, one- and two-day delivery orders customers can place, and Amazon says the limit "should reflect the number of orders you can reliably ship each day." <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-12"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-13"/>

## Where to read your own late-shipment, tracking and cancellation rates

Amazon's Seller Performance Report, `GET_V2_SELLER_PERFORMANCE_REPORT`, contains the individual performance metrics from the Account Health dashboard, including late-shipment, valid-tracking and pre-fulfillment cancellation rates. It can only be requested, not scheduled. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-31"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-32"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-33"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-34"/>

Each metric also has a `reportingDateRange`, so check which window a rate covers before comparing it with the weekly Sunday–Saturday SFP review. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-32"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-08"/> The targets in this report come from the Account Health dashboard; the SFP thresholds are in the program policy, and Amazon points SFP sellers to their Seller Fulfilled Prime performance dashboard for ongoing monitoring. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-31"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-21"/>

Inputs: in one reporting window, `orderCount` is 400 and `lateShipmentCount` is 6; `shipmentCount` is 390 and `validTrackingCount` is 382.

- Late shipments: 6 ÷ 400 = 1.5%.

## Spotting Prime orders in your own order data

If your order system reads Amazon's Orders API, three fields carry the Prime promise for each order. `IsPrime` is true when the order is a seller-fulfilled Amazon Prime order. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-36"/> `LatestShipDate` is the end of the period within which you committed to ship, returned only for seller-fulfilled orders, and `LatestDeliveryDate` is the end of the period within which you committed to fulfill the order. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-37"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-38"/> `ShipmentServiceLevelCategory` tells you the speed bucket; its values are Expedited, FreeEconomy, NextDay, Priority, SameDay, SecondDay, Scheduled and Standard. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-39"/>

Amazon says using Buy Shipping gives additional account-health protections, including Amazon refunding costs for claims when customers report that packages did not arrive. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-18"/> Its labels are also priced at pre-negotiated rates that Amazon says are over 31% lower on average than retail ground rates. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-22"/>

## Why sellers apply

Amazon's stated benefit is the badge itself: having it "helps increase your likelihood of becoming the Featured Offer." <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-19"/> The alternative route to the badge is FBA, where products are eligible for free two-day Prime shipping but sit in Amazon's network and pay storage by cubic foot — $0.78 per month from January to September and $2.40 from October to December for standard-size items. <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-23"/> <C id="seller-fulfilled-prime-24"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): storage you avoid in Q4">
Inputs: a seller holds 300 cubic feet of standard-size stock through October–December and can ship 120 orders a day reliably.

- In FBA, storage alone would be 300 × $2.40 = $720 per month, or $2,160 for the quarter.
- Under SFP that stock stays in the seller's own space, but the daily cap in "Control Prime order volume" should be set at no more than 120 orders, the number that can be shipped reliably.

Whether $2,160 is worth more than the staff, carrier pickups and weekend coverage needed to hold Prime speeds is the actual decision.
</Callout>
