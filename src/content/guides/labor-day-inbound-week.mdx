Nothing about a national holiday is surprising, which is what makes the annual damage it does to inbound plans so avoidable. The mechanism is simple: one date removes capacity from every link of the chain at once, and the chain was already running at Q4 load.

## What one day off actually costs

A holiday Monday is not one lost day. Trucking capacity tightens in the days before it as shippers race the date; ports and rail run reduced or no operations; receiving docks reopen Tuesday to Monday's backlog plus Tuesday's arrivals. The practical effect, repeated every year: appointments slip, check-in buffers stretch, and a shipment that was "arriving the week of the 7th" quietly becomes mid-month. If your [Q4 backwards count](/guides/q4-freight-booking-window/) assumed normal weeks, the first half of September is where that assumption breaks.

## The two free adjustments

**Pad everything the holiday touches.** Any transit estimate, carrier appointment or receiving expectation landing between early and mid September gets several extra working days in the plan. If the plan cannot absorb that padding, the plan — not the calendar — is the problem, and the [freight modes guide](/guides/freight-modes/) covers the split-shipment escape valve.

**Front-load the paperwork.** Bookings, ISF filings, supplier document requests, anything requiring a human on the other end: before the holiday. The Friday-before inbox is where Q4 timelines go to die quietly.
