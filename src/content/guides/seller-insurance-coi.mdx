If you sell physical products on Amazon or Walmart, liability insurance is part of the account rules, not just good practice. Amazon has stated that sellers have long been required to obtain product liability insurance. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-01"/> Walmart publishes its numbers: a seller that exceeds **$100,000 in GMV in any 12-month period** must submit a Certificate of Insurance (COI) showing general and product liability limits of **$1,000,000 per occurrence and $2,000,000 aggregate**. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-09"/> <C id="seller-insurance-coi-10"/> The decisions are what coverage to buy, at what limits, and how to word the certificate so it is accepted.

Amazon's own threshold, limits and certificate details are set in its Business Solutions Agreement, which is published inside Seller Central; check the current version there, because this page does not restate it.

## What Amazon has said publicly

Amazon's 2021 announcement of changes to the A-to-z Guarantee addressed insurance. Beginning September 1, 2021, for products sold through Amazon.com, Amazon said it would facilitate resolution of property damage and personal injury claims through that process. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-05"/> For valid claims under $1,000, which it said account for more than 80% of cases, Amazon said it would bear the costs and not seek reimbursement from sellers who follow its policies and hold valid insurance. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-06"/> <C id="seller-insurance-coi-07"/> If a seller doesn't respond to a claim, Amazon said it would step in to address the customer's concern, bear the cost itself and separately pursue the seller. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-08"/>

The insurance condition is the point for sellers: holding valid coverage is one of the two conditions Amazon attached to absorbing small claims. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-06"/>

In the same announcement Amazon described **Amazon Insurance Accelerator**, a network of vetted insurance providers that may offer liability insurance at competitive rates to qualifying sellers. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-02"/> Sellers pay only for the cost of the insurance itself, and they are not required to use a provider within the Accelerator. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-04"/> <C id="seller-insurance-coi-03"/> You can buy elsewhere, as long as the policy meets Amazon's terms.

## Walmart's COI rules in detail

Walmart publishes its requirement on its Marketplace Learn site, and it is specific enough to use as a checklist. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-09"/>

| Requirement | Walmart's rule |
|---|---|
| Who | Sellers exceeding $100,000 GMV in any 12-month period, or notified by Walmart |
| Coverage | General and product liability |
| Limits | $1,000,000 per occurrence / $2,000,000 aggregate |
| Named insured | Must match the legal business name in Seller Center |
| Additional insured | Walmart Inc., as certificate holder or in the description of operations |
| How you hear | Email asking you to submit the COI in Seller Center |

*Source: Walmart Marketplace Learn — Liability insurance policy, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="seller-insurance-coi-09"/> <C id="seller-insurance-coi-10"/> <C id="seller-insurance-coi-11"/> <C id="seller-insurance-coi-12"/> <C id="seller-insurance-coi-14"/>

The additional-insured line can be done one of two ways: Walmart Inc. as the certificate holder, addressed to its insurance compliance team in Bentonville, or the description of operations naming "Walmart Inc., its subsidiaries and its affiliates." <C id="seller-insurance-coi-12"/> <C id="seller-insurance-coi-13"/> Walmart also encourages sellers below the threshold to maintain insurance anyway. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-18"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: a new Walmart seller with no earlier sales has monthly GMV of $6,000 from January to June, then $14,000 a month from July to December.

Running 12-month totals: by the end of December the seller has $36,000 + $84,000 = **$120,000** for the calendar year. But the test is "any 12-month period", not the calendar year, so the trigger is the month the trailing total first passes $100,000. With the first six months at $36,000, the trailing total passes $100,000 once the $14,000 months add more than $64,000, which happens in the fifth such month: $36,000 + 5 × $14,000 = $106,000 at the end of November.

Getting quotes when the trailing total is approaching the threshold, rather than after the notice arrives, avoids a gap during which payments could be withheld.
</Callout>

## Which coverage a product seller needs

The SBA's business insurance guide lists the standard types. Two apply directly to marketplace sellers. General liability is listed for any business and covers financial loss from bodily injury, property damage and medical expenses. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-21"/> Product liability is listed for businesses that manufacture, wholesale, distribute and retail a product, and covers loss from a defective product that causes injury or bodily harm. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-30"/> <C id="seller-insurance-coi-22"/>

Other coverage depends on how you operate. Commercial property insurance protects company property against loss and damage from a wide variety of events. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-23"/> A home-based business rider on a homeowner's policy can protect a small amount of business equipment. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-24"/> A business owner's policy bundles the typical coverages into one package. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-25"/> If you have employees, federal rules require workers' compensation, unemployment and disability insurance, and some states require more. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-19"/> <C id="seller-insurance-coi-20"/>

## Buying the policy and getting the certificate right

SBA's buying advice is straightforward: think about what accidents, natural disasters or lawsuits could damage your business, and insure against things you couldn't pay for yourself. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-29"/> <C id="seller-insurance-coi-28"/> Agents are paid commissions by insurers, so compare rates, terms and benefits from several of them. <C id="seller-insurance-coi-26"/> <C id="seller-insurance-coi-27"/>

## How to confirm the requirement for your account

For Walmart, re-check the [Liability insurance policy page](https://marketplacelearn.walmart.com/guides/Policies%20&%20standards/Account/Liability-insurance-policy) and any email asking for your COI. For Amazon, the binding terms are in the Business Solutions Agreement and insurance help pages in Seller Central; Amazon's [2021 A-to-z Guarantee announcement](https://aboutamazon.com/news/how-amazon-works/new-a-to-z-guarantee-better-protects-amazon-customers-and-sellers) explains the claims process. The SBA's [business insurance guide](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/get-business-insurance) covers coverage types. Policy wording and adequate limits for your products are questions for a licensed agent.
