If you already run any traffic to your Amazon listings from outside Amazon — Meta ads, Google, an email list, creators — this programme changes the arithmetic of that spend. If you do not, it changes the case for starting. Either way it costs nothing to be enrolled in, which makes non-enrolment the strange default it reportedly still is.

## What the programme is

The Brand Referral Bonus credits part of the referral fee back to brands that drive **tracked** external traffic to their Amazon listings. The requirements reported consistently:

| Requirement | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Store | Amazon US |
| Registration | [Brand Registry](/guides/brand-registry-trademark/), with a Brand Representative role |
| Enrolment | Explicit — the programme must be joined in Seller Central |
| Tracking | Amazon Attribution tags on every external link |
| Excluded traffic | Amazon's own advertising — Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands video |

Amazon's own worked example makes the mechanism concrete: a $100 backpack carrying a 14% referral fee earns, say, a 7% bonus when the sale comes through a tagged external link — a credit that offsets referral fees on later sales, moving net proceeds on that unit from $86 to $93.

## The rate is a range, and the sources disagree

The headline 10% is reported everywhere as an **average**. Beyond that, published figures diverge in a way worth showing rather than smoothing over:

| Source | Reported category rates |
| --- | --- |
| Multiple 2025–2026 industry analyses | Amazon Device Accessories up to 30%; Automotive & Power Sports 7–8% |
| Nova Analytics, 2026 | Range roughly 5–25%; Luxury Beauty toward the top of it |
| SellerMetrics, 2026 | Toys and Apparel around 10%; Electronics around 5%; Jewelry up to 14% |
| Hector AI, 2026 | Clothing and Footwear 10–12%; Electronics and Grocery 5–8% |

These do not fully agree with each other, and that is the finding: **the only authoritative rate table is the Brand Referral Bonus help page inside your own Seller Central**, which every one of these sources ultimately defers to. Reporting also notes the realised amount moves with shipping and gift-wrap charges, and shrinks with cancellations and returns.

One planning implication reported for multi-category sellers: since the bonus rate varies this much, it can make sense to weight external ad spend toward the SKUs in higher-bonus categories — the same click is worth more credit there.

## The plumbing: Attribution tags

The entire programme runs on one mechanical foundation. **Untagged traffic earns nothing**, however much of it converts.

The reported workflow: in Seller Central, under Brands → Amazon Attribution, a unique tracking tag is created per traffic source — one for Google, one for Meta, one for the email list — and that tagged URL is what goes into the campaign. The tag follows the shopper from click to purchase, with a reported attribution window of **14 days**.

Three reported constraints on the plumbing:

- **Amazon's own ads are excluded.** The bonus is for traffic Amazon did not already charge you for.
- **Attribution tags cannot be combined with Amazon Affiliates links**, per Amazon's current tag guidance — a detail that matters if you work with affiliate partners.
- **Paid creators must disclose.** Reported guidance is the standard one: material connections disclosed clearly, placed with the endorsement, hard to miss.

## The money arrives late, and the reports arrive later

Two timing facts shape how this programme feels in practice.

The credit lands after a **waiting period of roughly two months**, reported as accommodating cancellations and returns — the same reason the figure can shrink between the sale and the credit. And the reporting on estimated bonus earnings is described as refreshing **weekly, with a lag of up to two months** behind qualifying sales. Earned bonuses are reviewable on the Transaction Details page.

The practical consequence: this is not a lever you can optimise week to week. It is a background rebate that rewards a decision you make once — tag everything — and it belongs in your quarterly economics rather than your weekly dashboard, alongside the payout timing effects covered in our [DD7 reserve guide](/guides/dd7-payout-reserve/).

## Where this sits in the bigger picture

External traffic has a second reported effect beyond the credit: sales from outside Amazon are described as feeding the ranking algorithm's velocity signals, with knock-on effects on organic rank. And unlike PPC spend, external channels build assets you keep — an email list, a social following — which is the argument our [diversification guide](/guides/off-amazon-diversification/) makes at full length.

The bonus does not make bad external marketing good. What it does is take marketing you were arguably doing anyway and hand back a mid-single-digit to double-digit percentage of the attributed sales — for the cost of using a different URL.

## What to do

**Enrol now if you are Brand Registered in the US store.** Reported enrolment effort is minutes, and there is no participation cost. The failure mode is not enrolling and running tagged-looking campaigns that credit nothing.

**Tag per channel, not per campaign burst.** One tag per source keeps the attribution data readable — which reporting notes is worth having even independently of the bonus, since it is external-conversion data Amazon otherwise never shows you.

**Check your own category's rate before shifting budget.** Given how much the published figures disagree, read the rate table in Seller Central rather than any blog — including this one.
