If your Amazon payout is smaller than the sales you see, part of the money may be sitting in one of two holding buckets that Amazon's own developer documentation names: a **deferred balance**, where sales wait for a release date under policies such as **DD7 (delivery date policy)**, and an **account level reserve** held against refunds, chargebacks and claims. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-01"/> <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-03"/> <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-04"/> The decision this page helps with is practical: which bucket is holding your cash, when it should come back, and how to plan inventory and bills around it.

Amazon's public seller blog gives the headline rule: with deliveries, it typically reserves funds for seven days so customers have time to inspect their purchases and get refunds if needed. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-23"/> The detailed rules are published inside Seller Central; this page uses Amazon's public blog and its Selling Partner API (SP-API) documentation.

## What "DD7" means in Amazon's own documentation

Amazon's Finances API attaches a "deferred context" to transactions that are held. It has two fields. The first, the deferral reason, is "the deferral policy applied to the transaction", and Amazon gives two examples: `B2B` for invoiced orders and `DD7` for the delivery date policy. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-01"/> The second, the maturity date, is defined as the release date of the transaction. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-02"/>

So DD+7 is not a fee. It is a deferral policy tied to delivery, and each held sale has a date when it is scheduled to be released. Amazon's description of the deferred balance says the same thing: a portion of sales revenue that is temporarily withheld before being transferred to the available balance after a specified period. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-03"/>

## The five balances and how they add up

Amazon's balance model breaks your money into named parts. Knowing the arithmetic between them is the fastest way to explain a short payout.

The two holds behave differently. A deferred sale has its own release date, so it clears on a schedule. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-02"/> The account level reserve is described as a safety measure sized on sales volume, product category and account history, so it reflects risk rather than a fixed calendar. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-04"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: a balance view shows **available $6,000**, **deferred $3,500** and **account level reserve $1,000**.

Reserved = $3,500 + $1,000 = **$4,500**. Total = $6,000 + $4,500 = **$10,500**.

Only the $6,000 is available now. The $3,500 should move to available as each deferred sale reaches its maturity date; the $1,000 reserve depends on Amazon's risk sizing, not on a release date. If you planned a $9,000 inventory order on the assumption that the full $10,500 was payable, you are $3,000 short until the deferred sales clear.
</Callout>

## From delivery to your bank account

Amazon's payments post sets out the cycle. In general it settles seller accounts every two weeks: it takes the beginning balance, adds sales, subtracts expenses, adjusts for refunds and transfers the payment after withholding an amount in reserve. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-18"/> <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-19"/> The reserved amount becomes the beginning balance for the next settlement period. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-20"/> Once Amazon initiates a payment, it can take up to five business days to reach your bank; if it has been longer than that, you can track the payment with the trace ID in the Disbursements section of the Payments Dashboard. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-21"/> <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-22"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): a long and a short gap for one order">
Inputs (assumed): a seven-day hold counted from delivery; a two-week settlement cycle, with payment initiated at each settlement close; the hold ends just after a settlement closes, so the next close is 14 days away; a bank deposit taking the full five business days (seven calendar days).

Days from delivery to cash: 7 (hold) + 14 (wait for the next settlement close) + 7 (deposit) = **28 days**. If instead the hold ends the day before a settlement closes and the bank posts the next day, it is 7 + 1 + 1 = **9 days**.
</Callout>

<C id="dd7-payout-reserve-23"/> <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-18"/> <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-21"/>

If the timing is the problem rather than the amount, Amazon says eligible sellers in its US store can enroll in Amazon Express Payout to receive payouts within 24 hours. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-25"/>

## Following a sale from deferred to paid

Each transaction carries a status. Amazon lists three values: `DEFERRED` (currently deferred), `RELEASED` (currently released) and `DEFERRED_RELEASED` (deferred in the past, now released). A deferred transaction is updated to `DEFERRED_RELEASED` when it is released. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-07"/> Amazon announced that status on April 30, 2025 for the Finances API v2024-06-19, across all marketplaces. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-08"/>

The older Finances API (v0) organizes money into financial event groups, each tied to a settlement period. A group is `Open` or `Closed`, and that status tells you whether its balance is settled. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-11"/> For a closed group the total is the disbursement or charge amount; for an open group it is the current balance. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-12"/> The fund transfer date, when the disbursement or charge was initiated, appears only for closed settlements. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-13"/> Each group also records a beginning balance, the balance at the start of the settlement period. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-17"/>

The newer API ties the pieces together with identifiers. A transaction can carry a `DEFERRED_TRANSACTION_ID` and a `RELEASE_TRANSACTION_ID`, linking the held sale to the transaction that released it, plus a `SETTLEMENT_ID` and a `DISBURSEMENT_ID`, "the disbursement ID for Amazon's bank transfer." <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-27"/> <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-28"/> Only the financial event group ID and the order ID can be used as filters, so a lookup starts from one of those two and reads the other identifiers from the results. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-29"/> A separate summary call returns net proceeds for a chosen time period or settlement period. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-30"/>

Reserves show up there too. Amazon's adjustment list includes a reserve event that is generated when a settlement period closes, occurring when some money from a seller's account is held back. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-10"/>

## The two reports that show held money

Two downloadable reports carry the same trail.

| Report | How you get it | Columns to read |
|---|---|---|
| Deferred Transaction Report (`GET_DATE_RANGE_FINANCIAL_HOLDS_DATA`) | Request it in the Payments Reports Repository in Seller Central; up to 10 years per report | settlement-id, type, order-id, sku, product-sales, selling-fees, fba-fees, total |
| Flat File V2 Settlement Report | Generated by Amazon on its own schedule | settlement-start-date, settlement-end-date, deposit-date, total-amount, amount-type, amount-description, amount |

*Source: Amazon Selling Partner API documentation, payment and settlement report types, checked Nov 9, 2026.* <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-32"/> <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-33"/> <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-34"/> <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-14"/>

Both reports carry a settlement-id column; the settlement file adds that settlement's start and end dates and its deposit-date. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-33"/> <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-34"/>

Amazon's own wording on the reserve is that it is held "for a period of time" to cover potential refunds or chargebacks; the post gives no fixed period for it. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-26"/> Two data limits are worth knowing if you or your bookkeeper pull this data through software. Recent orders lag: Amazon notes that financial events might not include orders from the last 48 hours. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-09"/> And a transaction query that spans more than 180 days between its start and end dates returns nothing, so pull long periods in pieces. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-16"/> Point-in-time balances are time-stamped in the marketplace's local time zone, which matters when you compare them with a bank statement kept in another zone. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-31"/>

## Where to check the rules for your account

The binding rules are in Seller Central: the delivery date-based reserve and account level reserve help pages and your Payments Dashboard. Amazon's public summary is its blog post [How Amazon seller payments work](https://sell.amazon.com/blog/amazon-seller-payments). For the definitions used on this page, re-check Amazon's [Finances API 2024-06-19 model](https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/models/finances-api-model/finances_2024-06-19.json), the [listTransactions reference](https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/reference/listtransactions) and the [settlement](https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/report-type-values-settlement) and [payment](https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/report-type-values-payment) report documentation. If a hold looks wrong for your account, raise it with Amazon through Seller Central with the transaction IDs and maturity dates to hand; this page explains the mechanics and is not accounting advice.
