If your Amazon payout is smaller than the sales you see, part of the money may be sitting in one of two holding buckets that Amazon's own developer documentation names: a **deferred balance**, where sales wait for a release date under policies such as **DD7 (delivery date policy)**, and an **account level reserve** held against refunds, chargebacks and claims. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-01"/> <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-03"/> <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-04"/> The decision this page helps with is practical: which bucket is holding your cash, when it should come back, and how to plan inventory and bills around it.

Amazon publishes the detailed DD+7 rules, including the holding period and which orders are covered, inside Seller Central, which this site cannot cite; check the current version there. This page sticks to what Amazon documents publicly in its Selling Partner API (SP-API) references.

## What "DD7" means in Amazon's own documentation

Amazon's Finances API attaches a "deferred context" to transactions that are held. It has two fields. The first, the deferral reason, is "the deferral policy applied to the transaction", and Amazon gives two examples: `B2B` for invoiced orders and `DD7` for the delivery date policy. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-01"/> The second, the maturity date, is defined as the release date of the transaction. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-02"/>

So DD+7 is not a fee. It is a deferral policy tied to delivery, and each held sale has a date when it is scheduled to be released. Amazon's description of the deferred balance says the same thing: a portion of sales revenue that is temporarily withheld before being transferred to the available balance after a specified period. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-03"/>

## The five balances and how they add up

The two holds behave differently. A deferred sale has its own release date, so it clears on a schedule. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-02"/> The account level reserve is described as a safety measure sized on sales volume, product category and account history, so it reflects risk rather than a fixed calendar. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-04"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: a balance view shows **available $6,000**, **deferred $3,500** and **account level reserve $1,000**.

Reserved = $3,500 + $1,000 = **$4,500**. Total = $6,000 + $4,500 = **$10,500**.

Only the $6,000 is available now. The $3,500 should move to available as each deferred sale reaches its maturity date; the $1,000 reserve depends on Amazon's risk sizing, not on a release date. If you planned a $9,000 inventory order on the assumption that the full $10,500 was payable, you are $3,000 short until the deferred sales clear.
</Callout>

## Following a sale from deferred to paid

Each transaction carries a status. Amazon lists three values: `DEFERRED` (currently deferred), `RELEASED` (currently released) and `DEFERRED_RELEASED` (deferred in the past, now released). A deferred transaction is updated to `DEFERRED_RELEASED` when it is released. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-07"/> Amazon announced that status on April 30, 2025 for the Finances API v2024-06-19, across all marketplaces. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-08"/>

The older Finances API (v0) organizes money into financial event groups, each tied to a settlement period. A group is `Open` or `Closed`, and that status tells you whether its balance is settled. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-11"/> For a closed group the total is the disbursement or charge amount; for an open group it is the current balance. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-12"/> The fund transfer date, when the disbursement or charge was initiated, appears only for closed settlements. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-13"/> Each group also records a beginning balance, the balance at the start of the settlement period. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-17"/>

Reserves show up there too. Amazon's adjustment list includes a reserve event that is generated when a settlement period closes, occurring when some money from a seller's account is held back. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-10"/>

Two data limits are worth knowing if you or your bookkeeper pull this data through software. Recent orders lag: Amazon notes that financial events might not include orders from the last 48 hours. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-09"/> And a transaction query that spans more than 180 days between its start and end dates returns nothing, so pull long periods in pieces. <C id="dd7-payout-reserve-16"/>

## How to confirm the current DD+7 rules

The binding rules are in Seller Central: the delivery date policy and account level reserve help pages and your Payments dashboard. For the definitions used on this page, re-check Amazon's [Finances API 2024-06-19 model](https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/models/finances-api-model/finances_2024-06-19.json), the [listTransactions reference](https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/reference/listtransactions) and the [settlement report documentation](https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/report-type-values-settlement). If a hold looks wrong for your account, raise it with Amazon through Seller Central with the transaction IDs and maturity dates to hand; this page explains the mechanics and is not accounting advice.
