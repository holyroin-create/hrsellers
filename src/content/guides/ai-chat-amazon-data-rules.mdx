If you are about to paste an order export, a buyer message or a shipping label into an AI chat, strip the buyer's name, address, email and phone first. Amazon's Orders API documentation lists those fields as customer PII, <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-18"/> and the Data Protection Policy (DPP) text Amazon quotes says that "at no point should PII be transmitted or stored unprotected." <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-03"/> The DPP is written for software that uses Amazon's Selling Partner API, so its scope matters, and so does what happens to your text after you press send: each AI provider publishes its own rules on training and retention, and those rules differ between consumer apps and business or API products. This page sets out both, with the exact wording, as checked on November 11, 2026.

## What Amazon's Data Protection Policy covers

Amazon's developer documentation describes the DPP as the policy "which includes requirements for all solution providers building SP-API applications." <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-04"/> A second part, Section 2, adds requirements for "performing restricted operations on SP-API that involve personally identifiable information (PII)." <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-05"/> Restricted operations are the API calls that "return customers' Personally Identifiable Information (PII)." <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-17"/>

Two recent changes affect how the policy reads. Amazon updated the DPP and the Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) effective November 25, 2025, <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-14"/> and the update replaced the term "Developer" with "Solution Provider" throughout both policies. <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-16"/> Amazon's notice states that continued use of Amazon Services API after November 25, 2025 "constitutes your acceptance." <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-15"/>

Keep the scope precise. The pages cited here describe obligations on solution providers that build SP-API applications. They do not say how the DPP applies to a seller who copies an order by hand from Seller Central into a chat window; the full DPP and AUP texts are linked, for each language and region, from Amazon's Policies and Agreements page, <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-22"/> and Seller Central's own rules on buyer information should be read there. What the DPP does show is how Amazon expects PII from its systems to be handled, which is a useful standard for anyone moving that data into another service.

### Which order fields Amazon treats as PII

Amazon's documentation for the Orders API v2026-01-01 states that "customer PII includes the following," and its list includes the buyer's email and name and the delivery address fields: recipient name, address lines, postal code and phone. <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-18"/> Fields not on that list, such as an order ID or an item total, are not named as PII on that page; whether they are personal data under a privacy law is a separate question the page does not answer.

## What the DPP says about handling PII

| Topic | What Amazon's documentation states |
|---|---|
| PII retention | Retain PII for no longer than 30 days after order delivery |
| Longer retention | Only if required by law |
| Unprotected data | At no point should PII be transmitted or stored unprotected |
| Encryption at rest | Encrypt all PII at rest |
| Encryption in transit | Encrypt all Amazon Information in transit |
| Key strength | AES-128 or larger (AES-256 preferred) or RSA-2048 or higher for all PII |
| Security incidents | Notify Amazon within 24 hours of detecting them |
| Access after termination or role change | Remove access within 24 hours for employees, contractors and third parties |
| Non-PII data | Not stored longer than 18 months unless law requires longer |
| Vendors and subcontractors | Third-party risk assessments required |
| Deleting data | Anonymization is not an acceptable deletion method |

*Source: Amazon Selling Partner API documentation (data retention, encryption and incident response guides; DPP/AUP update notice), checked Nov 11, 2026.* <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-01"/> <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-02"/> <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-03"/> <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-06"/> <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-07"/> <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-08"/> <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-09"/> <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-11"/> <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-12"/> <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-13"/> <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-19"/>

Read against an AI chat, three rows stand out. The 30-day PII limit <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-01"/> sits awkwardly beside provider retention periods that can run far longer (see the comparison below). If an SP-API application sends Amazon data to an AI service, that service is one of the application's vendors, and the November 2025 update added third-party risk assessments for vendors and subcontractors. <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-13"/> And because anonymization does not count as deletion, <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-19"/> redacting a copy is not the same as deleting the original you hold.

## Buyer contact details in messaging

Amazon's own messaging rules already keep contact data out of message text. For messages sent through the Messaging API, "Amazon emails a message from the seller to the buyer." <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-21"/> For a confirm-delivery-details message, "Only links related to order delivery are allowed. Do not include HTML or email addresses." <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-20"/> If you use an AI chat to draft buyer messages, the draft does not need the buyer's real name or address to be useful; a placeholder works, and you add the specifics in Seller Central. See [buyer-seller messaging](/guides/buyer-seller-messaging/) for the message types.

A few details matter for sellers. Turning off Anthropic's model-improvement setting stops use of "any new chats and coding sessions" for future training, <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-26"/> and OpenAI's equivalent switch means "new conversations will not be used to improve our models." <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-34"/> Both settings look forward, not back. Google says that with Keep Activity off, future chats "won't be used to train our AI models," unless you choose to send Google feedback, <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-44"/> and its privacy hub says: "Please don’t enter confidential information that you wouldn’t want a reviewer to see or Google to use to improve our services, including machine-learning technologies." <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-46"/> Its API terms go further for the free tier: "Do not submit sensitive, confidential, or personal information to the Unpaid Services." <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-49"/>

## Scope of this page

This page reports what Amazon's developer documentation and each AI provider's own pages state; it is not legal advice on privacy law. Amazon's policy pages and the provider's current terms for your plan are the authoritative texts. Whether a specific use of buyer data is lawful in your jurisdiction is a question for a privacy lawyer. For the reports these exports come from, see [Seller Central reports](/guides/seller-central-reports/).

## How to confirm it before you paste

- **Amazon:** open the Policies and Agreements page in the Selling Partner API documentation and follow the DPP and AUP links for your locale. <C id="ai-chat-amazon-data-rules-22"/> The data retention, encryption and incident response guides in the same documentation quote the DPP requirements used above, and the November 2025 update notice summarizes the changes.
- **Anthropic:** privacy.claude.com, in the Consumers collection for Free, Pro and Max, and the Commercial Customers collection for Claude for Work and the API.
- **OpenAI:** the Data Controls FAQ on help.openai.com, the Enterprise privacy page on openai.com, and the "your data" guide in the API documentation.
- **Google:** the Gemini Apps Privacy Hub on support.google.com and the Gemini API Additional Terms of Service on ai.google.dev.
- **Kimi:** the Kimi OpenPlatform Privacy Policy on platform.kimi.ai and the user privacy agreement on kimi.com.

Provider settings and terms change; check the page for your exact plan on the day you decide.
