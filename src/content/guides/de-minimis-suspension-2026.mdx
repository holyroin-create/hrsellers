The US de minimis exemption (Section 321 of the Tariff Act, 19 U.S.C. 1321) is not available to commercial shipments right now, whatever their value or origin and however they travel. Executive Order 14388, signed February 20, 2026, states that duty-free de minimis treatment does not apply to covered shipments "regardless of value, country of origin, mode of transportation, or method of entry." <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-03" /> That rule applies to goods entered on or after 12:01 a.m. eastern standard time on February 24, 2026. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-04" />

In practice this means a $40 parcel and a $4,000 pallet are both entered and pay every applicable duty, tax and fee. Non-postal shipments (express couriers, air cargo, ocean, truck) need an entry filed in ACE, <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-05" /> and since July 24, 2026, mail shipments have their own CBP entry process <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-12" /> with a bond and payment by the 7th of the following month. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-13" /> <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-14" /> A separate statutory end date, July 1, 2027, sits behind the executive suspension; it is covered in [the Section 321 repeal guide](/guides/section-321-repeal-2027/).

<Callout type="note" title="Scope of this page">
This page tracks the published notices. Because the rules for low-value shipments changed several times in 2026, check the notice dates against the date your shipment is entered.
</Callout>

## What the exemption was

The regulation for low-value importations is 19 CFR 10.151, titled "Importations not over $800." <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-08" /> It lets the port director pass free of duty and tax a shipment imported by one person on one day whose fair retail value in the country of shipment does not exceed $800, unless the shipment looks like one of several lots split from a single order to get free entry. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-19" /> CBP notes that the Trade Facilitation and Trade Enforcement Act of 2015 raised the cap from $200 to $800 in 2016. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-20" />

For marketplace sellers, this exemption was what made direct-to-consumer parcels from overseas suppliers, and some cross-border fulfillment models, duty-free. That is the piece that has been switched off.

### Executive Order 14324 (2025)

Executive Order 14324, signed July 30, 2025, suspended de minimis for non-postal shipments entered on or after 12:01 a.m. eastern daylight time on August 29, 2025. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-01" /> For postal items it gave carriers a choice: an ad valorem duty, or, for six months, a specific duty per item of $80, $160 or $200 depending on the origin country's tariff rate under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA). <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-02" /> Those per-item amounts were tied to a fixed period and to IEEPA rates, so treat them as history, not current rates.

### Executive Order 14388 (February 2026)

Executive Order 14388 replaced section 2 of the 2025 order. Shipments outside the postal network became subject to all applicable duties, taxes, fees, exactions and charges, with entries filed in ACE by a qualified party. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-05" /> For postal shipments, the order set an interim duty equal to the rate in the February 20, 2026 temporary import surcharge proclamation, to apply until the surcharge expired or CBP's new postal entry process took effect, whichever came first. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-06" />

That proclamation (Proclamation 11012, under section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974) imposed a 10 percent ad valorem surcharge for 150 days from February 24, 2026, running through 12:01 a.m. eastern daylight time on July 24, 2026 unless suspended, modified or terminated earlier, or extended by an Act of Congress. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-07" />

### CBP's two interim final rules (June 2026)

CBP then wrote the suspension into its regulations through two interim final rules published June 24, 2026.

- **Non-postal (CBP Dec. 26-12).** Effective June 24, 2026, 19 CFR 10.151 now states that the exemption is suspended for merchandise arriving by all modes other than the international postal network, until CBP decides otherwise and announces it in the Federal Register. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-08" />
- **Postal (CBP Dec. 26-13).** Effective July 24, 2026 (with its 19 CFR 145.31 amendment effective June 24, 2026), the rule adopts an indefinite suspension of de minimis for merchandise arriving through the international postal network. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-12" />

## What applies now: express, freight and other non-postal shipments

Under CBP Dec. 26-12, formerly de minimis shipments must use formal or informal entry; "release from manifest" is no longer available for them; CBP names Entry Type 11 as the main informal entry method (formal entry remains an option); and the Entry Type 86 test has been suspended. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-09" /> CBP's rule refers to the existing informal entry procedures for merchandise valued at $2,500 or less. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-11" /> The difference between the two entry routes is covered in [Entry Type 11 vs 01](/guides/entry-type-11-vs-01/).

Two personal exemptions are not touched: bona fide gifts valued at $100 or less ($200 from certain island possessions) and certain personal or household articles valued at $200 or less that accompany arriving travelers. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-10" />

## What applies now: mail shipments

CBP Dec. 26-13 sets out a postal informal entry process:

| Item | Rule under CBP Dec. 26-13 |
|---|---|
| Value limit for postal informal entry | $2,500 or less |
| Who may file | Owner or purchaser, or a licensed customs broker designated by the owner, purchaser or consignee |
| Bond | Filer must obtain a basic importation and entry bond |
| Data | Spreadsheet with filer code, bond number, country of origin, 10-digit HTSUS, value, foreign-post tracking number |
| Duty payment | Through Pay.gov by the 7th day of the month after arrival |

*Source: Office of the Federal Register, CBP Dec. 26-13 (91 FR 37801), checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-13" />

Payment is due through Pay.gov no later than the 7th day of the month following the shipment's arrival. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-14" /> Postal goods subject to quota or antidumping and countervailing duty orders generally need formal entry under 19 CFR 145.12(a). <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-16" />

Formal entry is also required for postal merchandise that is subject to partner government agency requirements, owes Chapter 98 or Chapter 99 duties, or claims duty-free treatment under Chapter 98 or a free trade agreement. CBP moved the compliance date for that requirement from October 22, 2026 to January 20, 2027. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-15" /> If your goods carry antidumping exposure, read [antidumping duties](/guides/antidumping-duties/) before relying on informal mail entry. If you are deciding whether to keep a supplier shipping direct-to-customer by post or move to bonded bulk import, [customs bonds](/guides/customs-bonds/) covers the bond side.

## What is coming next

CBP published a proposed rule titled "Low-Value Shipments" on October 8, 2026. It would change filing requirements for informal entries of goods valued at $2,500 or less, add an electronic informal entry type for mail, require an additional carrier data element for mail shipments, and add bonding requirements for certain informal entries. Comments are due December 7, 2026. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-18" />

Separately, CBP's non-postal rule states that the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, Public Law 119-21, enacted July 4, 2025, terminated the de minimis exemption effective July 1, 2027. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-17" /> That means the current suspension is an executive and regulatory measure today, with a statutory end point behind it.

## What this changes for a seller's landed cost

With no value floor, every unit you import carries its full duty stack: the HTSUS general rate plus any additional duties in force for its origin, and, on formal entries, the Merchandise Processing Fee. The order of operations and a worked example are in [the 2026 tariff stack guide](/guides/tariff-stack-2026/). Because duty now depends on classification and origin even for a single parcel, [HTS classification basics](/guides/hts-classification-basics/) and [country of origin](/guides/country-of-origin-sourcing/) matter for low-value goods in a way they did not when the shipment could clear duty-free.

## Route finder: which entry path a low-value parcel takes now

Two questions sort every parcel: how it travels, and whether it carries a flag such as an antidumping order or a partner agency requirement. The table combines the non-postal and postal rules above into one lookup.

| If your parcel is… | Entry path | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Express or freight, valued at $2,500 or less | Informal entry, mainly Entry Type 11 (formal entry also allowed); no release from manifest | CBP Dec. 26-12 <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-09" /> <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-11" /> |
| International mail, $2,500 or less, no flags | Postal informal entry by the owner, purchaser or designated broker, with a basic importation and entry bond | CBP Dec. 26-13 <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-13" /> |
| Mail, covered by an antidumping or countervailing duty order or quota | Formal entry, generally | 19 CFR 145.12(a) <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-16" /> |
| Mail, subject to partner agency requirements, Chapter 98/99 duties, or a Chapter 98/FTA duty-free claim | Formal entry, with compliance from January 20, 2027 | CBP extension <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-15" /> |
| A bona fide gift valued at $100 or less ($200 from certain island possessions) | Unchanged by the suspension | Personal exemptions <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-10" /> |

*Source: Office of the Federal Register, CBP Dec. 26-12 and CBP Dec. 26-13, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-09" /> <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-13" /> <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-15" /> <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-16" />

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
A seller's supplier mails an unflagged parcel that arrives in the United States on March 31. Under the postal informal entry process, the duty is due through Pay.gov no later than April 7, the 7th day of the following month. <C id="de-minimis-suspension-2026-14" /> A second parcel arriving one day later, on April 1, is due by May 7, so two parcels a day apart can fall in different payment months.
</Callout>

A single flag moves a mail parcel off the informal path, so check for flags before relying on informal mail entry.

## What to check yourself

1. Search the Federal Register API for "de minimis treatment" sorted by newest, and open anything published after November 8, 2026; a later order or CBP notice can modify or revoke the suspension.
2. Read the DATES section of CBP Dec. 26-12 (non-postal) and CBP Dec. 26-13 (mail) on federalregister.gov to confirm the effective dates for your mode.
3. Open 19 CFR 10.151 and 145.31 on eCFR and confirm the current suspension text.
4. Check the CBP extension notice of October 22, 2026 for the January 20, 2027 postal formal-entry compliance date.
5. Read the "Low-Value Shipments" proposed rule (docket USCBP-2026-0298) if you file informal entries; that rule may change what you submit.
6. For your HTS codes and origin, confirm the general and additional duty rates in force on your entry date before quoting landed cost.
