Most listing elements degrade gracefully when they are wrong — a weak bullet converts less well. The main image does not. It is the one element where a technical failure removes the listing from search entirely.

## The specification

**Main image, hard requirements:**

| Rule | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Background | Pure white, exactly **RGB 255, 255, 255** — not off-white, cream or light grey |
| Frame fill | Product occupies **at least 85%** of the image area |
| Subject | The actual product only — no props or accessories not included |
| Content | No text, logos, watermarks, badges, borders or inset images |
| Minimum size | **1,000 pixels** on the longest side (zoom activates here) |
| Recommended | 1,600 px minimum; 2,000 × 2,000 widely recommended |
| Maximum | Not above 10,000 px on any side; under 10 MB |
| Formats | JPEG, PNG, non-animated GIF, TIFF — JPEG recommended |
| Colour | sRGB or CMYK |
| Layers | Flattened before upload |

Two reported exceptions worth knowing: **necklaces may be cropped at the frame edge**, and for apparel, **ghost mannequin photography is required** — the garment should appear to float, with no mannequin or hanger visible.

**File naming** follows a structure: product identifier, a period, the variant code, then the extension — `B012345678.MAIN.jpg`. No spaces or dashes.

## Why compliant-looking images fail

The two causes that account for most rejections are both about "almost right."

**White balance is not a white background.** Correcting your camera's white balance fixes a colour cast; it does not force the backdrop to 255. One source's recommendation is the reliable one: **isolate the product and place it on a generated 255-white canvas** rather than trusting the camera.

**Compression introduces stray pixels.** Even shot on white, JPEG compression can produce off-white pixels near the product edge. Shadows touching the frame edge, or shadows creating grey gradients, are reported as the same failure. Verify with a colour picker at several points, not one.

**The 85% rule catches people differently.** A product photographed from a distance with lots of white space technically has a white background and still fails the fill requirement. Reporting's framing is right: **the product should dominate the frame.**

## The suppression mechanics

Reported most common causes of image-based suppression:

1. Background not pure white — off-white, grey or transparent
2. Product filling less than 85% of the frame
3. Prohibited text, logos, watermarks or promotional badges
4. Resolution below the 1,000-pixel minimum
5. Props or accessories shown that are not included
6. Product not matching the listing — a blue variant on a red listing
7. A lifestyle shot uploaded into the MAIN slot by mistake

**The silent version is the one to know about.** Reporting describes Amazon not always rejecting non-compliant images immediately: a listing runs fine for weeks, then a periodic compliance sweep suppresses it. **If sessions drop suddenly with no other explanation, check main image compliance first** — see the [reports guide](/guides/seller-central-reports/) for where to look.

**The fix** is Manage Inventory in Seller Central: identify the flagged ASIN and upload a compliant replacement immediately.

## The secondary slots, where the conversion is

The main image is a compliance problem. The other slots are a conversion opportunity, and the rules are much looser: lifestyle images, infographics, text overlays and comparison charts are all permitted. What is not: misleading claims, competitor logos and pricing references.

Reported conversion effects — all from vendors and study summaries without full method, so treat them as directional:

- Zoom-enabled images at 1,600 px or more: **15% to 25% higher conversion**
- Infographic secondary images: **15% to 30%** above plain product photos
- Lifestyle images in secondary slots: **22%** higher add-to-cart
- Size-reference images: **up to 25%** fewer size-related returns

That last one connects directly to the [returns guide](/guides/returns-cost-and-badge/). Size and expectation gaps are the reason codes that drive the returns processing fee and the Frequently Returned Item badge, and a size-reference image is the cheapest available fix.

Reporting suggests using all available slots — typically six to eight, with up to nine permitted — across main, angle, lifestyle, scale and infographic.

**One 2026 note:** reporting states AI tools are permitted to enhance images, create lifestyle backgrounds or generate infographics, provided the core product shown accurately represents the physical item. Fabricating a misleading product image is not permitted.

## What to do

**Verify RGB 255 with a colour picker** at several points, on every main image, before upload. Not by eye.

**Generate the white canvas rather than shooting it.** Isolate the product, place it on a true 255 background, keep contact shadows on the product rather than bleeding into the canvas.

**Shoot at 2,000 × 2,000.** Above the zoom threshold, under the file cap, room to crop.

**Crop to the 85% rule deliberately**, leaving a small margin.

**Check the MAIN slot on every listing.** Uploading a lifestyle shot into it is reported as more common than you would expect.

**Add a size-reference image** to the secondary slots on anything with size-related returns. It is a return-rate fix as much as a conversion one.

**If sessions drop for no reason, check the image before anything else.** Compliance sweeps do not send an obvious warning.
