The merchandise processing fee (MPF) is a CBP charge on merchandise that is formally or informally entered or released. On a formal entry it is 0.3464% of the value of your goods, but never less than $34.58 or more than $670.86 for fiscal year 2027; on an informal entry it is a flat $2.77 to $12.45. <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-01"/> <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-07"/> <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-11"/> <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-13"/> Those FY2027 amounts have been required since October 1, 2026. <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-08"/> Below: where the rule lives, how the math works at different shipment values, which goods are exempt, and how MPF differs from the harbor maintenance fee that often appears on the same entry summary.

## Where the fee comes from

The rule is 19 CFR 24.23. Paragraph (b)(1)(i)(A) says formally entered or released merchandise pays an ad valorem fee of 0.3464 percent, based on the value of the merchandise as determined under 19 U.S.C. 1401a. <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-01"/> <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-02"/> The regulation itself states the floor and ceiling as $25 and $485, "as adjusted" under 19 CFR 24.22(k). <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-03"/> That adjustment is the reason the regulation's numbers never match what you actually pay: CBP determines every year whether the fees and limitations need adjusting, and publishes the result in the Federal Register. <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-04"/>

CBP's FY2027 notice adjusts these amounts as customs user fees established by the Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act (COBRA). <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-21"/> In other words, MPF is a processing fee, not a duty rate tied to your HTS classification, and it sits on top of whatever duties apply.

## Formal vs informal: two different fee formulas

Unless an exemption applies, the formula depends on how the goods are entered.

**Formal entries** pay the percentage, clamped between the minimum and maximum. If the formal entry is filed manually rather than electronically, 19 CFR 24.23(b)(1)(ii) adds a surcharge, which is $4.15 for FY2027. <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-05"/> <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-10"/>

**Informal entries** pay a flat fee under 19 CFR 24.23(b)(2), and the amount depends on whether the entry is automated, manual, or prepared by CBP personnel. <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-06"/> For FY2027 that is $2.77, $8.30 or $12.45 respectively. <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-11"/> <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-12"/> <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-13"/> Informal entry is generally available for shipments not exceeding $2,500, except for articles valued over $250 that are classified in Chapter 99, Subchapters III and IV of the tariff schedule. <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-26"/> Our [type 11 vs type 01 guide](/guides/entry-type-11-vs-01/) covers when each entry type applies.

<Callout type="tip" title="Worked example (hypothetical): where the minimum stops mattering">
Dividing the limits by the rate gives the break points. For FY2027, a formal entry valued below about $9,983 pays the $34.58 minimum, and one valued above about $193,667 pays the $670.86 maximum. Everything in between pays exactly 0.3464% of value.
</Callout>

## Worked examples at several entry values

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): FY2027 MPF on formal and informal entries">
Inputs: FY2027 amounts above, electronic (not manual) filing, no exemption applies, value is the 1401a entered value. Duties, broker charges and freight excluded.

- **$1,500 shipment, informal, automated:** flat **$2.77**.
- **$1,500 shipment entered formally:** 0.3464% × $1,500 = $5.20, below the minimum, so **$34.58**.
- **$5,000 formal entry:** 0.3464% × $5,000 = $17.32, below the minimum, so **$34.58**.
- **$10,000 formal entry:** 0.3464% × $10,000 = **$34.64**, just above the minimum.
- **$50,000 formal entry:** 0.3464% × $50,000 = **$173.20**.
- **$250,000 formal entry:** 0.3464% × $250,000 = $866.00, above the maximum, so **$670.86**.
</Callout>

Two practical takeaways follow from the arithmetic. First, on small formal entries the minimum dominates: a $1,500 order and a $9,000 order cost the same in MPF, so consolidating several small formal entries into one larger one lowers the fee per unit. Second, on large containers the cap does the work, so MPF as a share of landed cost shrinks as value grows. Neither changes your duties, which are calculated separately.

## Goods exempt from MPF

19 CFR 24.23(c) lists exemptions, and several are relevant to sourcing decisions. Among those in paragraph (c)(1) are products of least-developed beneficiary developing countries (HTSUS General Note 4(b)(i)) and products of U.S. insular possessions (General Note 3(a)(iv)). <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-17"/> <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-18"/> Paragraph (c)(3) says the fee does not apply to goods originating within the meaning of General Note 11, HTSUS, entered for consumption on or after July 1, 2020. <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-19"/> Merchandise imported by mail is also exempt, except Inbound EMS items formally entered on or after September 3, 2020. <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-20"/> The regulation lists further exemptions tied to other trade programs, so check the full paragraph against your goods' origin.

<Callout type="warning" title="Exemptions follow the origin claim, not the supplier's address">
An exemption tied to a General Note depends on the goods originating within the meaning of that note. Buying from a supplier located in an eligible country is not the same thing. See our [country-of-origin guide](/guides/country-of-origin-sourcing/).
</Callout>

## MPF vs HMF: the other fee on your entry summary

Ocean shipments can carry a second small percentage next to MPF: the harbor maintenance fee (HMF), governed by 19 CFR 24.24. Under paragraph (a), commercial cargo loaded on or unloaded from a commercial vessel pays a port use fee of 0.125 percent of its value. <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-22"/>

HMF uses the CBP appraised value under 19 U.S.C. 1401a, the same basis used for duty. <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-23"/> The importer of the cargo is liable for it at the time of unloading and pays it by adding it to the duties, taxes and fees due at formal entry, where it is shown on the entry summary. <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-27"/> <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-24"/> It is not assessed on imported cargo where the shipment would be entitled to informal entry under 19 CFR 143.21. <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-25"/>

Worked example (hypothetical): a $50,000 ocean shipment entered formally pays $173.20 in MPF (0.3464%) plus $62.50 in HMF (0.125%), for $235.70 in combined fees before duty. Because HMF is defined by vessel loading and unloading, the same goods flown in on an air waybill would carry the MPF but not this port use fee. <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-01"/> <C id="merchandise-processing-fee-22"/>
