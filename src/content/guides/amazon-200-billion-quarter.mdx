Amazon's net sales for the fourth quarter of 2025 were above $200 billion. The company's release of February 5, 2026 reports net sales of $213.4 billion, up 14% from $187.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2024. <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-07"/> <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-01"/> For marketplace sellers the headline matters less than two lines inside it: third-party seller services, which is what sellers paid Amazon, and advertising services, which includes what sellers spent on ads. This page quotes only the official release figures and explains how to read those lines. It contains no stock or investment commentary.

## The quarter in the official figures

Excluding a $2.8 billion favorable effect from exchange rates, fourth-quarter 2025 net sales grew 12%. <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-02"/> Full-year 2025 net sales were $716.9 billion, up from $638.0 billion in 2024. <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-08"/>

The quarters around it show why Q4 2025 is the reference point. In the releases for the year before it, quarterly net sales were $155.7 billion (Q1 2025), $167.7 billion (Q2 2025), $180.2 billion (Q3 2025) and $187.8 billion (Q4 2024). <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-05"/> <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-04"/> <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-03"/> <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-01"/> Since then, Q1 2026 came in at $181.5 billion, Q2 2026 at $200.6 billion, and Q3 2026 at $201.9 billion. <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-05"/> <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-04"/> <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-06"/>

## The net sales lines, and which ones involve sellers

Amazon's release splits net sales into seven categories. Here is the fourth quarter of 2025 against the same quarter a year earlier.

|---|---|---|
| Online stores | $75,556 | $82,988 |
| Physical stores | $5,579 | $5,855 |
| Third-party seller services | $47,485 | $52,816 |
| Advertising services | $17,288 | $21,317 |
| Subscription services | $11,508 | $13,122 |
| AWS | $28,786 | $35,579 |
| Other | $1,590 | $1,709 |

The remaining lines sit further from marketplace selling. Subscription services includes the annual and monthly fees for Amazon Prime memberships, and physical stores covers product sales where customers pick items in a store. <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-26"/> <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-27"/> Seller commissions and fulfillment fees are reported under third-party seller services instead.

### Third-party seller services is a fee line, not seller sales

The most common misreading is to treat $52,816 million as what independent sellers sold. The footnote says the line "includes commissions and any related fulfillment and shipping fees, and other third-party seller services." <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-13"/> It is the sum of what Amazon charged sellers. The commissions are the referral fees each seller pays per sale — a percentage of the total price or a minimum, whichever is greater — and the related fulfillment and shipping fees are what sellers pay for Amazon's fulfillment and shipping services. <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-24"/> Our guides to [referral fees](/guides/amazon-referral-fees/) and to [the cost lines new sellers miss](/guides/product-research-cost-errors/) cover those charges from the seller's side.

The line grew 11% year over year in Q4 2025, or 10% excluding currency effects. <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-16"/> In Q3 2026 it was $46,492 million, up 9% (10% excluding currency effects). <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-18"/> <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-19"/>

### Advertising services includes, but is not limited to, sellers

The advertising footnote names "sellers, vendors, publishers, authors, and others," and programs "such as sponsored ads, display, and video advertising." <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-14"/> So the line includes sponsored ads bought by marketplace sellers, but it does not isolate them. Advertising grew 23% in Q4 2025 (22% excluding currency effects), faster than seller services. <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-16"/> In Q3 2026 it was $19,637 million, up 11%. <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-18"/> <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-19"/>

### Seller unit mix: sellers' share of paid units

The release also reports "WW seller unit mix" as a share of worldwide paid units: 61% in Q4 2025, against 62% in Q4 2024. <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-17"/> The Q3 2026 release again reports 61%. <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-20"/> Amazon defines units sold as paid units, excluding units associated with AWS and certain acquisitions, among other exclusions. <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-25"/> This metric measures sellers' share of what customers buy, rather than what Amazon earns from them.

Amazon's seller-facing site adds two seller-level data points for 2025: more than 75,000 independent sellers passed $1 million in sales, a 36% increase from 2024, and US independent sellers averaged more than $375,000 in annual sales in Amazon's store. <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-22"/> <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-23"/> An average says nothing about a typical seller's results; the release lines say nothing about any one seller's fees. Your own fee rate is set by the published fee schedule and your category, not by Amazon's totals.

## How to confirm the figures

The figures on this page come from Amazon's quarterly results releases on ir.aboutamazon.com: the fourth-quarter 2025 release of February 5, 2026, and the 2026 first-, second- and third-quarter releases. <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-07"/> <C id="amazon-200-billion-quarter-06"/> The category table and its footnotes are in the supplemental tables at the end of each release; quote them from there rather than from secondary summaries. This page explains published figures for sellers; it is not a basis for investment decisions, and Amazon's SEC filings are the formal record of its results.
