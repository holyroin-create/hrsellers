On a single US entry in late 2026, the amount you pay at customs is the sum of up to four layers: the general (Column 1) duty for the HTS code; any additional duties for the product and its origin, such as Section 301 or Section 232; antidumping or countervailing duties where an order applies; and the user fees, which on a formal ocean entry means the Merchandise Processing Fee (MPF) and the Harbor Maintenance Fee (HMF). The MPF on formal entries is an ad valorem fee of 0.3464 percent, <C id="tariff-stack-2026-01" /> bounded since October 1, 2026 by a $34.58 minimum and a $670.86 maximum. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-02" /> The HMF is 0.125 percent of the cargo's value. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-07" />

What changed most in 2026 is the "additional duties" layer. The duties imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), including the reciprocal tariffs, were terminated in February; <C id="tariff-stack-2026-10" /> a 150-day surcharge under section 122 of the Trade Act followed; <C id="tariff-stack-2026-11" /> and from July 24, 2026, new Section 301 duties apply to products of 60 economies. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-14" /> The order ending the IEEPA duties states that it does not affect Section 232 or Section 301 duties, <C id="tariff-stack-2026-10" /> and USTR stated on October 7, 2026 that the 2018 China Section 301 action remains in effect. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-16" />

<Callout type="note" title="Scope of this page">
This page adds up the layers as the notices describe them. The duties owed on a given entry are those assessed by CBP on that entry.
</Callout>

## Layer 1: the general HTS duty

Every entry starts with the general rate in the Harmonized Tariff Schedule for the article's classification. This page does not cite any individual rate because rates are product-specific; [HTS classification basics](/guides/hts-classification-basics/) covers how to land on the right code. Two of the additional-duty programs below define their rates relative to this "MFN" general rate, which is why classification comes first.

## Layer 2: additional duties in force in 2026

### IEEPA duties: ended

Executive Order 14389, signed February 20, 2026, terminated the IEEPA additional ad valorem duties imposed by a list of orders, including the reciprocal tariff order (EO 14257) and the orders on the northern border, the southern border and the China synthetic opioid supply chain (EOs 14193, 14194 and 14195), stating that they are no longer in effect and should stop being collected as soon as practicable. The same order says it does not affect any other duties, naming Section 232 and Section 301. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-10" />

CBP's October 2026 information-collection notice states that the Supreme Court ruled in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* on February 20, 2026 regarding tariffs imposed under IEEPA, and describes a refund process in which importers of record submit refund requests through the ACE Portal or through a licensed customs broker. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-09" /> If you paid IEEPA duties in 2025, that notice is the starting point for refund mechanics.

### Section 122 surcharge: February 24 to July 24, 2026

Proclamation 11012 imposed a 10 percent ad valorem temporary import surcharge under section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974, effective February 24, 2026, for 150 days, running through 12:01 a.m. EDT on July 24, 2026 unless suspended, modified or terminated earlier, or extended by an Act of Congress. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-11" /> It applied in addition to other duties, but not on top of Section 232 tariffs: where a 232 tariff covered part of an import, the surcharge applied only to the uncovered portion. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-12" /> Goods of Canada or Mexico entered duty-free under the USMCA were among the exceptions. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-13" />

### Section 301: the 2026 actions on 60 economies

The US Trade Representative's notice of July 28, 2026 imposes additional Section 301 duties on products of investigated economies entered on or after 12:01 a.m. eastern time on July 24, 2026. Rates are 10 percent or 12.5 percent; for some economies (including the European Union, Taiwan, Japan, South Korea and Switzerland) the rate is set net of the MFN rate, so MFN plus Section 301 reaches the target rate. Products of China and Vietnam are at 12.5 percent. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-14" />

These duties stack on the general rate: the notice provides that covered products are also subject to the general rates of duty in HTSUS chapters 1 to 97. Articles of aluminum, steel or copper and certain derivative aluminum or steel articles in listed Chapter 99 headings are exempt, as are products of Canada or Mexico entered free of duty under the USMCA. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-15" /> Product-level exemptions sit in the notice's annexes, so check them against your HTS code.

### Section 301: China (2018 actions)

The China Section 301 actions on technology transfer, intellectual property and innovation did not end at their second four-year review date. USTR's October 7, 2026 notice states that the July 6, 2018 action, as modified, did not terminate on July 6, 2026 and remains in effect, subject to possible further modification. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-16" /> That action is separate from the 2026 action on 60 economies, so check both for a Chinese-origin product.

### Section 232

Section 232 duties are product-based. Proclamation 11032, signed June 1, 2026, sets a 25 percent rate for steel and aluminum articles in its Annex I-C unless a lower rate applies, for goods entered on or after 12:01 a.m. EDT on June 8, 2026, with the modified schedule running until 11:59 p.m. EST on December 31, 2027. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-17" /> For listed jurisdictions including Japan, Korea, Switzerland, the United Kingdom, EU members and Taiwan, the Column 1 rate and the 232 rate together total 15 percent where the Column 1 rate is below 15 percent. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-18" /> Other product groups can have their own section 232 proclamation. For example, Proclamation 11052 of August 6, 2026 imposes an additional 15 percent ad valorem duty on polysilicon ingots and derivatives listed in its Annexes I and II, for goods entered on or after 12:01 a.m. eastern time on December 4, 2026. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-20" />

### Antidumping and countervailing duties

If your product and producer fall under an order, AD/CVD deposits are added per entry. See [antidumping duties](/guides/antidumping-duties/).

## Layer 3: user fees

| Fee | Rate or amount | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| MPF, formal entry | 0.3464% of value | Formally entered merchandise |
| MPF minimum (FY2027) | $34.58 | Per formal entry |
| MPF maximum (FY2027) | $670.86 | Per formal entry |
| MPF, informal entry, automated, not prepared by CBP (FY2027) | $2.77 | Per informal entry |
| HMF | 0.125% of value | Commercial cargo loaded on or unloaded from a commercial vessel at a covered port |

*Source: CBP Dec. 26-14 (Federal Register, July 31, 2026) and eCFR 19 CFR 24.23 and 24.24, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="tariff-stack-2026-01" /> <C id="tariff-stack-2026-02" /> <C id="tariff-stack-2026-03" /> <C id="tariff-stack-2026-07" />

The MPF is based on the value of the merchandise as determined under 19 U.S.C. 1401a and is due from the importer of record when the entry summary is filed. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-04" /> It does not apply to originating goods under the USMCA or to products of least-developed beneficiary developing countries. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-05" /> The minimum and maximum are base amounts adjusted for inflation; CBP publishes the adjusted figures in the Federal Register each year at least 60 days before they take effect. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-06" /> CBP's fiscal year 2027 notice left the 0.3464 percent rate unchanged and raised the limits, effective October 1, 2026. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-02" />

The HMF is not assessed on imported cargo that would be entitled to entry under informal entry procedures. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-08" /> Because the fee is tied to cargo moving on a commercial vessel at a covered port, check whether your routing involves one before adding it. Goods unloaded and admitted to a [foreign-trade zone](/guides/foreign-trade-zones/) still pay HMF, filed quarterly. <C id="tariff-stack-2026-19" />

## Worked example (hypothetical)

All inputs here are hypothetical. A formal entry of a consumer product of Vietnam arrives by ocean. Entered value: $10,000. Assumed general HTS rate: 4 percent. Assume the product is not a Section 232 article, is not in an exemption annex of the 2026 Section 301 notice, is not under an AD/CVD order, and is entered after the section 122 surcharge's scheduled end on July 24, 2026 with no extension in force.

| Layer | Basis | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| General duty | 4% × $10,000 (assumed rate) | $400.00 |
| Section 301 (2026 action, Vietnam) | 12.5% × $10,000 | $1,250.00 |
| MPF | 0.3464% × $10,000 = $34.64 (above the $34.58 minimum) | $34.64 |
| HMF | 0.125% × $10,000 | $12.50 |
| **Total** | | **$1,697.14** |

*Source: rates from USTR notice of July 28, 2026, CBP Dec. 26-14 and eCFR 19 CFR 24.24; the 4% rate and all inputs are hypothetical, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="tariff-stack-2026-14" /> <C id="tariff-stack-2026-02" /> <C id="tariff-stack-2026-07" />

Two edge cases change the result. At an entered value of $5,000, the MPF works out to $17.32, below the minimum, so the fee charged is $34.58. If the product were instead an article of steel, which the 2026 Section 301 notice exempts, <C id="tariff-stack-2026-15" /> that Section 301 line would drop out and you would check the Section 232 rate for that article instead.

## What to check yourself

1. Confirm your 10-digit HTS code and its general rate in the current HTSUS before anything else.
2. Search the Federal Register API for "section 301" and "section 232" with your product or origin; open the notice's annexes and match your HTS code against exemptions.
3. Check whether any action after July 24, 2026 extended or replaced the section 122 surcharge before assuming it no longer applies.
4. Read CBP Dec. 26-14 (July 31, 2026) for the MPF limits that apply to entries from October 1, 2026, and watch for the fiscal year 2028 notice in mid-2027.
5. Open 19 CFR 24.23 and 24.24 on eCFR for MPF exemptions and HMF scope on your routing.
6. If you paid IEEPA duties in 2025 or early 2026, read CBP's October 5, 2026 notice on court-ordered refunds.
