Amazon's 2026 Multi-Channel Fulfillment (MCF) rate card states one thing clearly: "a 3.5% fuel and logistics-related surcharge will be applied to MCF fulfillment fees in the US." <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-01"/> If you use MCF to ship orders from other channels out of Amazon's warehouses, that 3.5% belongs in your fulfillment cost. The card's peak fee tables run October 15, 2026 to January 14, 2027, and it does not say whether those listed peak rates already include the surcharge. <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-05"/> For orders placed on Amazon and fulfilled by FBA, the surcharge's scope, rate and start date are published inside Seller Central, which is not publicly readable — check the fee announcement there before you reprice. This page covers what Amazon has published openly and how to model it.

## What Amazon says the surcharge is for

Amazon's MCF rate card explains the reasoning in three steps. First: "Elevated fuel and logistics costs have increased operating expenses across the industry." <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-02"/> Second, Amazon says it had absorbed those increases until now. <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-03"/> Third, the 3.5% applies "to help offset a portion of these costs, consistent with other major carriers." <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-04"/>

## Which fees the 3.5% applies to

Amazon's wording names **fulfillment fees** as the base. <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-01"/> MCF fulfillment fees themselves depend on four inputs: product size, shipping weight, units per order and delivery speed. <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-07"/>

Storage, labeling, inbound and removal fees are a separate group. Amazon states that for MCF these non-fulfillment fees "will continue to be the same as Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA)." <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-06"/> The published surcharge sentence does not name them.

For other percentage surcharges, Amazon does spell out the base. The rate card says the Amazon Logistics block surcharge and the remote-area surcharge are calculated after any lithium battery fee is added to the base fulfillment fee. <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-18"/> The fuel and logistics surcharge text gives no such ordering rule, so check how it appears on your invoice if you sell battery items.

## What the card leaves open on peak rates

The same card carries tables headed "Peak fulfillment fees," effective October 15, 2026 to January 14, 2027. It does not say whether the listed peak rates already include the 3.5% or whether the 3.5% is added to them. <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-05"/> In the standard-speed peak table, the large standard row for over 1 lb to 2 lb lists $10.97 in its first ("1") column. <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-20"/> Compare a peak-period MCF invoice with the table to see which way your charges were built. The peak rates themselves, and when they apply, are covered in our guide to the [holiday peak fulfillment fee](/guides/holiday-peak-fulfilment-fee/).

The surcharge scales with the fee. Heavier and faster shipments carry larger fees, so they absorb more of the 3.5% in dollar terms, while small, light items barely move.

## Amazon has done this before

A fuel-related surcharge on FBA fees is not new. In a November 17, 2022 message, Amazon wrote that "earlier this year, we introduced a Fuel & Inflation Surcharge for our FBA fees." <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-08"/> The same message said Amazon would "remove the separate Fuel & Inflation Surcharge" and that it would raise FBA outbound fee rates by $0.22 on average. <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-09"/> <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-11"/> Those changes took effect January 17, 2023, except where noted. <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-10"/>

The 2026 FBA rate change itself was modest: Amazon said FBA fees would rise by an average of $0.08 per unit sold, effective January 15, 2026 unless otherwise noted. <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-12"/> <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-13"/>

## What it means for your channel mix

- **MCF for other channels.** Add 3.5% to every fulfillment fee in your model. <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-01"/>
- **Amazon Logistics blocks.** If you block Amazon Logistics as a carrier, the 5% block surcharge is waived for MCF orders, including Walmart, through January 14, 2027. <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-19"/> Recheck the rate card for blocked orders after that date.
- **Upstream storage.** AWD advertises "no seasonal surcharges." <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-14"/> That statement concerns AWD storage and processing pricing, not FBA or MCF fulfillment fees.

## How to confirm the surcharge on your account

Start with Amazon Supply Chain's MCF rate card at supplychain.amazon.com/docs/rate-card (linked from the MCF pricing page) and the 2026 card at supplychain.amazon.com/docs/2026-peak-card; they publish the 3.5% statement, the peak dates and the rate tables. <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-01"/> <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-05"/> For FBA orders on Amazon, open Seller Central and go to Reports, then Fulfillment, then Fee Preview. <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-15"/> The same data is available through the Selling Partner API as the FBA Fee Preview Report, which holds estimated selling and fulfillment fees for FBA inventory with active offers and is updated at least once every 72 hours. <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-16"/> <C id="fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge-2026-17"/> Then compare those estimates with the fees on a recent settlement. This page summarizes Amazon's published statements. If a charge on your account doesn't match them, raise it in a Seller Central support case.
