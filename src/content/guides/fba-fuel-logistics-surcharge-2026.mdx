If you priced your 2026 catalog in January, you priced it against less than half the change. The January increase came with three months' notice and wall-to-wall coverage. The surcharge came with fifteen days' notice. For most sellers it is the bigger number.

## What Amazon announced

The Seller Central announcement of April 2, 2026 states the terms directly. Elevated fuel and logistics costs across the industry; a temporary surcharge, as other major carriers apply, to recover part of the increase; calculated on fulfillment fees, not on item price.

| Effective | Program | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| April 17, 2026 | Fulfillment by Amazon | US and Canada |
| April 17, 2026 | Remote Fulfillment with FBA | US into Canada, Mexico and Brazil |
| May 2, 2026 | Buy with Prime | US |
| May 2, 2026 | Multi-Channel Fulfillment | US and Canada |

Amazon's stated average impact is $0.17 per unit for US FBA. That average, like January's $0.08, is a mean across a catalog nothing like yours. The Revenue Calculator, the Profit Analytics dashboard and the Fee and Economics Preview report were all updated to show the surcharge per unit and across your whole account.

What triggered it was the spring 2026 oil shock: the Iran conflict closed most transit through the Strait of Hormuz and crude moved from roughly $60 a barrel in early February to over $110 by early April. UPS and FedEx had already raised their fuel surcharges. Amazon's line is that 3.5% is lower than the carriers'; sellers' line, visible in the replies to the announcement, is that surcharges get renamed rather than removed. The 2022 precedent supports the sellers.

## Why a percentage is a different animal

A flat per-unit increase is regressive against price: eight cents matters on a $6 item and disappears on a $60 one. A percentage of the fulfillment fee is the opposite. It scales with the fee, and the fee scales with size and weight.

| Unit | Fulfillment fee | 3.5% surcharge | With the January +$0.08 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small standard, light | $3.50 | $0.12 | $0.20 |
| Standard, 2 lb | $5.60 | $0.20 | $0.28 |
| Large standard, heavy | $9.00 | $0.32 | $0.40 |
| Oversize | $18.00 | $0.63 | $0.71 |

Illustrative fees, to show the shape. The seller who read the January announcement and added eight cents to every SKU is under-forecasting by nine times on the oversize end. Nobody is over-forecasting.

## The compounding

Since January 2026 the selling price is an input to the FBA fulfillment fee through three bands: under $10, $10 to $50, and above $50. The surcharge is a percentage of that fee. So a price change now moves your fulfillment cost twice — once by crossing a band, again through the percentage on the new fee.

Raise a $49 item to $52 to protect margin and you may cross into the upper band, take a larger fulfillment fee, and take a larger surcharge on it. Discount a $10.49 item to $9.99 and it drops into Low-Price FBA rates, which average $0.86 lower — and the surcharge shrinks with the fee. A repricer that treats fulfillment cost as a constant stopped describing the platform in January.

From October 15 the stack has a fourth layer. The [holiday peak fulfillment fee](/guides/holiday-peak-fulfilment-fee/) adds an average $0.32 per unit and Amazon has confirmed the 3.5% applies on top of it:

```
Q4 unit fulfillment cost = (base 2026 fee + peak uplift) × 1.035
```

## Where to read your own figure

`Seller Central > Reports > Fulfillment > Payments > Fee Preview` — the fulfillment fee per FNSKU at current rates, surcharge included. Export the CSV and sort by fulfillment fee descending; the top of that list is where the surcharge costs most.

`Seller Central > Reports > Payments > Transaction View` — filter Service fee and look for the surcharge line on shipped orders since April 17. Ninety days of this is your actual exposure, not an estimate.

`Seller Central > Growth > Profit Analytics` — per-product unit economics with the fee lines separated.

## What to do about it

- **Recalculate on the fee, not on the announcement.** Pull actual fulfillment fees for your top SKUs and multiply by 1.035. Do not add $0.17 to everything.
- **Split "Amazon fees" into lines in your accounts.** Referral, fulfillment, surcharge, placement, storage and returns now move independently and on different dates. A combined line hides which one broke the margin.
- **Re-check the repricing floor on SKUs near $10 and $50.** They are where band crossing and the percentage compound.
- **Model the heavy end separately.** A percentage concentrates cost in the SKUs that were already marginal. For bulky items, run the [FBA versus FBM break-even](/guides/fba-vs-fbm-breakeven/) properly this year instead of assuming FBA wins on convenience.
- **Budget for it to stay.** Treat the surcharge as permanent until Amazon's January 2027 fee update says otherwise, and expect it to reappear as base rate rather than disappear.

**Run it on your own figures.** The [margin and break-even calculator](/tools/margin-calculator/) takes price, cost of goods, referral rate, fulfillment fee, returns and ad spend and returns net profit per unit, net margin and the maximum ACOS the product can carry. Nothing is stored and nothing leaves your browser.
