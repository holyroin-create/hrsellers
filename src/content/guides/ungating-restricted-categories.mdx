Before buying inventory for a new category or brand, find out which kind of gate it sits behind. Amazon separates four situations: categories open to all sellers, categories that need a Professional selling plan, categories that need Amazon's approval, and categories third-party sellers cannot sell in at all. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-01"/> Approval can be applied for; a category closed to third-party sellers cannot. Check each ASIN before you place a purchase order.

Amazon's category-by-category approval requirements are published inside Seller Central; this page covers what Amazon documents publicly about how the gates work and what an application involves. Check the current requirements for your category in Seller Central before applying.

## Which categories are gated

Amazon says that, to help ensure safety and authenticity, categories that commonly require approval include Beauty, Health, and Personal Care; Clothing and Accessories; Grocery and Gourmet; Jewelry; and Watches. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-02"/> Separately, some products may not be listed at all as a matter of compliance with legal or regulatory restrictions. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-04"/> Amazon's seller training pairs the two topics, covering its Restricted Products policy and which categories require approval. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-22"/>

Jewelry shows how granular this gets. Amazon says certain types of fine jewelry require approval, some items go through a certification process to meet its quality standards, <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-19"/> and to get approval for other fine jewelry and gemstones a seller should choose a Professional selling plan and enroll in Brand Registry. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-20"/> The Professional plan costs $39.99 per month plus a per-item referral fee that varies by category. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-23"/> For the trademark side of Brand Registry, see [Brand Registry and trademarks](/guides/brand-registry-trademark/).

## Category gate, brand gate, or hard block

The three are different problems with different fixes. Amazon's own example: to sell Nike shoes, a seller may need both category approval and brand authorization. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-03"/> Some branded products need permission from the brand or Amazon before they can be sold. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-24"/>

Amazon's restriction check (the Listings Restrictions API) can return three reason codes:

Two details matter when you check. First, restrictions can be filtered by condition type, <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-06"/> so an ASIN open to you in one condition can be restricted in another; check the condition you actually plan to sell. Second, if no restriction applies, the list returned is simply empty. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-12"/>

If you have already submitted a listing, the gate appears as a listing issue instead: QUALIFICATION_REQUIRED means approval or qualification is required to list the product. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-11"/> Diagnosing other listing problems is covered in [why an Amazon listing disappears](/guides/listing-disappeared/).

### Brand-name errors

Brand gates also surface as numbered errors. Error 5665 can appear when you enter a brand name that has not been approved by Amazon, <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-18"/> and error 5461 means you do not have permission to create new detail pages for that brand. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-25"/> Products enrolled in Transparency add another layer: Amazon requires a valid Transparency code to list or ship them. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-21"/>

The invoice Amazon names is one you received from a manufacturer or distributor. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-15"/> If instead you see a Sell this product button, you are already approved for that item. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-26"/> You can follow open applications from the Catalog menu in Seller Central. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-31"/>

## What two gated categories ask for

Two examples Amazon documents publicly:

For grocery, Amazon says labels in the US store must be in English and meet all FDA and USDA labeling guidelines, <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-32"/> and every unit should carry a clearly and permanently marked expiration date. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-33"/> For over-the-counter drugs it lists FDA registration and drug facts panel compliance. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-35"/> For supplements, Amazon estimates $500 to $2,000 per product for testing and a Certificate of Analysis. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-30"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): is approval worth the wait?">
Inputs: you plan a first order of 200 units at $9.00 each in a gated grocery item, and you would pay a supplier deposit of 30% before approval. Deposit at risk: 200 × $9.00 × 0.30 = $540. If the ASIN returns NOT_ELIGIBLE, there is no application route, so that $540 buys inventory you cannot list on Amazon. Running the restriction check before paying the deposit tells you whether there is an application route at all. <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-08"/>
</Callout>

## How to confirm it for your products

Search each ASIN in Seller Central's Add Products and note whether an Apply to sell button appears, <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-13"/> then read Amazon's own guidance in [3 ways to sell branded products on Amazon](https://sell.amazon.com/blog/sell-branded-products-on-amazon) and the category list in [How to sell online](https://sell.amazon.com/sell-online). <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-02"/> Developers can check restrictions with the [Listings Restrictions API](https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/listings-restrictions-api). <C id="ungating-restricted-categories-05"/>
