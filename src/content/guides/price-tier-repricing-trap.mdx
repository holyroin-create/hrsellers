For most of FBA's history, the fulfilment fee was a function of two things you could measure with a scale and a tape measure. Price was not one of them. You could reprice all day and the pick-and-pack cost stayed where it was.

That is no longer true, and the consequence is not obvious: it is now possible to raise a price and reduce your contribution per unit.

## What changed

From 15 January 2026, Amazon updated how fulfilment fees are calculated so that they reflect product price brackets alongside size and weight. Three bands are reported for US standard-size items:

| Band | Reported treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Under $10 | Low-Price FBA rates, reported at roughly $0.86 below standard on average |
| $10 to $50 | Standard rates; reported average increase of about $0.08 per unit |
| Above $50 | Standard rates; reported average increase of about $0.31 per unit |

Within the above-$50 band, reporting breaks the increase down further: about $0.51 for small standard and about $0.31 for large standard. In the $10–$50 band, about $0.25 for small standard and about $0.05 for large standard.

Note what that means. **The increase is roughly five times larger for small standard items above $50 than for small standard items between $10 and $50.** The widely quoted $0.08 average is the middle band's number, and it is the number Amazon led with.

## The two boundaries and what happens at them

There are two places on the price line where a small move produces a step change rather than a smooth one.

### The $10 boundary

Below it you are in Low-Price FBA, reported at around $0.86 per unit cheaper on average, with the discount ranging by size and weight from roughly $0.30 to $0.77 per unit on one account. Above it you pay standard rates.

For a product sitting at $9.49, a move to $10.49 is a 10.5% price increase that also adds most of a dollar to your fulfilment cost. On a low-price item that is frequently more than the extra revenue.

**Do the arithmetic before crossing upward.** Extra revenue is price increase minus the referral fee on it. Extra cost is the lost Low-Price discount plus the surcharge on the larger fee. For a $1.00 increase at a 15% referral rate you gain $0.85 and can lose most of it.

### The $50 boundary

Below it, standard rates with the smaller 2026 increase. Above it, standard rates with the larger one — reported at about $0.51 per unit for small standard.

One published analysis puts it directly: if your product sits just above $50, it is worth running the numbers on whether a pricing adjustment makes sense.

**This cuts both ways.** A product at $51 might do better at $49.99, keeping the lower fee and gaining the psychological price point. A product at $54 probably should not come down, because the fee difference is fixed while the revenue difference is not.

## Where it compounds

The 3.5% fuel and logistics surcharge, reported as effective 17 April 2026, is calculated on the fulfilment fee rather than on the sale price. So crossing a band upward raises the fee, and then raises the surcharge on the new fee.

The effect is small in absolute terms and worth understanding structurally:

| Scenario | Fulfilment fee | 3.5% surcharge | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small standard, $10–$50 band | $3.32 | $0.12 | $3.44 |
| Same item, above $50 | $3.83 | $0.13 | $3.96 |

Illustrative, using a reported small-standard rate of about $3.32 in the $10–$50 band and adding the reported $0.51 above-$50 increase. The point is not the 52 cents. The point is that **one price change moved three numbers**, and a margin model that treats fulfilment as fixed caught none of them.

## Why this breaks repricers specifically

A repricer works from a floor: the lowest price at which the SKU still makes an acceptable margin. That floor is calculated from cost of goods, referral fee, fulfilment fee and a target.

If the fulfilment fee in that calculation is a static number entered once, then:

**Your floor is wrong on any SKU near a boundary.** A repricer that drops a $10.40 item to $9.95 to win the buy box has actually improved your margin more than it thinks, because the item moved into Low-Price FBA. A repricer that raises a $49.50 item to $51 has worsened it more than it thinks.

**The error is invisible in reporting.** The repricer reports the margin it calculated, not the margin you got. The difference lands in your settlement report weeks later, blended with everything else.

**It is worst where it matters most.** Boundary-adjacent SKUs are usually the ones being actively repriced, because competitive price points cluster around round numbers.

## What to do about it

**List every SKU within 10% of a boundary.** Anything between $9 and $11, and anything between $45 and $55. That list is short, and it is where the entire problem lives. Everything else can wait.

**Get the real fee, per SKU, from Amazon.** The Selling Economics and Fee Preview report gives current fulfilment fees at SKU level. That is your input. An average from a rate card is not.

**Check whether your repricer supports variable fulfilment cost.** Some do; some accept a static cost per SKU that you must update yourself. If yours is static, the fix is to maintain the number for boundary SKUs manually, which is tolerable because the list is short.

**Set floors band by band, not catalogue-wide.** A single margin percentage applied across a catalogue produces a wrong floor on every boundary SKU. Three rules — one per band — costs almost nothing to maintain.

**Re-examine bundling.** Bundling small items raises the unit price, which can push the bundle across the $10 or $50 line, changing the fee band. It also changes the size tier and the dimensional weight. Reporting gives an example of three small items at about $3.22 each in fees against a single bundled unit at about $4.65 under 1.5 lb — a real saving, but one that has to be checked against the band effect and the new size tier rather than assumed.

**Do not chase the boundary at the expense of the customer.** Pricing at $49.99 to stay under $50 is sensible if the market supports it. Cutting a $58 product to $49.99 to save fifty cents of fee is not — you gave away eight dollars of revenue to save half of one.

**Re-run this in January.** Amazon has revised US fulfilment fees in January in recent cycles, with roughly three months' notice in the previous round. Whatever you build now should be reviewed against the next announcement rather than trusted indefinitely.
