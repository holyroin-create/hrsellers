A customs exam runs on a regulatory clock: under 19 CFR 151.16, goods that are not released within five business days of being presented for examination become "detained merchandise," and CBP must then send you a detention notice and reach a final admissibility decision within 30 days of presentation. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-09"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-10"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-15"/> A seizure is a different track with its own deadlines — most importantly, 30 days from the mailing of the seizure notice to file a petition. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-24"/> Knowing which track you are on decides what you should do next.

## How CBP chooses what to examine

The port director examines such packages or quantities of merchandise as he deems necessary, and the regulation sets a floor of not less than one package in every 10 unless a special regulation permits fewer. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-01"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-02"/> You will not be told in advance which packages: that information is not made available to the importer until the merchandise has arrived within the limits of the port of entry. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-03"/> Merchandise generally is not opened or examined until it has been entered (the rule lists exceptions, such as sampling by authorized government agencies), and examination happens at the place of arrival unless the port director requires or authorizes another location. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-04"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-05"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-07"/> The broader authority sits in 19 CFR 162.6: persons, baggage and merchandise arriving in the Customs territory of the United States are liable to inspection and search by a Customs officer. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-35"/>

The exam is not necessarily the end. If CBP later requires samples or additional packages after release and they are not provided, it may make a demand under your bond, and for conditionally released goods the bond commits you to redeliver them on timely demand in listed cases, including when they must be examined. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-19"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-34"/>

Note the units. The first two deadlines are business days; the 30-day final determination is counted from the date of presentation without that qualifier. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-09"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-15"/> The detention notice is the most useful document you will receive, because it tells you the specific reason, the anticipated length, the nature of the tests or inquiries, and what information could accelerate the outcome. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-11"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-12"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-13"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-14"/> If testing is involved, the importer or another party with an interest in the goods can make a written request and CBP will provide copies of the test results. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-17"/> Detention is not a safe harbor: if otherwise provided by law, detained merchandise may be seized and forfeited. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-18"/>

## Who pays for an exam

The regulations assign cost to the importer in plain terms, without setting an amount.

| Cost | Who pays, per 19 CFR |
|---|---|
| Preparing merchandise for examination | The importer bears any expense involved (except public-stores exams) |
| Exam held outside a port of entry or permanent Customs station | The importer pays additional expenses, including actual travel and subsistence |
| General order storage when goods are not entered in time | At the risk and expense of the consignee; ordinary storage rates and other expenses paid on entry |

*Source: eCFR — 19 CFR 151.6, 151.7 and Part 127, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-06"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-08"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-30"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-32"/>

The general order row catches sellers who wait. Entry is due within 15 calendar days after landing; merchandise not entered within the time provided by law becomes general order merchandise, and goods left in Customs custody for 6 months from importation without all estimated duties and storage or other charges paid are considered unclaimed and abandoned. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-33"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-29"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-31"/>

## If CBP seizes the goods

A seizure starts with a receipt given at the time of seizure to the person from whom the property is taken. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-21"/> The notice that follows states the provisions of law alleged to have been violated and your right to apply for relief under section 618 of the Tariff Act of 1930. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-22"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-23"/> Petitions for relief go to the Fines, Penalties, and Forfeitures office whose address is in the notice, within 30 days from the date the notice was mailed. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-25"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-24"/> For a commercial violation, the petition must be signed by you, your attorney or a customs broker. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-26"/>

<Stat value="20 days" label="To file a claim after the first published notice of a summary forfeiture" claim="customs-holds-and-exams-27" />

Summary forfeiture runs on an even shorter clock: a claim is due within 20 days of the first publication of the notice, accompanied by a bond in the penal sum of $5,000 or 10% of the value of the claimed property, whichever is lower, but not less than $250. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-27"/> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-28"/>

## Mistakes that cost sellers time

| Mistake | Why it hurts, per the regulations |
|---|---|
| Counting calendar days for the first deadline | The detention trigger is five business days from presentation, not five calendar days. |
| Waiting for the 30-day mark before responding | The detention notice names the information that may accelerate the decision; supplying it early is the lever you control. |
| Ignoring a missed day-30 deadline | Silence is a deemed exclusion, and the remedy — a protest — has to be used. |
| Treating a seizure notice like a detention notice | The petition deadline runs 30 days from the notice's mailing date, not from when you read it. |
| Leaving goods unentered while you decide | Unentered goods go to general order at the consignee's risk and expense. |

## How to confirm it for your shipment

Read 19 CFR 151.16 for detention, Part 162 for seizures and Part 171 for petitions on eCFR; the notice you receive will cite the provision CBP relies on. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-22"/> Seizure and forfeiture involve deadlines that cannot be recovered once missed, so a customs broker or trade attorney should review the notice the day it arrives — this page lays out the regulatory timetable, not the merits of your case.
