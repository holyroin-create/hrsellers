When CBP holds a shipment, the regulations set fixed deadlines. Once merchandise is presented for examination, CBP has five business days to decide whether to release or detain it. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-08" /> If it detains, it then has 30 days from presentation to make a final admissibility determination. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-12" /> Missing that deadline counts as a decision to exclude the goods, and you can protest it. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-13" /> The costs of preparing and moving the goods for the exam generally fall on the importer. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-10" />

A seizure is a different legal track from a detention, with its own notice and petition deadlines. This page sets out the examination rules in 19 CFR Part 151, the detention procedure in 19 CFR 151.16, and the seizure timelines in Parts 162 and 171.

<Callout type="note" title="Scope of this page">
This page explains the regulations that govern exams and detentions. What happens to a particular held shipment depends on the reason CBP gives, which your customs broker can act on.
</Callout>

## How CBP chooses what to examine

The port director examines whatever packages or quantities he deems necessary. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-01" /> The regulation's baseline is not less than one package in every 10. For uniform packages, the port director may examine fewer, but not less than one package per invoice. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-02" /> An authorized CBP official may also take samples for appraisement, classification or other official purposes. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-07" />

Examinations often turn on classification and labeling. If you sell goods where origin labeling matters, see [origin marking requirements](/guides/origin-marking-requirements/). For classification, see [HTS classification basics](/guides/hts-classification-basics/).

## Where the exam happens and who pays

Merchandise is examined at the place of arrival unless the port director requires or authorizes another place. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-03" /> Examination elsewhere, such as at the importer's premises, can be allowed. When it takes place outside port limits, the importer bears additional expenses such as travel and subsistence. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-05" /> When a shipment needs examination at a centralized examination station (CES), the entry filer requests permission to move it on Customs Form 3461 or an electronic equivalent, and the move is made under bond. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-06" />

On cost, two provisions point the same way. Section 151.6 says the importer bears any expense of preparing the merchandise for examination. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-04" /> Section 151.16(b) says that, except when merchandise is examined at the public stores, the importer must pay all costs of preparing and transporting the merchandise for CBP examination. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-10" />

| Cost item | Who pays under the regulation |
|---|---|
| Preparing merchandise for examination | Importer |
| Preparation and transportation for examination (other than at the public stores) | Importer |
| Travel and subsistence when examination is outside port limits | Importer |

## Detention: the 5-day and 30-day clocks

### When the clock starts

Every detention deadline runs from presentation. Merchandise is presented for examination when it is in a condition to be viewed and examined by a CBP officer. Presenting a container or cargo van alone does not count. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-09" /> If your goods are still sealed inside a container, the clock has not started.

### The sequence

The detention notice must state the specific reason for the detention, its anticipated length, and the kind of information that could speed up a decision if you supply it. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-11" /> That last element is the practical lever: the notice tells you what to send.

If no final admissibility determination is made within 30 days of presentation, the failure is treated as a decision to exclude the merchandise, and that deemed exclusion may be protested. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-13" /> If a protest of the exclusion is not decided, it is treated as denied on the 30th day after filing. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-16" /> Protest mechanics are covered in [prior disclosure, protests and liquidation](/guides/prior-disclosure-protests/).

### When these clocks do not apply

These detention provisions do not apply to detentions CBP makes on behalf of other agencies. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-14" /> If your hold comes from another agency's admissibility requirements, the 5-day and 30-day rules in 151.16 are not the governing timeline.

## Reading a hold from a marketplace seller's side

For a seller waiting on inventory for a fulfillment center, these provisions answer three practical questions. First, has the clock started? That depends on presentation, not on the vessel or flight arrival date. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-09" /> Second, what does CBP want? The detention notice has to say what information could speed things up. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-11" /> Third, what will it cost? Preparation and transport for the exam are on the importer unless the exam is at the public stores. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-10" /> Inbound appointment dates and stock-out planning should allow for the full 30-day window after presentation. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-12" />

## Seizure: a separate track

Detained merchandise may be seized and forfeited where the law provides. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-15" /> A Customs officer who has reasonable cause to believe there was a violation may seize property, and must give a receipt at the time of seizure. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-17" /> The seizure notice must tell each interested party of the right to apply for relief under section 618. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-18" />

Petitions for relief from seizure must be filed within 30 days of the mailing of the seizure notice. The Fines, Penalties, and Forfeitures Officer may grant extensions. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-19" /> The petition goes to the Fines, Penalties, and Forfeitures office named in the notice. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-22" /> In summary forfeiture, a claim must be filed within 20 days of the first publication of the notice. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-20" /> Publication may be delayed up to 30 days if a petition for administrative relief is expected. <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-21" />

| | Detention (19 CFR 151.16) | Seizure (19 CFR Parts 162 and 171) |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Merchandise presented for examination | Officer's reasonable cause to believe a violation occurred |
| First paper you get | Detention notice within 5 business days of the decision | Receipt at the time of seizure; notice of right to petition |
| Your main deadline | Supply information listed in the notice; protest a deemed exclusion | Petition within 30 days of mailing of the seizure notice |
| CBP's deadline | Final determination within 30 days of presentation | Publication delay capped at 30 days when a petition is expected |

## Common mistakes when a shipment is held

Most costly errors on a hold come from running the wrong clock or treating one legal track as another. The matrix pairs each mistake with what the regulation says and the consequence that follows from the rule itself.

| Mistake | What the rule actually says | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Counting the five-business-day decision window from vessel or flight arrival | The window runs from presentation, when goods can be viewed and examined; a container alone does not count <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-08" /> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-09" /> | Your count starts too early, so you expect a decision before CBP's clock has begun |
| Assuming CBP absorbs exam handling costs | The importer pays preparation and transport for examination, except at the public stores <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-10" /> | Exam charges belong in that shipment's landed cost |
| Treating silence after 30 days as "still pending" | No final determination within 30 days of presentation is treated as an exclusion, which may be protested <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-13" /> | Without a protest, the deemed exclusion stands unchallenged |
| Waiting indefinitely on a filed protest | An undecided protest of the exclusion is treated as denied on the 30th day after filing <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-16" /> | The protest is treated as denied on day 30 whether or not CBP responds |
| Applying the 151.16 clocks to every hold | They do not apply to detentions CBP makes on behalf of other agencies <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-14" /> | Your timeline may be set by a different process |
| Reading a seizure notice as a detention notice | A petition for relief is due within 30 days of the notice's mailing, filed with the office named in it <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-19" /> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-22" /> | Filing late depends on an extension the Fines, Penalties, and Forfeitures Officer may grant |
| Skimming past the detention notice's information request | The notice must list information that may accelerate disposition <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-11" /> | Not supplying it gives up a lever the regulation hands you |

*Source: Office of the Federal Register / National Archives (eCFR), 19 CFR 151.16, 171.2 and related sections, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-09" /> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-13" /> <C id="customs-holds-and-exams-19" />

The first and last rows interact: the date you diary is presentation, and the document you act on is the detention notice. Getting both right sets every later deadline in this matrix.

## What to check yourself

1. Read 19 CFR 151.16 on eCFR in full, including paragraph (a), to confirm whether your hold is a CBP detention or one made for another agency.
2. Confirm the presentation date with your broker, since every detention deadline runs from it.
3. Read 19 CFR 151.6 and 151.7 if CBP proposes examining goods away from the place of arrival.
4. If goods are seized, read 19 CFR 171.2 for the petition deadline and filing office, and 19 CFR 162.45 to 162.47 if summary forfeiture is published.
5. Check that your bond is in force for the entry (see [customs bonds](/guides/customs-bonds/)).
