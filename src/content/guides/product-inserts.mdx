A product insert can say thank you, explain how to use the product, and point to support, but three federal rules limit what it can say about reviews and warranties: no incentives tied to a review's sentiment, no pressure to keep a bad review off the page, and no warranty card that customers must return to keep a full warranty. <C id="product-inserts-01"/> <C id="product-inserts-02"/> <C id="product-inserts-04"/> Amazon's own product insert policy is published inside Seller Central, and that page is the one to check before you print.

## Inserts and branded packaging

Amazon presents packaging that ships under its SIPP program as a way to display brand and product messages and improve the unboxing experience. <C id="product-inserts-11"/> The federal rules below apply to what the message says, whether it is printed on the box or on a loose card.

## Reviews: what an insert cannot offer

Under 16 CFR 465.4 it is an unfair or deceptive practice for a business to provide compensation or other incentives in exchange for, or conditioned on, consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment, positive or negative. <C id="product-inserts-01"/> The condition can be express or implied.

Under 16 CFR 465.7, no one may use an unfounded or groundless legal threat, a physical threat, intimidation, or a public false accusation made knowingly or with reckless disregard for the truth, in an attempt to prevent a review or get one removed. <C id="product-inserts-02"/> Other provisions of 16 CFR Part 465 are covered in the [review manipulation guide](/guides/review-manipulation-risk/); this page sticks to what is printed in the box.

## Warranty and registration cards

16 CFR 700.7 defines a warranty registration card as one the consumer must return shortly after purchase, which the warranty states or implies is a condition of coverage. <C id="product-inserts-03"/> Requiring the consumer to return such a card as a condition of performance under a full warranty is an unreasonable duty. <C id="product-inserts-04"/> The regulation gives "This warranty is void unless the warranty registration card is returned to the warrantor" as an example of wording that is not allowed. <C id="product-inserts-05"/>

A full warranty may still provide that the consumer can fill out and return a card to place proof of the purchase date on file, <C id="product-inserts-06"/> but that suggestion must include notice that failure to return the card will not affect rights under the warranty, so long as the consumer can show the purchase date in a reasonable manner. <C id="product-inserts-07"/> The section does not stop a seller from collecting information the warrantor requests at the time of sale. <C id="product-inserts-12"/>

### Is your thank-you card a written warranty?

16 CFR 702.1 defines a written warranty to include a written affirmation of fact or written promise, made in connection with the sale of a consumer product, that relates to the material or workmanship and affirms it is defect free or will meet a specified level of performance over a specified period. <C id="product-inserts-13"/> A card promising, for example, that a product will perform for a stated number of years can fall within that definition, which brings the warranty rules into play.

### Making warranty terms available before the sale

The pre-sale availability rules in 16 CFR 702.3 apply to consumer products actually costing the consumer more than $15.00. <C id="product-inserts-08"/> A warrantor may provide the warranty terms in an accessible digital format on its own website, <C id="product-inserts-09"/> and must then indicate clearly and conspicuously, in the product manual or on the product or packaging, where the terms can be reviewed. <C id="product-inserts-10"/> For many sellers that makes the insert, or the box, the place where the warranty web address belongs.

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): which products need the pre-sale step?">
Inputs: a catalog with a $12.99 phone stand and a $24.99 desk lamp, both sold with a written warranty.

- Phone stand: $12.99 is not more than $15.00, so 16 CFR 702.3 does not reach it. <C id="product-inserts-08"/>
- Desk lamp: $24.99 is more than $15.00, so the warranty terms must be available before sale; if they are posted online, the lamp's manual, product or packaging must say where. <C id="product-inserts-10"/>

The threshold turns on what the consumer actually pays, so a discount that takes a product under $15.00 changes the answer for that sale.
</Callout>

## Inserts in Multi-Channel Fulfillment orders

If you fulfill off-Amazon orders through Amazon's Multi-Channel Fulfillment, MCF and Buy with Prime shipments do not include packing slips by default; they can be turned on at the account level or per MCF order. <C id="product-inserts-14"/> Unless you turn them on, any message you want customers to see has to be in or on the product packaging.
