Amazon announced its 2026 US fee update on 15 October 2025 and the headline changes took effect on 15 January 2026. The letter is accurate about what it covers. This guide covers what it does not, because a seller who budgeted for eight cents in January was paying materially more than that by May.

## The average hides the spread

An $0.08 average across a catalogue as wide as Amazon's tells you almost nothing about your own SKU. Third-party readings of the published tables put the range considerably wider: one logistics provider reads small standard-size items priced $10–$50 at roughly +$0.25 per unit, small standard above $50 at around +$0.51, and items under $10 at about +$0.12; an agency reading groups it as around +$0.12 for small standard, +$0.08 for standard and roughly +$0.31 for larger items. The two disagree on how to slice it, which tells you to trust neither over your own data.

Then add the surcharge. On a $6.00 fulfilment fee, 3.5% is $0.21 — nearly three times the headline increase, and it applies to the peak fee too.

`Seller Central > Reports > Fulfillment > Payments > Fee Preview` gives the per-FNSKU fulfilment fee at current rates. `Seller Central > Growth > Profit Analytics` (the dashboard Amazon introduced with the 2026 update) shows unit economics per product with the fee lines separated. Pull both; estimates from articles, including this one, are orientation.

## The structural change nobody led with

Product price is now a factor in the FBA fulfilment fee. Previously the fee was a function of size tier and weight. Now the selling-price band enters the calculation.

Consequence: **a price change can move your fulfilment cost.** Raise a $49 item to $52 to protect margin and you may cross into the over-$50 band and take a second fee increase for your trouble. Discount a $10.49 item to $9.99 and it drops into Low-Price FBA rates, which average $0.86 lower — a promotion that partly pays for itself.

Repricing and fulfilment cost are now coupled. Any automated repricer running against a fixed fee assumption describes a cost structure that ended in January.

## Europe went the other way

For 2026 Amazon lowered European fees by an average of £0.15 / €0.17 per unit sold, through FBA parcel fulfilment reductions, wider Low-Price FBA eligibility and referral cuts in high-volume categories including clothing, home, grocery, vitamins and pet products. If you have been treating US and EU unit economics as one spreadsheet with a currency conversion, 2026 is the year that stops working.

## What is worth doing about it

- **Repull fee data; do not estimate it.** Fee Preview and Profit Analytics give per-ASIN figures including the surcharge.
- **Model the surcharge as a multiplier on fulfilment, not a line item.** 1.035 × fulfilment fee, including the peak fee from 15 October.
- **Check SKUs near the $10 and $50 boundaries.** These are where the price-band logic bites and where a small repricing decision now has a second-order cost.
- **Split fulfilment from referral in your margin model.** They move independently and on different schedules.
- **Re-plan cash for DD+7.** A payout that used to arrive on shipment now arrives about a week after delivery; through Q4 that is a working-capital gap on top of peak fees.
- **Do not restructure your catalogue over eight cents.** Treat the price-band coupling and the surcharge as the things to engineer around, and the eight cents as the thing to know.

**Run it on your own figures.** The [margin and break-even calculator](/tools/margin-calculator/) takes price, cost of goods, referral rate, fulfilment fee, returns and ad spend and returns net profit per unit, net margin and the maximum ACOS the product can carry. Nothing is stored and nothing leaves your browser.
