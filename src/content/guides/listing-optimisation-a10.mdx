We found no Amazon page that uses the term "A10": none of the Amazon seller pages, Amazon news posts or Selling Partner API documents we read for this page mentions it, and none of them publishes a ranking formula. What Amazon does publish is its goal: when customers search, it shows them the products it thinks they will be most satisfied with. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-01"/> The useful decision for a seller is which of the signals Amazon names you can actually influence, and in what order to work on them.

## What Amazon says shapes search results

Amazon's own explanation of its store lists the signals it uses to refine results:

| Signal Amazon names | Amazon's wording |
|---|---|
| Customer actions | "how frequently an item was purchased by other customers" |
| Price and delivery speed | "the product's price, delivery speed" |
| What's hot and new | "what's hot and new" |
| Post-purchase satisfaction | "measures of post-purchase satisfaction like return rates" |

*Source: Amazon, "How customers get to pick the winners in Amazon's store" (Sept. 29, 2020), checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-02"/> <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-03"/> <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-04"/>

When customers signal dissatisfaction, Amazon says it ranks other products that customers are more likely to love higher in search results. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-05"/> Amazon's Australian store describes the same approach and adds that predictions use information about the item, such as the title, price and description. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-06"/>

Amazon's more recent description adds intent. It says its systems now better understand the intent behind a customer's search, <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-07"/> and that it is no longer only about matching keywords but about understanding what customers are looking to buy. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-08"/>

None of the Amazon statements we read gives weights or an order for these signals, so treat any published "ranking factor" percentages from outside Amazon with caution.

## Step one: make sure the listing can be found at all

Amazon's API documentation defines a "discoverable" item as one customers can find in search results, whether or not it is on sale, <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-10"/> and states that a search-suppressed listing is no longer discoverable. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-11"/> Amazon says listing issues point out problems that cause listings to go inactive, search suppression or other quality problems. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-12"/> Clear issues first; image-related problems are covered in [main image compliance](/guides/main-image-compliance/).

Amazon's seller guide says that adding high-ranking search terms or keywords can help customers find a listing through search results. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-22"/> Its marketing guide gives the same advice in general form: do keyword research and work the critical words and phrases into product listing copy. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-23"/> Title length rules, including the 75-character item name and searchable Item Highlights, are in [Amazon title limits](/guides/title-limit-984-million/).

For A+ Content on the detail page, Amazon says Basic A+ Content can increase sales by up to 8% and well-implemented Premium A+ Content by up to 20%, citing its internal data. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-20"/> Those are Amazon's upper-bound figures, not a forecast for any listing.

Amazon also says that, compared with non-AI content, its AI-generated listing content showed improvements in clarity, accuracy and detail, which can increase a product's discoverability when customers search in its store. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-24"/>

## Use Amazon's own search data instead of guessing

Brand Analytics is the official source for search behavior on your own products. It requires a Professional selling account and a Brand Representative role for a brand enrolled in Brand Registry. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-16"/> Through the Selling Partner API, the Search Query Performance and Search Catalog Performance reports can be requested by sellers who have the Brand Analytics role and are registered in Brand Registry; the Amazon Search Terms report also requires being a brand representative. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-27"/>

## Reading the search reports field by field

Amazon publishes the field definitions for its search reports in its Selling Partner API models. Three of them:

| Report (API report type) | What it covers | Key fields |
|---|---|---|
| Search Query Performance (`GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_SEARCH_QUERY_PERFORMANCE_REPORT`) | Impressions, clicks, cart adds and purchases for the ASINs you list | searchQueryVolume, asinImpressionShare, asinClickShare, asinCartAddShare, asinPurchaseShare |
| Search Catalog Performance (`GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_SEARCH_CATALOG_PERFORMANCE_REPORT`) | Search engagement metrics (impressions, clicks, cart adds, purchases) by ASIN | impressionCount, clickRate, conversionRate |
| Search Terms (`GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_SEARCH_TERMS_REPORT`) | The top three clicked ASINs by search keyword and department | searchFrequencyRank, clickShare, conversionShare |

*Source: Amazon Selling Partner API models, Brand Analytics report schemas, checked Nov 9, 2026.* <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-28"/> <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-29"/> <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-30"/>

What the main fields measure, in Amazon's definitions:

- **searchQueryVolume** is the number of times customers searched for a query in the period. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-31"/>
- **asinImpressionShare** is the fraction of impressions for your ASIN compared with all impressions for that query; asinClickShare, asinCartAddShare and asinPurchaseShare apply the same idea to clicks, cart adds and purchases. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-32"/> <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-33"/>
- **searchQueryScore** ranks a query against your ASIN's other queries on overall performance, with 1 as the highest rank. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-34"/>
- In Search Catalog Performance, **clickRate** is clicks divided by impressions and **conversionRate** is purchases divided by clicks. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-35"/> <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-36"/>

Three details affect how you read the numbers. Impressions in Search Catalog Performance include sponsored search results, <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-37"/> so the count is not an organic-only measure. Requests use WEEK, MONTH or QUARTER periods and cannot span more than one period, <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-28"/> and the ASIN list in a Search Query Performance request has a 200-character limit. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-38"/>

The reports also split clicks, cart adds and purchases by the delivery promise shown in search results, with separate counts for same-day, one-day and two-day delivery messages. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-39"/> Amazon names delivery speed as one of the signals it uses, <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-03"/> and these counts show how the listing performs under each delivery message.

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): finding where a query leaks">
Inputs from one week's Search Query Performance row for the query "insulated lunch bag": asinImpressionShare 0.12, asinClickShare 0.04, asinCartAddShare 0.05, asinPurchaseShare 0.05.

Reading: the ASIN gets 12% of the query's impressions but only 4% of its clicks, so its click share is one-third of its impression share (0.04 ÷ 0.12 ≈ 0.33). After the click, its share rises slightly (cart adds and purchases at 5%). On these inputs, the weak point is the search result itself (what the shopper sees in the results before clicking), not the detail page. A row where click share is high but purchase share drops would point the other way, to the detail page and offer.
</Callout>

## Paid placement is separate

Sponsored Products are cost-per-click ads for individual listings, and they may appear at the top of, alongside or within shopping results and on product pages. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-25"/> Products must be eligible for the Featured Offer to advertise. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-26"/> We found no statement on Amazon's Sponsored Products page that ads change organic ranking, so do not budget ads on that assumption.

The Featured Offer is a different contest from search. Amazon says it features the offer customers are most likely to choose, based on criteria such as low price including delivery and fast delivery, and that customer preference determines which offer is featured. <C id="listing-optimisation-a10-09"/>

## Where to check Amazon's own statements and your data

Amazon's own statements are the only reliable baseline: re-read "How customers get to pick the winners in Amazon's store" on aboutamazon.com for the signals it names, the Brand Analytics page on sell.amazon.com for report eligibility, the Brand Analytics report schemas in Amazon's selling-partner-api-models repository on GitHub for field definitions, and the Selling Partner API listing status guide for what "discoverable" and "suppressed" mean. For a specific listing, the issues shown in Seller Central or returned by the API show whether it is search suppressed; we found no Amazon page that publishes ranking weights.
