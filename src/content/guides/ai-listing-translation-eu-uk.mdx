You can use an AI model to draft an EU or UK listing in another language. The law does not stop you, but it sets the result you must deliver. Under the EU General Product Safety Regulation (GPSR), an online offer must show any warning or safety information in "a language which can be easily understood by consumers," as determined by the Member State where the product is sold. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-05"/> The instructions and safety information that ship with the product must meet the same language test. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-06"/> <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-07"/> So the AI draft is only a starting point. Safety text needs a human check in the target language, and the source and translated versions belong in your records.

## What has to be in the local language in an EU online offer

Regulation (EU) 2023/988 applies from December 13, 2024. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-11"/> Article 19 covers economic operators who make products available "online or through other means of distance sales." It requires the offer to "clearly and visibly indicate at least" the following. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-01"/>

| What the offer must show | Article | Language rule in the text |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacturer's name, registered trade name or registered trade mark, and postal and electronic address | Art. 19(a) | No language rule stated |
| Responsible person's name and postal and electronic address, where the manufacturer is not established in the Union | Art. 19(b) | No language rule stated |
| Information that identifies the product, including a picture, its type and any other product identifier | Art. 19(c) | No language rule stated |
| Any warning or safety information to be affixed to the product or packaging or included in an accompanying document | Art. 19(d) | A language easily understood by consumers, as determined by the Member State where the product is made available |

*Source: EUR-Lex, Regulation (EU) 2023/988, Article 19, checked Nov 11, 2026.* <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-02"/> <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-03"/> <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-04"/> <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-05"/>

The language rule attaches to warnings and safety information, and the Member State decides which language meets it. For a product made available to buyers in Germany, Germany's determination applies. The regulation also treats language as a sign of where an offer is aimed: recital 21 names "the languages available, used for the offer or for ordering" among the factors for deciding whether an offer targets consumers in the Union. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-13"/> The language you translate a listing into is therefore one of the signals the regulation looks at.

Online marketplaces have duties of their own. Article 22(9) requires providers of online marketplaces to design their interface so that traders can provide, for each product, at least the manufacturer details, the responsible person where applicable, product identification and any warning or safety information, and so that this is shown or easily accessible on the product listing. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-12"/> Recital 58 says a marketplace should not allow an offer to be listed unless the trader has provided all information related to product safety and traceability. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-14"/>

## The same language test applies to the product itself

The online offer is half the job. The physical product, its instructions and its safety leaflet carry their own duties, split by role:

| Role | What the regulation requires on language | Article |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacturer | Product accompanied by clear instructions and safety information in a language easily understood by consumers, as determined by the Member State, except where the product can be used safely and as intended without them | Art. 9(7) |
| Importer | Product accompanied by clear instructions and safety information in such a language, except where the product can be used safely and as intended without them | Art. 11(4) |
| Distributor | Before selling, verify that the manufacturer and importer have met Article 9(5), (6) and (7) and Article 11(3) and (4) | Art. 12(1) |

*Source: EUR-Lex, Regulation (EU) 2023/988, Articles 9, 11 and 12, checked Nov 11, 2026.* <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-06"/> <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-07"/> <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-08"/>

Manufacturers must also carry out an internal risk analysis and draw up technical documentation before placing a product on the market. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-09"/> They must keep that documentation for 10 years after the product is placed on the market. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-10"/> The regulation does not list translations as a required part of that file. Still, filing the source text with each translated version means you can later show what the product said, in which language, and on what date.

## Where the EU AI Act fits

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the AI Act, has a transparency chapter. Article 50 sits in Chapter IV, headed "Transparency obligations for providers and deployers of certain AI systems." <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-19"/> Two of its paragraphs deal with AI-made text, and they apply to different people:

- **Article 50(2)** is addressed to "providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content." They must ensure outputs are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-15"/> The duty sits with the provider of the AI system.
- **Article 50(4)** is addressed to deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates text "published with the purpose of informing the public on matters of public interest." They must disclose that the text was artificially generated or manipulated. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-16"/> The duty does not apply where the content "has undergone a process of human review or editorial control" and someone holds editorial responsibility for publishing it. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-17"/>

As written in the text on EUR-Lex, the AI Act applies from August 2, 2026. The exceptions listed in Article 113 cover Chapters I and II, certain other chapters and Article 6(1), and Chapter IV is not among them. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-18"/> The quoted wording of Article 50(4) is about text published to inform the public on matters of public interest. It does not refer to product offers. Whether a seller using an AI tool is a "provider" or a "deployer," and whether a listing could ever fall under paragraph 4, depends on the regulation's own definitions. Those questions belong with a specialist, not with this page. Either way, the human review that Article 50(4) mentions is the same step that GPSR safety text needs.

## What the UK guidance says

The UK has two regimes. Regulation (EU) 2023/988 replaced the General Product Safety Regulations 2005 in Northern Ireland on December 13, 2024. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-23"/> The UK government's Northern Ireland guidance says manufacturers must ensure products come with "clear instructions and safety information in language consumers can easily understand," where a product cannot be used safely as intended without them. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-24"/>

In Great Britain, the government's guidance on the 2005 regulations says producers must give consumers the relevant information to assess the risk inherent in a product over its normal or reasonably foreseeable period of use, where those risks are not immediately obvious. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-20"/> It also says producers must allow for traceability by indicating on the product or its packaging, where reasonable, the producer's name and address and the product reference or, where applicable, the batch. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-21"/> Some product-specific rules name English directly. The Great Britain toy safety guidance says hazard and risk information for a toy, and the ways to avoid those risks, "must be marked in English," in a clearly visible, easily legible, understandable and accurate manner. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-22"/> That is a rule for toys, not for every product. Exporters from non-English-speaking countries should treat English safety text with the same care as German or French.

## What Amazon and the AI providers publish

Amazon offers a Build International Listings tool to cross-list products in each of its Europe stores. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-25"/> Listing in one Europe store does not automatically list the product in the others. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-26"/> Amazon tells sellers to understand the product compliance, safety and listing requirements of each European country. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-27"/> Its global selling page says that for existing Amazon sellers, in many cases, customer reviews are carried over to other global stores and automatically translated. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-28"/> For content made with Amazon's own generative AI tools, Amazon says you are responsible for any changes needed so products are accurately represented. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-29"/> Using that content means you agree you have reviewed it and that it is accurate, complete and compliant. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-30"/>

The AI providers describe their language coverage in their own documentation:

- **Anthropic (Claude):** The docs say Claude handles input and output in most world languages that use standard Unicode characters. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-31"/> For production use, they advise stating the target language explicitly, most reliably in the system prompt. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-35"/> They also note that good communication often needs cultural and regional awareness beyond pure translation. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-36"/>
- **OpenAI (ChatGPT):** OpenAI's help center says the ChatGPT interface is offered in 50+ supported languages. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-37"/>
- **Google (Gemini):** Google Cloud's documentation says all Gemini models can understand a listed set of languages, including German, French, Italian, Spanish, Polish, Dutch and Turkish. <C id="ai-listing-translation-eu-uk-38"/>

## Scope of this page

This page reads the regulation text and official guidance. It does not settle which language a given Member State requires, or whether you count as a manufacturer, importer, distributor, provider or deployer. Those questions are decided by each country's market surveillance authority, applying the regulation text. For a specific product and Member State, ask a regulatory specialist who works in that country.

## How to confirm it for your products and markets

Read Articles 9, 11, 12, 19 and 22 of Regulation (EU) 2023/988, and Articles 50 and 113 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, on eur-lex.europa.eu. Check EUR-Lex for any later amending act, too. For the UK, read the General Product Safety Regulations 2005 guidance for Great Britain, the Northern Ireland GPSR guidance, and any product-specific guidance such as the toys guide, all on gov.uk. For Amazon's per-country requirements, start from Amazon's Europe global selling page on sell.amazon.com and the compliance tools it links to. For each AI tool you use, read that provider's own language documentation as it stands on the day you use it.
