Yes, you can get most of your import duty back if goods you imported leave the United States or are destroyed. The formal term is drawback. 19 CFR 190.2 defines it as a refund of duties, taxes and fees paid on imported merchandise that were imposed under Federal law at entry <C id="duty-drawback-01"/>. For marketplace sellers, three situations matter most. The first is unsold stock you ship abroad. The second is customer returns you export or destroy. The third is defective or wrong goods you send back to the supplier. Each one has its own rules, notice requirements and deadline. The refund is capped at 99% of what you paid <C id="duty-drawback-06"/>.

## What drawback refunds, and what it never refunds

Drawback is not limited to the duty line on your entry summary. The regulation lists merchandise processing fees among the charges that can be refunded <C id="duty-drawback-02"/>. One category is excluded outright: drawback is not allowable on antidumping and countervailing duties <C id="duty-drawback-03"/>. If most of your landed cost comes from an AD/CVD order, drawback will not recover it.

### Unused merchandise drawback

This covers inventory you never sold or used in the US, for example stock sent to a foreign marketplace warehouse. Under direct identification, the merchandise must not have been used within the United States before it is exported or destroyed <C id="duty-drawback-04"/>. It must also leave before the close of the 5-year period that starts on the import date <C id="duty-drawback-05"/>.

Operations that do not amount to manufacture or production are not a "use" of the merchandise <C id="duty-drawback-09"/>.

Substitution unused drawback lets you export or destroy substituted merchandise instead of the imported units. The substituted goods must be exported or destroyed within 5 years of the import date of the imported merchandise <C id="duty-drawback-07"/>. Section 190.32 refers to the 8-digit HTSUS substitution standard set out in 19 CFR 190.2 <C id="duty-drawback-08"/>.

### Rejected merchandise drawback

Use this when the supplier got it wrong. It applies to merchandise that did not conform to sample or specification, or that was shipped without the consignee's consent <C id="duty-drawback-12"/>. Drawback is denied if the goods are exported or destroyed after the statutory 5-year period <C id="duty-drawback-13"/>. You file the notice on CBP Form 7553 at least 5 working days before you return the goods to CBP custody <C id="duty-drawback-14"/>.

### Retail returns: the rule written for sellers

19 CFR 190.45 covers merchandise sold at retail by the importer (or by the party who received it from the importer) that is then returned to and accepted by the importer <C id="duty-drawback-15"/>. To claim it with substitution, you designate an import entry for merchandise imported within 1 year before the date of export or destruction <C id="duty-drawback-16"/>. You also certify that the same 8-digit HTSUS subheading and the same specific product identifier, such as a part number, SKU or product code, apply to both the designated and the returned goods <C id="duty-drawback-17"/>. The refund cannot exceed 99% of the duties paid on the imported merchandise <C id="duty-drawback-18"/>.

<Callout type="tip" title="SKU discipline pays here">
The retail-return certification depends on the same product identifier appearing on both the import and the export side <C id="duty-drawback-17"/>. If your listing SKUs, supplier part numbers and entry line descriptions don't match, you will have trouble certifying the match.
</Callout>

### Manufacturing drawback, briefly

If you make products in the US from imported parts and export the finished goods, manufacturing drawback can apply. The substitution version requires merchandise in the same 8-digit HTSUS subheading, used in manufacture within 5 years of importing the original merchandise <C id="duty-drawback-19"/>.

The 5-year filing limit is strict. Claims not completed within 5 years after the import date of the merchandise designated for the claim are considered abandoned <C id="duty-drawback-22"/>. The only other relief is for a major disaster, which can extend the period by up to 18 months <C id="duty-drawback-24"/>. All drawback entries go through a CBP-authorized electronic system <C id="duty-drawback-20"/>.

## Proving the goods left (or were destroyed)

Proof of export must establish the date and fact of exportation and the identity of the exporter <C id="duty-drawback-27"/>. Carrier records qualify, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, freight waybill or cargo manifest <C id="duty-drawback-28"/>. If you export by mail, the postal record must be identified on the drawback entry <C id="duty-drawback-29"/>. For one-off international orders shipped by post, that means linking each postal record to the claim line.

For drawback, destruction means the goods are destroyed to the point that they have no commercial value <C id="duty-drawback-36"/>. If CBP does not witness it, the evidence must come from a disinterested third party, such as a landfill operator <C id="duty-drawback-31"/>.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

Inputs: you paid $4,000 in duties and $200 in merchandise processing fees on an entry of imported goods. Later you export the entire lot unused, within 5 years and with proper notice. No AD/CVD applies.

- Refundable base: $4,000 + $200 = $4,200, since merchandise processing fees are drawback-eligible <C id="duty-drawback-02"/>.
- Maximum refund: 99% × $4,200 = $4,158 <C id="duty-drawback-06"/>.
- If $1,000 of the $4,000 duty had been antidumping duty, the base would fall to $3,200 and the cap to $3,168 <C id="duty-drawback-03"/>.

## Records, transfers and getting paid sooner

Keep drawback records for 3 years after the claim liquidates <C id="duty-drawback-25"/>. Every claim is subject to verification by CBP <C id="duty-drawback-34"/>, so those records are what you will be asked for. If you buy imported goods from another company and export them, check the transfer rules. The regulation allows transfers of drawback-eligible merchandise to another party when the transferring party meets listed conditions <C id="duty-drawback-26"/>.

Accelerated payment means CBP pays estimated drawback before liquidation of the drawback entry <C id="duty-drawback-32"/>. To get it, you must post a bond large enough to cover the estimated drawback <C id="duty-drawback-33"/>.

Sending goods into a foreign-trade zone can also count as an export. Exportation may be deemed to occur when drawback goods are admitted into a zone in zone-restricted status <C id="duty-drawback-35"/>.

## How to confirm it for your goods

Read 19 CFR Part 190 on the eCFR. Start with Subpart C for unused merchandise, Subpart D for rejected and retail-returned goods, and Subpart E for filing and deadlines. Before you build a returns program around drawback, check the exact certification wording in 19 CFR 190.45 against your own SKU and HTSUS data.
