The dividing line is value. CBP describes entry type 11 as "the general informal entry type for merchandise valued at $2,500 or less," and calls the general formal entry type "type 01." <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-01" /> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-02" /> The regulation behind the threshold, 19 CFR 143.21(a), lists shipments not exceeding $2,500 in value as eligible for informal entry, except articles valued over $250 that are classified in Chapter 99, Subchapters III and IV, of the HTSUS. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-03" />

What changes for a seller is mostly paperwork and money tied up: a formal entry needs a bond before release and an entry summary within a fixed deadline, while an informal entry is lighter. CBP can still require formal entry for any shipment, <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-04" /> and a rulemaking published in October 2026 would make type 11 heavier, including a bond for every type 11 filer. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-19" />

<Callout type="note" title="Scope of this page">
This page compares the two entry routes under the regulations. Which one a given shipment may use is confirmed when your broker or carrier files it. What happened to the $800 de minimis route is covered in [De minimis suspension 2026](/guides/de-minimis-suspension-2026/).
</Callout>

## Every shipment needs an entry

The starting rule in 19 CFR 141.4(a) is that "all merchandise imported into the United States is required to be entered, unless specifically excepted." <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-09" /> The question is which kind of entry, not whether.

Low-value shipments that used to move under the de minimis exemption now need an entry too. CBP's interim final rule suspending de minimis for all modes other than the international postal network took effect on June 24, 2026, and CBP stated that this leaves entry type 11 as the main appropriate informal entry method for those shipments, with formal entry still an option. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-08" />

## Type 11 vs type 01 side by side

| Point | Informal (type 11) | Formal (type 01) |
|---|---|---|
| Value | $2,500 or less, with the Chapter 99 exception over $250 | Any value; required above the informal ceiling or when CBP requires it |
| Entry deadline today | Within 15 calendar days after landing | Within 15 calendar days after landing |
| Bond | Currently required only in certain situations | Single entry or continuous bond before release |

*Source: eCFR, 19 CFR 142.2, 142.4 and 143.21; Federal Register, "Low-Value Shipments" (Oct 8, 2026), checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-03" /> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-11" /> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-26" /> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-12" /> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-20" />

## When CBP can insist on formal entry

Staying under $2,500 does not guarantee type 11. Under 19 CFR 143.22, CBP may require a formal consumption or appraisement entry for any merchandise if it considers it necessary for import admissibility enforcement, revenue protection or the efficient conduct of customs business. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-04" /> Individual shipments for the same consignee valued at $2,500 or less may be consolidated on one such entry. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-05" />

For marketplace sellers who split one purchase order into several cartons or flights, that consolidation rule is the one to keep in mind: a series of small shipments to the same consignee can end up on one formal entry.

## Who may file an informal entry

Section 143.26 sets out who may make informal entry:

- **Shipments valued between $800 and $2,500.** The owner or purchaser may make entry, or a licensed customs broker designated by the owner, purchaser or consignee. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-06" />
- **Shipments valued at $800 or less** that meet the statutory conditions: the owner, purchaser or consignee may make entry, or a licensed customs broker they designate. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-07" />

The October 2026 proposal summarizes the current position as filing being limited to parties with the right to make entry, "i.e., an owner or purchaser of the shipment." <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-21" /> Who counts as importer of record, and what a foreign seller needs, is the subject of [Who is the importer when your supplier quotes DDP or FOB](/guides/incoterms-who-is-importer/).

## What formal entry adds

A formal entry runs in two steps, with a bond in between.

  {t: "Bond on file", d: "Single entry or continuous bond on Form 301 with the 113.62 conditions."},
  {t: "Release", d: "Goods released once entry documentation and bond are accepted."},

- **Entry documents.** The entry is CBP Form 3461 or its electronic equivalent, with evidence of the right to make entry, a commercial invoice and a packing list where appropriate. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-10" />
- **Deadline.** Entry documentation is due within 15 calendar days after landing. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-11" />
- **Bond.** Merchandise is not released from CBP custody when CBP receives the entry documentation unless a single entry or continuous bond on Form 301, containing the 19 CFR 113.62 conditions, has been filed. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-12" /> The port director may waive the surety or cash deposit when the merchandise value does not exceed $2,500, the entry summary is filed and estimated duties are deposited before release, and the importer has not been delinquent or otherwise remiss with CBP; the waiver does not apply to quota merchandise. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-13" />
- **Entry summary.** The entry summary must be on CBP Form 7501 or its electronic equivalent, <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-14" /> filed within 10 working days after the time of entry unless it was filed at entry. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-15" />
- **Late summary.** If the entry summary is not filed on time, the port director "shall make an immediate demand for liquidated damages." <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-16" />

Bond types and amounts are covered in [Customs bonds](/guides/customs-bonds/).

## The 2026 proposal to tighten type 11

On October 8, 2026, CBP published a notice of proposed rulemaking, "Low-Value Shipments," docket USCBP-2026-0298, with comments due on or before December 7, 2026. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-17" /> It is a proposal, not a final rule. As proposed:

- Informal entries would have to be filed electronically, prior to or upon the date of importation, replacing today's 15-day window. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-18" /> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-26" />
- All entry type 11 filers would need a basic importation and entry bond. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-19" /> CBP notes that today, bonding for informal entries is only required in certain situations. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-20" />
- A consignee that is not an owner or purchaser would have to appoint a licensed customs broker. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-21" />
- The $250 limit on informal entry for certain Chapter 99 goods would be removed. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-22" />
- Type 11 filers would report a "Final Deliver-To Party." <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-23" />
- A new $1,000 minimum would apply for each breach of a listed bond condition. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-24" />

A separate interim final rule on foreign importers of record, published November 6, 2026, lets CBP require entry summary at time of entry or discontinue immediate delivery for an importer that inaccurately declares U.S. importer-of-record status, and applies those consequences to both formal and informal entries. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-25" />

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical values)">
A seller imports one carton of phone cases valued at $1,900 and, two days later, a second carton for the same consignee valued at $1,400. Each is under $2,500, so each could be eligible for type 11 on value alone. Under 19 CFR 143.22, CBP may still require formal entry and may consolidate both on one entry, which would then cover $3,300 of goods and need a bond and a Form 7501 entry summary.
</Callout>

## What changes for the seller

Put together, the choice between type 11 and type 01 changes four things on your side of the shipment:

- **Cash and paperwork at release.** Under Part 142, a formal entry needs a bond on file before release and an entry summary afterward, while informal entries are bonded only in certain situations today. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-12" /> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-20" />
- **Who can file.** For informal entries, the regulation names the owner, purchaser or, for the lowest-value tier, consignee, or a broker they designate. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-06" /> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-07" /> If your forwarder or carrier is the consignee but not the owner, check whose name it is using.
- **Deadlines you own.** The 10-working-day entry summary clock and its liquidated damages demand sit in the formal entry rules, so when a broker files type 01 in your name, ask whose bond it is filing under. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-15" /> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-16" />
- **Planning ahead.** If the Low-Value Shipments proposal is finalized as published, type 11 would need a bond and electronic filing prior to or upon the date of importation, which narrows the practical gap between the two entry types. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-18" /> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-19" />
