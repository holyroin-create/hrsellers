The short answer: a shipment valued at $2,500 or less can usually go through as an informal entry (CBP's entry type 11), except that articles valued over $250 classified in HTSUS Chapter 99, Subchapters III and IV are carved out of that rule; larger shipments, and anything CBP chooses to require it for, go through formal entry, type 01. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-03"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-07"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-08"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-04"/> The practical differences are the bond, the paperwork deadline and the processing fee, and several of those are under active rulemaking right now.

## The starting rule: everything gets entered

All merchandise imported into the United States must be entered unless specifically excepted, and entry is due within 15 calendar days after landing from a vessel, aircraft or vehicle. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-01"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-02"/> The only question is which kind of entry. If you used to rely on the de minimis exemption for small parcels, note that CBP says it has been suspended since at least August 29, 2025. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-26"/>

## Who can file each one

You do not need a broker license to import your own goods: an importer or exporter transacting customs business solely on his own account and in no sense on behalf of another is not required to be licensed. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-18"/> For informal shipments valued between $800 and $2,500, the regulation says the merchandise may be entered, using reasonable care, by the owner or purchaser of the shipment or, when designated, a licensed customs broker. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-06"/> On the formal side, the entry summary (Form 7501 or its electronic equivalent) may be prepared by importers or their agents. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-30"/>

In practice, the formal route has more moving parts: release when CBP receives the entry documents requires a bond on file, the entry summary has to follow inside the deadline, and a late summary triggers an immediate demand for liquidated damages, the entire bond amount on a single entry bond. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-09"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-11"/> A new layer also arrives on January 5, 2027, when CBP's interim final rule on foreign importers of record takes effect; CBP says the requirement operationally applies to informal types 11, 12 and 13 and all formal entry types. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-29"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-28"/>

## Common mistakes and what the rule actually says

| Mistake | What the regulation says |
|---|---|
| Assuming any shipment under $2,500 will clear informally | CBP may require a formal consumption or appraisement entry for any merchandise if it deems it necessary, and Chapter 99, Subchapter III–IV articles over $250 are excluded from the $2,500 rule. |
| Deciding on the entry type after the goods land | Entry is due within 15 calendar days after landing, so the decision belongs before departure. |
| Treating the formal entry summary as optional paperwork | If not filed at entry, it must be filed within 10 working days after entry; late filing brings an immediate demand for liquidated damages. |
| Expecting release on a formal entry without a bond in place | Release at the time CBP receives the entry documents requires a single entry or continuous bond on Form 301. |
| Thinking informal means no standard of care | For shipments valued between $800 and $2,500, the owner or purchaser enters informal merchandise "using reasonable care." |

*Source: eCFR — 19 CFR Parts 141, 142 and 143, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-04"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-03"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-02"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-10"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-11"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-09"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-06"/>

An informal entry is lighter paperwork, not a lighter obligation: the regulation attaches reasonable care to it even when the automated fee is only $2.77. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-06"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-14"/> If you split one purchase order into several small shipments, keep in mind that CBP's power to require formal entry is not tied to the $2,500 line at all. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-04"/>

## What each route costs in CBP fees

The merchandise processing fee is the CBP charge that differs most visibly. Formal entries pay an ad valorem fee of 0.3464 percent, within inflation-adjusted limits; for fiscal year 2027 those limits are a $34.58 minimum and a $670.86 maximum, required as of October 1, 2026. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-12"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-13"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-17"/> Informal entries pay a flat fee instead.

Two federal actions move the line between type 11 and type 01.

The Low-Value Shipments proposal is still a proposal, but its direction is clear. CBP proposes to require type 11 entries to be filed electronically upon or prior to the date of importation, and to require all type 11 and type 13 filers to obtain a basic importation and entry bond. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-20"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-23"/> Today, CBP says, bonding for informal entries is only required in certain situations. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-24"/> The proposal would also create a new entry type 13 for mail, require a consignee who is not the owner or purchaser to appoint a licensed broker as importer of record for shipments valued at $2,500 or less, and let CBP require formal entry on a blanket basis. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-22"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-21"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-25"/>

<Callout type="warning" title="Plan for the bond now">
If you rely on informal entries for small restocks, the proposed bond requirement would change your cost base even below $2,500. Pricing a continuous bond before any final rule lands avoids a scramble; see the customs bond guide.
</Callout>

## How to confirm it for your shipments

The governing text is 19 CFR Part 143, Subpart C (informal entries) and Part 142 (formal entry and entry summary) on eCFR; current fee amounts are in CBP's annual user-fee notice in the Federal Register. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-03"/> <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-13"/> For the pending changes, read the Low-Value Shipments proposed rule and submit comments by December 7, 2026 if it affects you. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-19"/> Because CBP may require formal entry for any merchandise, a broker or CBP itself, not a general guide, settles whether a particular shipment can go informal. <C id="entry-type-11-vs-01-04"/>
