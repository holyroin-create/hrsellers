A sourcing agent can save you months of factory hunting, but how the agent is paid and what the agent is allowed to do have legal consequences on the U.S. side. Two questions matter most. First, is the agent's fee part of the dutiable value of your goods? That depends on whose agent it is. Second, can the agent "handle customs" for you? Not unless it is a licensed customs broker, because transacting customs business as a broker requires a license. <C id="sourcing-agents-05"/>

## Whose agent is it? Buying vs. selling commissions

The international Customs Valuation Agreement adds commissions and brokerage to the price paid, "except buying commissions," to the extent they are incurred by the buyer and not included in the price. <C id="sourcing-agents-13"/> Its interpretative note defines buying commissions as fees paid by an importer to the importer's agent for the service of representing the importer abroad in the purchase of the goods. <C id="sourcing-agents-14"/>

The U.S. regulations add to the price any selling commission incurred by the buyer. <C id="sourcing-agents-16"/> A selling commission means any commission paid to the seller's agent who is related to or controlled by, or works for or on behalf of, the manufacturer or the seller. <C id="sourcing-agents-15"/> The full list of additions, and how related-party sales are treated, is in our [first-sale valuation guide](/guides/first-sale-valuation/).

*Source: WTO Customs Valuation Agreement and eCFR — 19 CFR 152.102–152.103, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="sourcing-agents-13"/>

The label on the contract does not settle the question by itself. If the "sourcing agent" is in fact related to, controlled by, or working for the factory, its commission fits that selling-commission definition. Indirect payments count too: an indirect payment includes the settlement by the buyer of a debt owed by the seller. <C id="sourcing-agents-18"/> If you pay the agent and the agent passes part of that to the factory on your behalf, that portion is part of the price.

The invoice must itemize all charges upon the merchandise by name and amount, including freight, insurance and commission. <C id="sourcing-agents-17"/> See [the supplier invoice](/guides/the-supplier-invoice/) for the full list. In practice, pay the agent on its own invoice rather than through the factory, and make sure any commission that does appear on the factory invoice is itemized. Separate paperwork makes it easy to show, if CBP asks, which party the fee was paid to and what it covered, and it keeps the factory price you declare free of charges that belong to a different relationship.

Inputs: factory price $40,000; agent fee 5% ($2,000); duty rate 25%.
If the fee is a bona fide buying commission paid to your own agent: duty base $40,000, duty $10,000.
If the same $2,000 is really a commission to the seller's agent, incurred by you: duty base $42,000, duty $10,500.
Difference on this one order: $500.
</Callout>

## An agent is not a customs broker

"Customs business" means activities involving transactions with CBP concerning the entry and admissibility of merchandise, <C id="sourcing-agents-01"/> including its classification and valuation and the payment of duties, taxes or other charges. <C id="sourcing-agents-02"/> A customs broker is a person licensed under 19 CFR Part 111 to transact customs business on behalf of others. <C id="sourcing-agents-04"/> The definition does not include the mere electronic transmission of data received for transmission to CBP. <C id="sourcing-agents-03"/>

You do not need a license to do your own customs business. An importer transacting customs business solely on its own account and in no sense on behalf of another is not required to be licensed, <C id="sourcing-agents-06"/> nor are its authorized regular employees or officers who act only for it. <C id="sourcing-agents-07"/> A sourcing agent is neither of those, so if it offers to clear your goods, it needs a broker to do the filing.

### When the agent hires the broker

If an agent or forwarder appoints a broker for you, note two rules. A broker must obtain a valid power of attorney to transact business in a principal's name, <C id="sourcing-agents-11"/> and Customs Form 5291 may be used to give one. <C id="sourcing-agents-12"/> When a broker is employed by an unlicensed person other than the importer, the broker must transmit to the actual importer a copy of its bill for services or a copy of the entry, unless the goods were bought on a delivered duty-paid basis or you waived transmittal in writing. <C id="sourcing-agents-09"/> If neither exception applies and you never receive those copies, ask for them.

If you are the importer of record, payment to the broker will not relieve you of liability for customs charges. <C id="sourcing-agents-08"/> Brokers must retain their records, other than powers of attorney, for at least 5 years after the date of entry. <C id="sourcing-agents-10"/> Who acts as importer of record, including under DDP terms, is covered in [who is the importer of record](/guides/incoterms-who-is-importer/).

## Product compliance: the agent's paperwork is still your certificate

For products under a CPSC rule that are made outside the United States, the importer is generally the finished product certifier that must issue the certificate. <C id="sourcing-agents-21"/> A certifier may rely on another party to certify finished products on its behalf, <C id="sourcing-agents-19"/> but remains legally responsible for the information in the certificate. <C id="sourcing-agents-20"/> A test report forwarded by your agent is an input you need to check, not a transfer of responsibility. Testing rules are in [CPSC certificates and testing](/guides/us-product-compliance/).

## Screening the agent itself

If your agent receives or routes your payments, screen it like a supplier: U.S. persons are generally prohibited from dealing with the individuals and entities on the SDN List. <C id="sourcing-agents-22"/> See [supplier verification](/guides/supplier-verification/).

<Callout type="note" title="Scope">
Whether a particular commission is a bona fide buying commission is decided by CBP on the facts and documents. If the amount is material, ask a licensed customs broker or seek a CBP ruling before you rely on excluding it.

## How to confirm it for your arrangement

Read 19 CFR 111.1 and 111.2 on the eCFR for the definition of customs business and the license requirement, and 19 CFR 152.102–152.103 for selling commissions. The buying-commission exclusion and its definition are in Article 8 of the WTO Customs Valuation Agreement and its interpretative notes on wto.org. Check 16 CFR 1110 for who certifies consumer products.
