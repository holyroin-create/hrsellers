If you already sell with FBA in the US, the lowest-effort way into Canada and Mexico is to leave your stock where it is. Amazon's program for that is called Remote Fulfillment with FBA, and it "simplifies international sales by letting you sell to customers in Canada, Mexico, and Brazil without sending inventory to those countries." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-01"/> The older "North America Remote Fulfillment" (NARF) name does not appear on Amazon's current program page; the program described there is the one this page covers. The decision is whether your products are eligible and whether the cross-border fee and the customer-paid duties leave you a competitive price.

## What you need before enrolling

Amazon lists three prerequisites: "a Professional selling plan, a North America and Brazil unified selling account," and enrollment "in FBA in the US with FBA Export enabled." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-02"/> The unified account is the same one Amazon describes for reaching "Amazon customers in the US, Canada, Mexico, and Brazil." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-20"/> If you link global selling accounts, Amazon says your monthly fee will be either the equivalent of USD 39.99 or the sum of regional fees for active listings, whichever is lower. <C id="narf-canada-mexico-21"/>

## Who pays duties and taxes

This is the feature that makes the program workable for small sellers: "Customers pay any import duties, taxes, and fees as the importer of record." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-08"/> Amazon spells out the consequence: "That means you don't need to pay taxes in Canada, Mexico, or Brazil." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-09"/> For what being importer of record means in general, see [who is the importer](/guides/incoterms-who-is-importer/).

That changes if you send inventory into Canada yourself. The Canada Revenue Agency says the Excise Tax Act was amended, effective July 1, 2021, with GST/HST provisions, and it lists qualifying goods "such as goods located in a fulfillment warehouse." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-22"/> <C id="narf-canada-mexico-27"/> For those supplies, "The normal GST/HST registration, reporting and remittance regime is applicable to these non-resident vendors." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-23"/> A business that imports commercial goods into Canada needs an import-export (RM) program account, which new importers and exporters with a 9-digit business number (BN9) can register for; the Canada Border Services Agency advises registering "well in advance of your import or export deadlines." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-24"/>

### What a Canadian customer pays at the border

Because the customer is the importer of record, Canada's border rules decide what duty and tax the shopper pays at import. For a shipment imported by courier from the US and Mexico, the Canada Border Services Agency sets three bands, in Canadian dollars of value for duty: <C id="narf-canada-mexico-46"/>

| Value for duty (C$) | Duty | Taxes |
|---|---|---|
| Up to $40 | Free | Free <C id="narf-canada-mexico-43"/> |
| Above $40 to $150 | Free | Apply <C id="narf-canada-mexico-44"/> |
| Above $150 | Apply | Apply <C id="narf-canada-mexico-45"/> |

*Source: Canada Border Services Agency, CUSMA low-value shipments, checked Nov 9, 2026.*

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): which band is your SKU in?">
Inputs: a US$25 item shipped to Canada by courier and an exchange rate of 1.40 Canadian dollars per US dollar, with value for duty taken as the item price converted. 25 × 1.40 = C$35, inside the C$40 band, so the shopper pays no duty or tax at import. <C id="narf-canada-mexico-43"/> A US$60 item converts to C$84: no duty, but taxes apply. <C id="narf-canada-mexico-44"/> A US$120 item converts to C$168, above C$150, so both apply. <C id="narf-canada-mexico-45"/> SKUs priced near a band edge can cross it when the exchange rate or the synchronized price moves.
</Callout>

### If you hold stock in Canada instead

The Canadian GST/HST measure turns on where the goods ship from. It covers goods held in Canada, such as in a fulfillment warehouse, or shipped from a place in Canada to a Canadian purchaser, <C id="narf-canada-mexico-36"/> and it does not include goods sent by mail or courier to a Canadian purchaser from an address outside Canada by the supplier or someone acting for it. <C id="narf-canada-mexico-35"/> Remote Fulfillment uses your US FBA inventory; <C id="narf-canada-mexico-28"/> stock you export to Canadian fulfillment centers is held in Canada, which the measure covers.

## Fees

Amazon's FAQ: sellers "will pay the Remote Fulfillment with FBA costs and the category referral fee for the store where the sale occurred." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-13"/> "These costs replace US FBA costs and referral fees." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-14"/> The per-unit Remote Fulfillment rate card is published inside Seller Central, not on the public program page; to find it, search Seller Central Help using words such as Remote Fulfillment with FBA fees (a suggested search, not a page title).

Pricing is automated by default. The listing tool "synchronizes your prices to preserve your profit margins" while "adjusting for differences in fulfillment costs, referral fees, and currency exchange rates." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-06"/> If you override it, "Any manual price adjustments in the destination store will break the connection." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-19"/>

### Pricing a destination order without the rate card in front of you

The public pages tell you which fees apply and which US fees they replace, but not the per-unit amounts. That is enough to set up the comparison correctly; the rate card only supplies the last number. <C id="narf-canada-mexico-13"/> <C id="narf-canada-mexico-14"/>

**Per-unit margin on a destination order = destination price (in US dollars) − destination referral fee − Remote Fulfillment cost − your product and inbound cost**

Three things follow from the structure, before any rate is known:

- **The two fee sets are substitutes, not additions.** You never pay the US FBA fee and the Remote Fulfillment cost on the same order, so the question is the *difference* between them, not the sum. <C id="narf-canada-mexico-14"/>
- **The referral fee follows the destination store's category rate**, so a product can sit in a different rate band in Canada or Mexico than in the US. Check its category in each store. <C id="narf-canada-mexico-13"/>
- **Duties and taxes do not enter your margin**, because the customer pays them as importer of record. They still raise the customer's total price, which is what local competitors are compared against. <C id="narf-canada-mexico-08"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example: the price rise needed to hold your US margin">
Write *P* for the US price, *r*<sub>US</sub> and *r*<sub>D</sub> for the US and destination referral rates, *F* for the US FBA fee and *R* for the Remote Fulfillment cost, all converted to US dollars; the fees come from Seller Central.

- What you keep on a US sale: *P* × (1 − *r*<sub>US</sub>) − *F*.
- Destination price that keeps the same amount: (*P* × (1 − *r*<sub>US</sub>) − *F* + *R*) ÷ (1 − *r*<sub>D</sub>).
- The division matters: because the referral fee is a share of the price, each extra dollar of fixed cost needs 1 ÷ (1 − *r*<sub>D</sub>) dollars of price, not one. If *R* is below *F* and the rates are equal, the destination order is cheaper to serve.

Compare the resulting price plus the customer's duties and taxes with local offers before enrolling the SKU.

Because the listing tool adjusts prices for fee and exchange-rate differences by default, this calculation is mainly a check on what it sets — and the basis for any manual price, which breaks that automatic link. <C id="narf-canada-mexico-06"/> <C id="narf-canada-mexico-19"/>
</Callout>

## What ships, and what doesn't

Every product must "Meet all trade compliance and destination-country regulations." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-16"/> Amazon names four product types that are not eligible:

| Not eligible | Destination lists Amazon links |
|---|---|
| Restricted products | Canada, Mexico, Brazil <C id="narf-canada-mexico-31"/> |
| Prohibited products | Canada, Mexico, Brazil <C id="narf-canada-mexico-31"/> |
| Dangerous goods | Canada, Mexico, Brazil <C id="narf-canada-mexico-31"/> |
| Blocked for importation | Mexico, Brazil <C id="narf-canada-mexico-32"/> |

*Source: Amazon, Remote Fulfillment with FBA page, checked Nov 9, 2026.* For hazmat products see the [dangerous goods guide](/guides/hazmat-dangerous-goods/).

To see which of your ASINs qualify, download the ASIN Eligibility Report from the Remote Fulfillment with FBA section; <C id="narf-canada-mexico-33"/> you can enroll or unenroll individual products by typing Yes or No in its Enable/Disable column and uploading the file. <C id="narf-canada-mexico-34"/>

After the sale, Amazon says it will "handle the export logistics, fulfillment, customer inquiries, and returns for your orders," and "Prime customers receive free shipping to Canada, Mexico, and Brazil." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-11"/> <C id="narf-canada-mexico-10"/> "Returns will be sent back to the US and are subject to the FBA customer returns policy." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-12"/>

Behind the scenes, Amazon says "we'll use your US FBA inventory to deliver orders across the border with Prime shipping." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-28"/> Among the requirements, a product must be "enabled for FBA in the US" with "valid offers in Canada, Mexico, or Brazil." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-29"/> If a particular SKU should not be sold abroad, you do not need to leave the program: "You can adjust pricing rules or exclude offers through the Build International Listings tool." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-30"/>

One operational catch: "While enrolled in Remote Fulfillment with FBA, seller-fulfilled offers can't use the same SKUs as FBA offers." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-18"/>

## Where to check before you enroll a SKU

Re-check Amazon's [Remote Fulfillment with FBA page](https://sell.amazon.com/fulfillment-by-amazon/remote-fulfillment) for requirements and exclusions and the [Global Selling page](https://sell.amazon.com/global-selling) for account structure; your own SKUs' status is in the ASIN Eligibility Report. For the Canadian shopper's border cost, see CBSA's [CUSMA low-value shipment thresholds](https://cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/services/cusma-aceum/lvs-efv-eng.html). If you will hold stock in Canada, read the CRA's [supply of qualifying goods rules](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy-gsthst/charge-collect/sales-good.html), its [GST/HST digital economy overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html) and the [import-export account page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/import-export-program-account.html). This page does not cover Mexico's import rules. Canadian tax status is settled by the CRA, not by Amazon's program terms.
