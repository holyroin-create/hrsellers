If you already sell with FBA in the US, the lowest-effort way into Canada and Mexico is to leave your stock where it is. Amazon's program for that is called Remote Fulfillment with FBA, and it "simplifies international sales by letting you sell to customers in Canada, Mexico, and Brazil without sending inventory to those countries." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-01"/> The older "North America Remote Fulfillment" (NARF) name does not appear on Amazon's current program page; the program described there is the one this page covers. The decision is whether your products are eligible and whether the cross-border fee and the customer-paid duties leave you a competitive price.

## What you need before enrolling

Amazon lists three prerequisites: "a Professional selling plan, a North America and Brazil unified selling account," and enrollment "in FBA in the US with FBA Export enabled." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-02"/> The unified account is the same one Amazon describes for reaching "Amazon customers in the US, Canada, Mexico, and Brazil." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-20"/> If you link global selling accounts, Amazon says your monthly fee will be either the equivalent of USD 39.99 or the sum of regional fees for active listings, whichever is lower. <C id="narf-canada-mexico-21"/>

## Who pays duties and taxes

This is the feature that makes the program workable for small sellers: "Customers pay any import duties, taxes, and fees as the importer of record." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-08"/> Amazon spells out the consequence: "That means you don't need to pay taxes in Canada, Mexico, or Brazil." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-09"/> For what being importer of record means in general, see [who is the importer](/guides/incoterms-who-is-importer/).

That changes if you send inventory into Canada yourself. The Canada Revenue Agency says the Excise Tax Act was amended, effective July 1, 2021, with GST/HST provisions, and it lists qualifying goods "such as goods located in a fulfillment warehouse." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-22"/> <C id="narf-canada-mexico-27"/> For those supplies, "The normal GST/HST registration, reporting and remittance regime is applicable to these non-resident vendors." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-23"/> Importing goods into Canada also calls for a nine-digit business number and an import-export (RM) program account, which the CRA says to register "before you import or export goods." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-24"/>

## Fees

Amazon's FAQ: sellers "will pay the Remote Fulfillment with FBA costs and the category referral fee for the store where the sale occurred." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-13"/> "These costs replace US FBA costs and referral fees." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-14"/> The per-unit Remote Fulfillment rate card is published inside Seller Central, not on the public program page, so check it there before you set prices.

Pricing is automated by default. The listing tool "synchronizes your prices to preserve your profit margins" while "adjusting for differences in fulfillment costs, referral fees, and currency exchange rates." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-06"/> If you override it, "Any manual price adjustments in the destination store will break the connection." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-19"/>

## What ships, and what doesn't

Every product must "Meet all trade compliance and destination-country regulations." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-16"/> Amazon's program page does not list the excluded product types in its public text, so check eligibility for each SKU in Seller Central; for hazmat products see the [dangerous goods guide](/guides/hazmat-dangerous-goods/).

After the sale, Amazon says it will "handle the export logistics, fulfillment, customer inquiries, and returns for your orders," and "Prime customers receive free shipping to Canada, Mexico, and Brazil." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-11"/> <C id="narf-canada-mexico-10"/> "Returns will be sent back to the US and are subject to the FBA customer returns policy." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-12"/>

Behind the scenes, Amazon says "we'll use your US FBA inventory to deliver orders across the border with Prime shipping." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-28"/> Among the requirements, a product must be "enabled for FBA in the US" with "valid offers in Canada, Mexico, or Brazil." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-29"/> If a particular SKU should not be sold abroad, you do not need to leave the program: "You can adjust pricing rules or exclude offers through the Build International Listings tool." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-30"/>

One operational catch: "While enrolled in Remote Fulfillment with FBA, seller-fulfilled offers can't use the same SKUs as FBA offers." <C id="narf-canada-mexico-18"/>

## How to confirm before you enroll

Re-check Amazon's [Remote Fulfillment with FBA page](https://sell.amazon.com/fulfillment-by-amazon/remote-fulfillment) for requirements and exclusions and the [Global Selling page](https://sell.amazon.com/global-selling) for account structure. The fee rate card is in Seller Central. If you will hold stock in Canada, read the CRA's [GST/HST digital economy overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html) and [import-export account page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/import-export-program-account.html). Canadian tax status is settled by the CRA, not by Amazon's program terms.
