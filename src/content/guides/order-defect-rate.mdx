Amazon's Order Defect Rate (ODR) is an Account Health metric built from three kinds of customer problem: negative feedback, A-to-z Guarantee claims and chargebacks. Amazon's own reporting documentation lists those three components next to the order count, the number of orders with defects and the rate, and gives a target value for comparison. <C id="order-defect-rate-02"/> To decide where to spend effort, find which component is driving the rate, for which fulfillment channel, and work on the cause at the order level. This page shows what Amazon publishes about the metric and how to pull the data; the exact target and measurement window are published inside Seller Central and should be read there.

## What the metric is made of

The Seller Performance Report, which "contains the individual performance metrics data from the Seller Central Account Health dashboard," is the clearest public description of ODR. <C id="order-defect-rate-04"/> For each fulfillment channel it reports a reporting date range, the order count, the orders with defects, and separate counts for claims, chargebacks and negative feedback, followed by a status, the rate, a target value and a target condition. <C id="order-defect-rate-02"/> The XML Customer Metrics Report (GET_V1_SELLER_PERFORMANCE_REPORT) lists similar fields, naming the components negativeFeedbacks, a_z_claims and chargebacks. <C id="order-defect-rate-03"/>

Because the report gives both the component counts and a separate orderWithDefects figure, you can compare them: if claims plus chargebacks plus negative feedback add up to more than the orders with defects, that suggests some orders carry more than one defect. <C id="order-defect-rate-02"/>

## FBA and seller-fulfilled orders are measured apart

The orderDefectRate object is split into afn (Amazon-fulfilled) and mfn (merchant-fulfilled) parts, each with its own counts, rate and target. <C id="order-defect-rate-01"/> That split matters for diagnosis. With FBA, Amazon says it will "pick, pack, and ship orders, as well as handle customer service and returns." <C id="order-defect-rate-09"/> Reading the afn and mfn lines separately shows whether defects sit with orders Amazon fulfilled or with orders you shipped yourself.

ODR sits alongside other order metrics in the same report: late shipment rate, on-time delivery rate, valid tracking rate and pre-fulfillment cancellation rate, each with a status, target value and target condition. <C id="order-defect-rate-12"/> Programs can add their own requirements on top: Seller Fulfilled Prime, for example, requires meeting performance requirements for late-shipment, valid-tracking and cancellation rates to prequalify. <C id="order-defect-rate-13"/>

## Why it matters

ODR is one of the metrics in the Seller Performance Report, which carries the Account Health dashboard data <C id="order-defect-rate-04"/>; Amazon describes that dashboard as the place to "monitor your customer service, shipping performance, and policy compliance." <C id="order-defect-rate-10"/> Amazon has said its Account Health Rating "will be the determining factor for account suspension based on accumulated policy violations," so order problems and policy problems are watched in the same place. <C id="order-defect-rate-11"/> The Amazon Seller app also shows "banner alerts related to your account health." <C id="order-defect-rate-14"/> If your account is already at risk, the [account suspension appeal guide](/guides/account-suspension-appeal/) covers the wider account health picture.

## Worked example: reading your own numbers

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: in one reporting date range, an mfn line shows 2,000 orders and 14 orders with defects; the component counts are 8 negative feedback, 5 claims and 3 chargebacks. Components total 8 + 5 + 3 = 16, which is 2 more than the 14 orders with defects, suggesting some orders carry more than one defect. For orientation only, 14 ÷ 2,000 = 0.7%; the rate Amazon calculates is the rate field on the same line, and Amazon's exact formula and window are published in Seller Central. Compare that rate with the targetValue and targetCondition shown on the same line rather than with a figure from elsewhere. Negative feedback is the largest component here (8 of 16), so the feedback report is the first file to open.
</Callout>

The same reading works for each line of the report: identify the channel (afn or mfn), the largest component, and how far the rate sits from its target. <C id="order-defect-rate-02"/>

## How to confirm the target and window

Amazon's ODR target, the length of the measurement window and the rules on which feedback or claims can be removed are published in Seller Central Help and on your Account Health dashboard; read them there, and use the targetValue, targetCondition and reportingDateRange fields in your own Seller Performance Report as the account-specific version. <C id="order-defect-rate-02"/> The report fields themselves are documented on the Performance Reports page of the Selling Partner API documentation at developer-docs.amazon.com. <C id="order-defect-rate-04"/>
