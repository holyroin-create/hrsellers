Amazon's Order Defect Rate (ODR) is an Account Health metric built from three kinds of customer problem: negative feedback, A-to-z Guarantee claims and chargebacks. Amazon's reporting documentation lists those three components next to the order count, the number of orders with defects and the rate, and gives a target value for comparison. <C id="order-defect-rate-02"/> To decide where to spend effort, find which component is driving the rate, for which fulfillment channel, and work on the cause at the order level. The ODR target and window that apply to you are shown in your own report fields, which this page teaches you to read; Amazon's written policy on the metric (including which defects can be removed) is published in Seller Central Help; look up "Order Defect Rate" there.

## What the metric is made of

The Seller Performance Report, which "contains the individual performance metrics data from the Seller Central Account Health dashboard," is the clearest public description of ODR. <C id="order-defect-rate-04"/> For each fulfillment channel it reports a reporting date range, the order count, the orders with defects, and separate counts for claims, chargebacks and negative feedback, followed by a status, the rate, a target value and a target condition. <C id="order-defect-rate-02"/> The XML Customer Metrics Report (GET_V1_SELLER_PERFORMANCE_REPORT) lists similar fields, naming the components negativeFeedbacks, a_z_claims and chargebacks. <C id="order-defect-rate-03"/>

Amazon's schema for the report describes claims, chargebacks and negativeFeedback each as "order defects due to" that cause, each with its own count. <C id="order-defect-rate-25"/> Because the report also gives a separate orderWithDefects figure, you can compare them: if the three component counts add up to more than the orders with defects, some orders carry more than one defect. <C id="order-defect-rate-02"/>

## Reading each field on your ODR line

Amazon publishes the JSON schema behind the Seller Performance Report (accountHealthReport-2020-11-18) in its public Selling Partner API models repository, and the field descriptions there tell you how to read your own numbers.

| Field | What Amazon's schema says | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| reportingDateRange | Start and end dates, given in UTC | Convert to your time zone before matching orders to the window |
| orderCount, orderWithDefects | Orders, and orders with defects, for one requested marketplace | Each marketplace is reported on its own line |
| claims, chargebacks, negativeFeedback | Order defects due to each cause, each with a count | Find the largest component first |
| rate | The metric rate "in percentage" | Compare it with targetValue, written in the same format |
| status | GOOD, BAD or NONE | BAD is the line to work on first |
| targetValue | The target value "to stay in good status" | The value your rate is compared against |
| targetCondition | GREATER_THAN, LESS_THAN or EQUALS | Tells you which side of the target is good |

*Source: Amazon Selling Partner API models, accountHealthReport-2020-11-18.json, checked Nov 9, 2026.* <C id="order-defect-rate-23"/> <C id="order-defect-rate-24"/> <C id="order-defect-rate-25"/> <C id="order-defect-rate-22"/> <C id="order-defect-rate-19"/> <C id="order-defect-rate-20"/> <C id="order-defect-rate-21"/>

The sample record in the same schema file shows an ODR entry with rate 0.009, targetValue 0.01 and targetCondition LESS_THAN. <C id="order-defect-rate-26"/> Read that as an illustration of the format, not as your target: although the description calls the rate a percentage, the sample writes it as a decimal, so check how your own export writes both numbers before comparing them. With a LESS_THAN condition, the metric stays in good status while the rate sits below the target value. <C id="order-defect-rate-21"/>

## FBA and seller-fulfilled orders are measured apart

The orderDefectRate object is split into afn (Amazon-fulfilled) and mfn (merchant-fulfilled) parts, each with its own counts, rate and target. <C id="order-defect-rate-01"/> That split matters for diagnosis. With FBA, Amazon says it will "pick, pack, and ship orders, as well as handle customer service and returns." <C id="order-defect-rate-09"/> Reading the afn and mfn lines separately shows whether defects sit with orders Amazon fulfilled or with orders you shipped yourself.

ODR sits alongside other order metrics in the same report: late shipment rate, on-time delivery rate, valid tracking rate and pre-fulfillment cancellation rate, each with a status, target value and target condition. <C id="order-defect-rate-12"/>

| Sibling metric | What it counts |
|---|---|
| Late shipment rate | lateShipmentCount, the order defects count due to late shipment |
| Pre-fulfillment cancellation rate | Seller-faulted cancelled orders |
| Valid tracking rate | Shipments with valid tracking against all shipments |
| Unit-level on-time delivery rate | On-time units; available only in the US marketplace |

*Source: Amazon Selling Partner API models, accountHealthReport-2020-11-18.json, checked Nov 9, 2026.* <C id="order-defect-rate-32"/> <C id="order-defect-rate-31"/> <C id="order-defect-rate-33"/> <C id="order-defect-rate-30"/>

Programs can add their own requirements on top: Seller Fulfilled Prime, for example, requires meeting performance requirements for late-shipment, valid-tracking and cancellation rates to prequalify. <C id="order-defect-rate-13"/>

## Why it matters

ODR is one of the metrics in the Seller Performance Report, which carries the Account Health dashboard data <C id="order-defect-rate-04"/>; Amazon describes that dashboard as the place to "monitor your customer service, shipping performance, and policy compliance." <C id="order-defect-rate-10"/> Amazon has said its Account Health Rating "will be the determining factor for account suspension based on accumulated policy violations," so order problems and policy problems are watched in the same place. <C id="order-defect-rate-11"/> The Amazon Seller app also shows "banner alerts related to your account health." <C id="order-defect-rate-14"/> If your account is already at risk, the [account suspension appeal guide](/guides/account-suspension-appeal/) covers the wider account health picture.

## Worked example: reading your own numbers

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: one mfn line shows reportingDateFrom 2026-08-27T00:00Z and reportingDateTo 2026-10-25T23:59Z, 2,000 orders and 14 orders with defects; the component counts are 8 negative feedback, 5 claims and 3 chargebacks; targetCondition is LESS_THAN.

- Window: August 27 to October 25 inclusive is 60 days, in UTC. An order placed late on October 25 in a US time zone may already fall on October 26 in UTC.
- Components total 8 + 5 + 3 = 16, which is 2 more than the 14 orders with defects, so some orders carry more than one defect.
- For orientation only, 14 ÷ 2,000 = 0.7%, written 0.007 as a decimal. The figure that counts is the rate field on the same line; compare it with targetValue in the same format, and with LESS_THAN the rate must stay below it.
- Negative feedback is the largest component (8 of 16), so the feedback report is the first file to open.
</Callout>

The same reading works for each line of the report: identify the channel (afn or mfn), the marketplace, the largest component, and how far the rate sits from its target. <C id="order-defect-rate-02"/> <C id="order-defect-rate-24"/>

## Where to check your own target and window

The target and measurement window that apply to you are the targetValue, targetCondition and reportingDateRange fields of your own Seller Performance Report. <C id="order-defect-rate-02"/> The report types are documented on the Performance Reports page of the Selling Partner API documentation at developer-docs.amazon.com, and the field definitions in accountHealthReport-2020-11-18.json in Amazon's selling-partner-api-models repository on GitHub. <C id="order-defect-rate-04"/> <C id="order-defect-rate-20"/> Amazon's rules on which feedback or claims can be removed are not in those public files.
