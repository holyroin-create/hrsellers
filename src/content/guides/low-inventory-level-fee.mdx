Amazon's low-inventory-level fee applies to FBA products that run low on stock relative to sales. Today it is assessed using trailing 30- and 90-day sales; Amazon's 2027 fee update says that, effective February 1, it will apply only when inventory falls below 28 days of supply, based on actual shipments in the four weeks before and after the assessment date. <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-07"/> <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-08"/> <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-09"/> The fee's per-unit rates, size-tier differences and exemptions are published inside Seller Central and should be checked there. What you need to decide is a stocking band: enough supply to clear the low-inventory test, not so much that you trip Amazon's excess and aged inventory lines.

## What Amazon has published about the fee

This page draws on two Amazon sources: the Selling Partner API changelog, which documents inventory metrics tied to the fee, and Amazon's 2027 fee announcement.

The changelog states: "On December 5, 2023, we launched three new metrics on the FBA Manage Inventory Health Report." <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-01"/> Each relates to the fee:

| Metric | What Amazon says it does |
|---|---|
| `fba-minimum-inventory-level` | Helps sellers plan inventory levels "and help them to avoid the recently announced low-inventory-level fee." <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-02"/> |
| `historical-days-of-supply` | Identifies products that held low inventory relative to sales "over the past 90 days, and may incur low-inventory-level fees." <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-03"/> |
| `fba-inventory-level-health-status` | Evaluates sellable inventory "against the recommended minimum level and estimated excess units." <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-04"/> |

*Source: Amazon Selling Partner API changelog, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-01"/>

Amazon's guidance in the same entry: "Keeping inventory above the minimum recommended level helps sellers improve delivery speeds" and avoid the fee. <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-05"/> The report is available for sellers and API integrators to pull via API. <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-06"/>

## How the fee is assessed today

In its 2027 fee announcement Amazon summarized the current method in one sentence: "Today, the fee is assessed using trailing 30- and 90-day sales." <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-07"/> Amazon says the coming change will reduce instances triggered by unexpected demand spikes or end-of-season and end-of-life products, which indicates those cases can trigger the fee today. <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-11"/>

## What changes on February 1 (2027 update)

Amazon says it is "updating the low-inventory-level fee to better reflect your actual demand." <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-12"/> Three things change:

- **A fixed threshold.** "Effective February 1, the fee will apply only when your inventory falls below 28 days of supply." <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-08"/>
- **A two-sided demand window.** Supply is measured "based on actual shipments in both the four weeks before and after the assessment date." <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-09"/>
- **A demand check.** Amazon says that evaluating the four weeks after the assessment "confirms whether low inventory reflects ongoing demand." <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-10"/>

Amazon states the intent: to "target SKUs that need replenishment and reduce instances triggered by unexpected demand spikes or end-of-season and end-of-life products." <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-11"/>

## Worked example (hypothetical): reading 28 days of supply

Amazon's announcement does not give a days-of-supply formula. The simple version below shows how a two-sided window behaves.

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: 90 sellable units on hand on the assessment date. Days of supply = units on hand ÷ average daily shipments over the 56 days (four weeks before plus four weeks after).

- **Continuing demand:** 120 units shipped in the four weeks before, 160 in the four weeks after. Daily average = 280 ÷ 56 = 5. Days of supply = 90 ÷ 5 = 18 days — below 28. <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-08"/>
- **End of season:** 120 before, 60 after. Daily average = 180 ÷ 56 ≈ 3.21. Days of supply = 90 ÷ 3.21 = 28 days — at the threshold, not below it.
</Callout>

The same 90 units and the same past sales give different answers once the four weeks after the assessment are counted. Under a backward-only test the two cases would look identical. That is the scenario Amazon says the new rule is designed to handle. <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-10"/>

## The stocking band: not too low, not too high

The low-inventory fee is one edge of a band; storage fees are the other.

Amazon's inventory guide says to "Ensure you have about two months of supply available," and lists over 90 days of supply, or at least one unit aged over 90 days, among the situations in which it considers you to have excess inventory. <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-15"/> <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-14"/> Items stored more than 181 days draw a monthly aged inventory charge. <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-18"/> Running too lean has a sales cost as well: Amazon says "Low stock levels can hurt your sales and brand reputation." <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-19"/>

One more 2027 change helps the upper side of the band: Amazon will eliminate the storage utilization surcharge on February 1, 2027, which it says "will make it easier to stock up ahead of high-demand events." <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-13"/> The storage side is covered in [FBA storage fees: the four layers](/guides/fba-storage-four-layers/).

## How to confirm the fee for your SKUs

- Amazon's 2027 fee announcement on sellingpartners.aboutamazon.com, for the February 1 rule. <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-08"/>
- The Selling Partner API changelog entry on the Manage Inventory Health report metrics. <C id="low-inventory-level-fee-01"/>
- In Seller Central: the low-inventory-level fee help page for per-unit rates, size tiers and exemptions, and the Manage Inventory Health report for your own SKUs.

This page describes Amazon's published method only. Whether a particular charge was correctly applied has to be raised with Amazon through your seller account.
