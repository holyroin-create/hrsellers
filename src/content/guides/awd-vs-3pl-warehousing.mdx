Three changes landed close together and reset this calculation: FBA storage got more punishing, Amazon raised AWD's own fees on 15 January 2026, and Amazon stopped prepping inventory. Most published comparisons predate at least one of them.

## What AWD is, precisely

Amazon Warehousing and Distribution is **upstream bulk storage that sits between your factory or 3PL and FBA.** Inventory ships in case packs, palletised, is stored at a flat rate, and moves to FBA in a reported three to seven days when you trigger replenishment.

**What it is not:** a sellable inventory layer. Customers cannot buy from AWD stock. That single fact settles the multi-channel question before any arithmetic.

## The storage rates, and why they disagree

| Source | Reported AWD storage |
| --- | --- |
| Seller Essentials | $0.43 East, $0.51 West (Smart Storage) |
| SKU Compass | $0.48 flat |
| WarehousingCosts | $0.48 general, $0.57 West |
| 3PL Guys (fees guide) | $0.56 |
| 3PL Guys (comparison) | $0.45–$0.75 |
| Racklify | As low as $0.43 with Smart Storage, described as a 10% discount for maintaining inventory levels |

The spread resolves into three variables: **region** — reporting describes Amazon introducing a West-region premium on 15 January 2026, with one source putting the West increase at about 19%; **programme** — Smart Storage is described as a 10% discount conditional on maintaining sufficient inventory; and **date**.

**The working position:** roughly $0.48 outside the West and roughly $0.55 in the West, before the Smart Storage discount. Verify in your own AWD console, because this is precisely the figure that moves.

## The fees underneath, which decide it

Storage is the headline and the smallest line. Reported additional AWD charges:

| Charge | Reported |
| --- | --- |
| Transportation | ~$1.40 per cubic foot (~$1.26 under Amazon-managed distribution) |
| Processing | $1.40 per box, inbound and outbound |
| Transfer to FBA | $0.40–$0.50 per unit, varying by case-pack count |
| Inbound from suppliers | Typically free if case-packed and palletised |
| Multichannel distribution | Additional per-shipment fees plus carrier rates |

**The transfer fee has a detail worth knowing.** It varies by case-pack count: a SKU packed twelve to a case costs less per unit to transfer than one packed four to a case. Reporting recommends running the calculation on your specific top twenty SKUs before changing storage strategy, which is sound.

One genuine advantage: reporting states AWD **eliminates the FBA inbound placement fee** by bundling that work into the service. Against the [placement arithmetic](/guides/inbound-placement-split-arithmetic/), that is worth real money at volume.

## The prep problem

**AWD does not prep.** Since Amazon ended US prep services on 1 January 2026 — see the [prep guide](/guides/amazon-stopped-prepping/) — inventory must arrive at AWD already FBA-ready.

Reporting frames the resulting comparison precisely: not "AWD storage against 3PL storage" but **"AWD storage plus prep somewhere else" against "3PL storage with prep built in."** At a reported $0.35 to $2.00 per unit for prep, that narrows the storage advantage considerably and can reverse it.

## The claims worth discounting

Two sources in this research are prep centres and 3PLs. Their claims about AWD deserve the same scepticism this site applies elsewhere.

Reported by 3PL operators: AWD inventory loss rates of 5% to 10%, receiving times of two to four weeks, and reimbursement at manufacturing cost typically 40% to 50% of what was paid.

**Treat these as vendor claims.** They are published by companies selling the alternative, without stated method or sample. The reimbursement point is at least verifiable: Amazon does reimburse at sourcing cost, as the [reimbursement guide](/guides/fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost/) covers — although that policy applies to FBA generally rather than being an AWD-specific disadvantage.

The receiving-time claim is the one to test rather than believe. If inventory sits in checked-in status for weeks, it is not sellable, and reporting is right that auto-replenishment does not account for its own delays. Whether that is typical or a bad experience being marketed is not established by the sources available.

## Where each option genuinely fits

**Ship factory-direct to FBA** when a SKU turns in 30 to 60 days. Reporting is direct that upstream storage adds operational complexity without saving anything on fast movers.

**AWD** when you hold buffer stock that would otherwise age past the aged-inventory trigger in FBA, or sit through the Q4 storage spike. Reporting describes AWD as winning for Amazon-dominant mid-market brands with 60% or more of revenue through FBA. Both AWD and a 3PL reserve skip the [Q4 storage spike](/guides/peak-season-cost-stack/) entirely, which is the largest single saving available.

**An independent 3PL** when you fulfil off Amazon, need kitting, custom packaging or inserts, or have SKUs requiring prep and customisation. A 3PL can serve Shopify, Walmart, B2B and direct-to-consumer; AWD cannot.

**Both** is reported as the right answer for many multi-channel sellers, and that is not a fudge. AWD feeds FBA; a 3PL feeds everything else.

## The comparison to actually run

Storage rate alone tells you nothing. Per SKU, per month:

```
AWD total   = (cu ft × storage rate)
            + transportation
            + processing per box
            + (units × transfer fee)
            + (units × prep cost, paid elsewhere)

3PL total   = (cu ft × storage rate)
            + receiving
            + (units × prep, usually included)
            + outbound to FBA
            + pick/pack if fulfilling directly

FBA direct  = (cu ft × FBA rate, tripling in Q4)
            + aged-inventory surcharge if it sits
            + inbound placement
            + (units × prep, paid elsewhere)
```

Reporting notes sampled 3PL reserve warehouses average around $0.90 per cubic foot once pallet pricing is converted — a standard pallet holding roughly 50 cubic feet — against AWD's Smart Storage rates. **Both beat FBA's standard rate and both skip the Q4 spike.** That is the finding that matters more than which of the two is cheaper.

## What to do

**Run it on your top twenty SKUs**, not on your catalogue. Case-pack counts, cubic feet and turn rates vary enough that a blended answer is wrong for most of them.

**Split by velocity, not by preference.** Fast movers direct to FBA, slow movers and buffer stock upstream. That single rule captures most of the available saving.

**Price prep separately** and add it to whichever option does not include it.

**Ask a prospective 3PL for pallet pricing converted to cubic feet.** Pallet rates are not comparable to AWD's per-cubic-foot pricing without the conversion, and the conversion is where quotes stop looking similar.

**Decide before September.** Both options exist to avoid the Q4 storage spike, and both need inventory in place before it starts.

**Check it on your own package.** The [dimensional weight calculator](/tools/dimensional-weight/) takes your packed dimensions and weight and returns billable weight and cubic feet — the two figures that drive freight and storage. Nothing is stored and nothing leaves your browser.
