If your Amazon selling account has been suspended, the first decision is not how to word the appeal but what Amazon is actually counting against you. Amazon has said publicly that its Account Health Rating "will be the determining factor for account suspension based on accumulated policy violations." <C id="account-suspension-appeal-02"/> That rating, the violations behind it and their severity are all visible to you before you write anything. This page covers what Amazon publishes outside Seller Central about that system, and how to assemble the facts. The appeal steps and the format Amazon expects are published inside Seller Central Help, which is the only place to read them; this page does not reproduce or guess at them.

## How Amazon decides an account is at risk

Amazon introduced the current Account Health Rating in a July 2022 announcement and began rolling it out in August to sellers in its U.S. and Canada stores. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-05"/> The rating is a numerical value, and that value places the account in one of three bands: "Healthy," "At-Risk" or "Unhealthy." <C id="account-suspension-appeal-01"/> The same article says a seller can "see the level of severity for each violation." <C id="account-suspension-appeal-03"/>

Two practical points follow. First, Amazon ties suspension to accumulated violations, so review every violation listed, not only the most recent notice. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-02"/> Second, the severity shown for each violation is a reasonable way to order your review.

The Account Health dashboard is where this lives. Amazon describes it as the place to "monitor your customer service, shipping performance, and policy compliance." <C id="account-suspension-appeal-06"/> Those three headings are also a useful way to sort the problem before an appeal: is this about orders (defects, late shipments, cancellations), about a listing or product policy, or about something Amazon has asked you to document?

## Account Health Assurance

Some sellers have an extra layer of protection. On November 16, 2022, Amazon announced Account Health Assurance, a free benefit "which formalizes our commitment to not deactivate a seller's account as long as they work with us to resolve any issues." <C id="account-suspension-appeal-19"/> Under it, "a member of the Amazon team will proactively reach out and work one-on-one" with sellers whose account may be at risk. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-20"/> Amazon says enrolled sellers see a badge at the top of their account page. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-21"/> Amazon's announcement ties the commitment to the seller working with Amazon on the issue, so if you have the badge and are contacted about an at-risk account, respond to that outreach. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-19"/>

## Account status, store by store

Sellers who use software connected through the Selling Partner API (SP-API) can see the account's state change without logging in. Amazon's ACCOUNT_STATUS_CHANGED notification "is published whenever the selling partner's account status changes between NORMAL, AT_RISK, and DEACTIVATED." <C id="account-suspension-appeal-07"/> Its payload carries both previousAccountStatus and currentAccountStatus, so a log of these messages records the direction of every change. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-24"/>

Status is also held per store. The Seller Performance Report's accountStatuses list gives the account status for each requested marketplace, <C id="account-suspension-appeal-22"/> using the same three values: NORMAL, AT_RISK or DEACTIVATED. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-23"/> If you sell in more than one Amazon store, check every marketplace line. On a phone, the Amazon Seller app shows "banner alerts related to your account health." <C id="account-suspension-appeal-14"/>

## What the Seller Performance Report contains

The most complete public description of what Amazon tracks is the documentation for the Seller Performance Report (report type GET_V2_SELLER_PERFORMANCE_REPORT), which "contains the individual performance metrics data from the Seller Central Account Health dashboard." <C id="account-suspension-appeal-08"/> It includes an accountHealthRating object with a status and a score (ahrStatus and ahrScore) for a stated reporting date range. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-09"/>

It also names the policy categories Amazon tracks: listing policy violations, product authenticity complaints, product condition complaints, product safety complaints, received intellectual property complaints, restricted product policy violations and customer product review policy violations. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-10"/> Two further fields matter for appeals: documentRequests, and policyViolationWarnings with a warning count. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-12"/> Each metric carries a reporting date range, a status, and a target value and target condition next to the actual count or rate, so the report shows which window Amazon measured and where you stood against its target. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-11"/>

### The policy fields, one by one

Amazon's published JSON schema for this report (accountHealthReport-2020-11-18, in its selling-partner-api-models repository on GitHub) also lists suspectedIntellectualPropertyViolations, foodAndProductSafetyIssues and otherPolicyViolations, alongside documentRequests. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-25"/> Every one of them uses the same shape: a reportingDateRange, a defectsCount, a status, a targetValue and a targetCondition. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-26"/> The schema's sample record shows a policy metric with 86 defects, status BAD, targetValue 0 and targetCondition EQUALS <C id="account-suspension-appeal-27"/> — an illustration of the format, not a benchmark. In that sample, staying in good status would require a count equal to 0. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-26"/>

| Field in the report | What the field name covers |
|---|---|
| listingPolicyViolations, restrictedProductPolicyViolations | Listing policy and restricted product policy violations |
| productAuthenticityCustomerComplaints | Customer complaints about product authenticity |
| productConditionCustomerComplaints, productSafetyCustomerComplaints | Customer complaints about product condition or safety |
| receivedIntellectualPropertyComplaints, suspectedIntellectualPropertyViolations | IP complaints received, and suspected IP violations |
| foodAndProductSafetyIssues, otherPolicyViolations | Food and product safety issues; other policy violations |
| documentRequests | Documents Amazon has requested |

*Source: Amazon Selling Partner API documentation and models (accountHealthReport-2020-11-18.json), checked Nov 9, 2026.* <C id="account-suspension-appeal-10"/> <C id="account-suspension-appeal-25"/>

One field to ignore: warningStates is still in the schema, but Amazon's documentation says it is deprecated and always returns an empty array, so an empty list there tells you nothing. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-28"/>

### Listing-level enforcement

In the Listings Items API, an issue can carry enforcement actions: LISTING_SUPPRESSED "takes down the current listing item's buyability," <C id="account-suspension-appeal-30"/> and SEARCH_SUPPRESSED means the catalog item is hidden from search results. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-31"/> Each issue also has an exemption status of EXEMPT, EXEMPT_UNTIL_EXPIRY_DATE or NOT_EXEMPT; <C id="account-suspension-appeal-32"/> under a temporary exemption, Amazon enforces the listed actions after the expiry date, so the expiry date is when enforcement starts. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-33"/> The LISTINGS_ITEM_ISSUES_CHANGE notification is delivered whenever issues are created, changed or resolved for a listing, so a resolved issue is reported too. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-34"/> Why individual listings drop out is covered in [why an Amazon listing disappears](/guides/listing-disappeared/).

If a reviewer from your side, or an advisor, needs to see the numbers, this report is the version to export, because it is the same data as the dashboard. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-08"/> It can only be requested, not scheduled, so request it on the day the status changes. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-29"/> Where no retention period is set for a report type, Amazon keeps a generated report for 90 days, so download a copy at the time of the deactivation rather than relying on regenerating it later. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-18"/>

## Why account health matters beyond the appeal

A weak rating can affect more than the account's status. Amazon's Seller Wallet, for example, says that to be eligible to enroll "you should have a seller account in the US store that is in good account health." <C id="account-suspension-appeal-16"/> Programs can also be withdrawn on their own terms: in Seller Fulfilled Prime, "ongoing failure to meet requirements may lead to Prime offers being disabled and disenrollment from the program." <C id="account-suspension-appeal-17"/> When you plan the fix, check which programs and tools you rely on and whether their own requirements are also affected.

## How to confirm before you appeal

Re-read the current Account Health dashboard and the appeal instructions in Seller Central Help on the day you submit, because both are maintained only inside Seller Central. For the metric definitions Amazon exposes to software, check the Performance Reports page in the Selling Partner API documentation at developer-docs.amazon.com and the accountHealthReport-2020-11-18.json schema in Amazon's selling-partner-api-models repository; <C id="account-suspension-appeal-26"/> for the rating's purpose and bands, Amazon's July 21, 2022 announcement on aboutamazon.com is the public reference, and Amazon's November 16, 2022 post on sell.amazon.com describes Account Health Assurance. <C id="account-suspension-appeal-08"/> <C id="account-suspension-appeal-01"/>
