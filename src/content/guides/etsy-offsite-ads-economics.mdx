Cross $10,000 in trailing sales on Etsy and your fee structure changes for the life of the shop. No signature, no notice period, no way back. Every other Etsy fee line is small and predictable. Offsite Ads is the exception, and the rules below come straight from Etsy's Fees & Payments Policy. The rules come from Etsy's own fees policy, so this one can be stated rather than estimated.

## The mechanism, from the policy

Etsy advertises listings on external platforms — search and social — at its own discretion. When a shopper clicks such an ad and orders within the attribution window, the fee applies to that order. The tiering, per Etsy's policy: under $10,000 in prior-365-day sales (assessed on the first of each month), the rate is **15% and participation is optional**; at or above $10,000, the rate is **12% and participation is required for the life of the shop** — including for sellers who had previously opted out, and regardless of whether sales later fall back under the threshold. Reported guardrails: the fee is capped at $100 on a single order, and attribution runs 30 days from click.

Stack the rail and an attributed order at handmade price points can carry roughly a fifth to a quarter of its value in platform fees before materials and labor. That is not an argument against the program — attributed orders are incremental reach you did not buy elsewhere — it is an argument for **pricing from the full stack up**, which is the same discipline every marketplace on this site gets held to.

## The decision, made before the threshold makes it

<MiniCalc variant="margin" />

Three positions, compared: **Under $10K and thin-margin** — opting out is legitimate; the 15% tier on low-margin work can turn attributed orders into charity. **Under $10K and growing** — price as if enrolled at 12% now, because the threshold is coming and the policy voids your earlier opt-out the day it arrives. **Over $10K** — the question is closed; the open question is product mix: push the catalog toward items whose margins carry a 12% line, and let the $100 cap work for you on high-ticket orders, where the effective rate falls below 12%.
