Etsy's Offsite Ads fee is 15% of the total order amount on any order attributed to an Offsite Ad, dropping to 12% once your shop has made $10,000 or more in sales over the prior 365 days — and no single order pays more than $100. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-01"/> <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-02"/> <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-08"/> On a $25 attributed order that is $3.75 at 15%, which more than doubles Etsy's other per-order fees on the same sale. The mechanics below — what counts as attributed, what the percentage applies to, and when you can opt out — decide how often you pay it.

## How the fee is triggered

The fee is charged only on "Attributed Orders." Etsy's policy describes the trigger as an ad that "a buyer clicks on, and then places any orders from your shop within 30 days of that click." <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-06"/> Two consequences follow:

- **Any item counts.** The policy covers orders "from your shop," so the item bought does not have to be the one in the ad. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-06"/>
- **The window is 30 days.** A buyer who clicks today and returns three weeks later through your shop link still produces an attributed order. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-06"/>

## What the percentage applies to

The fee is charged as a percentage of the total order amount, which Etsy defines as the price you display for each listing plus the amount you charge for shipping and gift wrapping. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-07"/> For US sellers, "Offsite Ads fees will not apply to sales tax." <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-09"/>

Because shipping is in the base, moving cost from the item price to the shipping charge does not lower the fee. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-07"/>

The threshold looks at "sales of $10,000 USD or more over the prior 365 days," calculated on the first day of the month; the 12% rate then applies from the first day of the month following qualification. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-02"/> Crossing it changes two things at once: the rate falls from 15% to 12%, and the option to opt out disappears. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-04"/> The 12% rate then stays even if sales drop, because Etsy states you "will still only be charged a 12% advertising fee for the lifetime of your shop." <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-05"/>

## The full fee stack on an attributed order

Offsite Ads is charged on top of Etsy's standard selling fees. Those are a $0.20 listing fee per item listed, a 6.5% transaction fee on the displayed price plus shipping and gift wrapping, and — for sellers with a US bank account — a payment processing fee of 3% + $0.25. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-10"/> <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-11"/> <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-12"/> The processing fee is assessed on the gross order amount, including shipping and tax where applicable. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-13"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): one-item order, US seller, no sales tax">
Assumes the listing fee is counted once against the sale and the order total equals the item price including shipping.
</Callout>

| Total order | Etsy fees without an ad | With Offsite Ads at 15% | With Offsite Ads at 12% |
|---|---|---|---|
| $25 | $2.83 | $6.58 (26.3% of order) | $5.83 (23.3%) |
| $50 | $5.20 | $12.70 (25.4%) | $11.20 (22.4%) |
| $100 | $9.95 | $24.95 (25.0%) | $21.95 (22.0%) |

*Source: fee rates from Etsy's Fees & Payments Policy and Payments Policy, checked Nov 8, 2026; arithmetic is ours.* <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-11"/> <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-12"/>

The $25 row breaks down as $0.20 listing + $1.63 transaction (6.5%) + $1.00 processing (3% + $0.25) = $2.83, then $3.75 for the ad at 15%. At $25, $50 and $100 alike, the ad fee is larger than all the other Etsy fees combined.

## Pricing to absorb the fee

Because the ad fee is a percentage of the order, it can be built into the price ahead of time. The question is how much of your volume Offsite Ads will attribute, which you can only estimate from your own order history. The fee rates themselves are fixed by the policy: 6.5% transaction, 3% + $0.25 processing for US bank accounts, and 15% or 12% for attributed orders. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-11"/> <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-12"/> <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-01"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): keeping the same net per order">
A $25 order with no ad leaves $25.00 − $2.825 = $22.175 (about $22.18) after Etsy fees. To leave the same amount when every order is attributed at 15%, the price must solve P − ($0.45 + 9.5% × P) − 15% × P = $22.175, which gives about $29.97. If only one order in five is attributed, the expected ad cost is 3% of price, and the price that holds the same average net is about $25.86.
</Callout>

The gap between those two answers is the practical point: the share of attributed orders matters more than the headline rate. A shop paying 12% has the same arithmetic with a lower rate. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-05"/> Because the listing fee and the $0.25 processing charge are fixed while the ad fee is a straight percentage, the ad fee takes the same 15% (or 12%) of every attributed order whatever its price.

## Multi-item and repeat orders

Because attribution covers any order from your shop within 30 days of the click, the fee can land on a larger basket than the advertised item. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-06"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
A buyer clicks an ad for a $20 mug, then 10 days later orders a $60 lamp with $8 shipping. The attributed order total is $68, so the fee at 15% is $10.20 — on an item that was never advertised.
</Callout>

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What the policy says |
|---|---|
| Expecting the fee only on the advertised listing | Any order from your shop within 30 days of the click is attributed. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-06"/> |
| Calculating on item price only | The base includes shipping and gift wrapping. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-07"/> |
| Planning to opt out after growing past $10,000 | The prior opt-out no longer applies once the threshold is reached. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-04"/> |
| Assuming the rate returns to 15% if sales fall | The 12% rate applies for the lifetime of the shop. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-05"/> |
| Assuming the cap limits everyday orders | It only binds above roughly $667 (15%) or $833 (12%). <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-08"/> |
| Adding sales tax to the base | For US sellers the Offsite Ads fee does not apply to sales tax. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-09"/> |

## How to confirm it in Etsy's policy

The Offsite Ads terms sit in the Fees & Payments Policy at etsy.com/legal/fees, and the US payment processing rate is in the Etsy Payments Policy at etsy.com/legal/etsy-payments. <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-01"/> <C id="etsy-offsite-ads-economics-12"/> Read the Offsite Ads section of the current version before pricing, since Etsy revises its policies. This page explains the published fee terms only; a disputed attribution or charge on your bill is settled through Etsy's own support channel under that policy.
