Section 301 tariffs are extra import duties on products of China that the U.S. Trade Representative (USTR) imposed after a Section 301 investigation into China's technology transfer, intellectual property and innovation practices. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-01"/> If your product's HTS subheading is on one of the lists, you pay the normal duty plus 25% (Lists 1, 2 and 3) or plus 7.5% (List 4A), and some strategic products pay more. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-12"/> <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-13"/> <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-14"/> <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-15"/> As of November 2026 the actions remain in effect. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-09"/> <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-10"/>

## Where the duties come from

USTR opened the investigation on August 24, 2017 and determined that the practices under investigation were unreasonable or discriminatory and burden or restrict U.S. commerce. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-01"/> <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-02"/> That finding allowed tariff action, which came in stages:

- **July 6, 2018 action:** additional 25% duties on 818 tariff subheadings, roughly $34 billion in annual trade. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-03"/>
- **August 23, 2018 action:** additional 25% duties on 279 subheadings, roughly $16 billion in annual trade. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-04"/>
- **List 3 (September 2018) and List 4A (August 2019):** supplemental lists that USTR records as later modifications of the two original actions. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-05"/>
- **Product exclusions and the 2024 review:** USTR has since both removed duties temporarily through exclusions and raised duties on certain products after the first four-year review. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-06"/>

The duties apply to articles the product of China. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-12"/> Our [country-of-origin guide](/guides/country-of-origin-sourcing/) explains how origin is determined when parts or assembly involve more than one country.

## How the lists work in the tariff schedule

Each list is implemented as a heading in Chapter 99 of the Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTSUS). The heading states the extra duty and points to U.S. note 20, which describes the covered articles and, for Lists 2, 3 and 4A, enumerates the covered subheadings. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-13"/> <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-14"/> <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-15"/>

| Action | Chapter 99 heading | U.S. note 20 reference | Duty |
|---|---|---|---|
| List 1 | 9903.88.01 | 20(a) | Applicable duty + 25% |
| List 2 | 9903.88.02 | 20(c); subheadings in 20(d) | Applicable duty + 25% |
| List 3 | 9903.88.03 | 20(e); subheadings in 20(f) | Applicable duty + 25% |
| List 4A | 9903.88.15 | 20(r); subheadings in 20(s) | Applicable duty + 7.5% |
| Exclusions | 9903.88.69 / 9903.88.70 | 20(vvv) / 20(www) | Applicable duty only |

*Source: U.S. International Trade Commission, HTSUS Chapter 99 search, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-12"/> <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-13"/> <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-14"/> <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-15"/> <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-16"/>

USTR's notices record Lists 3 and 4A, with their Federal Register citations, as modifications of the two original actions. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-05"/> What matters for your landed cost is which heading and note cover your subheading.

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): the List 3 layer on one shipment">
Inputs: $10,000 entered value; product of China; HTS subheading with a 4% general duty; subheading listed in U.S. note 20(f), so heading 9903.88.03 applies; no exclusion.
**General duty:** 4% × $10,000 = $400.
**Section 301 (List 3):** 25% × $10,000 = $2,500.
**Duty from these two layers:** $2,900, or 29% of value.
The same goods on List 4A instead would carry 7.5% × $10,000 = $750 for the Section 301 layer. Other layers, such as the forced-labor action below or Section 232, are separate; see our [2026 tariff stack guide](/guides/tariff-stack-2026/).
</Callout>

## The 2024 increases on strategic products

The first four-year review ended with higher rates on specific subheadings, some effective in 2024 and others phased in. Increases scheduled for 2025 and 2026 apply to goods entered on or after January 1 of the corresponding year, so all of them are now in force. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-25"/>

## Current status in November 2026

Section 301 actions do not last indefinitely by default. Under Section 307(c)(1) of the Trade Act, an action terminates at the close of its four-year period unless continuation is timely requested. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-07"/> For the second four-year review, USTR opened windows in 2026 for domestic beneficiaries to request continuation. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-08"/> Those requests came in, and USTR announced that the July 6, 2018 action, as modified, did not terminate on July 6, 2026, and the August 23, 2018 action did not terminate on August 23, 2026; both remain in effect. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-09"/> <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-10"/> USTR says it will publish a separate notice describing the review, including a request for comments on how effective the actions have been. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-11"/>

## Exclusions: narrow and time-limited

An exclusion removes the Section 301 duty for a product that matches a specific written description. In its December 1, 2025 notice, USTR extended the 178 exclusions then in force, listed under headings 9903.88.69 and 9903.88.70, through 11:59 p.m. eastern daylight time on November 9, 2026. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-26"/> <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-29"/> <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-27"/> An extended exclusion is available for any product that meets its description, not only the company that requested it, and CBP issues the entry guidance. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-28"/> Descriptions are tied to statistical reporting numbers, which is why USTR made a conforming amendment after the USITC changed certain HTSUS categories effective July 1, 2026. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-30"/>

<Callout type="warning" title="Check the end date before you price a shipment">
The December 1, 2025 extension covers goods entered before 11:59 p.m. eastern daylight time on November 9, 2026, the day this page was last checked. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-27"/> The notice says USTR may continue to consider further extensions and/or additional modifications. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-39"/> A Federal Register search of USTR documents on November 9, 2026 showed the October 7, 2026 conforming amendment as the newest exclusion notice, with no later extension. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-38"/> For goods entered after the November 9 cut-off, an exclusion applies only if a later USTR notice extends it, so search the Federal Register before relying on one.
</Callout>

### Pricing a shipment while the extension question is open

Whether or not a further extension appears, the cost difference is easy to bound in advance, because an exclusion removes the Section 301 duty for a product that matches its description. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-28"/>

- **Exposure per entry = entered value × the Section 301 rate for your product's list.** That is the amount an exclusion saves, and the amount you pay if it lapses.
- **Price on the no-exclusion case.** If the product only works with the exclusion, treat any shipment entered after the cut-off as carrying the full duty until a notice says otherwise.
- **Watch the date that counts at customs, not the order date.** The extension runs to a clock time on November 9, 2026, so confirm with your broker which date on the entry is measured against it for a container still on the water at the deadline. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-27"/>
- **Keep the exclusion description with the entry file.** An exclusion applies to any product that meets its description, so the evidence that your product matches it is what a broker or CBP will look at. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-28"/>

## Other Section 301 actions that reach Chinese goods

The technology-transfer lists are not the only Section 301 duties on products of China.

**Forced-labor action (2026).** In investigations of several economies over their failure to ban imports of goods made with forced labor, USTR determined to impose 12.5% tariffs on products of China, except as provided in the notice's Annex I and Annex II, Part A. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-33"/> They apply to goods entered on or after 12:01 a.m. eastern time on July 24, 2026, with a narrow exception for goods already in transit. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-34"/> Its U.S. note 52(a) says covered products "shall also be subject to any additional duty provided for in this subchapter or in subchapter IV of chapter 99," as well as general duty rates, and heading 9903.05.90 exempts listed articles such as articles of aluminum, steel or copper. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-35"/> <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-36"/>

**Semiconductor action (2025).** A separate determination on China's targeting of the semiconductor industry took effect December 23, 2025 at an initial 0%, rising on June 23, 2027 to a rate USTR will announce. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-31"/> USTR says those tariffs would be additional to the existing 50% Section 301 tariff on semiconductors from China. <C id="section-301-china-tariffs-32"/>
