The payment term you agree with a supplier decides who is carrying the risk while goods are being made and shipped. The International Trade Administration (ITA) describes five primary methods of payment for international transactions. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-01"/> Its guidance is written for U.S. exporters, but it states the importer's position just as plainly: importers want to receive the goods as soon as possible but to delay payment as long as possible. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-02"/> Or, in ITA's words, "for importers, any payment is a donation until the goods are received." <C id="supplier-payment-terms-03"/> This page reads each method from the buyer's side.

### Cash-in-advance (including deposits)

ITA calls payment in advance the least attractive option for the buyer. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-04"/> Wire transfers and credit cards are the most commonly used cash-in-advance options. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-28"/> For small transactions, escrow services are becoming another cash-in-advance option. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-05"/>

Escrow adds a check before the money is released. It places the funds with a trusted third party until the specified conditions are met; on delivery, the importer has a pre-determined time to inspect and accept the goods — ITA's example is five days — and if the buyer returns the goods during that period, the exporter is not paid. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-29"/> <C id="supplier-payment-terms-30"/> <C id="supplier-payment-terms-31"/>

ITA tells exporters to consider cash-in-advance when the importer is a new customer or has a less-established purchasing history. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-32"/> Any deposit or balance you prepay is exposed until goods are in your hands, so put [supplier verification](/guides/supplier-verification/) and a pre-shipment [QC inspection](/guides/qc-inspections/) in front of the balance payment.

### Letters of credit

A letter of credit is a contractual commitment by the foreign buyer's bank to pay once the exporter ships the goods. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-07"/> The importer applies to its bank to open the letter of credit in favor of the exporter. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-08"/> ITA says LCs are designed to protect both exporters and importers, <C id="supplier-payment-terms-27"/> and an LC protects the buyer since no payment obligation arises until the goods have been shipped as promised. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-06"/>

ITA also recommends LCs when the importer's credit is unacceptable or not available. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-39"/>

The costs are real. LCs can be labor-intensive and relatively expensive due to bank fees. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-09"/> The required documents are detailed and prone to errors and discrepancies, <C id="supplier-payment-terms-10"/> and any errors must be amended and resubmitted. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-11"/> ITA recommends them for higher-risk situations. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-12"/> For a buyer, the practical lever is the document list; ask your bank whether an inspection certificate can be included among the required documents. ITA's list of questions to ask the bank includes "How much does a Letter of Credit cost? Who pays the fees?" <C id="supplier-payment-terms-40"/>

### Documentary collections

Payment under a collection is either at sight (documents against payment) or on a specified date (documents against acceptance). <C id="supplier-payment-terms-33"/> The supplier's bill of exchange, also called a draft, tells the banks which documents are required. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-34"/> Here the shipping documents travel through the banks and are released to you in one of two ways: upon receipt of your full payment, <C id="supplier-payment-terms-13"/> or upon your signed acceptance of terms that commit to future payment. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-14"/> The banks do not verify that the documents are accurate and do not guarantee payment as they do with letters of credit. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-15"/> ITA notes collections offer no verification process and limited recourse in the event of non-payment, <C id="supplier-payment-terms-26"/> that they are generally less expensive than LCs, <C id="supplier-payment-terms-16"/> and that it recommends them only for established trade relationships in economically and politically stable markets. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-17"/> ITA also describes the exporter's position under a collection: if the importer does not pay, the exporter typically has to find another buyer, pay for return transportation or abandon the goods. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-35"/>

### Open account

The goods ship and payment is due later, typically in 30, 60 or 90 days in international sales. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-18"/> ITA calls this one of the most advantageous options to the importer in terms of cash flow and cost. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-19"/>

### Consignment

Payment is sent to the exporter only after the goods have been sold by the foreign distributor to the end customer. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-20"/>

## How payment terms touch customs value

The payment instrument does not take a payment outside the price: payment may be made by letters of credit or negotiable instruments and may be made directly or indirectly. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-21"/> An indirect payment includes the settlement by the buyer, in whole or in part, of a debt owed by the seller. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-22"/> If you pay a supplier's raw-material bill to keep an order moving, that payment can form part of the price.

The price actually paid or payable is considered without regard to its method of derivation. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-43"/> If you pay a delivered price that includes ocean freight and insurance, those charges incident to the international shipment are excluded from the price actually paid or payable. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-44"/> What the invoice must show is covered in [the supplier invoice](/guides/the-supplier-invoice/).

Credits that come later do not reduce the entered value: any rebate of, or other decrease in, the price made after the date of importation is disregarded in determining transaction value. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-23"/> Settle quality claims and discounts before the goods are imported if you want them reflected in the value. Valuation details are in our [first-sale valuation guide](/guides/first-sale-valuation/).

## Sanctions and fraud: the two payment risks no term removes

No payment method makes it lawful to pay a blocked party; U.S. persons are generally prohibited from dealing with the individuals and entities on the SDN List. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-24"/> And none of them helps if you pay the wrong account: the FBI's IC3 recommends using secondary channels and/or two-factor authentication to verify requests for changes in account information. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-25"/>

ITA's payment-problems guidance is written for exporters who are owed money: it calls contacting and negotiating with the customer the simplest and least costly solution, and says arbitration is often faster and cheaper than legal action. <C id="supplier-payment-terms-41"/> <C id="supplier-payment-terms-42"/>

## Where to read the official guidance

Read ITA's "Methods of Payment," "Cash-in-Advance," "Letter of Credit," "Documentary Collections" and "Payment Problems" pages on trade.gov. For customs value, read 19 CFR 152.102(f) and 152.103 on the eCFR. Screen the payee through OFAC's Sanctions List Search, and read the IC3 business email compromise announcement on ic3.gov.
