If any of your listing images or ads show a realistic person who was generated by AI, you have two rule sets to check: Amazon's own labeling rule, and New York's synthetic performer disclosure law. The New York law requires anyone dealing in property or services who, for a commercial purpose, produces an advertisement to conspicuously disclose a synthetic performer in it, where they have actual knowledge of it, <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-04"/> <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-05"/> and New York's governor announced it in effect on June 9, 2026. <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-12"/> Amazon's rule on how to tag AI-generated people in listing images is published inside Seller Central; read it there, because this page does not restate it. What follows is the public law, Amazon's public statements about AI content, and how to organize your image files so you can comply with either.

## What New York's law requires

The rule is in New York General Business Law § 396-b. Its key terms:

| Element | What the statute says |
|---|---|
| Synthetic performer | A digitally created asset, made or modified by computer using generative AI or a software algorithm |
| The impression it creates | That the asset is giving a visual or audiovisual performance of a human performer |
| Who it is not | A human performer recognizable as any identifiable natural performer |
| Who must disclose | Any person in the business of dealing in property or services who, for any commercial purpose, produces or creates an ad |
| What they must do | Conspicuously disclose in the ad that a synthetic performer is in it |
| Knowledge standard | Only where the person has actual knowledge |

*Source: New York State Senate, General Business Law § 396-B, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-01"/> <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-02"/> <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-03"/> <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-04"/> <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-05"/>

The definition targets invented people. A real, identifiable person is outside this definition, which turns on a performer "not recognizable as any identifiable natural performer." <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-02"/> The statute also excludes audio advertisements and uses of AI that solely translate a human performer's language. <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-08"/> <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-09"/>

<Stat value="$1,000 / $5,000" label="Civil penalty for a first violation / each later violation under GBL § 396-b" claim="ai-image-tagging-rule-07" />

The bill (S8420) was signed as chapter 617 on December 11, 2025, <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-10"/> with the act taking effect on the 180th day after it became law. <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-11"/> The governor's office described it as requiring people who produce or create an advertisement to identify whether it includes AI-generated synthetic performers. <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-13"/> We found no mention of online marketplaces in the statute or in the governor's release; whether a particular listing image counts as an "advertisement" under the law is a legal question for New York counsel, not something this page can settle.

## What Amazon publishes about AI-made content

Amazon builds generative AI into its own seller and advertiser tools, and its seller guide puts responsibility for AI-written listing content on the seller.

- Amazon Ads offers a free AI image generator for lifestyle and brand-themed images. <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-14"/> Its own guide warns that generative AI outputs can make mistakes. <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-15"/>
- For AI-written listing content, Amazon's seller guide says you are responsible for making the changes needed so your products are accurately represented, <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-16"/> and that using the proposed content means you agree you have reviewed it and that it is accurate, complete and compliant. <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-17"/>

AI images can therefore reach a listing through more than one route. Besides ad creative, Amazon's AI features help create text and images for A+ Content modules on the detail page, <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-23"/> and generated images can be saved to your Creative Assets library or added straight to a campaign. <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-24"/> Any audit of AI-generated people should cover all of these places, not only the main image and gallery, because an image made for one placement is easily reused in another.

Amazon Ads' moderation guide also applies to ad images regardless of how they were made; for example, low-quality images will be rejected. <C id="ai-image-tagging-rule-18"/>

## How to confirm

For the law, read General Business Law § 396-B and Senate Bill S8420 on nysenate.gov, and the governor's June 9, 2026 release on governor.ny.gov. For Amazon, the binding rule on AI-generated people in listing images is in Seller Central (search its help and news for AI-generated images); this page does not reproduce it. Whether a specific image or ad needs a New York disclosure is a legal question; a New York-licensed attorney, not Amazon Seller Support, is the right route for that answer.
