A consumer product becomes a hazardous material for US shipping when it falls into one of the hazard classes in 49 CFR 173.2. In a typical marketplace catalog the items that raise the question are lithium batteries, aerosols, and flammable liquids such as perfume or nail polish. Hazmat status does not mean the item cannot ship; it means the person who offers it for transport must classify, package, mark and document it under the Hazardous Materials Regulations, or use an exception the regulations provide.

For most sellers the working tools are three exceptions: the small lithium battery provisions in 49 CFR 173.185(c), the limited quantity provisions for each hazard class (for example 173.150(b) for flammable liquids and 173.306 for aerosols), and the training requirement in 49 CFR 172.704 that applies to anyone who prepares those packages. Each is summarized below with the paragraph to check.

<Callout type="note" title="Scope of this page">
This page summarizes the Hazardous Materials Regulations for ground shipments within the US. Air transport adds further limits. Carrier and marketplace dangerous goods programs are separate and are not covered here. For a determination on a specific product, a hazmat consultant or the carrier's dangerous goods desk is the route.
</Callout>

## When a product is a hazardous material

49 CFR 171.8 defines a hazardous material as "a substance or material that the Secretary of Transportation has determined is capable of posing an unreasonable risk" to health, safety and property in commerce. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-01" /> The class of a hazardous material "is indicated by either the class or division number or the class name," set out in the table in 173.2. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-02" />

| Class | Division | Name |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.1–1.6 | Explosives (six divisions) |
| 2 | 2.1 | Flammable gas |
| 2 | 2.2 | Non-flammable compressed gas |
| 2 | 2.3 | Poisonous gas |
| 3 | — | Flammable and combustible liquid |
| 4 | 4.1 / 4.2 / 4.3 | Flammable solid / Spontaneously combustible / Dangerous when wet |
| 5 | 5.1 / 5.2 | Oxidizer / Organic peroxide |
| 6 | 6.1 / 6.2 | Poisonous materials / Infectious substance |
| 7 | — | Radioactive material |
| 8 | — | Corrosive material |
| 9 | — | Miscellaneous hazardous material |

*Source: Office of the Federal Register (eCFR), 49 CFR 173.2, Table 1, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-03" />

Three definitions in 171.8 come up constantly for consumer goods:

- **Aerosol:** "an article consisting of a non-refillable receptacle containing a gas," as further specified in the definition. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-04" />
- **Limited quantity:** "the maximum amount of a hazardous material for which there is a specific labeling or packaging exception." <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-05" />
- **Consumer commodity:** a material "packaged and distributed in a form intended or suitable for sale through retail sales agencies," as further specified in the definition. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-06" />

## Who is responsible

Under 49 CFR 171.2(b), "each person who offers a hazardous material for transportation in commerce must comply" with the applicable requirements, and each offeror is responsible for the pre-transportation functions it performs. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-07" /> If you print the label and seal the box, you are the offeror for that package, even if a 3PL or carrier moves it afterwards. A business that uses at least one person who performs these functions is a "hazmat employer" under 171.8. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-08" /> A "hazmat employee" under the same section includes a person employed on a full-time, part-time or temporary basis by a hazmat employer, subject to the further conditions in that definition. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-11" />

## Lithium batteries: 49 CFR 173.185

Two requirements apply to every lithium cell or battery, whether shipped alone, packed with equipment, or installed in a device:

1. **UN 38.3 testing.** Each lithium cell or battery "must be of the type proven to meet the criteria in part III, sub-section 38.3" of the UN Manual of Tests and Criteria. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-09" />
2. **Test summary.** For cells and batteries manufactured on or after January 1, 2008, the manufacturer and any subsequent distributor must make a test summary available, with an exception for button cells installed in equipment. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-10" /> A reseller is a subsequent distributor, so ask your supplier for the summary before the first order ships.

The small-battery exception in 173.185(c) relaxes packaging and documentation requirements for batteries under set size limits:

- The Watt-hour rating "may not exceed 20 Wh for a lithium ion cell or 100 Wh for a lithium ion battery." <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-12" />
- For lithium metal, the lithium content "may not exceed 1 g for a lithium metal cell or 2 g for a lithium metal battery." <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-13" />
- Except where batteries are packed with or contained in equipment, each package "must not exceed 30 kg (66 pounds) gross weight." <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-14" />
- Each package "must display the lithium battery mark," subject to the exceptions listed in the paragraph. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-15" />

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical inputs)">
A power bank is rated 3.7 V and 20,000 mAh. Watt-hours are volts times amp-hours: 3.7 × 20 = 74 Wh. That is under the 100 Wh battery limit in 173.185(c), so the small-battery provisions can be considered. A 3.7 V, 30,000 mAh unit would be 111 Wh and over the limit.
</Callout>

## Limited quantities: flammable liquids and aerosols

Limited quantity exceptions are material-specific. Under 173.156(a), they are "permitted only if this section is referenced for the specific hazardous material in the § 172.101 Table." <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-16" /> Look up the product's UN number in the Hazardous Materials Table first.

### Flammable liquids (173.150(b))

Inner packagings go in a strong outer packaging, and each package "may not exceed 30 kg (66 pounds) gross weight." <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-18" /> Limited quantities under this paragraph "are excepted from labeling requirements, unless the material is offered for transportation or transported by aircraft," and a conforming package is not subject to the shipping paper requirements of subpart C of part 172, subject to the stated conditions. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-19" />

A flammable liquid with a flash point at or above 38 °C (100 °F) that meets no other hazard class may be reclassed as a combustible liquid, and a combustible liquid in non-bulk packaging is largely outside the regulations, with exceptions for hazardous substances, hazardous wastes and marine pollutants. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-20" />

### Aerosols (173.306)

For limited quantity aerosols, "the capacity of the container must not exceed 1 L (61.0 cubic inches)," <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-21" /> and each container, after filling, "must be subjected to a test performed in a hot water bath," or an allowed alternative. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-22" /> Limited quantities of compressed gases are "excepted from labeling, except when offered for transportation or transported by air." <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-23" />

### Package marking and weight

Under 173.156(b), limited quantity packages carry the marking required by 172.315, and the 30 kg (66 pounds) gross weight limit applies, except for marked packages moved by highway or rail between a manufacturer, distribution center and retail outlet under the stated conditions. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-17" />

## Training: 49 CFR 172.702 and 172.704

"A hazmat employer shall ensure that each of its hazmat employees is trained." <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-24" /> The rules in 172.704 set the content, timing and records:

<Flow title="Initial hazmat training under 49 CFR 172.704(c)(1)" claim="hazmat-dangerous-goods-26" steps={[
  {t: "New hire or changed job function", d: "Hazmat duties start with a new or changed role"},
  {t: "Supervised work only", d: "Under direct supervision of a properly trained hazmat employee"},
  {t: "Training completed", d: "Within 90 days after employment or change in job function"}
]} />

- **Content.** Training covers general awareness/familiarization, function-specific, safety, security awareness, and in-depth security training where a security plan is required. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-25" />
- **Timing.** Initial training must be completed within 90 days after employment or a change in job function, with work in the meantime only under direct supervision. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-26" /> Recurrent training is required "at least once every three years." <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-27" />
- **Records.** The employer keeps a record of current training for the preceding three years "for as long as that employee is employed by that employer as a hazmat employee and for 90 days thereafter," including the employee's name, completion date, materials, trainer, and a certification. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-28" />

## What to check yourself

1. Read 49 CFR 173.2 on eCFR and locate your product's class and division.
2. Look up the proper shipping name and UN number in the 172.101 Hazardous Materials Table and note which exception sections it references.
3. Read 49 CFR 173.185 in full for lithium products, including the paragraph on batteries packed with or contained in equipment.
4. Read the class-specific limited quantity section (173.150 for flammable liquids, 173.306 for aerosols) and 173.156 for marking and weight.
5. Read 49 CFR 172.704 and compare your training records with paragraph (d).
