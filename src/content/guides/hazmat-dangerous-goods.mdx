Most sellers first meet this subject when a listing they thought was ordinary gets flagged. Headphones. A cordless tool. A toy with a light in it. The classification is not about how dangerous the product feels; it is about what is inside it and how it behaves in a warehouse or an aircraft hold.

## What actually triggers it

Reporting describes the net as much wider than most sellers assume: **anything containing a lithium-ion or lithium-metal cell, anything pressurised, anything flammable, anything chemically reactive, and anything strongly magnetic** — even when the product itself feels mundane.

Named examples across sources:

| Group | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Lithium battery products | Laptops, cameras, phones, tablets, chargers, power banks, cordless tools, smart-home devices, e-readers, drones, smartwatches, battery-powered toys |
| Magnetised materials | Speakers, headphones, light meters, some children's toys |
| Non-harmful chemicals | Glue and adhesives, spray deodorants, hairspray, hair dye, pest control products |
| Aerosols and pressurised | Sprays of most kinds |
| Reactive or flammable | Cleaning chemicals, perfumes, hand sanitiser |

**Lithium batteries are described as the largest single group**, and the reason catches people: anything containing a lithium cell falls into Class 9 dangerous goods, and that captures most modern consumer electronics — including products where the battery is integrated and non-removable.

## What a review costs you

This is the part that makes it urgent rather than administrative.

Once an ASIN enters the review queue:

- **You cannot send new inventory** of that product to FBA
- **Stock already at a fulfilment centre may be placed on hold**
- Reported review times: **2 to 5 business days** with complete documentation in one account, **3 to 5** in another
- Incomplete safety data sheets, lithium battery verification problems or high queue volumes are described as extending it significantly

Think about that against a fast-moving SKU. A week of blocked inbound plus held stock is a stockout, which means lost rank — see the [listing guide](/guides/listing-optimisation-a10/) on how ranking decays before the stockout even happens — plus the [low-inventory-level fee](/guides/low-inventory-level-fee/) on whatever does sell.

**The review is not the cost. The block is.**

## The three outcomes

Reporting describes three possible results:

**Not a dangerous good.** Cleared for standard FBA, no special handling. Described as where most flagged products end up once an exemption sheet or SDS confirms there is no real hazard.

**Restricted dangerous.** It is hazmat, and Amazon will accept it through FBA provided you are enrolled in the FBA Dangerous Goods programme and the product meets storage and shipping requirements.

**Not accepted.** Some classes are sellable through FBA only in limited quantities, and some not at all.

## The two documents

**Safety Data Sheet (SDS).** A 16-section document prepared by the manufacturer describing chemical properties, hazards and handling requirements. Reported requirements: GHS-compliant, dated within five years, in the language of the target marketplace.

**Exemption sheet.** A simpler declaration, and the rule about it is strict. Reporting states it is accepted for **only two categories**:

1. **Battery and battery-powered products**
2. **Products confirmed to contain no harmful chemicals**

Submitting an exemption sheet for anything outside those two is reported to be rejected. That is a wasted cycle of several business days on a blocked ASIN.

**Why documents get rejected.** Reporting is specific that many delays occur not because a document is missing but because it **does not match Amazon's formatting or hazard disclosure standards.** The frustration sellers describe is rejected documentation rather than absent documentation.

## The lithium battery specifics

Reported requirements for battery products, and the level of detail matters:

- **Watt-hours (Wh)** are required for all lithium battery products. The calculation is `Wh = Voltage (V) × Capacity (Ah)`
- **Lithium content disclosure**
- **Alignment with UN 38.3 transport testing** — the test summary is commonly requested
- **Battery weight** — the total weight of all batteries included, not the weight of the device. Reporting notes the current exemption sheet may not include a weight field, but the data remains useful documentation
- **Integrated, non-removable batteries must still be correctly declared**

Reporting describes battery products frequently entering review because the battery information was incomplete at listing creation — which is an avoidable cause.

## The April 2026 change

Reporting states that **from 30 April 2026, all FBA-eligible dangerous goods qualify for Amazon's Partnered Carrier programme**, removing the need to arrange separate hazmat carriers.

That is a meaningful operational simplification for anyone who has been booking specialist carriers. It also interacts with the [inbound placement arithmetic](/guides/inbound-placement-split-arithmetic/), since partnered carrier changes both your freight rate and your placement treatment.

## Importing dangerous goods

Documentation applies at two levels — Amazon's internal requirements, and international transport and customs regulations. Reported requirements for the second:

- GHS-compliant SDS
- UN-certified packaging certification
- IMO or IATA dangerous goods declarations, with air declarations completed by a certified person
- Labels showing hazard class, UN number and packing group
- An accurate commercial invoice with correct HTS codes — see the [classification guide](/guides/hts-classification-basics/)
- Quantity limits under IATA rules for air freight

## What to do

**Check classification before sourcing.** Seller Central shows ASIN classifications, and the Manage Dangerous Goods Classification dashboard is where documents are uploaded and templates downloaded.

**Build SDS requirements into supplier negotiations.** Reporting recommends this specifically for private label: costing documentation in from the start is more efficient than scrambling after a flag. A supplier who cannot produce a GHS-compliant SDS is a supplier who will eventually block your ASIN.

**Get battery specifications at listing creation.** Watt-hours, voltage, capacity, lithium content, UN 38.3 summary. Reporting names incomplete battery information at listing creation as a common cause of review.

**Use the right document.** Exemption sheet only for the two permitted categories. Anything else needs an SDS.

**Complete it fully and check the formatting.** Rejections are described as more often about format and hazard disclosure than about absence.

**Remember it is marketplace-specific.** Dangerous goods approval in one marketplace does not carry to others.

**Keep classification stable.** Temporary clearance followed by re-flagging weeks later is described as what happens when the underlying classification signal was never addressed.
