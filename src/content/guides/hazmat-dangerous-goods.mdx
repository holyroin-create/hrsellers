A consumer product is hazmat for shipping when it contains a substance the Department of Transportation has determined can pose an unreasonable risk — and the legal duty to identify that sits with you as the shipper. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-01"/> <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-02"/> For marketplace sellers, the usual triggers are lithium batteries, aerosols, flammable liquids such as perfume or nail polish, and corrosive cleaners. Being sold in a retail store does not take a product out of the rules: 49 CFR defines a "consumer commodity" as a material packaged and distributed in a form intended or suitable for retail sale for personal care or household use. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-09"/>

## The shipper's job: class and describe

Before a hazardous material moves, the person offering it must class and describe it in accordance with 49 CFR parts 172 and 173. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-02"/> If you hand inventory to a carrier for delivery to a fulfillment center or third-party warehouse, plan on being treated as the person offering it. Your supplier's safety data sheet is the usual starting point, but the classification decision under these parts is yours.

## The nine hazard classes, with seller examples

Table 1 to 49 CFR 173.2 sets out the classes and divisions. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-03"/> These are the ones most likely to touch a consumer-goods catalog:

| Class / division | Name in 49 CFR 173.2 | Where sellers meet it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explosives | Rare outside fireworks and novelty items |
| 4.1 | Flammable solid | Some fire starters and matches |
| 2.1 / 2.2 | Flammable gas / non-flammable compressed gas | Aerosol sprays, gas canisters |
| 3 | Flammable and combustible liquid | Fragrances, nail polish, some adhesives |
| 5.1 | Oxidizer | Some pool and cleaning chemicals |
| 8 | Corrosive material | Drain cleaners, some batteries' electrolytes |
| 9 | Miscellaneous hazardous material | Lithium batteries and other items without a better fit |

Classes 5.2 (organic peroxide), 6 (poisonous materials and infectious substances) and 7 (radioactive material) complete the table. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-37"/> <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-38"/> <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-39"/>

## Lithium batteries: the rules that catch most sellers

Lithium batteries show up in far more listings than people expect: earbuds, phone cases with power banks, LED toys, electric toothbrushes, smart watches. The core rule in 49 CFR 173.185 is that every lithium cell and battery must be of a type proven to meet the criteria in part III, sub-section 38.3 of the UN Manual of Tests and Criteria. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-21"/> For cells and batteries manufactured on or after January 1, 2008, manufacturers and subsequent distributors must make a test summary available (button cells installed in equipment are excepted). <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-22"/> If your supplier cannot produce a UN 38.3 test summary for the exact cell or battery, stop and get one before booking freight.

Qualifying packages still have conditions. Each must display the lithium battery mark, subject to listed exceptions. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-25"/> Unless the batteries are packed with or contained in equipment, each package must not exceed 30 kg (66 pounds) gross weight. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-26"/> Unless they are contained in equipment, the package (or the completed package when packed with equipment) must withstand a 1.2 meter drop test in any orientation. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-27"/>

Batteries do not escape the rules by being installed. Paragraph (b) of 173.185 sets requirements for lithium cells or batteries packed with, or contained in, equipment. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-28"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): is this power bank under 100 Wh?">
Inputs: a power bank label reads 3.7 V and 20,000 mAh. Watt-hours = volts × amp-hours = 3.7 × 20 = 74 Wh. That is under the 100 Wh battery limit for the lithium ion small-battery exception. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-23"/> A 3.7 V, 30,000 mAh unit works out to 111 Wh and would be over it. The battery still needs its UN 38.3 test summary either way. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-21"/>

## Limited quantities: smaller containers, fewer requirements

A limited quantity is the maximum amount of a hazardous material for which there is a specific labeling or packaging exception. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-10"/> The exception is not automatic: 49 CFR 173.156 applies only when it is referenced for the specific material in the 172.101 Hazardous Materials Table or a packaging section. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-17"/>

Flammable liquids (Class 3) show how it works. Limited quantities are excepted from labeling requirements unless offered for transport, or transported, by aircraft, <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-11"/> and are not subject to the placarding requirements of subpart F. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-15"/> The inner packaging limits depend on packing group:

A completed limited quantity package of flammable liquids may not exceed 30 kg (66 pounds) gross weight. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-16"/>

### The limited quantity mark

The mark is a square-on-point whose top and bottom portions and border are black. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-18"/> Each side must be at least 100 mm, with a reduced size of no less than 50 mm permitted where the package is too small. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-19"/> For air shipments, a black "Y" sits in the center of the mark. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-20"/>

## Training: who needs it and how often

If you employ or use even one hazmat employee, full-time, part-time or temporary, and you transport hazardous materials or cause them to be transported in commerce, you are a hazmat employer. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-29"/> In a small seller operation, that can be the person who packs battery products or prints shipping labels for them.

## What a violation can cost

Civil penalties for knowing violations are set in 49 CFR 107.329:

| Situation | Amount |
|---|---|
| Maximum per knowing violation | $102,348 |
| Maximum if the violation results in death, serious illness or severe injury | $238,809 |
| Minimum for violations relating to training | $617 |

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What the rule says |
|---|---|
| Assuming a battery inside the device is exempt | 173.185(b) covers cells packed with or contained in equipment |
| Shipping without a UN 38.3 test summary | Cells and batteries must be a tested type, and distributors must make the summary available |
| Using limited quantity for any small bottle | The exception applies only where the 172.101 Table references it |
| Letting untrained staff pack battery orders indefinitely | Training must be completed within 90 days |

## How to confirm it for your product

Read 49 CFR 173.185 for lithium batteries, 173.2 for the hazard classes, 173.150 and 173.156 for limited quantities, and 172.704 for training on eCFR. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-21"/> <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-03"/> Then look up your exact material in the 172.101 Hazardous Materials Table, which determines whether an exception is available at all. <C id="hazmat-dangerous-goods-17"/>
