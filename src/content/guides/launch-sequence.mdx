A launch on Amazon is mostly a sequencing problem. Several of the benefits Amazon lists for new sellers only apply if you act within 90 days of listing, and one ad credit expires after 30 days. <C id="launch-sequence-12"/> <C id="launch-sequence-16"/> <C id="launch-sequence-17"/> The slow items — identity verification and, for brands, a trademark filing — have to come before the clock starts. This guide puts Amazon's published steps and incentive conditions in the order that keeps those windows open.

## The decision: when does your 90-day clock start?

Amazon's beginner guide lists six steps: get ready to sell, list products, price products, select your fulfillment method, promote and advertise, and get product reviews. <C id="launch-sequence-01"/> It does not tell you how fast to move between them. The incentive rules do. Most of the New Seller Incentives on Amazon's New Seller Guide page are tied to an action "within 90 days of listing," and automatic enrollment in the FBA New Selection program requires that you "create an FBA shipment within 90 days of listing your first buyable product." <C id="launch-sequence-16"/> <C id="launch-sequence-12"/>

That means listing a product before the inventory, the ad plan and the brand paperwork are ready burns part of the window. The practical rule: finish everything that does not depend on the listing first, then list, then ship and advertise in quick succession.

## Stage 1: account and paperwork (before day 0)

Amazon says registration can often be completed "in just a few hours," but the identity check that follows "usually takes three business days or less." <C id="launch-sequence-02"/> <C id="launch-sequence-03"/> The registration guide asks for proof of residential business address "dated from the last 180 days, like a bank or credit card statement," and the verification step may involve joining or scheduling a video call with an Amazon associate. <C id="launch-sequence-04"/> <C id="launch-sequence-06"/> If your most recent statement is older than that, get a new one before you begin.

Two other items belong here because they take the longest outside Amazon:

- **Product codes.** Listings need a product identifier; Amazon notes that "the most common GTIN used by Amazon sellers is a UPC." <C id="launch-sequence-05"/> Our guide on [GS1 barcodes and FNSKU labels](/guides/gs1-barcodes-fnsku/) covers where codes come from.
- **A trademark filing, if you sell a brand.** Brand Registry requires "a pending or registered trademark for your brand name or logo." <C id="launch-sequence-20"/> Pending is enough to enroll, so the filing date, not the registration date, is what gates your brand steps. The [Brand Registry and trademark guide](/guides/brand-registry-trademark/) explains the filing side.

A Professional selling account, which the New Seller Guide's action list is written for, costs $39.99 per month plus selling fees. <C id="launch-sequence-07"/>

## Stage 2: product choice and pricing (before day 0)

Product research also sits before the listing, because changing your mind afterward wastes window days. Amazon's Product Opportunity Explorer, found in Seller Central under Growth, shows "search terms and volume, customer reviews, and return activity." <C id="launch-sequence-26"/> <C id="launch-sequence-27"/> The return data is worth a look before committing to a product; our guide to [the cost lines new sellers miss](/guides/product-research-cost-errors/) shows why returns and storage belong in the margin from the start.

For pricing, Amazon's New Seller Guide tells Professional sellers to set dynamic prices using Automate Pricing, which Amazon describes as a free tool that adjusts prices automatically. <C id="launch-sequence-09"/> <C id="launch-sequence-28"/> Set your floor price before you turn it on, using a full cost model rather than the referral fee alone.

## Stage 3: the first 90 days, in order

Amazon claims that sellers who use the New Seller Guide in their first 90 days generate "approximately 6x more first-year sales on average." <C id="launch-sequence-08"/> That figure is Amazon's own; the more useful part is the guide's action list, which shows what Amazon asks a new seller to do. For Professional sellers it covers Automate Pricing, Sponsored Products ads, FBA enrollment and an inventory shipment, Multi-Channel Fulfillment orders, Lightning or 7-Day Deals, and Amazon Coupons. <C id="launch-sequence-09"/> Brand owners add Brand Registry, Vine, Sponsored Brands ads and A+ Content. <C id="launch-sequence-10"/>

The inbound credit row is backed by a second line in the same table: the $100 Partnered Carrier discount. <C id="launch-sequence-15"/> And the Vine step has prerequisites of its own: a Professional selling account, a Brand Representative or Reseller role for a brand enrolled in Brand Registry (or generic products in your catalog), and eligible FBA offers. <C id="launch-sequence-23"/> <C id="launch-sequence-22"/> The $200 Vine credit is listed under the Brand Registry incentives. <C id="launch-sequence-24"/>

## The incentives and the conditions attached

Amazon's New Seller Guide page (sell.amazon.com/grow) advertises "over $50,000 in New Seller Incentives." <C id="launch-sequence-11"/> Almost all of that headline figure comes from one item: the Brand Registry cash-back of 10% on the first $50,000 in branded sales, then 5% through the first year up to $1,000,000. <C id="launch-sequence-19"/> A seller without a brand is left with the smaller, fixed credits. The table below is what the /grow page lists on the date checked; confirm the amounts that apply to your account in Seller Central rather than treating them as a standing offer.

| Incentive | Amount | Condition or limit |
|---|---|---|
| Brand Registry cash-back | 10% of first $50,000 branded sales, then 5% | First year, up to $1,000,000 |
| Inbound placement credit | $400 | Eligible products sent within 90 days of listing; expires after one year |
| Partnered Carrier discount | $100 | Eligible products sent via the program within 90 days of listing |
| Vine credit | $200 | Listed for brands enrolled in Brand Registry |
| Sponsored Products or Brands credit | $50 | Campaign within 90 days of listing; expires after 30 days |
| Coupon credit | $50 | Coupon for an eligible product within 90 days of listing |
| Low-inventory-level charges | Exemption | Expires one year after FBA inventory is first received |

## Common sequencing mistakes

| Mistake | Why it costs you |
|---|---|
| Listing weeks before inventory exists | The 90-day incentive windows run from listing, not from first sale. <C id="launch-sequence-16"/> |
| Turning on the ad credit with no campaign plan | The $50 credit expires after 30 days. <C id="launch-sequence-17"/> |
| Waiting to file a trademark until sales start | Brand Registry needs at least a pending trademark, and the brand steps depend on it. <C id="launch-sequence-20"/> |
| Starting registration with an old bank statement | Proof of address must be dated within the last 180 days. <C id="launch-sequence-04"/> |
| Enrolling an existing product in Vine after reviews pile up | Enrollment is open to products with fewer than 30 reviews. <C id="launch-sequence-21"/> |

Reviews obtained outside programs like Vine carry their own risks; see [review manipulation risk](/guides/review-manipulation-risk/) before planning any review outreach.

## How to confirm the current terms

Incentive amounts and conditions are set on Amazon's New Seller Guide page at sell.amazon.com/grow, and the New Selection rules on sell.amazon.com/fulfillment-by-amazon. <C id="launch-sequence-11"/> <C id="launch-sequence-12"/> Recheck both on the day you list; eligibility for each credit is shown in your Seller Central account. Registration requirements are on sell.amazon.com/sell/registration-guide, and Vine terms on sell.amazon.com/programs/vine. This page describes Amazon's published program terms; whether a specific product qualifies for a credit is decided by Amazon in your account, not by this summary.
