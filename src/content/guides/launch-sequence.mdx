Launch guidance usually runs in the order the tasks feel natural: find a product, find a supplier, make a listing, run ads. That order maximizes the chance of discovering a blocker after you have paid for inventory.

This is the same work ordered by **who controls the clock.**

## The lead times you do not control

Before anything else, the durations that decide the sequence:

| Step | Reported lead time |
| --- | --- |
| Trademark to full registration | 8–14 months (weeks via the pending-application route) |
| Third-party product testing | Weeks, per packaging design and standard |
| VAT registration where required | Weeks to months, per country |
| Ungating approval | Days to weeks, plus invoice age requirements |
| Dangerous goods review | 2–5 business days, with inbound blocked |
| GS1 GTINs | Immediate |
| Brand Registry enrollment | About two weeks once the trademark checks out |

**Everything above the last two lines is a reason to start earlier than feels necessary.**

## Phase 1 — Decide, before spending

**1. Validate cost, not just demand.** The cost side is knowable from published schedules; demand is a forecast. Spending two weeks on search volume and estimating the fee stack is the wrong way round. → [Product research](/guides/product-research-cost-errors/)

**2. Get packaged dimensions from the supplier.** They set the size tier, the fulfillment fee and every layer of the storage bill, permanently. → [Storage](/guides/fba-storage-four-layers/)

**3. Check the price against the bands.** Under $10, $10–$50, above $50. A product that only works at a price sitting just above a boundary is fragile. → [Price bands](/guides/price-tier-repricing-trap/)

**4. Check the gates.** Category, brand and ASIN. Sourcing inventory for a gated product and then seeking approval is the expensive order. → [Ungating](/guides/ungating-restricted-categories/)

**5. Check for dangerous goods.** Anything with a lithium cell, pressurised, flammable, reactive or strongly magnetic. → [Hazmat](/guides/hazmat-dangerous-goods/)

**6. Build landed cost, not invoice price.** Duty, user fees, broker, bond, prep. → [Landed cost](/guides/landed-cost-fee-stack/)

## Phase 2 — Start the slow clocks

Run these in parallel with everything below.

**7. File the trademark** if you are building a brand. Eight to fourteen months, or weeks to Brand Registry access via a pending application. → [Brand Registry](/guides/brand-registry-trademark/)

**8. Buy GS1 GTINs** — one per variation. Directly from GS1; resold codes fail verification. → [Barcodes](/guides/gs1-barcodes-fnsku/)

**9. Commission product testing** where required. CPC or GCC, from an accepted laboratory. Since July 8, 2026 the certificate is also filed electronically at import. → [Compliance](/guides/us-product-compliance/)

**10. Sort insurance** if you expect to cross the threshold — at $10,000 in sales across three consecutive months. → [Insurance](/guides/seller-insurance-coi/)

## Phase 3 — Supplier, and the documents

**11. Verify the supplier before paying.** Business license at the source registry, scope of business, bank account matching the licensed entity. → [Supplier verification](/guides/supplier-verification/)

**12. Get a sample invoice and check the fields.** This is the step almost nobody does, and it decides six later outcomes. → [The supplier invoice](/guides/the-supplier-invoice/)

**13. Specify permanent branding.** Printed, molded, embroidered or engraved. **Stickers are the most common Brand Registry rejection cause** — and fixing it means another production run. → [Brand Registry](/guides/brand-registry-trademark/)

**14. Agree the Incoterm, the named place and the version, in writing.** And confirm whose name goes on the customs entry. → [Incoterms](/guides/incoterms-who-is-importer/)

**15. Arrange prep.** Amazon stopped doing it on January 1, 2026. Supplier, prep center or in-house — decide before the goods ship. → [Prep](/guides/amazon-stopped-prepping/)

## Phase 4 — Import

**16. Classify the product.** Ten digits, documented reasoning. The importer of record carries the liability and cannot delegate it. → [HTS classification](/guides/hts-classification-basics/)

**17. Sort the entry type and the bond.** Type 11 informal or Type 01 formal. → [Entry types](/guides/entry-type-11-vs-01/)

**18. Budget for an examination.** At $150 to $2,500 and beyond, before demurrage, and the importer pays. → [Customs holds](/guides/customs-holds-and-exams/)

## Phase 5 — Listing, before advertising

**19. Build the listing for conversion.** Conversion is consistently described as the dominant ranking signal, and it is the half you control. → [Listing optimization](/guides/listing-optimisation-a10/)

**20. Fill every field.** Backend search terms within 249 bytes, Item Highlights, attributes. Free indexing, routinely left empty.

**21. Deploy A+ Content** once Brand Registry is live. Reported as the fastest conversion impact among the registry tools. → [Brand tools](/guides/aplus-content-brand-tools/)

**22. Decide on Vine — carefully.** Once per ASIN, for its lifetime. Enrolling before the product and listing are finished buys permanent, detailed criticism. → [Vine](/guides/vine-and-early-reviews/)

## Phase 6 — Then advertise

**23. Calculate break-even ACOS per SKU**, after all fees. Not per catalog. → [PPC benchmarks](/guides/amazon-ppc-benchmarks/)

**24. Start with Sponsored Products.** the other formats as amplifying a working funnel rather than fixing a broken one.

**25. Mine search-term reports for organic terms.** Paid discovers, organic keeps.

## Phase 7 — The things that decide whether it survives

**26. Set a reorder trigger** covering supplier lead time, transit, customs and Amazon receiving — because the low-inventory fee is charged on sales made while thin. → [Low inventory](/guides/low-inventory-level-fee/)

**27. Model cash on delivery date.** DD+7 means order-to-bank of 14 to 27 days for FBA. → [DD+7](/guides/dd7-payout-reserve/)

**28. Watch ODR daily.** One metric can suspend an account on its own, and at low order volume a single defect is a large percentage. → [Order defect rate](/guides/order-defect-rate/)

**29. Diarize the 150-day inventory review.** The aged-inventory surcharge starts at day 181, and a removal order takes time. → [Removal decision](/guides/removal-vs-hold-decision/)

**30. Read return reason codes monthly.** They separate an expectation gap from a product problem, and they are the specification for your next listing revision. → [Returns](/guides/returns-cost-and-badge/)

## The five decisions that are hard to reverse

Worth isolating, because everything else can be fixed later:

1. **Packaging dimensions** — set the size tier and every storage layer, permanently
2. **Permanent branding** — a production decision, not a listing one
3. **Vine enrollment** — once per ASIN, for its lifetime
4. **The supplier you can or cannot get documents from** — decides six later systems
5. **The trademark name** — must match everywhere, exactly, including punctuation

**Everything on that list costs a production run or a lifetime to change.** Everything else is an afternoon.
