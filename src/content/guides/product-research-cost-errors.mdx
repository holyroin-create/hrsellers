Most product-research errors are not about demand; they are about cost lines that never made it into the spreadsheet. A typical first model takes the sale price, subtracts the landed cost and the referral percentage, and calls the rest margin. Amazon's own published fee pages add at least six more lines: the referral fee's wider base, the plan fee, FBA fulfillment, monthly storage, aged inventory, and returns processing. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-02"/> <C id="product-research-cost-errors-05"/> <C id="product-research-cost-errors-07"/> <C id="product-research-cost-errors-08"/> <C id="product-research-cost-errors-09"/> <C id="product-research-cost-errors-10"/> This page lists them and shows, with a hypothetical example, how much they move the result.

## Error 1: applying the referral rate to the item price only

Amazon charges "a percentage of the total price or a minimum amount, whichever is greater." <C id="product-research-cost-errors-01"/> The total price "includes its list price, as well as shipping costs and any gift-wrapping charges." <C id="product-research-cost-errors-02"/> If you fulfill orders yourself and charge shipping, the percentage applies to that shipping line as well. In Home and Kitchen, for example, the rate is 15% with a $0.30 minimum. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-03"/>

The category matters more than many researchers assume. Pet Supplies is 15%, but veterinary diets are 22%. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-23"/> Our [referral fee guide](/guides/amazon-referral-fees/) covers the category tiers and the price cliffs in detail; for research purposes, the rule is to look up the fee category for the exact product and apply the rate to the full price the buyer pays.

## Error 2: forgetting the plan fee and the closing fee

The Individual plan costs $0.99 per item sold; the Professional plan costs $39.99 per month plus selling fees. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-04"/> <C id="product-research-cost-errors-05"/> On Professional, the monthly fee has to be spread over your expected unit volume — small at 500 units a month, noticeable at 50. Media items also pay a $1.80 closing fee per item on top of the referral fee. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-06"/>

## Error 3: treating FBA as one fee

Amazon describes FBA as "a pay-as-you-go service. You pay for storage and fulfillment for each unit." <C id="product-research-cost-errors-22"/> Those are two different lines that behave differently:

- **Fulfillment costs** per unit include "picking and packing your orders, shipping and handling, customer service, and product returns," and are based on the product's weight and dimensions. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-07"/> A product that ships in an oversized box pays for the box, not just the item.
- **Storage costs** are charged monthly, based on the daily average volume in cubic feet that your inventory occupies in Amazon fulfillment centers. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-08"/> They grow with the number of units you send and the time they sit, so a cautious order quantity is a cost decision, not only a cash decision.

Amazon also lists an inbound placement service; for standard and large bulky products its cost reflects distributing inventory to fulfillment centers close to customers. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-12"/> Amazon's New Seller Guide page lists a $400 credit toward inbound placement costs for eligible products sent within 90 days of listing, and the credit expires after one year. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-20"/>

The exact fulfillment and storage rates are published by Amazon in its FBA fee schedule; Amazon's Revenue Calculator applies them to a specific product when you enter its dimensions, weight, category, price and shipping charges. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-19"/> Use that output as an input to your model rather than guessing.

## Error 4: assuming slow stock only costs storage

Two charges hit inventory that does not sell as planned:

- **Aged inventory** is "charged monthly for all items stored in a fulfillment center for more than 181 days." <C id="product-research-cost-errors-09"/>
- **Removal, disposal and liquidation** carry a per-item charge when you have Amazon return, dispose of or liquidate stock. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-11"/>

So an over-ordered product costs storage for six months, then storage plus the aged charge, then a per-item fee to get rid of it. New sellers get some relief: Amazon's New Seller Guide page lists exemptions from low-inventory-level charges and storage utilization surcharges that expire one year after FBA inventory is first received, and the New Selection program offers free monthly storage, rebates and waivers. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-13"/> <C id="product-research-cost-errors-21"/> Model the product without those benefits too, because they end.

## Error 5: leaving returns out of the margin

Returns processing is "charged on orders when Amazon provides a customer with free return shipping." <C id="product-research-cost-errors-10"/> That is in addition to the cost of the returned unit if it cannot be resold. Product Opportunity Explorer analyzes trends in pricing, reviews and returns, so the return pattern of a niche is visible before you commit. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-18"/> A category where shoppers return often needs a return-rate line in the model, not a footnote.

## Error 6: launch programs priced at zero

Getting a new product seen is not free. Amazon Ads offers cost-per-click options, so the ad cost per unit is the click cost divided by the share of clicks that convert. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-14"/> Vine, used to get early reviews, carries an enrollment cost per parent product: $0 for up to 2 units, $75 for 3–10 units and $200 for 11–30 units. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-15"/> Amazon does not charge it until 30 days after enrollment and after the first review is published. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-16"/> If you plan to use Transparency, enrollment is free, but you pay for the codes you generate. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-17"/> The launch sequence and the credits that offset some of these costs are covered in [the Amazon launch sequence guide](/guides/launch-sequence/).

## Worked example: the same product, two models

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: a Home and Kitchen item sold via FBA at $24.99; landed cost $6.00 per unit; FBA fulfillment fee $5.00 per unit (assumed — take the real figure from Amazon's Revenue Calculator); storage $0.10 per unit per month for 3 months; Professional plan $39.99 spread over 100 units a month ($0.40 per unit); ad spend $2.00 per unit sold; 5% of units returned and not resellable (5% × $6.00 = $0.30 per unit sold); returns processing ignored for simplicity. Referral fee: 15% × $24.99 = $3.75.
</Callout>

| Line | First model | Full model |

| Sale price | $24.99 | $24.99 |
| Landed cost | −$6.00 | −$6.00 |
| Referral fee (15%) | −$3.75 | −$3.75 |
| FBA fulfillment | — | −$5.00 |
| Storage (3 months) | — | −$0.30 |
| Plan fee per unit | — | −$0.40 |
| Advertising | — | −$2.00 |
| Lost returned units | — | −$0.30 |
| **Profit per unit** | **$15.24 (61%)** | **$7.24 (29%)** |

*Source: referral rate and plan fee from Amazon, sell.amazon.com/pricing, checked Nov 8, 2026; all other inputs are hypothetical and the arithmetic is ours.* <C id="product-research-cost-errors-03"/> <C id="product-research-cost-errors-05"/>

The product still makes money, but less than half of what the first model promised. If the stock sat past 181 days, the aged-inventory charge would come out of that $7.24 as well. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-09"/> Run your own numbers in our [margin calculator](/tools/margin-calculator/), using Amazon's Revenue Calculator output for the fulfillment fee.

## How to confirm the fees for your product

Referral rates, minimums and plan prices are on Amazon's pricing page at sell.amazon.com/pricing, and the FBA cost categories on sell.amazon.com/fulfillment-by-amazon. <C id="product-research-cost-errors-01"/> <C id="product-research-cost-errors-07"/> The current per-unit FBA rates are applied by the Revenue Calculator at sell.amazon.com/pricing/estimate, and the detailed fee schedule is published inside Seller Central — check it there before you order. Vine costs are on sell.amazon.com/programs/vine. This page covers published fee categories for research; the fees actually charged on your orders appear in your Seller Central reports, which are the record to reconcile against.
