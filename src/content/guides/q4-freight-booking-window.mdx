Book Q4 freight by working backward from the date your inventory must be available at Amazon, adding every leg's lead time and a buffer for slippage — and weigh that against the cost of arriving too early, because Amazon's standard-size FBA storage rises from $0.78 to $2.40 per cubic foot per month for October through December. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-01"/> <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-02"/> Amazon's public pages say demand can increase when the holidays roll around and advise optimizing stock for moments like Black Friday, but they do not post a single holiday inbound cutoff. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-12"/> <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-13"/> Any current Q4 receiving deadline and your restock limits are published inside Seller Central, so check them there before you fix dates.

## What fixes the window

Four constraints set the earliest and latest sensible booking dates.

The aging line is the long-range limit: stock received too far ahead of the season, and still unsold after 181 days, draws a monthly charge. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-11"/> Amazon also treats over 90 days of supply as excess inventory, while recommending about two months of supply on hand. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-14"/> <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-15"/> How to set the normal level is covered in [Demand forecasting and reorder points](/guides/demand-forecasting-reorder/).

Storage is charged monthly on the daily average volume your inventory occupies, in cubic feet. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-05"/> Additional storage costs apply to dangerous goods and to sellers holding a high volume of inventory relative to recent weekly sales. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-06"/>

## AWD as a buffer

Amazon lists Amazon Warehousing and Distribution (AWD) storage at $0.48 per cubic foot per month in both the January–September and October–December periods, and describes Ship to AWD pricing as pay-as-you-go with no seasonal surcharges. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-08"/> <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-07"/> If you leave auto-replenishment enabled, Amazon says it manages your replenishments so you don't have to worry about FBA capacity limits, and leaving the feature defaulted gets a waiver for FBA storage-utilization surcharges and low-inventory-level costs. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-09"/> Whatever warehouse you use, Amazon's own advice is that space should accommodate seasonal fluctuations in demand, like holidays. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-16"/>

The Restock Inventory report carries a Recommended ship date field, which is a useful sanity check against your own schedule. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-18"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs (all assumed): you want units available at FBA by November 1; you allow 14 days for receiving, 7 days for the domestic truck leg after customs, 30 days door-to-port by ocean and 14 days of factory buffer. Backward: delivery to the fulfillment center by October 18 → cleared at the U.S. port by October 11 → vessel departs September 11 → ISF filed by September 10 (24 hours before loading) → goods ready at the factory by August 28.

Storage trade-off for 400 cubic feet of standard-size goods: a month at the January–September rate costs 400 × $0.78 = $312; a month at the October–December rate costs 400 × $2.40 = $960. Landing in September costs $312 per month held before October, while every month held in Q4 costs $960 regardless of arrival date — so arriving earlier only adds the cheaper months, but the 181-day aging clock starts sooner.
</Callout>

## Split the order: base load and top-ups

One pattern is to bring the bulk of the season's units by ocean early enough to clear the restock-limit and receiving checks, then plan one or more smaller top-ups whose mode you choose late. Amazon's freight program gives a reference point for each option: Fast Ocean is described as faster than Standard Ocean to the US, and air as reaching the fulfillment network within 7–10 days. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-21"/> <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-20"/> The base load carries the most storage exposure, so it is the part to route through AWD if your FBA space is tight, since AWD storage does not step up for October–December in Amazon's comparison. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-08"/> The top-up is sized from live sales, not from the forecast you made in summer.

## Reading your position before you book the top-up

Amazon's FBA Manage Inventory Health report gives the data for this decision. It provides a consolidated view of your sales over 7, 30, 60 and 90 days alongside shipment statuses, and it covers estimated storage and long-term storage fees together with aged and excess units. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-25"/> <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-26"/> Two of its fields to check before any late booking are estimated-storage-cost-next-month and estimated-excess-quantity. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-27"/> <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-28"/>

Under 46 CFR Part 541, a demurrage or detention invoice must be issued within 30 calendar days of the date the charge was last incurred, and you get at least 30 calendar days from issuance to request mitigation, refund or waiver. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-23"/> <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-24"/> The full billing rules are in [Choosing a freight forwarder](/guides/choosing-a-freight-forwarder/).

## How to confirm this year's dates

Check the storage table on Amazon's FBA page and the AWD rates on its supply-chain page, then read Seller Central for this season's receiving deadlines and your restock limits — those are the dates your schedule must hit. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-02"/> <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-08"/> <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-10"/> The ISF deadline is in 19 CFR Part 149 on eCFR. <C id="q4-freight-booking-window-19"/> The schedule above is a planning method; your forwarder's written transit quote, not a published average, is what to plan against.
