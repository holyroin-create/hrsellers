If your product is covered by an antidumping (AD) or countervailing duty (CVD) order, you will generally pay a cash deposit of estimated duties when the goods enter, and the real bill is decided later. <C id="antidumping-duties-01"/> The U.S. system is retrospective: final AD/CVD liability is determined after the merchandise is imported. <C id="antidumping-duties-02"/> For a small importer, the two things that cause the most damage are not knowing that a product is in scope, and not knowing that the final amount can be higher than the deposit you paid years earlier.

## How an order hits your shipment

AD/CVD orders cover specific products from specific countries, and rates can differ by exporter, as the shrimp results below show. Before you place a purchase order, check the Federal Register for orders on that product and country.

## Annual reviews: the importer's lever

Reviews are requested each year during the anniversary month of the order's publication. <C id="antidumping-duties-04"/> In that same month, an importer may request in writing a review of an exporter or producer whose goods it imported, and an exporter may request a review of itself. <C id="antidumping-duties-05"/> <C id="antidumping-duties-06"/> An antidumping review normally covers entries during the 12 months before the most recent anniversary month. <C id="antidumping-duties-08"/> A requester may withdraw within 90 days of publication of the initiation notice. <C id="antidumping-duties-07"/>

Why request one? If your exporter's real dumping margin is lower than its deposit rate, a review can bring refunds. The risk runs both ways: the final rate can also come out higher. A new exporter that did not ship during the original investigation, and is not affiliated with one that did, may instead seek a new shipper review. <C id="antidumping-duties-11"/>

<Callout type="tip" title="Mark the anniversary month">
Find your order's anniversary month in its Federal Register notice and put it on your calendar. Deadlines after initiation are short: in Commerce's November 5, 2026 initiation notice, separate rate applications were due within 14 calendar days of publication. <C id="antidumping-duties-32"/>
</Callout>

## Scope: is your product covered?

Treat scope as a question about the product description in the order, not only about your HTS code. An interested party may submit a scope ruling application asking Commerce whether a product is covered. <C id="antidumping-duties-14"/> Commerce's primary interpretive sources include the descriptions of the merchandise in the petition. <C id="antidumping-duties-15"/> Once a scope inquiry begins, Commerce will direct CBP to continue suspending liquidation of entries already suspended. <C id="antidumping-duties-16"/>

### Third-country finishing and circumvention

On November 6, 2026, Commerce found that steel wire garment hangers completed in Cambodia from steel wire, or steel wire and paper accessories, produced in China or Vietnam, circumvent the AD order on hangers from China or the AD and CVD orders on hangers from Vietnam. <C id="antidumping-duties-28"/> Commerce will direct CBP to continue suspending liquidation and to require cash deposits on those hangers. <C id="antidumping-duties-29"/> The suspension reaches back to entries on or after August 12, 2025, when the inquiry began. <C id="antidumping-duties-30"/> Importers who fail the certification requirements may face a de facto determination that all covered entries are in scope. <C id="antidumping-duties-31"/> Note the gap between initiation and decision: entries made more than a year earlier were affected. Pair this with the [country of origin guide](/guides/country-of-origin-sourcing/) before moving production to avoid China-specific duties.

## The reimbursement certificate

Export price is reduced by any AD or CVD the exporter paid on the importer's behalf or reimbursed to the importer. <C id="antidumping-duties-17"/> You must certify with CBP before liquidation whether you have been reimbursed or agreed to be. <C id="antidumping-duties-18"/> If you do not file the certificate, Commerce may presume reimbursement. <C id="antidumping-duties-19"/> A supplier offering to "cover the duties" is offering something that can raise the margin applied to your own entries.

## Sunset reviews

Orders do not last forever by default. No later than once every five years, Commerce must determine whether dumping or countervailable subsidies would be likely to continue or resume if an order were revoked. <C id="antidumping-duties-12"/> A sunset review starts no later than 30 days before the fifth anniversary of the order. <C id="antidumping-duties-13"/>

## Pricing marketplace listings when AD/CVD applies

Because the final rate can move after you have already sold the goods, AD/CVD changes how you should price. A listing priced off the deposit rate alone assumes the review will not raise it, and that assumption can be wrong for entries you sold through long ago. <C id="antidumping-duties-02"/> One way to handle this is to set aside a reserve per unit until the entries liquidate, sized to the gap between the deposit rate and the highest rate in the latest final results for that order. In the shrimp example, that gap between the separate rate of 7.59 percent and the country-wide 25.76 percent is wide. <C id="antidumping-duties-23"/> <C id="antidumping-duties-24"/>

Supplier choice is the other lever. Two exporters in the same country can carry very different rates, so ask for the exporter's exact legal name and look it up in the most recent final results before you compare quotes. <C id="antidumping-duties-21"/> <C id="antidumping-duties-22"/> A slightly higher unit price from an exporter with a lower deposit rate can produce the lower landed cost.

Finally, keep your entry records organized by order and exporter. When final results publish, you will want to see quickly which entries are affected and whether you are owed money or owe more.

## How to confirm it for your product

Read 19 CFR Part 351, subpart B, on the eCFR for orders, assessment, reviews and scope, and 19 CFR 351.402(f) for the reimbursement certificate. Then search federalregister.gov for your product name together with "antidumping" or "countervailing" to find the order, the latest final results with exporter-specific rates, and any circumvention or scope notices.
