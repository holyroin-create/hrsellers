Antidumping (AD) and countervailing (CVD) duties are extra duties that apply only to a specific product from a specific country, under an order issued by the Department of Commerce. When an order is in place, Commerce instructs CBP to assess AD or CVD duties on the subject merchandise <C id="antidumping-duties-01" /> and to require cash deposits of estimated duties. <C id="antidumping-duties-02" /> The deposit is an estimate: the final amount is determined later in a review covering a set period, <C id="antidumping-duties-03" /> or, if no one asks for a review, at the deposit rate. <C id="antidumping-duties-05" />

For a small importer, the practical risks are a product you did not realize was covered, a deposit rate far above the regular duty, and a final bill that can arrive long after you sold the goods. The rest of this page follows an order from entry to liquidation using the regulations in 19 CFR Part 351 and two 2026 Federal Register notices.

<Callout type="note" title="Not a ruling">
This page explains how antidumping orders and reviews work. Whether your product is inside an order's scope is a question for a Commerce scope ruling, not for this page.
</Callout>

## Orders are product-by-country

An order covers a described product from a named country, which is why [country of origin](/guides/country-of-origin-sourcing/) matters as much as classification. Commerce's September 1, 2026 notice of opportunity to request reviews lists, for example, separate mattress orders for India (A-533-919), Mexico (A-201-859), Spain (A-469-826) and Kosovo (A-803-001), each with a review period of 9/1/25 to 8/31/26. <C id="antidumping-duties-17" /> The same list includes consumer-adjacent products such as kitchen appliance shelving and racks from China (A-570-941). <C id="antidumping-duties-17" />

## Step 1: cash deposits at entry

Under 19 CFR 351.211(b), an order instructs CBP to assess duties and to require a cash deposit of estimated duties at the rates in Commerce's determination. <C id="antidumping-duties-01" /> <C id="antidumping-duties-02" /> Part 351 provides for cash deposit rates and producer/exporter combination rates, and for calculating an all-others rate. <C id="antidumping-duties-24" />

The September 1, 2026 final results for circular welded carbon steel pipes and tubes from Thailand show how this works. Commerce found 0.00 percent weighted-average dumping margins for Saha Thai Steel Pipe and Thai Premium Pipe for the period March 1, 2024 through February 28, 2025, <C id="antidumping-duties-18" /> while the cash deposit rate for all other producers or exporters "will continue to be 15.67 percent." <C id="antidumping-duties-19" /> The cash deposit rates for the two reviewed companies equal their 0.00 percent margins, and the new deposit rates apply to all shipments entered, or withdrawn from warehouse, for consumption on or after the publication date of the final results. <C id="antidumping-duties-20" /> A 0.00 percent rate means no antidumping cash deposit on those two companies' entries, and for the reviewed entries Commerce said it will instruct CBP to liquidate without regard to antidumping duties. <C id="antidumping-duties-21" />

| Producer/exporter (circular welded pipe, Thailand) | Cash deposit rate for entries on or after Sept. 1, 2026 |
|---|---|
| Saha Thai Steel Pipe Public Co., Ltd. | 0.00% |
| Thai Premium Pipe Co. Ltd | 0.00% |
| All other producers or exporters | 15.67% |

*Source: Office of the Federal Register, Commerce/ITA notice 2026-17771, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="antidumping-duties-20" /> <C id="antidumping-duties-19" />

The difference matters to a reseller: the same pipe from a producer without its own rate would carry the all-others deposit. Knowing which producer made your goods, not only which company invoiced them, decides the rate.

## Step 2: the anniversary month and administrative reviews

Each order has an anniversary month, defined as the calendar month in which the anniversary of the order's publication date falls. <C id="antidumping-duties-15" /> Each year during that month, a domestic interested party or certain other interested parties may request an administrative review of specified exporters or producers, <C id="antidumping-duties-06" /> and an importer of the merchandise may request in writing a review of an exporter or producer that supplied it. <C id="antidumping-duties-07" />

A review normally covers the 12 months immediately preceding the most recent anniversary month for antidumping orders, and the most recently completed calendar year for countervailing duty orders. <C id="antidumping-duties-08" /> After a review, Commerce normally calculates an assessment rate for each importer. <C id="antidumping-duties-04" />

For orders with September anniversary dates in 2026, the Commerce notice set the request deadline as "not later than the last day of September 2026." <C id="antidumping-duties-16" />

## Step 3: automatic assessment when no review is requested

If no review is requested, CBP assesses duties at rates equal to the cash deposit of, or bond for, estimated duties, and continues to collect cash deposits. <C id="antidumping-duties-05" /> In that case the deposit effectively becomes the final duty.

## Step 4: liquidation instructions

After final results, Commerce issues instructions to CBP. In the Thailand pipe notice, Commerce said that because the margins were zero it will instruct CBP to liquidate the entries without regard to antidumping duties, and that it intends to issue assessment instructions no earlier than 35 days after publication. <C id="antidumping-duties-21" />

## Scope: is your product covered?

A scope ruling decides whether a particular product is covered by the scope of an AD or CVD order, and an interested party may submit a scope ruling application asking Commerce to conduct the inquiry. <C id="antidumping-duties-09" /> Commerce reads the order's language using primary interpretive sources, which include the descriptions of the merchandise in the petition. <C id="antidumping-duties-10" />

When Commerce initiates a scope inquiry, it tells CBP to continue suspension of liquidation for entries of the products at issue that were already suspended, and to apply the cash deposit rate that would apply if the product were covered. <C id="antidumping-duties-11" /> If the final ruling finds the product is not covered and no other segment suspends the entries, Commerce orders CBP to end the suspension and refund the cash deposits. <C id="antidumping-duties-12" />

## The reimbursement certificate

Before liquidation, the importer must file a certificate with CBP stating whether it has or has not been reimbursed for the duties or entered into an agreement for reimbursement. <C id="antidumping-duties-13" /> If the importer does not file it, Commerce may presume the exporter or producer paid or reimbursed the duties. <C id="antidumping-duties-14" /> The Thailand pipe notice repeats this warning to importers and states that failure to comply could lead to the assessment of double antidumping duties. <C id="antidumping-duties-22" />

## When orders end: sunset reviews

Orders do not last indefinitely without review. The U.S. International Trade Commission's September 1, 2026 notice instituting the sixth five-year review of the order on petroleum wax candles from China (Investigation No. 731-TA-282) explains that the review will determine whether revoking the order, first issued on August 28, 1986, would be likely to lead to continuation or recurrence of material injury. <C id="antidumping-duties-23" /> Candles are a common marketplace product, and that order dates from 1986, so an old order can still be in force.

## Decision table: which rate and which step apply to your entry

The same order produces different bills depending on who made the goods and what you do in the anniversary month. The table maps common importer situations to the rule that governs each one, using the Thailand pipe order as the rate example.

| If your situation is… | What applies | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Pipe made by Saha Thai or Thai Premium Pipe, entered on or after Sept. 1, 2026 | Cash deposit at 0.00% | Company rate equals its margin <C id="antidumping-duties-20" /> |
| Pipe from any other Thai producer or exporter | Cash deposit at 15.67% | All-others rate <C id="antidumping-duties-19" /> |
| No review is requested for the period | The deposit rate becomes the assessed duty | 19 CFR 351.212(c) <C id="antidumping-duties-05" /> |
| You want the duty on your supplier's entries determined in a review rather than at the deposit rate | You may request in writing a review of that exporter or producer | 19 CFR 351.212(a), 351.213(b)(3) <C id="antidumping-duties-03" /> <C id="antidumping-duties-07" /> |
| You are unsure the product is covered at all | Scope ruling application; already-suspended entries keep the deposit rate during the inquiry, with a refund if found not covered and no other segment suspends them | 19 CFR 351.225 <C id="antidumping-duties-09" /> <C id="antidumping-duties-11" /> <C id="antidumping-duties-12" /> |
| Your entries are approaching liquidation | File the reimbursement certificate | 19 CFR 351.402(f) <C id="antidumping-duties-13" /> |

*Source: Office of the Federal Register / National Archives (eCFR), 19 CFR Part 351, and Commerce/ITA notice 2026-17771, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="antidumping-duties-19" /> <C id="antidumping-duties-20" />

Two rows are actions only you control. The first is the review request: if no one asks, the deposit is what you pay, so the importer's written request is one route to having the duty on a supplier's entries determined in a review. <C id="antidumping-duties-05" /> <C id="antidumping-duties-07" /> The second is the certificate: if it is missing, Commerce may presume the duties were reimbursed, <C id="antidumping-duties-14" /> and the Thailand notice says that could lead to double antidumping duties. <C id="antidumping-duties-22" /> The rate rows, by contrast, are settled by the producer's identity, which is why the producer's name belongs in the purchase order file before the goods ship.
