Amazon's referral fee is a percentage of each item's total price — list price plus any shipping and gift-wrap charges — or a per-item minimum, whichever is greater. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-01"/> <C id="amazon-referral-fees-02"/> In Home and Kitchen the rate is 15% with a $0.30 minimum, so a $25 sale costs $3.75 in referral fees. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-03"/> The details that trip sellers up are the shipping in the base, the two different kinds of price tiers, and the category Amazon actually assigns to your product.

## The rule in one line

Amazon puts it this way: "For every item sold, you'll pay a percentage of the total price or a minimum amount, whichever is greater." <C id="amazon-referral-fees-01"/> Two numbers decide your fee:

- **The percentage** for your product's fee category, which may change with price.
- **The per-item minimum**, which is $0.30 in most categories (Home and Kitchen, Consumer Electronics, Toys and Games and many others). <C id="amazon-referral-fees-03"/> <C id="amazon-referral-fees-10"/> <C id="amazon-referral-fees-19"/>

The minimum only matters for very cheap items. At 15%, the percentage drops below $0.30 for any total price under $2.00 — so the minimum is a floor for low-priced add-ons, not a factor for a typical $15–$50 product. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-03"/>

## What "total price" means

Amazon's pricing page states that "an item's total price includes its list price, as well as shipping costs and any gift-wrapping charges." <C id="amazon-referral-fees-02"/> That matters most if you fulfill orders yourself and charge a separate shipping fee: the percentage applies to the shipping line too. Two listings that cost the buyer the same total — $25.98 with free shipping, or $19.99 plus $5.99 shipping — produce the same referral fee.

It also matters for tier boundaries. In Clothing and Accessories the rate is set by the item's total sales price, so a shipping charge can push a sale into a higher tier (see the worked examples below). <C id="amazon-referral-fees-04"/> <C id="amazon-referral-fees-05"/>

## Two kinds of tiers: whole price vs. portion

The category table uses two different tier mechanics, and the wording is the only clue.

**Whole-price tiers.** The entire sale gets one rate, chosen by the total sales price.

- Clothing and Accessories: 5% at $15.00 or less; 10% above $15.00 up to $20.00; 17% above $20.00. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-04"/> <C id="amazon-referral-fees-05"/> <C id="amazon-referral-fees-06"/>
- Beauty, Health, and Personal Care: 8% at $10.00 or less; 15% above $10.00. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-07"/>
- Grocery and Gourmet: 8% at $15.00 or less; 15% above $15.00. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-13"/>

**Portion tiers.** The higher rate applies to the first slice of the price and the lower rate only to "the portion of the total sales price" above the threshold.

- Electronics Accessories: 15% on the portion up to $100.00, 8% on any portion above. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-08"/>
- Jewelry: 20% on the portion up to $250.00, 5% on any portion above. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-09"/>
- Furniture: 15% on the portion up to $200.00, 10% on any portion above. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-11"/>
- Watches: 16% on the portion up to $1,500.00, 3% on any portion above. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-18"/>

## Worked examples at several prices

|---|---|---|---|

| Beauty, Health, and Personal Care | $9.99 | 8% | $0.80 |

*Source: rates from Amazon, sell.amazon.com pricing page, checked Nov 8, 2026; arithmetic is ours.* <C id="amazon-referral-fees-03"/> <C id="amazon-referral-fees-07"/>

Three things stand out. The $12.99 shirt with $4.99 shipping pays 10% instead of 5% because shipping lifts the total above $15.00. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-05"/> The $10.49 serum pays almost double the $9.99 one, because crossing $10.00 moves the whole price to 15%. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-07"/> And the $400 necklace pays an effective 14.4%, not 20%, because only the first $250 carries the higher rate. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-09"/>

## Tier cliffs: when a higher price nets less

Whole-price tiers create cliffs. Because the higher rate applies to the entire total once you cross the threshold, raising the price by a few cents can leave you with less money after the referral fee. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-04"/> <C id="amazon-referral-fees-06"/> <C id="amazon-referral-fees-07"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): net after referral fee only">
Clothing at $14.99 pays 5% ($0.75) and leaves $14.24; at $15.50 it pays 10% ($1.55) and leaves $13.95. Clothing at $20.00 pays 10% ($2.00) and leaves $18.00; at $20.50 it pays 17% ($3.49) and leaves $17.01. Beauty at $10.00 pays 8% ($0.80) and leaves $9.20; at $10.50 it pays 15% ($1.58) and leaves $8.92.
</Callout>

In each pair the lower price keeps more of the sale. Portion tiers have no cliff: in Jewelry or Furniture, a higher price always nets more, because only the slice above the threshold moves to the different rate. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-09"/> <C id="amazon-referral-fees-11"/> If you sell in Clothing, Beauty or Grocery, check whether your price, plus any shipping you charge, lands just above $10.00, $15.00 or $20.00.

## Referral fees plus the plan fee

The referral fee is charged per sale regardless of plan. On top of it, the Individual plan costs $0.99 per item sold, and the Professional plan costs $39.99 per month. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-16"/> <C id="amazon-referral-fees-17"/> Amazon describes the Professional account as "$39.99/month + selling fees." <C id="amazon-referral-fees-17"/>

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What actually happens |
|---|---|
| Calculating the fee on the item price only | The base is the total price, including shipping and gift wrap you charge. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-02"/> |
| Assuming the minimum is an extra charge | It is a floor: you pay the minimum only when it is larger than the percentage. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-01"/> |
| Applying the Jewelry 20% to the full price | 20% applies only to the portion up to $250.00; the rest is 5%. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-09"/> |
| Applying a split to Clothing or Beauty | Those tiers switch the rate for the whole total once the threshold is crossed. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-04"/> <C id="amazon-referral-fees-07"/> |
| Pricing off the storefront category | The fee category can differ from the category customers see. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-14"/> |
| Forgetting the media closing fee | Books, music, video, DVD and software add $1.80 per item. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-12"/> |

## How to confirm the fee for your product

The authoritative numbers are on Amazon's pricing page at sell.amazon.com/pricing, which lists every category, its tiers and its minimum. <C id="amazon-referral-fees-01"/> Rates and categories change, so recheck that page before repricing a catalog, and compare the fee Amazon actually charged on a recent order of the same product to confirm which fee category it sits in. This page explains the published fee mechanics; it does not settle a disputed charge — a fee-category disagreement is resolved through your seller account's support case with Amazon.
