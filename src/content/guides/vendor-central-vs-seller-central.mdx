The choice comes down to one question: do you want to sell **in** Amazon's store, or sell **to** Amazon? In Seller Central you are a third-party seller; Amazon's annual report says plainly, "We are not the seller of record in these transactions," and Amazon earns fees from you. <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-02"/> <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-03"/> In Vendor Central you are a supplier: Amazon buys your goods and resells them from its own inventory, recording that revenue "gross" as its own product sales. <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-04"/> Everything else, from who sets the shelf price to how you get paid, follows from that split.

## Two business models, as Amazon itself accounts for them

Amazon's Form 10-K for 2025 is the clearest public statement of the difference. On the seller side, Amazon offers "programs that enable sellers to grow their businesses, sell their products in our stores." <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-01"/> It is paid through "fixed fees, a percentage of sales, per-unit activity fees, interest, or some combination thereof." <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-03"/>

On the retail side, Amazon recognizes "gross revenue from items we sell from our inventory as product sales" and its "net share of revenue of items sold by third-party sellers as service sales." <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-04"/> <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-05"/> The two lines are reported separately:

## How a vendor relationship works day to day

Vendor work is document exchange. Amazon's Selling Partner API documentation for vendors lays out the cycle:

  {t:"Invoice", d:"Submit invoices for shipped items; submit credit notes for returns, pricing corrections and allowances.", claim:"vendor-central-vs-seller-central-18"}
]} />

The credit-note step is where vendor economics show up. Amazon's 10-K says it receives funds from vendors for items such as cooperative marketing, promotions, incentives and volume rebates, and that "We generally consider these amounts received from vendors to be a reduction of the prices we pay for their goods." <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-08"/> It adds that "Volume rebates typically depend on reaching minimum purchase thresholds." <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-09"/> In other words, the invoice price is not the whole story; allowances agreed in your vendor terms reduce what you net.

Amazon also runs a direct-fulfillment program for vendors, in which the vendor retrieves orders, acknowledges them, generates a shipping label, ships and confirms, then invoices to release payment. <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-22"/> Amazon's guide says "Vendors should submit accurate acknowledgements as fast as possible, within at most 24 hours." <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-19"/>

The retail procurement shipments API also lets vendors "Get shipment status and pickup details" and "Generate shipping labels from Amazon." <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-23"/> In direct fulfillment the order of operations is fixed: "You must acknowledge an order before you submit a shipping label request," and items "should be invoiced only after they are confirmed and shipped." <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-24"/> <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-25"/>

## How a seller account works

In Seller Central you list and price your own offers and pay published fees. Amazon's plans are $0.99 per item sold on the Individual plan or $39.99 per month on the Professional plan. <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-11"/> <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-12"/> On each sale, "you'll pay a percentage of the total price or a minimum amount, whichever is greater." <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-13"/> The category rates are in the [referral fee guide](/guides/amazon-referral-fees/), and the business-buyer tools available to sellers are in the [Amazon Business guide](/guides/amazon-business-b2b/).

## What both sides share

- **Advertising.** Amazon Ads says "Sponsored Products are available for professional sellers, vendors, book vendors," among others. <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-10"/>
- **APIs.** The Selling Partner API "helps sellers and vendors access their data on orders, shipments, payments, inventory, and other business information." <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-20"/>
- **Brand Registry.** Amazon's Brand Registry FAQ notes that "If you have a Seller Central or Vendor Central account," you can use the same login to create a Brand Registry account. <C id="vendor-central-vs-seller-central-21"/> See the [Brand Registry guide](/guides/brand-registry-trademark/).

## How to confirm the terms that apply to you

For sellers, the [selling plans and fees page](https://sell.amazon.com/pricing) is the public reference. For vendors, Amazon's public sources are its [Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm) and the vendor sections of the Selling Partner API documentation; payment terms, allowances and chargebacks are set in the vendor agreement and shown inside Vendor Central, so check them there. This page compares the two models; the contract you sign controls your actual terms.
