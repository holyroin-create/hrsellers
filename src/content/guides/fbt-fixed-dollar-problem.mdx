On Amazon, the fees on a low-priced item do not fall in proportion to its price. FBA fulfillment is a per-unit charge based on the product's price, weight and dimensions, and referral fees are a percentage of the price or a minimum amount, whichever is greater, so an $8 item can lose a far larger share of its price to fees than a $24 item that is the same size. <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-28"/> <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-04"/> Before you launch or reprice a cheap item, work out the fee share at your target price — and check whether that price sits just above or below one of Amazon's thresholds.

## The per-unit charges in Amazon's fee structure

Amazon says FBA costs depend on the products you sell and the services you use. <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-03"/> Three charges behave like fixed amounts per sale:

| Charge | How Amazon describes it |
|---|---|
| FBA fulfillment | Picking and packing, shipping and handling, customer service and product returns; based on price, weight and dimensions |
| Referral fee | A percentage of the total price or a minimum amount, whichever is greater |
| Individual selling plan | $0.99 per item sold (the Professional plan is $39.99 a month instead) |

*Source: Amazon, sell.amazon.com "Fulfillment by Amazon" and "Pricing," checked Nov 9, 2026.* <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-02"/> <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-28"/> <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-04"/> <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-07"/> <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-08"/>

Price is one of the inputs to the fulfillment fee, but the result is still a dollar amount per unit, not a percentage. Storage is separate: it is charged monthly based on daily average volume in cubic feet, <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-15"/> so it scales with how long and how bulky the stock is, not with the price. The per-unit FBA fee tables themselves are published in Seller Central (look up "FBA fulfillment fees" there); this page works from the structure Amazon publishes openly and from the fee figures your own reports return.

## Where referral rates step at low prices

In Home and Kitchen the rate is 15% with a $0.30 minimum, <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-05"/> so the minimum applies only below $2.00 (15% × $2.00 = $0.30). Several categories instead set a lower rate for cheap items and step it up at a price threshold:

| Category | Rate at or below the threshold | Rate above | Minimum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beauty, Health and Personal Care | 8% up to $10.00 | 15% | $0.30 |
| Baby Products | 8% up to $10.00 | 15% | $0.30 |
| Grocery and Gourmet | 8% up to $15.00 | 15% | None listed |
| Clothing and Accessories | 5% up to $15.00; 10% over $15.00 to $20.00 | 17% over $20.00 | $0.30 |
| Home and Kitchen | 15% at every price | 15% | $0.30 |

*Source: Amazon, sell.amazon.com/pricing, checked Nov 9, 2026.* <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-06"/> <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-17"/> <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-18"/> <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-16"/> <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-05"/>

The apparel steps date from Amazon's December 2023 announcement, which cut referral fees from 17% to 5% for items under $15 and to 10% for items between $15 and $20, starting January 15, 2024. <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-20"/> These rates apply to the whole price, so a price just over a threshold can leave you with less than a price just under it.

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): crossing a referral threshold">
Inputs: published rates only; fulfillment fee left out.

- **Beauty item at $10.00 vs $10.50.** 8% × $10.00 = $0.80; 15% × $10.50 = $1.58 (rounded). The 50-cent rise costs $0.78 more in referral fees, so the seller nets $0.28 less. <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-06"/>
- **Apparel item at $20.00 vs $21.00.** 10% × $20.00 = $2.00; 17% × $21.00 = $3.57. The $1.00 rise costs $1.57 more, so the seller nets $0.57 less. <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-16"/>

Above the threshold you need a price well clear of it before the higher rate pays off: at 15%, the beauty item must reach $10.83 (fee $1.62, net $9.21) to beat $10.00 at 8% (net $9.20).
</Callout>

## The low-price FBA discount

The fulfillment fee has its own price break. In its December 2023 fee announcement, Amazon said "Products priced below $10 will continue to have an additional $0.77 discount on per-unit fees." <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-19"/> That was the figure stated for 2024; the current low-price rates are in the Seller Central fee tables, and Amazon's 2027 announcement refers to further updates for small and large standard-size items and items priced above $50 in a separate summary. <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-23"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): two price breaks at once">
Inputs: a Beauty, Health and Personal Care item; a $0.77 low-price discount assumed to apply below $10, as Amazon stated for 2024 (confirm the current amount in Seller Central); referral rates from Amazon's pricing page.

Moving from $9.99 to $10.49: referral goes from 8% × $9.99 = $0.80 to 15% × $10.49 = $1.57 (+$0.77), and the item loses the assumed $0.77 fulfillment discount. Fees rise by about $1.54 for a 50-cent price rise, so the seller nets about $1.04 less per unit. <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-19"/> <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-06"/>
</Callout>

## The same dollar increase at different prices

Amazon announced on October 15, 2025 that FBA fees would rise by an average of $0.08 per unit sold for 2026, less than 0.5% of an average item's selling price, effective January 15, 2026 unless otherwise noted. <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-09"/> <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-10"/> Amazon said it added granularity to the fee structure to offer lower fees where its costs are lower. <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-11"/> For 2027 it says FBA fulfillment fees will rise by an average of $0.19 per unit sold, about 0.8% of the average item's selling price, with most changes effective January 15, 2027. <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-21"/> <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-22"/>

A fee Amazon does publish in full makes the point. On the MCF rate card, the peak standard-speed fee in the first ("1") column for a small standard item up to 4 oz is $7.53. <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-27"/> On a $10 item sold through another channel, that is 75% of the price; on a $30 item, 25% (our arithmetic).

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): the same product at $8 and at $24">
Inputs: a small Home and Kitchen item; referral fee 15% with a $0.30 minimum <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-05"/>; an assumed FBA fulfillment fee of $3.50 per unit, used only for illustration (get the real figure for your product from Amazon's Revenue Calculator <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-13"/>); Professional plan, so no per-item plan fee.

| | Sold at $8.00 | Sold as a 3-pack at $24.00 |
|---|---|---|
| Referral fee (15%) | $1.20 | $3.60 |
| FBA fulfillment (assumed) | $3.50 | $4.25 (assumed for the larger, heavier pack) |
| Total Amazon fees | $4.70 | $7.85 |
| Fees as share of price | 58.8% | 32.7% |
| Fees per item inside | $4.70 | $2.62 |

The referral fee scales with price, but the fulfillment fee does not, so it dominates the cheap sale. Selling three units as one pack spreads one fulfillment fee over three items.
</Callout>

## Testing a price point with Amazon's own fee data

The Fee Preview report has the fields to compute a fee share per SKU: `your-price` and `sales-price`, `estimated-referral-fee-per-unit`, `estimated-fee-total`, and for US listings `product-size-tier` and `expected-fulfillment-fee-per-unit`. <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-24"/> For prices you have not set yet, Amazon's Product Fees API takes a proposed price and can be called "before the selling partner sets the item's price." <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-25"/> Estimates requested by ASIN use the catalog size, which "might not always match the actual size of the item sent to Amazon," so treat them as provisional until the item has been measured. <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-26"/>

## Where to check the fees for your product

Re-check the referral rates and plan prices on sell.amazon.com/pricing, <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-04"/> the FBA cost description on sell.amazon.com/fulfillment-by-amazon, <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-28"/> and Amazon's 2026 and 2027 fee announcements on sellingpartners.aboutamazon.com, <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-09"/> <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-21"/> then run your product through the Revenue Calculator. <C id="fbt-fixed-dollar-problem-13"/> The fee Amazon actually charges is the one on your settlement; the figures here are for planning a price.
