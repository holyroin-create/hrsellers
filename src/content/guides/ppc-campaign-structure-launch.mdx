For a new product, Amazon's own advice gives a clear order: start with Sponsored Products, run an automatic campaign for about two weeks, then build manual keyword and product-targeting campaigns from what the automatic campaign found. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-02"/> <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-08"/> <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-09"/> The structure matters because Amazon Ads says campaign targeting cannot be changed once a campaign is live, so a mixed or badly scoped first campaign has to be rebuilt rather than edited. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-22"/> This guide lays out that structure, the bids and budgets that go with it, and when to add negatives.

## Check the product can be advertised

Sponsored Products are cost-per-click ads that promote individual product listings in the Amazon store. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-01"/> To advertise, a product must be in an eligible category and be eligible for the Featured Offer; adult, used and refurbished products and products in closed categories are excluded. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-03"/> <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-04"/> Check Featured Offer eligibility before building campaigns; Amazon Ads lists it as a condition for advertising. Account-level launch steps and new-seller credits are covered in [the Amazon launch sequence](/guides/launch-sequence/).

## The launch structure, campaign by campaign

### The automatic campaign

With automatic targeting, your ad is matched with keywords and products similar to the product you advertise. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-05"/> Amazon Ads splits that matching into four groups: close match and loose match (shoppers using search terms closely or loosely related to your product), and substitutes and complements (shoppers on detail pages of similar or complementary products). <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-06"/> <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-07"/> Its job at launch is discovery: after about two weeks, its search term report shows which queries and products produced clicks and orders. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-08"/> <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-09"/>

Amazon Ads calls automatic targeting "a great supplement to manual campaigns," and says advertisers often find success running both, so it does not need to be switched off once manual campaigns exist. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-10"/>

### The manual keyword campaign

Manual keyword targeting uses three match types:

| Match type | How Amazon Ads describes it | Suggested bid level |
|---|---|---|
| Broad | Query can contain the keyword terms in any order; broad exposure | Lowest |
| Phrase | More restrictive than broad; generally more relevant placements | Middle |
| Exact | Matched word for word, same words, same order | Highest |

*Source: Amazon Ads, Targeting with Sponsored Products, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-11"/> <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-12"/> <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-13"/> <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-15"/>

Each keyword is limited to 10 words and 80 characters. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-14"/> Amazon Ads recommends testing match types "for at least 1-2 weeks" before pausing any keyword. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-16"/>

### The manual product-targeting campaign

Amazon Ads points to the automatic campaign's search term report for product targets too: targeted ASINs that generate clicks or conversions show up in it, so the same two-week data feeds both manual campaigns. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-09"/> Keeping it separate from the keyword campaign follows the no-mixing rule above. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-20"/>

### Ad groups

An ad group can hold up to 1,000 products. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-21"/> For a launch, a practical approach is one product (or one tightly related variation family) per ad group, so the search term data belongs clearly to that product.

## Bids and bidding strategy

In Sponsored Products you bid the maximum you are willing to pay per click. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-27"/> Two Amazon Ads recommendations shape launch bids:

- **Bid by match type.** Highest on exact, lower on phrase, lowest on broad. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-15"/>
- **Pick the bidding strategy by goal.** Amazon Ads recommends dynamic bidding up and down to help maximize performance, and suggests dynamic down only if you are optimizing for return on ad spend. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-23"/> <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-24"/>

Whether a bid is affordable depends on your margin and conversion rate; [Amazon PPC benchmarks](/guides/amazon-ppc-benchmarks/) shows how to work out break-even ACOS and break-even CPC for a product.

## Daily budgets: why small ones run out early

Sponsored Products budgets are set per day, and Amazon Ads states that "daily budgets are not paced throughout the day," so a small daily budget can be spent within minutes when shopper interest is high. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-25"/> The monthly exposure is larger than the daily figure suggests: with a $100 daily budget, you may receive up to $3,100 worth of clicks in a calendar month. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-26"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): launch budget">

## Negatives: when to add them

Negative keywords stop your ad showing on irrelevant queries. Amazon Ads recommends negative phrase (blocks queries containing the complete phrase or close variations) and negative exact (blocks the exact phrase or close variations). <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-17"/> Its timing guidance prevents cutting terms too early:

- Evaluate a keyword after it has received at least 20 clicks before making it a negative. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-18"/>
- Let negative keywords run for two weeks or more before changing strategy again. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-19"/>

One option at launch: when a query moves from the automatic campaign into an exact-match manual campaign, add it as a negative exact in the automatic campaign so the two do not compete for the same search. That is a structural choice, not an Amazon requirement.

## Mistakes that force a rebuild

| Mistake | Why it hurts |
|---|---|
| Automatic and manual targets in one campaign | Amazon Ads recommends against mixing targeting strategies, and targeting cannot be changed after launch. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-20"/> <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-22"/> |
| Same bid on every match type | Amazon Ads suggests exact highest, broad lowest. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-15"/> |
| Pausing keywords after a few days | It recommends at least 1–2 weeks per match type. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-16"/> |
| Adding negatives on a handful of clicks | It recommends waiting for at least 20 clicks. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-18"/> |
| A tiny daily budget on a busy term | Budgets are not paced, so it can be spent in minutes. <C id="ppc-campaign-structure-launch-25"/> |

## How to confirm the current rules

Match-type definitions, the two-week guidance, negative-keyword timing and the 1,000-product ad-group limit are on the Amazon Ads guide [Targeting with Sponsored Products](https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/targeting-with-sponsored-products). Eligibility, bidding and budget terms are on the [Sponsored Products page](https://advertising.amazon.com/solutions/products/sponsored-products), and Amazon's seller-side overview is at [sell.amazon.com/advertising](https://sell.amazon.com/advertising). Check them before a launch.
