TikTok Shop's fee schedule looks like the cheapest place to sell in the United States. It is, on the line item everyone quotes, and that line item describes a minority of what selling there costs.

## The disagreement to resolve first

In January 2026 TikTok told US sellers Seller Shipping was ending: existing sellers moving to TikTok-controlled logistics from 25 February, hard cutoff 31 March, and no transition period for anyone onboarding after 9 February.

**On 17 February, reporting states TikTok emailed merchants and withdrew it** — Seller Shipping unchanged, previously shared deadlines not going into effect, continue as usual. The reversal is described as following brands publicly saying they would narrow assortments or leave the platform, and as covered by Modern Retail and Digiday.

**Two other sources, both dated after that, state independent shipping ended on 31 March 2026** and that all US sellers must use FBT, Upgraded TikTok Shipping or Collections by TikTok.

**These cannot both be true.** The reversal account is more specific, names the date, describes the mechanism and cites outside coverage — which makes it the more credible version. But this is the single most consequential operational question for a US TikTok Shop seller, and **you should confirm it in Seller Center rather than from any article, including this one.**

It is also a useful demonstration of why this site dates everything: a policy announced, then withdrawn six weeks later, produces a published record where the correct answer depends entirely on when the writer stopped checking.

## The fee structure, and where sources split again

**The referral fee.** Reported at **6% for most US categories**, with one source giving 12.5% for non-standard categories. Calculated on item price before shipping and tax.

**Payment processing — three different answers:**

| Source | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Canopy, TikAdSuite | 6% is a **unified** fee combining commission and processing; no separate transaction fee for US sellers |
| Dashboardly, FeesCal | 6% referral **plus** 1.02%–3.78% processing, giving a blended 7.02% |
| Darkroom | 8% referral plus $0.30 per transaction plus 2.0% processing |

The unified account appears in two sources describing it explicitly as what trips up sellers coming from Shopify or Etsy. The blended figures appear in two others. **Check your own settlement statement**, which resolves it in one look — see the [reports guide](/guides/seller-central-reports/).

**Other reported charges:**

- **Refund Administration Fee** — on a return, TikTok refunds the referral fee but keeps 20% of it, capped at $5 per SKU
- **Smart Promotion fee** — one source reports 3.5% where Smart Promo is enabled, described as required for full ad eligibility
- **Return shipping share** — reported at 20% if your Shop Performance Score is 4 or above, otherwise 50%
- **Withdrawal fees** — $0.05 flat to a bank account, 0.9% via PayPal
- **Hub Placement Fees** — from a reported $0.31 per unit where you do not ship to multiple regional fulfilment centres
- **FBT Incident Fees** — for non-compliant inbound: wrong labelling, incorrect routing, quantity discrepancies

That last pair should look familiar. It is the same structure as Amazon's [inbound placement](/guides/inbound-placement-split-arithmetic/) and [defect fees](/guides/amazon-stopped-prepping/).

## The number that actually matters

**Creator commissions.** You set the rate. Reported ranges: near 0% to about 50%, with most sellers at 5% to 20%, and category medians of roughly 20% for beauty and supplements, 15% for fashion, 10% to 15% for home, 8% to 12% for electronics.

That is not an optional marketing expense. Reporting is consistent that TikTok's discovery-driven model **typically requires 10% to 20% in affiliate commissions to generate consistent traffic**, while Amazon delivers built-in buyer intent through search.

**Which produces the real comparison:**

| | Headline | Effective on an affiliate-driven sale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TikTok Shop | 6% | Reported 20%–30%+ |
| Amazon | 8%–15% | Referral plus advertising — see the [PPC guide](/guides/amazon-ppc-benchmarks/) |

One source's total platform-side cost on a $32 beauty SKU: **roughly 35% to 40% of GMV before cost of goods and ad spend.**

**The honest framing:** TikTok Shop is not cheaper than Amazon. It has a lower platform fee and a higher customer acquisition cost, and the total lands in a similar region. What differs is where the money goes and how predictable it is.

## Fulfilment

**FBT** — reported from about $3.58 per unit, **falling to about $2.86 at four or more units.** Another source gives $4.28 for lightweight single units; another gives pick-and-pack of $2.50 to $4.50 with storage at $0.75 to $1.50 per cubic foot.

**The basket-size effect is the actionable part.** Because per-unit fees fall with order size, bundle and basket optimisation is a direct profit lever rather than a marketing preference. See the [bundling guide](/guides/bundles-and-multipacks/) — the same arithmetic, a different platform.

Reported free storage: 30 days in one source, 60 in another. Reported since 16 June 2025: FBT products automatically qualify for free shipping with no minimum order value.

**Settlement timing** is reported at roughly 15 days for standard sellers, 1 to 8 days for Star Shops, and up to 31 days for flagged accounts — which is a cash-cycle consideration alongside [DD+7](/guides/dd7-payout-reserve/) if you sell on both.

## Where it fits

**It works** for visually demonstrable products with impulse appeal, in categories where creators are active — beauty, supplements, fashion, home. Reporting projects $23.4 billion US GMV for 2026, described as roughly 70% year-on-year growth.

**It does not** for low average order value. On a $12 item, a $4.28 fulfilment fee is a third of revenue before anything else. One source is direct that small sellers with low-priced products are especially exposed.

**And it is a different business**, not a second listing. Amazon rewards a listing that answers a search; TikTok rewards content that creates demand. A seller who copies listings across and waits will conclude the platform does not work.

## What to do

**Confirm the shipping position in Seller Center first.** The published record contradicts itself on the most consequential operational question.

**Build the effective take-rate**, not the referral fee: referral, plus processing if separate, plus expected affiliate commission, plus allocated ad spend, as one percentage.

**Check your settlement statement** to resolve whether processing is bundled.

**Model at your actual average order value.** The fixed fulfilment component is what decides viability at low price points.

**Optimise basket size deliberately.** FBT per-unit fees fall with units per order.

**Negotiate creator commissions on volume and exclusivity** rather than running open marketplace campaigns at premium rates.

**Fix listing accuracy to reduce returns.** Reporting attributes most TikTok Shop returns to mismatched expectations — size charts, colour inconsistency, and overpromising in video. Returns cost twice: the lost sale and the retained refund administration fee.

**Run it on your own figures.** The [margin and break-even calculator](/tools/margin-calculator/) takes your price, cost of goods, referral rate, fulfilment fee, returns and ad spend and returns net profit per unit, net margin and the maximum ACOS the product can carry.
