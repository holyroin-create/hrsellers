FBA storage is not one fee. It is a monthly charge on cubic feet, with up to three further layers that depend on the calendar, on how much you store relative to what you sell, and on how old each unit is. <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-01"/> <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-03"/> <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-04"/> <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-06"/> The decision that matters now is timing: on February 1, 2027 Amazon removes two of those layers and replaces them with a single age-based rate, so storage billed from February 2027 follows different rules from storage billed today. <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-08"/> <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-09"/>

## Layer 1: the monthly volume fee

The base is simple to describe. Amazon says "Inventory storage costs are charged monthly based on the daily average volume (measured in cubic feet) for the space your inventory occupies in Amazon fulfillment centers." <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-01"/>

Two words in that sentence carry most of the cost logic:

- **Volume, not units.** A larger package costs more to store than a smaller one, because the bill is per cubic foot.
- **Daily average.** The volume is averaged across the days of the month, so selling down mid-month lowers that month's bill in proportion; it does not have to be at zero on a specific date.

Check the current per-cubic-foot rates on Amazon's FBA cost page and in Seller Central. For a running estimate per ASIN, the FBA Storage Fees report "contains estimated monthly inventory storage fees for each ASIN" of your inventory in Amazon fulfillment centers. <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-14"/>

## Layer 2: the season

Amazon's November 2022 fee announcement referred to "the monthly off-peak storage fee (January to September)" — set separately for standard-size and oversized products — and to "Peak storage fees (October to December)." <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-02"/> <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-03"/> Stock sent early for the holidays sits through the October–December peak period, so check the current off-peak and peak rates in Seller Central before you time a shipment.

## Layer 3: the storage utilization surcharge

The third layer looked at how much you store compared with what you sell. Amazon introduced it in its November 2022 announcement as "a storage utilization surcharge for sellers who have a high cube of inventory stored in our fulfillment centers relative to the cube of their recent weekly sales." <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-04"/> It took effect April 1, 2023, and Amazon estimated it would affect about 7.5% of sellers — those using the highest volume of storage relative to their sales volume. <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-05"/>

Restock limits also bear on storage cost. Amazon's FBA page states: "If you exceed your restock limit, you may see an increase in your storage costs." <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-11"/>

**This layer ends on February 1, 2027.** Amazon says eliminating it "will make it easier to stock up ahead of high-demand events." <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-08"/>

## Layer 4: aged inventory

The fourth layer is age. Amazon's FBA page describes a charge applied "monthly for all items stored in a fulfillment center for more than 181 days." <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-06"/> Its age bands have changed over time: for the April 15, 2023 assessment, Amazon added surcharges on inventory stored 180–270 days, excluding Apparel, Shoes, Bags, Jewelry and Watches. <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-07"/> The current bands and rates are listed in Seller Central; the oldest band is covered in [the aged inventory surcharge and its 15-month tier](/guides/aged-inventory-15-month-tier/).

**This layer is merged into storage on February 1, 2027.** Amazon will "remove the aged inventory surcharge and combine it with monthly storage fees into a single, age-based rate." <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-09"/>

## Why Amazon is collapsing the layers

Amazon framed the 2027 change around predictability: "You've told us that storage fees are hard to predict, and we want to change that." <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-17"/> Its stated result is "flat or lower fees for inventory that turns in under six months, higher fees for aged inventory." <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-10"/> In plain terms: inventory that turns quickly pays the same or less, aged inventory pays more, and the utilization surcharge disappears.

## Worked example (hypothetical): turning product size into cubic feet

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: a packaged product measures 12 × 10 × 6 inches. One cubic foot is 1,728 cubic inches. In a 30-day month you hold 600 units for the first 15 days and 300 units for the last 15 days.

- Volume per unit: 12 × 10 × 6 = 720 cubic inches ÷ 1,728 = 0.4167 cubic feet.
- Daily average units: (600 × 15 + 300 × 15) ÷ 30 = 450 units.
- Daily average volume: 450 × 0.4167 = 187.5 cubic feet.
- Your monthly fee = 187.5 × the per-cubic-foot rate Amazon currently charges for that size tier and month (and, from February 1, 2027, that inventory age).

For comparison, the same 187.5 cubic feet in Amazon Warehousing and Distribution at its published base rates would be 187.5 × $0.57 = $106.88 a month on the West Coast, or 187.5 × $0.48 = $90.00 in the East Coast, South East and South Central regions. <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-12"/>
</Callout>

The example shows why packaging matters: trimming one inch off the 6-inch side (to 12 × 10 × 5) cuts the volume per unit to 0.3472 cubic feet, about 17% less, and at the same per-cubic-foot rate the storage bill falls by the same share. <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-01"/>

## Where AWD fits

Amazon Warehousing and Distribution is Amazon's bulk storage service, with storage rates on its public page and, in Amazon's words, "Pay as you go, with available discounts and no seasonal surcharges." <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-13"/> <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-12"/> Amazon also says AWD pricing covers FBA inbound placement, so there is no separate charge for that service. <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-18"/> How the placement fee works on direct FBA shipments is covered in [inbound placement split arithmetic](/guides/inbound-placement-split-arithmetic/).

## How to confirm the storage rates that apply to you

- Amazon's FBA cost overview at sell.amazon.com/fulfillment-by-amazon, for the definition of storage and aged inventory charges. <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-01"/>
- Amazon's 2027 fee announcement on sellingpartners.aboutamazon.com, for the February 1, 2027 changes. <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-08"/>
- In Seller Central: the monthly storage fee and aged inventory pages, and the FBA Storage Fees report for per-ASIN estimates. <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-14"/>
- sell.amazon.com/programs/warehousing for AWD's published rates. <C id="fba-storage-four-layers-12"/>

These are Amazon's published descriptions, not a ruling on your invoice: a storage charge you believe is wrong has to be raised with Amazon through your seller account.
