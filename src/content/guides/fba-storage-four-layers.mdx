You probably carry one storage number in your head: a rate per cubic foot per month. It's real. It's also about a third of what you pay once inventory stops moving. Four separate charges sit on the same box, and this guide takes them apart, prices each one at 2026 rates, then stacks them back up so you can see the total.

## The unit everything is measured in

Every storage charge is per **cubic foot per month**, on your daily average inventory volume, billed between the 7th and 15th of the following month. Cubic feet come from the packaged unit as Amazon measures it, not the bare product:

```
cubic feet = (length × width × height, inches) ÷ 1,728
```

A 12 × 10 × 8 inch box is 0.556 cubic feet. The same product in an 8 × 8 × 6 box is 0.222 cubic feet — 60% less on every layer, every month, permanently. Packaging is a storage decision before it is a shipping one.

## Layer 1 — the base monthly rate (2026)

| Size tier | January–September | October–December |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard-size | $0.78 / cu ft | $2.40 / cu ft |
| Oversize (bulky) | $0.56 / cu ft | $1.40 / cu ft |
| Dangerous goods, standard | $0.99 / cu ft | $3.63 / cu ft |
| Dangerous goods, oversize | $0.78 / cu ft | $2.43 / cu ft |

These are unchanged from 2025. Amazon's 2026 fee update raised fulfillment fees by an average of $0.08 per unit and left monthly storage rates alone.

**On the $0.87 figure.** The previous version of this guide, and several sites still in circulation, quoted a change effective July 1, 2026 taking the standard rate to $0.87 and moving the aged-inventory trigger from 271 to 180 days. That report traces to one analytics vendor's news page and to nothing else. Amazon published no such change; the mid-year 2026 changes were the 3.5% fuel and logistics surcharge on fulfillment fees from April 17 and the peak fulfillment fee window from October 15. If you built a Q4 budget on $0.87 you have over-provisioned by 12%, which is the least harmful direction to be wrong in — but fix the model.

## Layer 2 — Q4 peak pricing

From October through December the base rate itself rises: standard $0.78 to $2.40, oversize $0.56 to $1.40. Two consequences that are easy to miss.

**It applies to what is already there.** It is not a charge on units sent in October. It is the rate on the daily average volume sitting in the network during those months, including a pallet that arrived in June and has not moved.

**It does not change the surcharge tiers above it.** The utilization and aged surcharges keep the same rates in Q4; only the base underneath changes. Peak pricing multiplies your worst inventory rather than replacing the penalty on it.

Planning rule: send what you can sell in eight to ten weeks during peak, not ninety days of cover. A September shipment of ninety days' supply pays $2.40 through November and December on the part that does not move.

## Layer 3 — the aged-inventory surcharge

Charged on **how long a unit has been in the network**, assessed on the 15th of each month, on top of the base rate. The ladder has started at day 181 since Amazon replaced the old long-term storage fee; advice built around day 271 or day 365 is describing rules that ended in 2023.

| Days in fulfillment center | Surcharge, per cu ft per month | Per-unit minimum (whichever is greater) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 181–210 | $0.50 | — |
| 211–240 | $1.00 | — |
| 241–270 | $1.50 | — |
| 271–300 | $3.80 | $0.30 (raised from $0.15 for 2026) |
| 301–330 | $4.00 | $0.30 |
| 331–365 | $4.20 | $0.30 |
| 366–450 (12–15 months) | $6.90 | $0.30 |
| 451+ (15 months and over) | $7.90 | $0.35 — new tier for 2026 |

The 2026 change is at the top: the per-unit floor for the 271–365-day bands rose to $0.30, and a 15-month-plus tier was added at $7.90 per cubic foot. Nothing changed at the entry point. The exact day boundary of the new tier is stated on the Aged inventory surcharge page in Seller Central; the reporting we have seen gives it as "over 15 months", and the table above uses the calendar equivalent.

**Treat 180 days as the deadline and 150 as the decision point.** A removal order takes days to process and a promotional sell-down takes weeks, and the surcharge is assessed on the 15th — a unit that turns 181 days on the 14th is charged for the whole month.

## Layer 4 — the storage utilization surcharge

Charged on a completely different basis: **weeks of cover**, not age and not volume.

```
storage utilization ratio = average daily inventory volume (cu ft)
                          ÷ average daily shipped volume (cu ft), trailing 13 weeks
```

| Ratio (weeks) | Standard-size, per cu ft per month | Oversize, per cu ft per month |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 22–28 | $0.44 | $0.23 |
| 28–36 | $0.76 | $0.46 |
| 36–44 | $1.16 | $0.63 |
| 44–52 | $1.58 | $0.76 |
| Over 52 | $1.88 | $1.26 |

**Exemptions**, which take a large share of small sellers out entirely: it does not apply if your average daily inventory in that size tier is below 25 cubic feet; it does not apply for 365 days after your first FBA shipment; inventory under 30 days old is excluded from the calculation; dangerous goods are not charged it at all.

If you are under 25 cubic feet in a size tier, stop thinking about this layer. If you are over it, note that the measure is a ratio — so the surcharge gets worse when sales slow, even if you send in nothing. The opposite failure — running so thin that the [low-inventory-level fee](/guides/low-inventory-level-fee/) switches on — is the other wall of the corridor.

## How they stack

One standard-size box, 0.556 cubic feet, received in March, on 30 weeks of cover, in November:

| Layer | Rate | Monthly cost for this box |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base rate, Q4 | $2.40 / cu ft | $1.33 |
| Aged inventory, 241–270 days | $1.50 / cu ft | $0.83 |
| Utilization surcharge, 28–36 weeks | $0.76 / cu ft | $0.42 |
| **Total** | | **$2.58 per month** |

A seller modeling storage at the off-peak base rate would have budgeted $0.43 for that box. The real figure is six times that. No single rate is shocking; it is four charges on four bases producing a number nobody reaches by holding one figure in their head.

## The reports to pull

`Seller Central > Reports > Fulfillment > Monthly Storage Fees` — what you were charged, per ASIN, with the cubic feet used. This is a measurement of your bill; any blog table, including this one, is a model of the fee schedule.

`Seller Central > Inventory > Inventory Planning > Manage Inventory Health` — inventory age buckets, estimated aged-inventory surcharge and storage utilization ratio per FNSKU. Sort by age; anything over 150 days goes on the decision list.

`Seller Central > Reports > Fulfillment > Inventory > Inventory Age` — the CSV, if you want to filter by age bucket and sell-through in a spreadsheet.

`Seller Central > Reports > Fulfillment > Storage Utilization Surcharge` — whether the 25-cubic-foot floor and 22-week ratio apply to you at all.

## What to do about it

- **Audit packaging before anything else.** Cubic feet multiply every layer, and the packaged size also sets your fulfillment fee tier. It is the only change here that pays back on every future unit.
- **Set a 150-day review, not a 271-day one.** Build it into a monthly routine using the Inventory Age report, and run each flagged SKU through the [remove-or-hold decision](/guides/removal-vs-hold-decision/).
- **Check whether the utilization surcharge applies to you at all**, using the report above, before optimizing against a charge you may not pay.
- **Decide your Q4 position in August and act by September 30.** Every corrective action — removal, liquidation, promotion — costs more and works slower during peak.
- **Re-check rates each January.** Amazon's annual fee update takes effect mid-January. Rate tables built the previous spring will be stale by then.

**Check it on your own package.** The [dimensional weight calculator](/tools/dimensional-weight/) takes packed dimensions and weight and returns billable weight and cubic feet — the two figures that drive freight and storage. Nothing is stored and nothing leaves your browser.
