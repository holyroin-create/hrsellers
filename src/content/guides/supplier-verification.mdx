Before you send a deposit to a new overseas factory, you need to answer three questions: is it legal to pay this company, can its goods legally enter the United States, and is the bank account really the supplier's? The first is an OFAC sanctions question, because U.S. persons are generally prohibited from dealing with the individuals and entities on the SDN List. <C id="supplier-verification-02"/> The second, for anything with a China supply chain, is a Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (UFLPA) question. <C id="supplier-verification-12"/> The third is a fraud question. Factory audits and product quality are separate topics; see [QC inspections](/guides/qc-inspections/).

## Check 1: sanctions screening (OFAC)

All U.S. persons must comply with OFAC sanctions, including U.S. citizens and permanent residents. <C id="supplier-verification-01"/> Blocked persons include those on OFAC's List of Specially Designated Nationals and Blocked Persons (the SDN List). <C id="supplier-verification-34"/> Violations may result in civil and, in some cases, criminal penalties. <C id="supplier-verification-04"/>

Searching the supplier's trading name is not enough. OFAC treats as blocked any entity owned individually or in the aggregate, directly or indirectly, 50 percent or more by one or more blocked persons. <C id="supplier-verification-03"/> A factory that is not named on any list can still be blocked through its owners, so search the parent company and any shareholder names the supplier gives you.

OFAC points users to its Sanctions List Search tool for searching its current sanctions lists. <C id="supplier-verification-05"/>

## Check 2: the Consolidated Screening List

The International Trade Administration's Consolidated Screening List (CSL) is a list of parties for which the U.S. government maintains restrictions on certain exports. <C id="supplier-verification-06"/> It consolidates multiple export screening lists of the Departments of Commerce, State and the Treasury, <C id="supplier-verification-07"/> including the Entity List and OFAC's Specially Designated Nationals List. <C id="supplier-verification-11"/> Its fuzzy name search allows a search without knowing the exact spelling of an entity's name, <C id="supplier-verification-08"/> which matters when a Chinese company name has several romanized spellings.

The CSL is a screening aid, not the final word. ITA says the user must check the official publication of restricted parties in the Federal Register, <C id="supplier-verification-10"/> and that if a listed party appears to match a party in your transaction, additional due diligence should be conducted before proceeding. <C id="supplier-verification-09"/>

## Check 3: UFLPA exposure

The UFLPA (Public Law 117-78, approved December 23, 2021) <C id="supplier-verification-18"/> creates a presumption covering goods mined, produced or manufactured wholly or in part in the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region, <C id="supplier-verification-12"/> or produced by an entity on the lists required by the Act. <C id="supplier-verification-13"/> Covered goods are not entitled to entry at any U.S. port. <C id="supplier-verification-14"/> DHS describes the same rule as a rebuttable presumption that such goods are prohibited from importation under 19 U.S.C. 1307. <C id="supplier-verification-35"/>

### The UFLPA Entity List

Additions are announced by the chair of the DHS-led Forced Labor Enforcement Task Force. <C id="supplier-verification-24"/> Listed entities are subject to the UFLPA's rebuttable presumption that products they produce, wholly or in part, are made with forced labor. <C id="supplier-verification-21"/>

The August 3, 2026 notice states that 187 entities meeting the criteria of the four required lists are specified on the UFLPA Entity List, <C id="supplier-verification-20"/> and that the list will be published on the DHS UFLPA website. <C id="supplier-verification-23"/> A listed entity may submit a request for removal, <C id="supplier-verification-22"/> so the list changes in both directions; re-check it on each new order, not only at onboarding.

The Entity List only covers named companies. Your supplier being absent from it does not answer the Xinjiang-input question. Ask where key raw materials come from, and read the [country of origin guide](/guides/country-of-origin-sourcing/) for how origin is decided.

### What the exception requires

The presumption can be overcome only if CBP determines, among other things, that the importer completely and substantively responded to all inquiries for information <C id="supplier-verification-15"/> and also determines, by clear and convincing evidence, that the goods were not made wholly or in part by forced labor. <C id="supplier-verification-16"/> The Act directs guidance to importers on due diligence, effective supply chain tracing and supply chain management measures. <C id="supplier-verification-17"/> Because the importer has to answer CBP's inquiries completely and substantively, agree with your supplier before the first order which traceability documents it will provide.

## Check 4: is the paperwork from the company you vetted?

The commercial invoice your supplier issues has to identify the parties to the sale: when, where and by whom the goods were sold, and to whom. <C id="supplier-verification-26"/> It must also identify by name a responsible employee of the exporter. <C id="supplier-verification-25"/> Compare the seller on the invoice, the beneficiary on the bank account and the company you screened. If they differ, ask why before paying. Our [supplier invoice guide](/guides/the-supplier-invoice/) covers every required field.

## Check 5: payment-instruction fraud

The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) describes business email compromise as targeting businesses and individuals who perform legitimate transfer-of-funds requests. <C id="supplier-verification-27"/> It reported a domestic and international exposed dollar loss of $55,499,915,582 <C id="supplier-verification-28"/> for the period October 2013 to December 2023. <C id="supplier-verification-29"/> Its recommendation: use secondary channels and/or two-factor authentication to verify requests for changes in account information. <C id="supplier-verification-30"/>

<Callout type="warning" title="Scope">
Screening tools flag names; they do not clear a transaction. Whether a specific dealing is prohibited is for OFAC (licensing and guidance) and, for UFLPA detentions, CBP to decide on the facts. Get sanctions or trade counsel involved before acting on any possible match.
</Callout>

## How to confirm it before each order

Run the supplier and its owners through OFAC's Sanctions List Search and read OFAC's basic sanctions FAQs at ofac.treasury.gov. Search trade.gov's Consolidated Screening List and resolve near-matches against the Federal Register. Check the current UFLPA Entity List at dhs.gov/uflpa-entity-list and the latest "Notice Regarding the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act Entity List" on federalregister.gov. Read the IC3 public service announcement on business email compromise at ic3.gov before you set up payment procedures.
