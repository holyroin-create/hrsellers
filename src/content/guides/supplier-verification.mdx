Advice on this topic usually stops at "use a trusted supplier", which is circular: the entire problem is establishing whether you can trust one.

This guide is about the checks that produce evidence rather than reassurance — and about a consequence most sourcing content has not caught up with, which is that your supplier's paperwork now determines what Amazon pays you when it loses your stock.

## What the badges actually certify

| Badge | Reported meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Gold Supplier** | A paid membership, reported at roughly $4,500 to $5,000 a year. Paying is the criterion. |
| **Verified Supplier** | A light-touch verification programme, the depth of which varies. |
| **Assessed Supplier** | A third-party auditor visited a facility and produced a report — which you should download and read rather than trust the label of. |
| **Trade Assurance** | Payment protection, capped, and only where the transaction runs entirely inside the platform. |

None of them establish that the person messaging you can sign a contract, that the factory in the photographs produces your product, or that the bank account you are about to pay belongs to the licensed entity.

One firm reports that among badge holders that failed audits in 2025, a substantial share turned out to be trading companies rather than manufacturers. An audit badge may represent a real visit, years ago, to an entity that has since changed hands.

## The checks that produce evidence

These take a few hours and they happen away from the supplier's profile page.

**1. Get the business licence and check it at the source.** Ask for the Chinese business licence (营业执照) and verify it at the national enterprise credit information system at gsxt.gov.cn. You are checking that the company exists, that it is in good standing, and — critically — what its registered scope of business is.

**2. Read the scope of business.** This is the highest-value five minutes in the whole process. A licence describing 贸易 (trade), 进出口 (import/export) or 商务 (commerce) is a trading company. Manufacturing is 制造 or 生产. A trading company is not automatically a problem — many are competent — but a trading company presenting itself as the factory is telling you something about how the rest of the relationship will go.

**3. Match the bank account to the licence.** Wire only to an account in the same legal name as the business licence. The most commonly reported scam is a request to pay a "subsidiary" account, often in Hong Kong, framed as avoiding platform fees. The reported outcome is consistent: the supplier stops replying, or claims a rogue employee, and there is no recourse because the transaction left the platform.

**4. Video call the factory, unannounced in timing.** Any legitimate manufacturer will do a live tour. What you are looking for is not production values — it is whether the machines match the product, whether the person on the call can answer technical questions, and whether the facility matches the photographs.

**5. Reverse image search the listing photos.** Stock and stolen photography is a documented pattern.

**6. Cross-check certifications against the issuing body.** ISO, CE, FDA and RoHS logos on a website can be fabricated or lifted. Every one of those schemes has a searchable register.

**7. Order paid samples from two or three suppliers.** A supplier making excuses about samples is telling you they cannot deliver quality. Compare rather than evaluate in isolation.

**8. Commission a third-party inspection above a threshold.** Reported practice is to use an established inspection firm for orders above a few thousand dollars, and to avoid very cheap inspection services on the grounds that they are unreliable.

## Red flags that recur

- Pressure and urgency, used to prevent exactly the checks above
- Refusal to take a video call
- Vague answers to specific technical questions
- Product categories that do not belong together on one listing — phone cases, furniture and machinery
- A quoted lead time shorter than the standard production time for that product
- A request to pay by an untraceable method, or to a personal account
- A bank account that changes mid-relationship

That last one deserves separate attention. A supplier you have paid three times without incident can have their platform account compromised, after which the correspondence continues in the same thread with new bank details. **A change of bank account is always verified by voice call to a number you already had**, never by replying to the email that requested it.

## The paperwork consequence nobody mentions

Here is where sourcing intersects with something this site has covered in detail.

Since 31 March 2025, Amazon reimburses inventory lost before a customer orders at your **sourcing cost**, not the sale price — see our [reimbursement guide](/guides/fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost/). To document that cost, agency analysis of the requirements lists manufacturer, wholesale, purchase and commercial invoices, and Chinese fapiao, and expects them to carry the document name and number, the date, buyer and issuer entity details, product description and SKU, quantity, price and currency.

**A supplier who cannot issue that document is now costing you money on every lost unit**, whether or not they ever defraud you. This is a new and quite practical reason to prefer a properly registered manufacturer over the cheapest quote: one of them can produce an invoice that Amazon will accept, and one of them cannot.

Add it to your qualification questions. Ask for a sample invoice before the first order, and check it carries the fields above.

## Concentration risk, which is not fraud but ends the same way

Even a supplier who passes every check can fail. Reported causes include fire, regulatory change, an ownership dispute that splits a factory, and slow quality decline.

One guide's stated threshold is that by the time a SKU reaches around $100,000 in annual volume, a qualified secondary supplier capable of taking 30% to 50% of it should exist. Whether that specific figure fits your business is your judgment; the structural point does not depend on it. A single-supplier product line is a single point of failure, and the time to qualify a second source is while the first one is working.

**Verification is not a one-time event.** A supplier clean eighteen months ago may now have court judgments, tax penalties or new owners. Re-run the registry check annually on anything material.

## The order to do this in

1. Business licence, verified at gsxt.gov.cn — before any substantive conversation
2. Scope of business read and understood — factory or trader
3. Video factory tour
4. Paid samples from two or three candidates
5. Sample invoice checked for the fields Amazon requires
6. Bank account matched to the licensed entity
7. First order small, paid through escrow inside the platform
8. Third-party inspection before the balance on anything substantial
9. Second source qualified before the SKU becomes load-bearing

Steps 1 and 2 cost nothing and eliminate the majority of the risk. Almost nobody does them.
