The right freight mode is the one whose cost, speed and paperwork clock fit the shipment: ocean is the mode with the earliest data deadline — the Importer Security Filing is due 24 hours before the cargo is even loaded abroad — while air, rail and truck data are due hours before arrival. <C id="freight-modes-03"/> <C id="freight-modes-07"/> <C id="freight-modes-09"/> <C id="freight-modes-10"/> Published sources give firm deadlines but not market freight rates, so this page compares the modes on what is fixed by regulation and by Amazon's published program terms, and leaves price to your quotes.

All four can be arranged through an intermediary: trade.gov notes that freight forwarders may reserve space on a vessel, aircraft, train or truck. <C id="freight-modes-11"/> How to vet that intermediary is in [Choosing a freight forwarder](/guides/choosing-a-freight-forwarder/).

## Ocean: FCL, LCL and the ISF

Amazon's freight program books ocean freight as a full container load (FCL), which it pitches as a way to maximize space and optimize costs, or as less-than-container load (LCL). <C id="freight-modes-12"/> For LCL, Amazon consolidates your LCL shipments with inventory from other Amazon sellers. <C id="freight-modes-13"/> Amazon also offers floor loading, which places inventory directly in the container without pallets or other packaging. <C id="freight-modes-17"/>

The ocean mode carries a filing no other mode has. The Importer Security Filing applies to cargo arriving by vessel, excluding bulk cargo, and the responsible "ISF Importer" is the party causing goods to arrive within the limits of a U.S. port by vessel. <C id="freight-modes-02"/> <C id="freight-modes-01"/> Timing is front-loaded:

The practical consequence: your supplier's shipping data must reach the filer at least 24 hours before the cargo is loaded, not when it arrives. The bond side is covered in [Customs bonds](/guides/customs-bonds/).

Within Amazon's program, two ocean service levels exist. Amazon positions Standard Ocean as its most cost-effective mode from China or Vietnam, and says Fast Ocean offers faster transit times than Standard Ocean when shipping to the US. <C id="freight-modes-14"/> <C id="freight-modes-15"/> Amazon does not publish ocean transit days on that page, so get a written transit estimate in any quote.

## Air: speed with a later data deadline

For flights from most foreign areas, advance air cargo data must reach CBP no later than 4 hours before the aircraft arrives in the United States. <C id="freight-modes-07"/> From North America — including Mexico, Central America, South America north of the Equator, the Caribbean and Bermuda — the deadline is the aircraft's departure for the United States. <C id="freight-modes-08"/> There is no ISF for air cargo, because Part 149 covers vessel cargo. <C id="freight-modes-02"/>

As one published benchmark for speed, Amazon says its air freight service provides transportation directly into its fulfillment network within 7–10 days. <C id="freight-modes-16"/> Air restrictions on batteries and other dangerous goods are separate rules: see [Shipping lithium batteries](/guides/shipping-lithium-batteries/) and [Hazmat and dangerous goods](/guides/hazmat-dangerous-goods/).

## Rail and truck: cross-border land freight

For rail, advance electronic cargo information is due no later than 2 hours before the cargo reaches the first U.S. port of arrival. <C id="freight-modes-09"/> For trucks it is due 30 minutes or 1 hour before the carrier reaches the first port of arrival, or such lesser time as authorized based on the CBP-approved system used. <C id="freight-modes-10"/> Confirm in writing who files the cargo data for your shipment.

## The domestic leg into Amazon

Once cleared, goods still have to reach a fulfillment center. Amazon's inbound tools support small parcel deliveries (SPD) or pallets moving as LTL or FTL, using either an Amazon-partnered or a non-partnered carrier. <C id="freight-modes-22"/> Amazon's own guide names UPS, FedEx and USPS as carriers that handle many small parcel deliveries, and LTL and FTL as the freight options. <C id="freight-modes-20"/> <C id="freight-modes-21"/> Shipments are created in the Send to Amazon workflow, and every product must be prepped and labeled before it is sent. <C id="freight-modes-23"/> <C id="freight-modes-24"/>

If you use Amazon's program for the international leg, its Managed Placement option caps a 40-foot container at 2,000 cartons, and direct shipping to Amazon Warehousing and Distribution is currently available only from China to the US. <C id="freight-modes-18"/> <C id="freight-modes-19"/>

## Mistakes that cost time

| Mistake | What the rules say |
|---|---|
| Collecting supplier data after the ship sails | ISF elements are due 24 hours before lading abroad. |
| Assuming air cargo needs an ISF | Part 149 applies to cargo arriving by vessel. |
| Leaving the ISF stale after a change | The filer must update it when information changes. |
| Shipping unprepped cartons to Amazon | Products must be prepped and labeled before sending. |

## How to confirm the deadline for your mode

Read 19 CFR Part 149 for ocean, 19 CFR 122.48a for air and 19 CFR 123.91–123.92 for rail and truck on eCFR, and check Amazon's program page for current service levels before booking. <C id="freight-modes-02"/> <C id="freight-modes-07"/> <C id="freight-modes-09"/> <C id="freight-modes-14"/> This page compares the regulatory clocks; your carrier, forwarder or customs broker confirms who files what for a given booking.
