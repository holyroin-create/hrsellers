Customs law answers "who is the importer?" by liability, not by contract: under 19 CFR 101.1 the importer is the person primarily liable for the payment of any duties, and that liability is a personal debt due to the United States. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-01"/> <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-03"/> The trade term on your purchase order — DDP, DAP or anything else — does not appear in that test. What does change from January 5, 2027 is how a foreign company can serve as importer of record, under a CBP interim final rule published on November 6, 2026. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-13"/> <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-32"/>

## What the regulations mean by "importer"

19 CFR 101.1 says the importer may be the consignee, the importer of record, or the actual owner of the merchandise if an actual owner's declaration and superseding bond has been filed. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-02"/> That last option is how a consignee can be relieved from statutory liability for increased and additional duties: it must file the actual owner's declaration within 90 days from the time of entry and file a bond of the actual owner on Customs Form 301. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-04"/> <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-05"/>

Filing does not require a license if you file for yourself. An importer or exporter transacting customs business solely on its own account is not required to be licensed, but anyone transacting customs business as a broker for others needs a license. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-09"/> <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-10"/> When you use a broker, you sign a power of attorney — Customs Form 5291 may be used — and the broker keeps it with its books and papers. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-11"/> <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-12"/> To be identified at all, an importer of record registers with CBP by submitting CBP Form 5106 (Create/Update Importer Identity Form). <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-28"/>

## Nonresident importers under the existing rules

Long before the 2027 rule, the regulations put conditions on importers outside the United States.

| Who | Existing requirement |
|---|---|
| Nonresident consignee | Any bond taken in connection with the entry must have a resident corporate surety |
| Nonresident corporation | May not enter goods for consumption without a resident agent in the State where the port of entry is located (or the State where a remote location filing originates) |
| Nonresident corporation | Its bond must have a resident corporate surety to secure payment of any increased and additional duties |

*Source: eCFR — 19 CFR 141.17 and 141.18, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-06"/> <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-07"/> <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-08"/>

CBP itself describes these as a low bar: in the 2026 rule it says the existing nonresident references capture importers who have met a low threshold to qualify as nonresident importers. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-29"/>

## The foreign importer-of-record rule (FR Doc. 2026-22735)

CBP published "Heightened Requirements for Foreign Importers of Record" as an interim final rule implementing Executive Order 14411, Strengthening Customs Enforcement, signed June 3, 2026. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-32"/> <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-16"/>

The rule turns on a new definition. A foreign importer of record is simply an importer of record that does not meet the criteria of a U.S. importer of record. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-17"/> For a company, being a U.S. IOR means being organized under U.S. law and located in the United States, and at all times either having at least 51% beneficial ownership by U.S. citizens or lawful permanent residents who control the entity, or directly owning a significant amount of unencumbered real property in the United States. A beneficial owner is a natural person who exercises substantial control over the entity or owns or controls not less than 25 percent of its ownership interests. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-18"/> <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-19"/>

Brokers carry the checking duty: they must verify that a client qualifies as a U.S. IOR. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-24"/> Misstating status has a cost: where an importer inaccurately declares that it qualifies as a U.S. IOR, CBP now has the ability to require live entry and discontinue immediate delivery privileges. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-27"/> The executive order also directed a ban on informal entry by foreign IORs, but CBP has not implemented that prohibition in this interim rule. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-26"/>

## When a supplier offers to "handle customs"

An offer to ship "duties paid" or "we handle customs" can mean two very different things on the entry. Ask which party will be named as importer of record, because that party owns the duty debt and the paperwork. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-01"/>

Keep your own file either way. The record-keeping rules reach owners, importers, consignees and importers of record, and records relating to an entry are kept for 5 years from the date of entry. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-30"/> <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-31"/>

## Common misreadings of the rules

| What sellers assume | What the text says |
|---|---|
| "On DDP terms I am not the importer." | The regulations define the importer as the person primarily liable for duties; trade terms are not part of that definition. |
| "My U.S. LLC is automatically a U.S. IOR." | An entity must be organized under U.S. law and located in the U.S., and also meet a 51% U.S.-beneficial-ownership test or own significant unencumbered U.S. real property. |
| "The 2027 rule bans foreign IORs from informal entry." | The executive order directed that ban, but CBP has not implemented it in this rule. |
| "Only brokers have to change anything." | The IOR, or the broker on its behalf, must tick the U.S.-IOR checkbox on Forms 3461 and 7501. |
| "It is final, so comments are pointless." | It is an interim final rule; comments are accepted until January 5, 2027. |

*Source: eCFR — 19 CFR 101.1; Federal Register, FR Doc. 2026-22735; checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-01"/> <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-18"/> <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-26"/> <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-23"/> <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-14"/>

The second row is the one to check first. The ownership test looks through the company to the people behind it: a beneficial owner is a natural person with substantial control or at least a 25 percent ownership interest. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-18"/> <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-19"/> A U.S.-registered company with foreign owners should not assume it qualifies until its broker has completed the verification. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-24"/>

## How to confirm it for your company

The definitions sit in 19 CFR 101.1 and the nonresident rules in 19 CFR 141.17, 141.18 and 141.20 on eCFR. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-02"/> <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-06"/> The 2027 requirements are in FR Doc. 2026-22735 (91 FR 69977), which is open for comment until January 5, 2027. <C id="incoterms-who-is-importer-14"/> Whether your company qualifies as a U.S. IOR depends on its ownership and assets, so have your broker run the verification it will be required to perform before your first 2027 shipment rather than after it.
