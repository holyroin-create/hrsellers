You can sometimes pay duty on the price the factory charged your trading company rather than the higher price you paid the trading company. CBP has described its interpretation as basing transaction value on the price paid in the first or earlier sale, provided the importer shows by sufficient evidence that the sale was at arm's length and the goods were clearly destined for the United States at that time. <C id="first-sale-valuation-23"/> <C id="first-sale-valuation-24"/> <C id="first-sale-valuation-25"/> Whichever sale you use, the same additions apply, so first sale only works when your valuation basics are already right.

## Transaction value, the starting point

Imported goods are appraised under a fixed order of methods, starting with transaction value. <C id="first-sale-valuation-01"/> Transaction value is the price actually paid or payable when the goods are sold for exportation to the United States, plus specified additions. <C id="first-sale-valuation-02"/> The price actually paid or payable is the total payment, direct or indirect, made by the buyer to or for the benefit of the seller. <C id="first-sale-valuation-03"/> It excludes charges for transportation, insurance and related services for the international shipment. <C id="first-sale-valuation-04"/>

### The five additions

| Addition | What gets added |
|---|---|
| Packing | Packing costs incurred by the buyer <C id="first-sale-valuation-05"/> |
| Selling commission | Any selling commission incurred by the buyer <C id="first-sale-valuation-06"/> |
| Assists | The value of any assist, apportioned as appropriate <C id="first-sale-valuation-07"/> |
| Royalties and license fees | Those related to the goods that the buyer is required to pay <C id="first-sale-valuation-08"/> |
| Resale proceeds | Proceeds of later resale, disposal or use that accrue to the seller <C id="first-sale-valuation-09"/> |

*Source: eCFR — 19 CFR 152.103, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="first-sale-valuation-05"/>

Additions are made only to the extent the amount is not already included in the price. <C id="first-sale-valuation-10"/> They must also be based on sufficient information; without it, transaction value is treated as one that cannot be determined. <C id="first-sale-valuation-11"/>

### Commissions

A selling commission, the dutiable kind, is any commission paid to the seller's agent who is related to or controlled by, or works for or on behalf of, the manufacturer or the seller. <C id="first-sale-valuation-12"/> If your sourcing agent works for you and is paid by you, label and document that relationship clearly so it is not mistaken for a seller-side commission.

### Assists: the cost sellers forget

Private-label sellers often pay for molds or send artwork to the factory. Both can be assists. The list includes materials and components incorporated in the goods; tools, dies and molds used to produce them; and engineering, development, artwork, design work, plans and sketches. <C id="first-sale-valuation-13"/> <C id="first-sale-valuation-14"/> <C id="first-sale-valuation-15"/> Design-type work counts only when undertaken elsewhere than in the United States. <C id="first-sale-valuation-16"/>

An assist bought from an unrelated seller is valued at its acquisition cost; one you or a related party produced is valued at its cost of production. <C id="first-sale-valuation-17"/> <C id="first-sale-valuation-18"/>

### Royalties

A royalty is dutiable when the buyer must pay it as a condition of sale of the goods for exportation to the United States. <C id="first-sale-valuation-19"/> Royalties for patents covering the processes used to manufacture the goods are generally dutiable. <C id="first-sale-valuation-20"/>

## How first sale works

In a series of sales — factory to trading company, trading company to you — the question is which sale is the one "for exportation to the United States." CBP's 2008 notice describes the Nissho Iwai test: the sale must be at arm's length, and the goods must be clearly destined for export to the U.S. <C id="first-sale-valuation-26"/> In the Synergy case, as CBP described it, garments were found clearly destined for export by virtue of their labels. <C id="first-sale-valuation-27"/>

CBP noted that an intermediary often sells the same merchandise to buyers in the U.S. and in other countries. <C id="first-sale-valuation-28"/> That is why "clearly destined" evidence matters: U.S.-specific labels, packaging, specifications or order references tied to your purchase order at the time the factory made the sale.

### Related parties

Many first-sale structures involve an affiliated trading company. CBP will not disregard a transaction value solely because the buyer and seller are related. <C id="first-sale-valuation-21"/> Transaction value applies if the parties are unrelated, or related but the value is acceptable. <C id="first-sale-valuation-22"/>

## Common first-sale mistakes

| Mistake | Why it fails | Better approach |
|---|---|---|
| Declaring the factory price without the factory invoice in hand | The importer must establish the arm's length sale by sufficient evidence <C id="first-sale-valuation-24"/> | Collect both invoices and payment proof before filing |
| Dropping the mold or artwork cost because "the price is lower now" | Assists are added whichever sale you use <C id="first-sale-valuation-07"/> | Keep a running assist log per product |
| Treating a sourcing agent's fee as non-dutiable without paperwork | A commission paid to an agent who is related to, controlled by, or works for or on behalf of the seller or manufacturer is a dutiable selling commission <C id="first-sale-valuation-12"/> | Use a written agency agreement and pay the agent directly |
| Using first sale for stock the middleman held for any buyer | The goods must be clearly destined for the U.S. at the time of that sale <C id="first-sale-valuation-25"/> | Tie the factory order to your purchase order |
| Adding a royalty only when it is invoiced separately | Royalties required as a condition of sale are dutiable <C id="first-sale-valuation-19"/> | Review license terms with each new product |

*Source: eCFR — 19 CFR 152.102–152.103 and CBP, 73 FR (Jan 24, 2008), checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="first-sale-valuation-23"/>

Most of these come down to the same habit: build the valuation file when you place the order, not when CBP asks for it. First sale depends on the middleman's records, so agree on disclosure in the supply contract before you plan any savings around it.

## How to confirm it for your goods

Read 19 CFR 152.100 through 152.108 on the eCFR for the valuation rules, additions and related-party tests. For the first-sale position, read CBP's January 24, 2008 proposed interpretation and the September 29, 2010 withdrawal notice on federalregister.gov, and compare their conditions with the documents you can actually obtain from your supply chain.
