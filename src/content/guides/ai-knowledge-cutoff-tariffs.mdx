An AI chatbot can quote an out-of-date tariff rate because each model learns from data up to a fixed date, its knowledge cutoff, and US import rules kept changing after those dates. As listed on November 11, 2026, Google's Gemini 2.5 Flash page shows a knowledge cutoff of January 2025, <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-09"/> while its newer Gemini 4 Argon page lists none, <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-10"/> OpenAI lists dates between Apr 30 and Jun 1, 2026 for its flagship models, <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-04"/> <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-06"/> and Anthropic lists Jun 2026 for its current Claude models. <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-01"/> Since then, a 10% surcharge ended and a new 12.5% Section 301 tariff on products of China, with listed exceptions, began on July 24, 2026, <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-19"/> <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-24"/> <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-25"/> and USITC published a new HTS revision on November 10, 2026. <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-29"/> Treat any AI-supplied duty rate as a lead to check against the official schedule, not as the answer.

## What the providers publish about knowledge cutoffs

Two practical points follow. A month-level date such as "Jun 2026" does not tell you whether a change published late that month is included. And a missing cutoff is not the same as an up-to-date model; it only means you cannot date the model's knowledge from its documentation.

Read against the cutoffs above, a model with a January 2025 cutoff predates every item on this list. A model with an Apr 30, 2026 cutoff could know about the IEEPA duties ending and the Section 122 surcharge, but not the June 24 CBP rule, the July 24 switch, the FY2027 fees or the November exclusion expiry. Even a model with a Jun 2026 cutoff predates everything from July 24, 2026 on. Some of these changes also interact: Executive Order 14389 left Section 232 and Section 301 duties untouched, <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-17"/> and the Section 122 surcharge did not apply on top of Section 232 tariffs. <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-20"/> A model that knows one change but not the next can combine them wrongly.

Some changes are also scheduled ahead. CBP states that the One Big Beautiful Bill Act terminated the de minimis exemption effective July 1, 2027, <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-23"/> and the semiconductor tariff is set to rise on June 23, 2027 to a rate not yet announced. <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-15"/> No model can know a rate that has not been published.

## Why a chatbot answer can be out of date

A model answering only from its training can reflect rules up to its cutoff and no later. That is the practical consequence, and it applies whatever the model or provider. Providers do offer live search: Anthropic says its web search tool lets Claude answer "with up-to-date information beyond its knowledge cutoff," <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-03"/> OpenAI lists web search as supported for GPT-6 Astra, <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-08"/> and Google lists search grounding as supported for Gemini 4 Argon. <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-12"/> Moonshot AI's platform page says its models "do not directly access external resources such as the internet or databases by default." <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-13"/> Whether search was used for a given answer depends on the product and settings, so look for cited sources in the answer itself. Even a searched answer can miss a Chapter 99 heading or quote a news summary instead of the notice.

Tariff questions are especially exposed because the additional duty is usually not in the line an AI might quote. A heading in Chapter 99 adds to the base rate: HTSUS 9903.88.03, for covered products of China, carries "the duty provided in the applicable subheading + 25%." <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-30"/> An answer that gives only the subheading rate leaves that out.

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): a missed additional duty">
Inputs, all assumed for illustration: 1,000 units with a customs value of $10,000; a base subheading rate of 4%; and an additional Chapter 99 duty of 25% that the AI answer did not mention. Base duty only: $10,000 × 4% = $400, or $0.40 per unit. With the additional duty: $10,000 × (4% + 25%) = $2,900, or $2.90 per unit. The missed duty is $2,500 on the shipment, $2.50 per unit. Fees such as the merchandise processing fee are left out to keep the arithmetic to the duty itself.
</Callout>

On a product priced from the first figure, that $2.50 per unit comes straight out of margin.

USITC itself says its classification help "is not binding advice," <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-33"/> that importers are ultimately responsible for knowing CBP requirements, <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-31"/> and that you can request a binding electronic ruling from CBP. <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-32"/> USITC's China tariff table is also not an authoritative source on its own, so read the HTS heading it points to. <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-39"/> A ruling under 19 CFR Part 177 can be requested by a person with "a direct and demonstrable interest" in the question, <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-34"/> generally only for prospective transactions. <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-35"/> Once issued, it binds all Customs Service personnel until modified or revoked, <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-36"/> but a classification ruling applies only to articles identical to the sample submitted. <C id="ai-knowledge-cutoff-tariffs-37"/> For how classification itself works, see [HTS classification basics](/guides/hts-classification-basics/).

## How to confirm it for your shipment

Start at hts.usitc.gov for the subheading and any Chapter 99 heading, and check usitc.gov's Harmonized Tariff Information page for the current revision. Read the Federal Register documents themselves for each action: Executive Order 14389, Proclamations 11012 and 11021, CBP's June 24, 2026 interim final rule, USTR's July 28 and October 7, 2026 notices, and CBP's FY2027 user fee notice. For a model's cutoff, read its provider's own model page on the day you use it, since these listings change. Then, before you file or price, confirm with a licensed customs broker or a CBP ruling under 19 CFR Part 177 (read it on ecfr.gov).
