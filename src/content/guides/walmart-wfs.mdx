Walmart Fulfillment Services charges a per-unit fulfillment fee set by shipping weight — $3.45 for 1 lb or less, rising to $5.75 plus $0.40 per pound over 4 lb for 4–20 lb items — and a storage fee of $0.75 per cubic foot per month outside peak season. <C id="walmart-wfs-01"/> <C id="walmart-wfs-04"/> <C id="walmart-wfs-17"/> Surcharges, dimensional weight and the October–December storage add-on can move the number, so the safest approach is to run each product through Walmart's own formula, step by step, as below. Your normal Walmart referral fee still applies to every sale on top of WFS fees. <C id="walmart-wfs-26"/>

## What the fulfillment fee pays for

Walmart's WFS pricing page describes its estimate as the cost "for us to store, pick, pack and quickly ship your item(s) to customers," and lists "Customer support via chat, email and phone" as included. <C id="walmart-wfs-29"/> <C id="walmart-wfs-28"/> Return processing fees and long-term storage fees are listed under "Not included in cost." <C id="walmart-wfs-24"/>

## Step 1: Is the item "standard"?

The weight table below applies to standard items, which Walmart defines as a unit weight of 150 lb or less, a longest side of 108 inches or less, and longest side plus girth of 165 inches or less. <C id="walmart-wfs-15"/> Walmart's big & bulky example is a massage chair, classified that way because it exceeds 150 lb; at a 231 lb shipping weight its fee comes to $155 + ($0.80 × 141 lb) = $267.80. <C id="walmart-wfs-23"/>

The 139 divisor is what makes light but bulky goods expensive. A 3.2 lb item in a 14 × 10 × 8 inch box has a dimensional weight of 8.06 lb (1,120 ÷ 139), and that is the number that drives the fee. <C id="walmart-wfs-08"/> <C id="walmart-wfs-09"/>

Shrinking its box to 12 × 9 × 7 inches would cut dimensional weight to 5.44 lb (756 ÷ 139), giving a 6 lb shipping weight and a fee of $6.55 — a $1.20 saving per unit from packaging alone. <C id="walmart-wfs-04"/> <C id="walmart-wfs-11"/>

## Storage fees

Storage is billed per cubic foot per month, where unit cubic feet equal L × W × H in inches divided by 1,728. <C id="walmart-wfs-22"/>

| Period | Rate per cubic foot per month |
|---|---|
| January–September | $0.75 |
| October–December, stored up to 30 days | $0.75 |
| October–December, stored more than 30 days | add $1.50 |
| Stored 366 to 450 days | $2.25 |
| Stored more than 450 days | $7.50 |

*Source: Walmart Marketplace, WFS pricing page, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="walmart-wfs-17"/> <C id="walmart-wfs-18"/> <C id="walmart-wfs-19"/> <C id="walmart-wfs-20"/> <C id="walmart-wfs-21"/>

Walmart's storage example: a 10.0 × 5.9 × 5.9 inch widget (0.201 cubic feet) stored 90 days costs 0.201 × 90 × $0.025 = $0.45 per unit, using a daily rate of $0.75 ÷ 30. <C id="walmart-wfs-30"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): 1,000 units in 12 × 10 × 6 inch boxes">
Each box is 720 ÷ 1,728 = 0.417 cubic feet. In a January–September month: 0.417 × $0.75 ≈ $0.31 per unit, about $313 for 1,000 units. In an October–December month for units already stored more than 30 days: 0.417 × ($0.75 + $1.50) ≈ $0.94 per unit, about $938. Past 450 days: 0.417 × $7.50 ≈ $3.13 per unit per month.
</Callout>

The jump from $0.75 to $7.50 for inventory older than 450 days is tenfold, so slow movers are where storage cost concentrates. <C id="walmart-wfs-17"/> <C id="walmart-wfs-21"/>

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct approach |
|---|---|
| Using unit weight for a bulky 1 lb+ item | Use the greater of unit weight and L × W × H ÷ 139. <C id="walmart-wfs-09"/> |
| Forgetting the 0.25 lb packaging allowance | Add 0.25 lb before rounding up. <C id="walmart-wfs-11"/> |
| Pricing a $9.99 item without the surcharge | Items priced under $10 add $1 per unit. <C id="walmart-wfs-14"/> |
| Using the $0.75 rate all year | Units stored more than 30 days in October–December add $1.50 per cubic foot per month. <C id="walmart-wfs-19"/> |
| Treating the estimator total as all-in | Return processing and long-term storage are excluded. <C id="walmart-wfs-24"/> |

## How to confirm WFS fees for your items

Walmart publishes the fee table, surcharges, storage rates and an estimator on its WFS pricing page at marketplace.walmart.com, and says fees "are subject to change." <C id="walmart-wfs-33"/> Measure the packaged unit, rerun the steps above against the live page before sending inventory, and keep the measurements: if a charge differs from your estimate, a support case through Seller Center is the route for a correction, not this page.
