Your supplier writes the commercial invoice, but you, as importer, file it and answer for it. A commercial invoice must be filed for each shipment of merchandise not exempted <C id="the-supplier-invoice-01"/> and presented at the time the entry summary is filed, <C id="the-supplier-invoice-33"/> and 19 CFR 141.86 sets out the price, origin and description details it must carry. If the supplier's template is missing a required field, the fix is cheapest before the goods ship. This page goes through what 19 CFR 141.86 requires, field by field, and what to do when the invoice is wrong or absent.

## One invoice, one shipment

An invoice shall not represent more than one distinct shipment of merchandise. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-02"/> There is an exception for installments of a single order that arrive within a period not exceeding 10 consecutive days. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-29"/> If your supplier splits an order across two vessels weeks apart, expect two invoices.

Where installments do share an invoice, it must, when practicable, show the quantities and values for each installment and the date each one shipped. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-34"/> If the invoice is not filed with the first entry of the series, a pro forma invoice goes with each entry until the real one is produced, and liquidated damages accrue on each entry if more than 6 months pass without it. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-35"/> <C id="the-supplier-invoice-36"/> A bona fide installment valued at not over $2,500 may instead be entered on an informal entry, subject to an exception for certain Chapter 99 articles over $250. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-37"/>

The reverse case — one invoice, goods entered in pieces — is also covered. If part of the quantity fails to arrive, or only part is entered at first, a photocopy of the invoice used for the first entry can support the later entry. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-42"/>

## The eleven required items

19 CFR 141.86(a) lists what each invoice must set forth:

| # | Required item |
|---|---|
| 1 | The port of entry to which the goods are destined <C id="the-supplier-invoice-03"/> |
| 2 | When, where, by whom and to whom the goods were sold <C id="the-supplier-invoice-04"/> |
| 3 | A detailed description, including the name each item is known by <C id="the-supplier-invoice-05"/> and its grade or quality and the marks, numbers and symbols under which sold <C id="the-supplier-invoice-06"/> |
| 4 | Quantities in the weights and measures of the country or place of shipment <C id="the-supplier-invoice-07"/> |
| 5 | The purchase price of each item in the currency of the purchase, for goods shipped under a purchase <C id="the-supplier-invoice-08"/> |
| 6 | For goods not purchased, the value of each item in the currency transactions are usually made in <C id="the-supplier-invoice-09"/> |
| 7 | The kind of currency <C id="the-supplier-invoice-10"/> |
| 8 | All charges itemized by name and amount, including freight, insurance and commission <C id="the-supplier-invoice-11"/> and cases, containers, coverings and cost of packing; packing, cases, containers and inland freight to the port of exportation need not be itemized by amount if included in the invoice price and so identified <C id="the-supplier-invoice-12"/> |
| 9 | Rebates, drawbacks and bounties allowed on exportation, separately itemized <C id="the-supplier-invoice-13"/> |
| 10 | The country of origin <C id="the-supplier-invoice-14"/> |
| 11 | Goods or services furnished for production, such as assists like dies, molds, tools and engineering work, not included in the invoice price (goods or services furnished in the U.S. excluded) <C id="the-supplier-invoice-15"/> |

*Source: eCFR — 19 CFR 141.86(a), checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="the-supplier-invoice-03"/>

The invoice is prepared in the manner customary in the trade, but it must contain the information required by 141.86 through 141.89, and CBP may accept a copy in place of the original. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-38"/> <C id="the-supplier-invoice-39"/>

Items 8 and 11 feed directly into customs value; the full rules on additions such as packing, commissions and assists are in our [first-sale valuation guide](/guides/first-sale-valuation/). Item 10 is about where the goods were produced, which is decided under the rules in [country of origin for tariffs](/guides/country-of-origin-sourcing/), not by where they shipped from. Commissions on the invoice are explained in [sourcing agents](/guides/sourcing-agents/).

## The general requirements

Beyond the eleven items, 141.86 sets formatting and disclosure rules:

- **Language.** The invoice and all attachments must be in English or have an accurate translation. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-16"/>
- **Packing.** Each invoice must state in adequate detail what merchandise is contained in each individual package. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-17"/>
- **Weights and measures.** If the invoice does not disclose the weight, gage or measure needed to ascertain duties, the consignee must pay the expense of weighing, gaging or measuring. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-18"/>
- **Discounts.** Each invoice must set forth in detail, for each class or kind of merchandise, every discount. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-19"/>
- **Numbering.** Each invoice with its attachments must be numbered consecutively by the importer. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-20"/>
- **Attachments.** The required information may be set forth either on the invoice or on an attachment. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-21"/>
- **Exporter contact.** Each invoice must identify by name a responsible employee of the exporter. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-22"/>

Some goods need more. Where classification or appraisement depends on component materials, the invoice must give a breakdown of the value, weight or other necessary measurement. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-23"/> And 141.89 requires additional information on invoices for listed classes of merchandise, <C id="the-supplier-invoice-30"/> including hosiery, wearing apparel and textile fiber products. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-31"/>

## From invoice lines to entered value

Customs value starts from the price actually paid or payable: the total payment by the buyer to, or for the benefit of, the seller, excluding charges for transportation, insurance and related services incident to the international shipment. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-48"/> <C id="the-supplier-invoice-47"/> Five amounts are then added, to the extent each is not already in the price: packing costs, selling commissions, assists, certain royalties or license fees, and resale proceeds that accrue to the seller. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-49"/> Here is how the invoice lines line up with the valuation rules:

| Invoice line (141.86) | Treatment under Part 152 |
|---|---|
| Unit price in the currency of purchase | Starting point: price actually paid or payable |
| Ocean or air freight and insurance to the U.S., itemized | Excluded, as charges incident to the international shipment |
| Cases, containers, coverings and packing | Packing costs incurred by the buyer are added; they include packing labor and materials |
| Commissions | Selling commissions incurred by the buyer are added |
| Assists not included in the price (dies, molds, tools, engineering) | Added at their apportioned value |
| Freight inside the U.S. after arrival | Not part of transaction value, if identified separately from the price |

*Source: eCFR — 19 CFR 141.86(a) and 152.102–152.103, checked Nov 9, 2026.* <C id="the-supplier-invoice-11"/> <C id="the-supplier-invoice-47"/> <C id="the-supplier-invoice-50"/> <C id="the-supplier-invoice-49"/> <C id="the-supplier-invoice-15"/> <C id="the-supplier-invoice-51"/>

Whether a commission paid to your own buying agent belongs on that line is covered in [sourcing agents](/guides/sourcing-agents/). If CBP tells you that computed value is needed for a product, the invoice must also carry a verified statement of computed value from the manufacturer or producer. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-43"/>

## What the importer adds

The importer or customs broker must include on the invoice or with the invoice data the appropriate HTSUS subheading. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-24"/> The importer must also show, on the invoice or an attached statement, the computation of all deductions from total invoice value, such as nondutiable charges, and all additions to invoice value. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-32"/> Handwritten notations by the importer or broker must be in blue or black ink, unless the invoice data goes in electronically. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-44"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: invoice total $25,600, of which $600 is ocean freight itemized separately and assumed to be a nondutiable charge; you separately paid $2,000 for a mold used only for this order; 8% duty.
Statement attached to the invoice: invoice total $25,600 − freight $600 + assist $2,000 = $27,000.
Duty: $27,000 × 8% = $2,160. If the freight had been buried in the unit price, there would be no itemized figure on the invoice to support deducting it.
</Callout>

Price changes after the fact do not fix the invoice: any rebate of, or other decrease in, the price made after the date of importation is disregarded in determining transaction value. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-28"/>

### When no commercial invoice is required

141.83(d) lists shipments that do not need one. Two of the listed exemptions are merchandise not intended for sale or any commercial use and not brought in on commission for another person, and goods returned after being exported for repair or alteration under subheadings 9802.00.40 and 9802.00.60. <C id="the-supplier-invoice-40"/> <C id="the-supplier-invoice-41"/> Product samples you intend to sell, or to use commercially, do not fit the first exemption.

## Where to read the invoice rules

Read 19 CFR Part 141, Subpart F (141.81 through 141.92) on the eCFR: 141.82 for installments, 141.83 for exemptions, 141.86 for invoice contents, 141.89 for product-specific information, 141.90 for the importer's notations and 141.91–141.92 for missing invoices and waivers. Check 19 CFR 152.102 for the definitions of price actually paid or payable and packing costs, and 152.103 for the additions that turn an invoice price into transaction value.
