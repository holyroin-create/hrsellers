You can ship products containing lithium batteries, but they are hazardous materials under federal rules: the battery type must have passed UN 38.3 testing, the package must stop short circuits and accidental switch-on, and most packages need a lithium battery mark. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-01"/> <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-03"/> <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-09"/> Batteries shipped inside or with the device they power get the easiest treatment, especially by air and through USPS; loose batteries get the strictest. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-16"/> <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-21"/> <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-22"/> The federal rule is 49 CFR 173.185. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-01"/>

## Three configurations, three rule sets

Before anything else, identify how the battery travels, because almost every limit below depends on it:

- **Contained in equipment** — installed in the product (a phone, a cordless tool, a toy with a sealed pack).
- **Packed with equipment** — in the same box as the device but not installed (a spare pack in the retail box).
- **By itself** — batteries or power packs sold without the device.

## Step one: testing and the test summary

Every lithium cell or battery must be of a type proven to meet part III, sub-section 38.3 of the UN Manual of Tests and Criteria. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-01"/> That applies whether you ship one unit or a pallet. Each manufacturer and subsequent distributor of cells or batteries manufactured on or after January 1, 2008 must make a test summary available. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-02"/> If you import finished goods, ask your supplier for the UN 38.3 test summary for the exact battery model before the first shipment and keep it with your product file. If you resell batteries, the "subsequent distributor" wording is worth reading closely.

Cells or batteries that do not conform to 173.185 may move only under conditions approved by the Associate Administrator named in the regulation. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-20"/> Prototypes and low production runs of not more than 100 cells or batteries a year have their own paragraph, and those packages are not permitted on passenger-carrying aircraft. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-18"/>

## The smaller-battery exception in 173.185(c)

Paragraph (c) is the exception for smaller cells or batteries. To qualify, each cell or battery must stay within these limits:

| Battery chemistry | Per cell | Per battery |
|---|---|---|
| Lithium ion | 20 Wh maximum | 100 Wh maximum |
| Lithium metal | 1 g lithium content maximum | 2 g lithium content maximum |

*Source: eCFR — 49 CFR 173.185(c)(1), checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-06"/> <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-08"/>

After December 31, 2015, each lithium ion battery shipped under this provision must show its watt-hour rating on the outside case. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-07"/> If a battery in a product you source has no Wh rating printed on it, that is a compliance gap to fix with the supplier, not something to estimate.

### Packaging requirements

Lithium cells or batteries, including those packed with or contained in equipment, must be packaged to prevent short circuits, damage from shifting or placement within the package, and accidental activation of the equipment. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-03"/> Except when the batteries are inside equipment, each package must withstand a 1.2 meter drop test in any orientation without damage to the cells or batteries, and packages of batteries not packed with or in equipment must not exceed 30 kg (66 pounds) gross. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-14"/> <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-13"/>

### The lithium battery mark

Packages under the exception must display the lithium battery mark, at least 100 mm wide by 100 mm high (a 100 mm by 70 mm version is allowed on packages too small for the full size). <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-09"/> <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-11"/> There is a small-shipment carve-out: no mark is needed for a consignment of two packages or fewer where each package contains not more than four cells or two batteries contained in equipment. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-10"/> Marks made to the version in effect on May 9, 2024 may be used until December 31, 2026, so replace older label stock before year-end. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-12"/>

## Air shipments

The package marking under the exception depends on configuration, and it decides whether the package may go on a passenger aircraft. Except for cells or batteries packed with or contained in equipment in quantities not exceeding 5 kg net, the outer package must bear a "forbidden for transport aboard passenger aircraft" marking (for example, "LITHIUM ION BATTERIES—FORBIDDEN FOR TRANSPORT ABOARD PASSENGER AIRCRAFT") or a CARGO AIRCRAFT ONLY label. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-15"/> For smaller batteries packed with or contained in equipment going by air, each package is limited to the minimum number of batteries needed to power the equipment plus two spare sets, with no more than 5 kg net of batteries in the completed package. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-16"/>

## Batteries over the exception limits

Batteries above the (c) limits, such as large power stations or e-bike packs, are fully regulated. They go in non-metallic inner packagings that completely enclose them, inside outer packagings that meet performance standards at the Packing Group II level. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-04"/> <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-05"/> Batteries weighing 12 kg (26.5 pounds) or more with a strong, impact-resistant outer casing have separate packaging options. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-19"/> The wider hazmat obligations that come with a fully regulated shipment are covered in our [hazmat and dangerous goods guide](/guides/hazmat-dangerous-goods/).

## Returns and damaged units

Cells or batteries that are damaged, or identified by the manufacturer as defective for safety reasons, may be transported by highway, rail or vessel only. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-17"/> On the USPS side, used, damaged or defective electronic devices containing lithium batteries go by domestic surface transportation only, and damaged, defective or recalled batteries are prohibited unless approved. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-27"/> <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-28"/> Never send a swollen or recalled unit back through an air service.

## USPS rules (Publication 52, Packaging Instruction 9D)

USPS sets its own domestic limits in Publication 52, Packaging Instruction 9D. Lithium metal and lithium-ion cells and batteries installed in or packed with equipment are mailable domestically by air or surface. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-21"/> Individual batteries mailed without equipment are generally limited to surface transportation, with a limited air exception for mail from and to Alaska, and must be in their originally sealed packaging. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-22"/>

| USPS domestic limit | Value |
|---|---|
| Lithium-ion, per cell | 20 Wh maximum |
| Lithium-ion, per battery | 100 Wh maximum |
| Lithium metal, per cell | 1.0 g lithium content maximum |
| Installed in or packed with equipment | No more than 8 cells or 2 batteries per mailpiece |
| Individual batteries without equipment | Mailpiece not over 5 pounds |

*Source: USPS — Publication 52, Packaging Instruction 9D, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-23"/> <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-24"/> <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-25"/> <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-26"/>

Mailpieces must be rigid, sealed and of adequate size, and labels and markings go on the address side unless specified otherwise. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-29"/> <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-30"/> If you also ship through a private carrier or a marketplace fulfillment program, check its battery requirements before the first shipment.

## How to confirm the rules for your products

The binding text is 49 CFR 173.185 on eCFR, and USPS's limits are in Publication 52, Packaging Instruction 9D. <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-01"/> <C id="shipping-lithium-batteries-21"/> Read the paragraph that matches your configuration rather than a summary, including this one. For anything outside the exceptions, or where a battery's status is unclear, put the question to the federal hazmat regulator or your carrier's dangerous goods desk in writing before shipping; a general guide cannot clear a package.
