Amazon announced a change to how the Featured Offer is awarded, and most of the coverage I have seen reads it as a bigger deal than the text supports. The announcement is four paragraphs. It removes one of two steps. It leaves the step that decides who actually wins untouched, and it says nothing about the pricing rules that suppress the box on healthy accounts every day. Read the four paragraphs before you change anything.

## What the announcement says

The post, titled "Updates to Featured Offer eligibility requirements" and published by Amazon's News account in Seller Forums on July 6, 2026, states that Amazon will begin removing seller eligibility requirements for the Featured Offer starting in July 2026, rolling out gradually across all Amazon stores globally and completing by the end of 2026. Existing offers are included automatically.

The mechanism it describes is a two-step process. Step one: Amazon determines which sellers are eligible, based on seller performance criteria. Step two: Amazon ranks all offers listed by eligible sellers for a product and selects the Featured Offer from that pool. Amazon says step one is no longer delivering additional value to customers and is being removed.

Then the sentence that matters: Amazon is not changing how the Featured Offer is selected. It will continue to evaluate offers and pick the Featured Offer on the criteria it says matter most to customers — competitive pricing, delivery speed, and performance — and being considered does not guarantee being featured.

## What it does not say

The announcement does not name the performance criteria being retired, and the replies under it asked repeatedly without an answer from Amazon. It does not say that pricing-based suppression ends. It does not say that a brand owner who is the only seller on a listing will now hold the box regardless of price — which is the question most of the 84 replies were really asking. On all three points the text is silent, so this article is too.

What can be said is where the remaining gates are, because Amazon publishes those elsewhere. Its own selling guide lists the reasons an offer becomes ineligible to be featured: out of stock, price too high, price too low — the last one triggering a suspected-pricing-error hold that the seller has to clear. And the FAQ on the Pricing Health page describes the price test itself: an offer is ineligible when its total price, item plus shipping, is above the Competitive External Price where Amazon has one, or otherwise significantly higher than recent prices. None of that is in scope of the July change.

## Who this actually helps

**Accounts that were locked out at the seller level.** New accounts, accounts with a defect history, accounts on a plan or status that kept them out of the pool. If your ASINs showed as not eligible in Manage All Inventory while your price sat at or below the Featured Offer price, the change is aimed at you. You enter the ranking. Whether you win it is still decided on price, delivery speed and performance, and an FBA or Seller Fulfilled Prime offer with fast delivery still has the structural edge described in the [Buy Box guide](/guides/buy-box-featured-offer/).

**Sole-seller brand owners.** Much of the anger in the replies came from private-label sellers who lose the box on their own listing. If the loss was an account gate, the change fixes it. If it was a price gate — a Competitive External Price scraped from another retailer, or a price that moved up after a promotion — nothing in this announcement changes the outcome. The [cross-channel price parity guide](/guides/cross-channel-price-parity/) covers why a promotion on one channel can suppress the box on Amazon.

**Everyone else.** More offers in the pool means more competition for the rotation on contested ASINs. Amazon has not published anything on the size of that effect and neither has anyone else with data, so I will not put a number on it. The direction is not in your favor if you were winning on account health rather than price.

## What to check in your account

1. **Turn on the eligibility column.** Inventory → Manage All Inventory → Preferences → enable "Featured Offer eligible." This is the check Amazon's own forum moderators point sellers to. Export it once now and once after the rollout reaches your store so you have a before-and-after.
2. **Read Pricing Health, both tabs.** Pricing → Pricing Health. Inactive Offers lists SKUs deactivated for a potential pricing error; Pricing Opportunities lists offers that are ineligible and offers that are eligible and priced within 5% of the Featured Offer price. Anything on the ineligible list after the rollout is there for price, not account, reasons. The [Pricing Health guide](/guides/pricing-health-featured-offer-fix-order/) walks the fix order.
3. **Do not reprice on the announcement.** Nothing about the price test changed. If a SKU is ineligible against a Competitive External Price you cannot match at margin, the decision is the same one it was before July: hold and sell without the box, or move the price and check the contribution margin first.
4. **Watch the rotation share, not the eligibility flag.** Eligibility going green is the change working. Your share of the box on a contested ASIN is the number that pays, and it is decided by the step Amazon did not touch.

Pull the eligibility column export this week, before the rollout reaches your store, so you can prove later which ASINs the change actually moved.
