Amazon confirms in public that coupons carry "a redemption cost each time a coupon is used" and that deals may carry costs, but it publishes the actual fee amounts only inside Seller Central, so check the current figures there before you create one. <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-10"/> <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-06"/> What you can plan from public sources is everything around the fee: which tool fits your goal, who is eligible, how long each runs, and how the charges appear in your settlement data. That is enough to decide whether a promotion can pay for itself before you see the fee screen.

## Deals, coupons and price discounts side by side

Amazon's seller guide describes deals, coupons and price discounts as separate tools, alongside promotions such as percentage-off, buy one get one, and social media promo codes. <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-22"/>

All of these need a Professional selling plan, which Amazon prices at $39.99 a month plus selling fees. <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-16"/>

## Deals: what is public

Amazon describes deals as limited-time promotional offers of two types. <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-01"/> A Lightning Deal "can run from 4 to 12 hours, as determined by Amazon"; a Best Deal "can run for a select number of days." <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-02"/> <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-03"/> Deals apply automatically without a code and can carry special merchandising such as a Deals Badge. <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-04"/>

To create deals you need a Professional plan and "an overall rating of at least four stars." <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-05"/> On cost, the public guide says only that "there may also be costs associated with your deal." <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-06"/>

The fee for a deal you ran does surface in data Amazon documents publicly. The Selling Partner API's Finances model defines a SellerDealPaymentEvent as "an event linked to the payment of a fee related to the specified deal," with an event type of SellerDealComplete and, in the published model, a fee type of RunLightningDealFee. <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-17"/> <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-18"/> If you reconcile settlements, those are the records to match to each deal.

## Coupons: what is public

A coupon gives shoppers a percentage off or a fixed amount off one product or a set of products. <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-07"/> Any shopper can use it; it appears on the product detail page, and shoppers clip it or enter a code. <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-08"/> Eligibility needs a Professional plan and an overall rating of at least 3.5 stars. <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-09"/>

Three timing and control rules shape the cost:

- **Per-use cost.** "There is a redemption cost each time a coupon is used." <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-10"/> More redemptions mean more fees, on top of the discount itself.
- **Usage limits.** You can set how many times a customer can use the coupon. <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-13"/>
- **Duration.** All coupons expire after 90 days, and Amazon suggests a duration of two to three months so customers have time to find it. <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-11"/> <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-12"/>

In settlement data, coupon charges arrive as CouponPaymentEvent records. Each carries the coupon's identifier, "the description provided by the seller when they created the coupon," and a ClipOrRedemptionCount — "the number of coupon clips or redemptions." <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-21"/> <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-19"/> Its TotalAmount is "the FeeComponent value plus the ChargeComponent value." <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-20"/> Giving each coupon a distinct description makes those records easy to tie back to a campaign.

## Price discounts

Price discounts are time-bound campaigns that offer a lower price "for up to 30 days on a limited number of units," either to Prime customers or to all customers. <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-14"/> They must be submitted for validation; discounts with a validated reference price appear with a strike-through price. <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-15"/> The public guide lists no fee for them.

## Can the promotion pay for itself?

Because the fee amounts sit in Seller Central, the useful pre-check is how much fee per unit you can absorb. The method below needs only your own numbers; enter Amazon's current fee once you see it.

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): coupon headroom">
Inputs: price $40.00; profit per unit before the promotion $12.00 (after product cost, referral and fulfillment fees); a $5.00 money-off coupon. Profit per redeemed unit before the coupon fee = $12.00 − $5.00 = $7.00. That $7.00 is the most that total coupon fees, divided by the number of redeemed units, can reach before each coupon sale stops earning. If the coupon also lifts sales of non-coupon units, count that separately — this check covers only the redeemed units.
</Callout>

| Discount on a $40 item | Profit left before fee (from $12) | Fee headroom per redeemed unit |
|---|---|---|
| $2 off (5%) | $10.00 | $10.00 |
| $4 off (10%) | $8.00 | $8.00 |
| $6 off (15%) | $6.00 | $6.00 |
| $8 off (20%) | $4.00 | $4.00 |

*Hypothetical inputs; arithmetic is ours. Compare the headroom with the current fee shown in Seller Central.* <C id="deal-and-coupon-fees-10"/>

For deals, apply the same idea to the whole event: total deal fee ÷ expected extra units = fee per extra unit, which must be below the profit each extra unit earns at the deal price.

## How to confirm the current fees

Fee amounts for deals and coupons are published in Seller Central, which is the only place to confirm them before submitting. Eligibility, durations and the coupon redemption-cost rule are on Amazon's [Guide to running Amazon seller promotions, coupons, and deals](https://sell.amazon.com/blog/seller-promotions). The settlement record formats are in the [Selling Partner API Finances model](https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/models/finances-api-model/financesV0.json). For event timing in Q4, see [Q4 2026 deal dates](/guides/q4-deal-deadlines-2026/).
