Whether you hire an outside reimbursement service or do the work in-house, the decision turns on two things Amazon controls: what data the job needs, and the conditions Amazon places on anyone who accesses that data for you. This page covers both from Amazon's own documentation. It does not name, review or rank any provider; it gives you the questions Amazon's rules let you ask of all of them.

## The work is built on data you already have

Every reimbursement review starts from Amazon's own records. The FBA Reimbursements Report lists your inventory reimbursements item by item, including the reason, and is updated daily. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-02"/> The Inventory Ledger Detailed View holds 18 months of inventory movements, including units lost, damaged and found. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-04"/> In financial data, these payments appear with the adjustment type FBAInventoryReimbursement. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-28"/>

Through the Selling Partner API, the Reimbursements Report can only be requested on demand, not scheduled. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-03"/> And a financial-events request whose date range is longer than 180 days returns an empty response, so longer histories must be pulled in slices. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-27"/> Neither limit stops you from doing the comparison yourself: the inputs are the same whether a person or a program reads them.

## What any API-connected provider must have from Amazon

A service that connects through the Selling Partner API does not get blanket access. Amazon says a developer must request and qualify for each role that gives access to the operations it needs. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-06"/> The Reimbursements Report requires two of them, Pricing and Amazon Fulfillment; the Amazon Fulfillment role is the one generally used for FBA reports and fulfillment operations. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-01"/> <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-05"/>

Some roles are restricted, meaning they involve sensitive information that may include personally identifiable information (PII). For those, the developer must give Amazon additional information about its data use and security controls. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-07"/> <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-08"/> Reimbursement work centers on inventory and financial records, so a request for restricted roles is worth a direct question.

## The data-protection rules that bind the provider

Amazon's SP-API compliance policies include the Data Protection Policy (DPP) and the Acceptable Use Policy (AUP); where translations differ, the English text prevails. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-12"/> <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-13"/> This page relies on the change notices Amazon publishes in its developer documentation. A notice effective November 25, 2025 updated both policies and renamed "Developer" to "Solution Provider" in them. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-14"/> <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-15"/>

| Requirement (DPP, effective Nov 25, 2025) | What it means for your data |
|---|---|
| Non-PII data not stored beyond 18 months unless the law requires longer | Your inventory and reimbursement data should not be kept indefinitely |
| Minimum log retention of 12 months | Access to your data should be traceable |
| Account lockout after ten failed logins | Basic access control is mandatory |
| TLS 1.2 or higher | Data in transit must be encrypted |
| Critical vulnerabilities fixed within 7 days, high-risk within 30 | Known security holes have deadlines |
| Third-party risk assessments of vendors and subcontractors | The provider's own subcontractors are in scope |

*Source: Amazon Selling Partner API changelog, "Updates to the Data Protection Policy and Acceptable Use Policy," checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-16"/> <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-17"/> <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-18"/> <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-19"/> <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-20"/> <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-21"/>

An earlier AUP update clarified when developers must disclose affiliated entities involved in their application or service. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-22"/> Amazon also publishes a voluntary security self-assessment, but says it does not replace its formal security assessment requirements. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-29"/>

## Authorization: how access starts and renews

You authorize an application starting from its website or from the Selling Partner Appstore. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-09"/> For public applications, reauthorization is required annually, which gives you a yearly review point. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-10"/> Reauthorization starts at the Manage Your Apps page in Seller Central. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-11"/> The steps for removing an app's access are not described on that developer page; check them in Seller Central.

## Claims are still yours

A provider can find discrepancies, but the claim outcome follows Amazon's FBA inventory reimbursement policy. The version cited here is a PDF Amazon hosts whose file is marked as a redline ("Redlined_FBA_Reimbursement_policy_US"), so it can carry old and new wording; only plainly stated rules are used, and the live policy in Seller Central is the one to check. You can file only for eligible items Amazon has not already reimbursed, and Amazon may decline a claim that does not follow the applicable process or omits requested information. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-23"/> <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-24"/> Amazon may decline a sourcing cost submission if documentation is forged, tampered with or illegible. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-25"/> If you don't agree with Amazon's valuation of a unit, the policy text says you can file a claim through Contact Us in Seller Central within 60 days after the reimbursement is issued. <C id="reimbursement-recovery-services-26"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs (your own figures): in one quarter, a review finds $1,800 of eligible reimbursements, and doing the work in-house takes you an estimated 6 hours. The provider's fee is written *f*, the share of recovered amounts in the quote you receive; providers set their own terms, so there is no standard rate to assume.

- Provider cost for the quarter: $1,800 × *f*.
- In-house cost: 6 hours of your time.
- Break-even value of your time: ($1,800 × *f*) ÷ 6 per hour. Each percentage point of *f* is worth $3 an hour of your time on these inputs.

If your time is worth less than that break-even figure, in-house wins on cost; if it is worth more, the provider may be the cheaper route — before weighing the data-access questions above. Small recoveries relative to your hours lower the break-even figure and push the answer toward the provider; large recoveries raise the percentage fee and push it toward in-house.
</Callout>
