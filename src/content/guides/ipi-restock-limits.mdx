Your restock limit decides how much you can send to FBA next month, and your Inventory Performance Index (IPI) is one input into it — but not the only one. Amazon says FBA capacity limits are influenced by your IPI score plus sales forecasts, shipment lead time and fulfillment center capacity. <C id="ipi-restock-limits-08"/> So the practical decision each month is: does the limit cover the reorder you are about to place, and if not, do you cut the order, clear space, or pay to reserve more? This page sets out what Amazon publishes on each of those levers.

## Restock limits and capacity limits are the same system now

Amazon's public FBA page still uses the phrase "restock limits," which it says help you determine how much inventory you can ship into its fulfillment network. <C id="ipi-restock-limits-04"/> Amazon's capacity announcement explains why it moved to one system: navigating two sets of limits measured differently — storage limits and restock limits — was confusing. <C id="ipi-restock-limits-07"/> The replacement offers a single monthly limit covering how much inventory you can send to and store at Amazon, and the announcement says the new capacity management system goes into effect March 1, 2023. <C id="ipi-restock-limits-06"/> <C id="ipi-restock-limits-18"/>

Two details matter when you plan purchase orders:

- **The unit is volume, not units.** Amazon sets limits and measures usage in cubic feet, while still showing usage in units for reference. <C id="ipi-restock-limits-09"/> <C id="ipi-restock-limits-10"/> A bulky SKU can therefore consume your limit far faster than its unit count suggests.
- **Going over has a cost.** Amazon says that if you exceed your restock limit, you may see an increase in your storage costs, and that storage costs are tied to your usage of space in its network. <C id="ipi-restock-limits-05"/> <C id="ipi-restock-limits-28"/>

The practical consequence: an order with a long production and freight lead time has to be sized against the estimate, not the confirmed number, and the estimate depends on how you manage the inventory already in Amazon's buildings.

## What the IPI measures, according to Amazon

Amazon describes the IPI as a metric that measures how efficiently you manage FBA inventory. <C id="ipi-restock-limits-01"/> Its public seller site says the score is based on how well you replenish popular products, maintain optimal inventory levels and fix listing problems, and that you should keep it high to lower storage costs. <C id="ipi-restock-limits-02"/> <C id="ipi-restock-limits-03"/> The FBA Dashboard in Seller Central shows the factors contributing to your IPI and your storage utilization ratio, along with recommendations such as restocking or creating outlet deals. <C id="ipi-restock-limits-19"/> <C id="ipi-restock-limits-20"/>

Amazon's public pages do not publish the IPI formula or its score range. One public threshold does exist: Amazon's FBA New Selection program requires an IPI of 300 or higher. <C id="returns-cost-and-badge-08"/> Any other cut-offs, and your current score, are shown inside Seller Central.

The public page does define the inventory conditions the three drivers point at:

| Condition | How Amazon defines it |
|---|---|
| Excess inventory | Over 90 days of supply, or at least one unit aged over 90 days (among other criteria) |
| Stranded inventory | Leads to lost sales, storage costs and lost storage capacity |
| Sell-through rate | Units sold divided by units received |
| Aged inventory charge | Charged monthly for items stored more than 181 days |

*Source: Amazon, sell.amazon.com inventory-management and FBA pages, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="ipi-restock-limits-21"/> <C id="ipi-restock-limits-22"/> <C id="ipi-restock-limits-23"/> <C id="ipi-restock-limits-26"/>

Amazon's general guidance is to keep about two months of supply available. <C id="ipi-restock-limits-27"/> About two months (roughly 60 days) of supply sits below the over-90-days-of-supply line in Amazon's excess-inventory definition.

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: in the last period you received 600 units of a SKU and sold 450; you now hold 600 units and sell about 5 per day; each unit occupies 0.25 cubic feet.

- Sell-through rate = 450 ÷ 600 = 0.75.
- Days of supply = 600 ÷ 5 = 120 days — above the 90-days-of-supply line Amazon uses for excess inventory.
- Two months of supply at 5 per day is about 300 units, or 75 cubic feet (300 × 0.25). Holding 600 units uses 150 cubic feet of your limit.

Holding 300 units instead of 600 would free about 75 cubic feet (150 − 75) for other SKUs. The arithmetic is illustrative only; your forecast and Amazon's recommendations will differ.
</Callout>

## Managing a score whose formula is not public

You cannot reverse-engineer a private formula, and you do not need to. Amazon names the three behaviours the score is built on, and each has a measure you can calculate from your own reports before the score moves. <C id="ipi-restock-limits-02"/> Manage those measures and treat the score as the result.

| What Amazon says the IPI rewards | Measure you can track yourself | Where the data comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Replenishing popular products | Whether your best sellers are in stock, against the suggested reorder quantities and dates | Restock Inventory Report <C id="ipi-restock-limits-24"/> |
| Keeping optimal inventory levels | Days of supply per SKU against the 90-day excess line, and sell-through (units sold ÷ units received) | Inventory health data; Amazon's definitions <C id="ipi-restock-limits-21"/> <C id="ipi-restock-limits-23"/> |
| Fixing listing problems | Count of stranded units and the days each has been stranded | Seller Central inventory pages; Amazon on stranded stock <C id="ipi-restock-limits-22"/> |

Three working rules follow:

- **Watch direction, not the number.** Without a published scale, a single score says little; the useful signal is whether it moves with the measures above. If the measures improve and the score does not, check the factor breakdown on the FBA Dashboard, which is the one place that shows what is pulling it. <C id="ipi-restock-limits-19"/>
- **Fix the cheapest driver first.** Stranded units cost sales, storage fees and capacity at once, and clearing them needs no new purchase. <C id="ipi-restock-limits-22"/>
- **Know the published line.** If you plan to launch new parent ASINs and want the New Selection benefits, the 300 threshold is a real constraint; plan the clean-up before the launch, not after. <C id="returns-cost-and-badge-08"/> <C id="returns-cost-and-badge-09"/>

The reservation route is a bid: you name the fee, and Amazon grants requests starting with the highest fee per cubic foot. <C id="ipi-restock-limits-15"/> <C id="ipi-restock-limits-16"/> Before bidding, compare the fee per cubic foot with the margin the extra cubic feet would actually earn in the month.

## Reports that feed the decision

If you or your developer pull data through Amazon's Selling Partner API, two documented report types map directly onto this decision. The Restock Inventory Report provides restock recommendations, suggested order quantities and reorder dates, updated in near real time. <C id="ipi-restock-limits-24"/> The FBA Manage Inventory Health Report (report type GET_FBA_INVENTORY_PLANNING_DATA) gives a consolidated view of sales over 7, 30, 60 and 90 days and of shipment statuses (working, received and shipped). <C id="ipi-restock-limits-25"/> Exporting both each month, just before the third-week announcement, gives you the sell-through and days-of-supply inputs used in the example above.

The scope here is Amazon's public material; your account's own limit and score are the authority. Re-check:

- Amazon's capacity announcement on the Sell on Amazon blog (sell.amazon.com/blog/announcements/fba-capacity) for how limits and reservation fees work.
- The FBA page on sell.amazon.com (FAQ: "How do restock limits and storage costs work?") and the inventory-management page under sell.amazon.com/learn.
- Inside Seller Central: the Capacity Monitor, the FBA Dashboard and the IPI page for your current numbers.
