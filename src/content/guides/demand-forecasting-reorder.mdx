Your reorder point is the stock level at which you must place the next purchase order so that new units arrive before you sell out: average daily sales times the full lead time from order to sellable at Amazon, plus a cushion of backup units. Amazon's own guidance supplies the guardrails around that number — keep about two months of supply, treat more than 90 days of supply as excess, and keep backup units against supply chain mishaps. <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-04"/> <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-02"/> <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-06"/> This page shows how to compute the point, which Amazon reports to feed it, and where Amazon's published thresholds should override your instinct to buy more.

## The three inputs

Amazon frames ordering decisions around the same variables: it notes that economic order quantity considers lead time, carrying costs and demand variability. <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-05"/> For a reorder point you need three of them in units you can measure.

A recent stockout drags a simple sales average down. Amazon's planning data separates the views you need: its Inventory Health report includes short-term and long-term historical days of supply as well as current days of supply. <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-19"/> <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-20"/> Seasonality is the other trap — Amazon notes that when the holidays roll around, demand can increase, so a summer average can understate the run into Q4. <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-12"/> Booking that peak is covered in [Q4 freight booking window](/guides/q4-freight-booking-window/).

Count inbound units when you compare stock to the trigger. Amazon's Restock Inventory report does this in its Total Days of Supply figure, which includes units from open shipments; if your own spreadsheet counts only sellable units, it will fire the reorder too early and stack purchase orders on top of shipments already on the water. <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-27"/> The reverse error — counting a shipment as inbound before the supplier has actually handed it to the carrier — fires the order too late.

Lead time must include every leg, not just the factory. The freight portion depends on mode; the deadlines and options per mode are in [Freight modes](/guides/freight-modes/).

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs (assumed): 600 units sold in the last 30 days, so 20 units per day; total lead time 60 days (25 production, 30 freight and customs, 5 receiving); 14 days of backup stock.

Reorder point = 20 × (60 + 14) = 1,480 units. When sellable plus inbound units fall to 1,480, place the order.

Guardrails: about two months of supply at this pace is 20 × 60 = 1,200 units; more than 90 days of supply — over 1,800 units — would put you in Amazon's excess range. By the time the order lands, about 280 units remain (1,480 − 20 × 60). If it brings 1,600 more, you hold 1,880 units, or 94 days — over the line. An order of 1,500 leaves 1,780 units, or 89 days.
</Callout>

Amazon says it uses machine learning models to offer recommendations through the FBA Restock tool, the Inventory Performance Index and the inventory age report. <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-07"/> The data behind those tools is also available as reports:

Treat the Restock report's suggested quantity as a second opinion, not an order. The Ledger report, which Amazon likens to a bank statement of your inventory, is where to check the "units received" in your sell-through math. <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-22"/> Amazon defines sell-through as units sold divided by units received. <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-01"/>

## The fees that punish both mistakes

Running too lean and too heavy both cost money at Amazon. On the lean side, the Inventory Health report flags whether the Low-Inventory-Level fee applied in the current week. <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-21"/> The thresholds for that fee are published inside Seller Central, so check them there rather than relying on a summary. On the heavy side, excess inventory can tie up resources and run up storage costs, and items stored more than 181 days draw a monthly aged-inventory charge. <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-11"/> <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-24"/> Amazon also links the Inventory Performance Index to cost: the score measures how efficiently you manage FBA inventory, is based on how well you replenish popular products, keep optimal inventory levels and fix listing problems, and Amazon advises keeping it high to lower storage costs. <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-08"/> <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-09"/> <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-10"/>

If the lean-side risk is the one you keep hitting, Amazon says AWD automated replenishment periodically monitors inventory levels in Prime-ready fulfillment centers and triggers replenishments; with auto-replenishment enabled it manages replenishments so you don't have to worry about FBA capacity limits, and leaving it defaulted gets a waiver for FBA storage-utilization surcharges and low-inventory-level costs. <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-26"/> <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-25"/>

## Choosing a method per SKU

Amazon describes ABC analysis as a way to categorize inventory based on importance, which lets you forecast top sellers carefully and run simple rules on the tail. <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-14"/> Just-in-Time, which Amazon describes as ordering products just before you need them, sits in tension with its advice to keep backup units against supply chain mishaps. <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-13"/> <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-06"/> Whatever the method, Amazon suggests auditing inventory at least one to two times a year so the counts feeding the forecast are right. <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-15"/> How inventory values flow into cost of goods sold is covered in [Accrual accounting and COGS](/guides/accrual-accounting-cogs/).

## How to confirm the current thresholds

Re-read Amazon's inventory-management guide on sell.amazon.com for the excess-inventory definition and the FBA page for the aged-inventory rule, and pull the Restock and Inventory Health reports from your own account. <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-02"/> <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-24"/> <C id="demand-forecasting-reorder-16"/> Restock limits and the Low-Inventory-Level fee thresholds are set inside Seller Central and can change; the arithmetic here is a planning method, and your own sales history is the input that decides it.
