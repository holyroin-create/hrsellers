Subscribe & Save carries no fee of its own: Amazon says there are "no additional selling costs" for the program, and participants pay standard selling fees and normal FBA costs. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-12"/> <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-13"/> The economic decision is therefore one number — the seller-funded discount, which starts at 0% and can be raised to 5% or 10%. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-08"/> This guide shows what each tier gives the customer, what it costs you per unit, and how much extra volume a higher tier has to bring in to break even.

## How the program works

Subscribe & Save gives Amazon customers regular deliveries and discounts. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-01"/> A subscribing customer sets up a recurring order on a cycle of weeks or months, and can change the shipping date or frequency and cancel whenever they want. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-14"/> <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-15"/> That last point shapes the economics: a subscription is a repeat order you can lose at any time, not a contract.

Brand Registry is a prerequisite; see [Brand Registry and trademarks](/guides/brand-registry-trademark/). All of this sits on top of a Professional selling plan at $39.99 a month plus selling fees. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-19"/>

### Extra criteria if you fulfill orders yourself

Auto-enrollment is for products sold through FBA, and Fulfilled by Merchant offers must also meet additional criteria for at least three consecutive months to be eligible. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-03"/> <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-21"/> Check the current list of those criteria in Seller Central before you request enrollment.

If a product does not show as eligible, the Manage products tab of the Subscribe & Save tool has a self-service eligibility check that says why. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-28"/>

## What each discount tier gives the customer

Eligible FBA products start at 0% seller funding. Amazon funds a 5% discount on Subscribe & Save orders of five items or more, and adds it on top of whatever the seller provides; the customer maximum is 15%. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-09"/> <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-20"/> <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-11"/>

The 0% tier is the important one to understand: Amazon states that at 0% funding "the customer doesn't get a discount on subscriptions of four items or fewer." <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-10"/> At 0%, a customer subscribing to one to four units a cycle sees no subscription saving.

## The margin cost of each tier

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: price $25.00; product and inbound cost $6.00; referral and FBA fees $9.00, held constant across tiers for simplicity; profit per unit with no discount $10.00. Subscription orders of one to four units.
</Callout>

| Seller funding | Discount per unit | Profit per unit | Extra volume needed to keep total profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | $0.00 | $10.00 | — |
| 5% | $1.25 | $8.75 | +14.3% ($10.00 ÷ $8.75) |
| 10% | $2.50 | $7.50 | +33.3% ($10.00 ÷ $7.50) |

*Tiers from Amazon's Subscribe & Save page, checked Nov 8, 2026; inputs and arithmetic are hypothetical.* <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-08"/>

Two things make the higher tiers easier to justify than the table suggests:

- **Conversion.** Amazon says that, on average, products with a 10%–15% discount "can drive up to a 1.8x increase in conversion," based on Amazon internal data. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-16"/> That is Amazon's average, not a forecast for your product, but it is the size of effect the discount has to produce.
- **Repeat orders.** At 10% in the example, a subscriber who stays for six monthly cycles brings 6 × $7.50 = $45.00 of profit, against $10.00 for a single one-off sale. The gain depends entirely on how long subscribers stay, since they can cancel at any time. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-15"/>

The same discount applies to subscribers who would have bought anyway, so the extra volume has to come from new or more frequent buyers to pay for it.

## Measuring whether a higher tier pays for itself

Amazon's own advice is to test: run some tests to find the right seller-funded level, starting with 5%. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-27"/> The test only works if you read the right numbers afterwards. The Subscribe & Save dashboard and its downloadable performance report carry them, <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-25"/> and Amazon's Replenishment API, available to vendors and FBA selling partners, defines each metric. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-29"/>

| Metric (API name) | What it measures | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| SIGNUP_CONVERSION_BY_SELLER_FUNDING | Subscriptions created in a discount tier as a share of all new orders, one-time purchases included, past 12 months <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-38"/> | Does 10% convert more shoppers into subscribers than 5%? |
| REVENUE_PENETRATION_BY_SELLER_FUNDING | Share of subscription revenue from each seller-funded tier, last 12 months <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-37"/> | Which tier your subscription revenue actually comes from |
| SUBSCRIBER_RETENTION | Share of subscriptions retained 30 and 90 days after creation <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-36"/> | How many repeat cycles the discount buys |
| SUBSCRIBER_NON_SUBSCRIBER_AVERAGE_REVENUE | Average revenue per subscriber vs. non-subscriber, past 12 months for sellers <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-39"/> | Whether subscribers are worth more than one-time buyers |
| NOT_DELIVERED_DUE_TO_OOS | Share of units not shipped because of stock-outs <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-34"/> | How much of the program you are losing to inventory |

*Source: Amazon Selling Partner API models, Replenishment API 2022-11-07, checked Nov 9, 2026.* Metrics cover only the trailing two years. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-44"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): reading a 5% test with retention">
Inputs: the per-unit figures from the table above ($8.75 profit at 5% funding, $10.00 on a one-off sale); 100 new subscriptions in a month, each for one unit a month; your SUBSCRIBER_RETENTION shows 60 still active at 90 days. Assume the 40 that cancelled each took one delivery and the 60 took three over the first 90 days. Deliveries: 40 × 1 + 60 × 3 = 220. Profit: 220 × $8.75 = $1,925.00. If, without the program, those 100 customers had each bought once at full price, profit would be 100 × $10.00 = $1,000.00. The 5% tier wins here only because of the repeat deliveries; at 20% retention (20 × 3 + 80 × 1 = 140 deliveries, $1,225.00) the margin is thin.
</Callout>

## Eligibility status and stock-outs: what happens to existing subscribers

Eligibility needs an in-stock rate greater than 90%, and Amazon calls keeping products in stock "a key consideration" for the program. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-06"/> <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-17"/> The dashboard's inventory planning section shows missed deliveries and the revenue lost to stock-outs, <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-26"/> which the API calls LOST_REVENUE_DUE_TO_OOS: the revenue that would have been generated without the stock-out. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-35"/>

Losing eligibility does not always mean the same thing. The status of each offer decides what happens to the subscriptions you already have:

INELIGIBLE is the expensive one: the subscriber base you paid discounts to build is cancelled. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-31"/> Two early-warning flags look 30 days ahead: deliveries paused because of a pricing issue, <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-41"/> and deliveries paused because the offer is not buyable. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-42"/> For replenishment planning, the forecast metrics give expected subscription units for the next 30, 60 and 90 days. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-40"/>

To reconcile subscription sales in your own books, order items sold through the program carry the program value SUBSCRIBE_AND_SAVE in Amazon's order data. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-43"/>

## Coupons for subscribers

Amazon lists coupons that "encourage customers to subscribe to a product for additional savings" among its coupon types. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-18"/> A time-limited subscriber coupon can test demand before you commit to a permanent higher funding tier. Coupons carry their own redemption cost; see [Amazon deal and coupon fees](/guides/deal-and-coupon-fees/).

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What Amazon's rules say |
|---|---|
| Expecting a program fee | There are no additional selling costs; standard fees apply. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-12"/> |
| Assuming 0% still gives subscribers a discount | Not on subscriptions of four items or fewer. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-10"/> |
| Raising price before enrolling | Eligibility requires an average selling price without exaggerated increases. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-07"/> |
| Ignoring stock levels | An in-stock rate above 90% is part of eligibility. <C id="subscribe-and-save-economics-06"/> |
