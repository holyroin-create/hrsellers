Amazon's shopping assistant reads your listing when shoppers ask about your product: on product pages, Amazon says it generates answers from listing details, customer reviews and community Q&As. <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-03"/> Rufus no longer exists under that name; on May 13, 2026 it was renamed Alexa for Shopping. <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-04"/> We found no Amazon page that explains how the assistant selects or ranks products, so the practical question for a seller is narrower: which of those inputs you control, and whether your listing answers the questions shoppers ask.

## What the assistant is, in Amazon's words

Amazon described Rufus at launch as an expert shopping assistant trained on its product catalog and information from across the web. <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-02"/> Its May 2026 successor is asked directly in the main Amazon search bar, <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-06"/> and all Amazon customers can use it free when signed in. <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-07"/> Amazon says it can create a custom shopping guide that compares features, prices and reviews across Amazon and the web. <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-08"/>

Amazon's scale figures explain why sellers care. Amazon says Rufus helped over 300 million customers in 2025, <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-09"/> and that customers who use Rufus during a shopping journey are 60%+ more likely to convert. <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-10"/> That is Amazon's figure for shoppers who use the assistant, not a measure of what any listing change will do.

The assistant also personalizes. Amazon says it now has account memory, understanding customers based on their individual shopping activity, <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-12"/> and that a related feature, Help Me Decide, analyzes browsing activity, searches, shopping history and preferences. <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-19"/>

It also looks beyond Amazon. Amazon says Rufus brings up products from other merchants as well as products on Amazon, <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-11"/> and its Shop Direct feature surfaces products not currently sold on Amazon. <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-13"/>

## What shoppers can do with it that affects you

Amazon lists features that change how a shopper evaluates your offer:

- A 30-, 90- and 365-day price history. <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-14"/>
- Price alerts that complete the purchase with the default payment method when the item reaches the target price. <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-15"/>
- AI overviews on product detail pages. <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-16"/>

Price history means short-lived price spikes and markdowns are visible to the shopper. Auto-buy at a target price means a shopper may have committed to buy your item once its price drops to a set point.

## Making your listing details answer real questions

We found no Amazon rulebook for listings written specifically for the assistant, so work from the inputs Amazon names. The listing details are yours; make them specific enough that a factual question has a factual answer on the page.

## Using Amazon's AI listing tools to fill the gaps

Amazon offers its own generative AI tools for writing listing details, and many sellers use them. In September 2024 Amazon said more than 900,000 selling partners had used these tools, <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-22"/> and that sellers accepted the AI-generated content with little or no editing about 90% of the time. <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-23"/> High acceptance is not the same as accuracy. Amazon's seller guide to these tools says you are responsible for making any changes needed so your products are accurately represented, <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-24"/> and that using the proposed content means you agree you have reviewed it and that it is accurate, complete and compliant. <C id="rufus-listing-optimization-25"/>

That matters more because the assistant answers from listing details: a wrong dimension or an overstated feature in the listing becomes an input to the answer the shopper reads. Check generated text against the product itself, line by line, before accepting it.

## What the Amazon sources do not cover

The Amazon announcements we read describe what the assistant uses and what shoppers can do with it. We found none that describes how it picks which products to recommend, how it weighs reviews against listing details, or whether any listing field counts for more than another. Advice that claims to know those weights cites no Amazon source we could find, so judge it on that basis.

Title structure and lengths are covered in [Amazon title limits](/guides/title-limit-984-million/), and what Amazon says about ordinary search ranking is in [what Amazon says about search](/guides/listing-optimisation-a10/). Attempts to shape reviews are a separate risk; see [review manipulation risk](/guides/review-manipulation-risk/).

## How to confirm

Amazon describes the assistant only in its own announcements: re-read "Meet Alexa for Shopping" (May 13, 2026) and the original Rufus announcement on aboutamazon.com, which carries the renaming note, for what the assistant uses. Amazon updates these features often, so check the dates on those pages. For your own listing, the Search Query Performance dashboard in Brand Analytics and the listing's community Q&As are the Amazon-provided evidence of what shoppers are actually asking.
