Every algorithm change on Amazon produces the same two crowds: sellers who ignore it and sellers who panic-buy tools because of it. Rufus deserves neither response. The numbers say it is big and growing; the same numbers say classic search still carries four fifths of discovery. What follows is the fee-desk version of the story: what is reported, what is confirmed, and what a listing owner should actually change — ordered by cost, starting at free.

## The scale, dated and attributed

<Figure value="38%" kind="cost" source="Reported share of Black Friday 2025 shopping sessions involving Rufus; checked August 2026">
  One analysis reports Rufus present in 38% of Black Friday 2025 sessions, with users 60% more likely to complete a purchase — and Amazon projecting $10 billion in annualized sales impact. Marketing-adjacent figures, but directionally consistent across sources.
</Figure>

The countervailing number belongs in the same paragraph: as of early 2026, traditional search is still reported at roughly **80-85% of discovery traffic**, with Rufus growing from about 13% of searches in late 2024 toward projections of 35%. Both things are true at once — which is why the right response is re-prioritising listing work, not rebuilding it.

One honesty note this site owes you: the COSMO connection — the "commonsense knowledge graph" widely described as the intelligence under Rufus — **is not officially confirmed by Amazon**. Practitioners infer it from patent filings and observed behaviour. We treat it as probable and label it accordingly; be suspicious of anyone selling certainty here.

## What actually changes in how a listing is read

Classic ranking matches words. The AI layer is described as matching **intent**: a query like "quiet vacuum for a small apartment with a shedding dog" gets decomposed into use cases and constraints, then matched against what your listing *demonstrates*, not just what it repeats. Three reported consequences follow:

| Reported behaviour | Practical meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Structured backend fields are preferred over bullet claims — bullets read as "marketing fluff", fields as verified data | The Category Listing Report's blank columns are unclaimed ranking surface |
| A+ image alt text and on-image text are read as data by the AI layer | "Woman crushing ice with tamper tool" tells the machine compatibility facts a pretty lifestyle shot never will |
| Listings that justify value — durability, lifespan, cost-per-use — are reported more likely to be recommended | Price justification is no longer just conversion copy; it is machine-readable evidence |

## The action list, ordered by cost

**Free, this week — backend attributes.** Download your Category Listing Report from Seller Central and fill every relevant blank column with **standard taxonomy values** — the reported guidance is blunt: if the dropdown says Navy Blue, do not write Midnight Ocean. Pay special attention to the "Specific Uses for Product" field: specific use cases ("searing", "induction cooking") over generic room names, because these are described as hard-coding the product into intent buckets.

**Free, this week — answer Rufus's own questions.** Open your listing as a shopper and read the questions Rufus prompts under your product. That list is Amazon telling you what the AI wants to answer about your product. Make sure bullets, A+ and Q&A answer them directly — it is the cheapest market research on the platform.

**Free, next content pass — images as data.** Rewrite A+ alt text descriptively and put key noun phrases on your infographic images. Keep the main image clean for humans and [compliance](/guides/main-image-compliance/); make the second image carry the machine-readable text.

**Already-planned money, redirected.** Nothing above requires a subscription. Your existing [listing and conversion work](/guides/listing-optimisation-a10/) still does most of the lifting — conversion rate remains the lever that moves your [cost per ad order](/guides/amazon-ppc-benchmarks/) more than any bid change, and the AI layer reads the same improved listing.

**Measurement, with expectations set.** Brand-registered sellers are reported to find partial Rufus attribution inside Brand Analytics. Incomplete and directional — read it monthly for trend, not weekly for decisions.

## What not to do

Do not delete keyword work: four fifths of discovery is still classic search. Do not buy "AI readiness" tooling before exhausting the free structural list above — the vendors quoting the scariest Rufus numbers are, without exception, selling the antidote. And do not chase certainty about COSMO internals that Amazon has not published; optimise for the observable behaviour, dated, and revisit when the reporting moves.
