A foreign-trade zone (FTZ) is a designated site where foreign goods can be stored or processed without first being entered into US customs territory. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-02" /> Activated zones are treated as outside the customs territory of the United States for the tariff and entry purposes specified in the Foreign-Trade Zones Act. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-01" /> Duty on foreign goods generally applies only when they enter customs territory for domestic consumption, which is what makes zones a duty-deferral tool. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-02" />

The two rulebooks are 15 CFR Part 400 (the Foreign-Trade Zones Board: who gets a zone and what activity is authorized) and 19 CFR Part 146 (CBP: how merchandise is admitted, given a status, and transferred out). For a marketplace seller, the practical questions are what status your goods carry inside the zone, which duty rate applies when they leave, and how often you have to file entries.

<Callout type="note" title="Scope of this page">
This page explains the zone regulations. Using a zone means working with a zone operator under its own approvals, which this page does not cover.
</Callout>

## Who runs a zone

The Foreign-Trade Zones Board consists of the Secretary of Commerce, as chairman, and the Secretary of the Treasury. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-05" /> A zone can include a subzone, defined as a site or group of sites established for a specific use. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-23" /> Under the alternative site framework, a magnet site is meant to serve multiple operators or users, while a usage-driven site is set up for a single operator or user. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-22" />

Day to day, an operator runs the zone or subzone under an agreement with the zone grantee, and a user is the person or firm storing, handling or processing merchandise there. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-10" /> A seller using a zone through a third-party warehouse would normally be a user, not the operator. Before merchandise can be handled in zone status, the site must be activated, which means approval by the grantee and the CBP port director. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-11" />

## Zone status: the decision that sets your duty

Every foreign item in a zone carries a status, and that status determines how it is classified and valued when it leaves.

| Status | Who it covers | Key consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Privileged foreign | Foreign merchandise not yet manipulated or manufactured so as to change its tariff classification; applied for on Customs Form 214 | Classified at the rate in force on the date the application is filed; cannot be abandoned |
| Nonprivileged foreign | Foreign merchandise that is neither privileged foreign nor zone-restricted | Classified according to its character, condition and quantity when entered |
| Domestic | US goods with internal revenue taxes paid, duty-paid imports, previously entered duty-free goods | Granted under 19 CFR 146.43 |
| Zone-restricted | Goods taken in solely for exportation, destruction or storage | Cannot be abandoned once granted |

*Source: Office of the Federal Register / National Archives (eCFR), 19 CFR Part 146 subparts D and F, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="foreign-trade-zones-12" /> <C id="foreign-trade-zones-13" /> <C id="foreign-trade-zones-14" /> <C id="foreign-trade-zones-15" /> <C id="foreign-trade-zones-16" /> <C id="foreign-trade-zones-17" />

### Privileged foreign status

Privileged foreign status is granted on application to the port director, using Customs Form 214, for foreign merchandise that has not been manipulated or manufactured so as to change its tariff classification; once granted, it cannot be abandoned. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-12" /> Its effect is to freeze the duty rate: privileged foreign merchandise is classified at the rate of duty and tax in force on the date the application is filed. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-13" />

Status is not always optional. Items subject to antidumping or countervailing duty orders must be placed in privileged foreign status when admitted to a zone or subzone. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-08" /> See [antidumping duties](/guides/antidumping-duties/) for how those orders work.

### Nonprivileged foreign status

Nonprivileged foreign status is the default for foreign merchandise that is neither privileged foreign nor zone-restricted. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-15" /> Nonprivileged goods are classified according to their character, condition and quantity at the point they are constructively transferred to customs territory. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-14" />

### Domestic and zone-restricted status

Domestic status covers US goods with applicable internal revenue taxes paid, previously imported goods on which duty and tax were paid, and previously entered duty-free goods. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-16" /> Zone-restricted status applies to merchandise taken in solely for exportation, destruction or storage, and it cannot be abandoned once granted. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-17" />

## The duty rate choice

When goods leave a zone for domestic consumption, the importer ordinarily has a choice: pay duty at the rate applicable to the foreign material as admitted, or, if the material was used in production activity, at the rate applicable to the emerging product. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-03" /> Production is defined as activity involving the substantial transformation of a foreign article resulting in a new and different article, or a change in the article's condition that changes its customs classification or its eligibility for entry for consumption. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-06" /> Production cannot start without prior authorization from the Board. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-04" />

Privileged foreign status locks in the component rate on the application date. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-13" /> Nonprivileged foreign status lets the finished product's classification apply at entry. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-14" /> That is the mechanism behind the rate choice.

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical inputs)">
An importer admits $50,000 of a component dutiable at a hypothetical 8% and assembles it in the zone, with Board authorization, into a product dutiable at a hypothetical 2%. Entered under nonprivileged foreign status, duty is based on the finished product: 2% x $50,000 = $1,000 (valuation of the finished product can differ in practice). Had the component been put in privileged foreign status, duty would be 8% x $50,000 = $4,000. The rates here are illustrative only; your HTSUS rates decide which status helps.
</Callout>

## Leaving the zone: entry, warehouse, weekly entry, export

An accepted entry constructively transfers merchandise to customs territory even while it is still physically inside the zone. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-20" /> Privileged foreign merchandise may not be entered for warehouse from a zone. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-19" />

For zones that change goods continuously, the port director may allow a single entry on Customs Form 3461 covering estimated removals during a calendar week. This applies to merchandise manufactured or otherwise changed in the zone, excluding packing alone, within 24 hours before physical transfer. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-18" /> The regulation ties this weekly procedure to goods changed in the zone. Storage-only inventory pulled for marketplace fulfillment should not assume it qualifies.

Goods do not have to enter US commerce at all. Merchandise moved into a zone for export in zone-restricted status may be considered exported for purposes including customs drawback and federal excise tax rebates. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-07" /> Compare the refund route in [duty drawback](/guides/duty-drawback/).

## CBP supervision and records

Zone operations are supervised by CBP: the port director may direct officers to supervise transactions through methods such as periodic audits, inventory counts or spot checks. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-21" /> Customs territory itself is defined as the territory of the US in which the general tariff laws apply. <C id="foreign-trade-zones-09" />

## What to check yourself

1. Read 15 CFR 400.1(c) and 400.14 on eCFR to confirm the duty rate choice and the Board authorization rule for production.
2. Read 19 CFR 146.41 to 146.44 to confirm which status your goods can take and what cannot be abandoned.
3. Read 19 CFR 146.65 for how privileged and nonprivileged merchandise is classified and valued at entry.
4. Look up the HTSUS rate for both the input and the finished product before choosing a status (see [HTS classification basics](/guides/hts-classification-basics/)).
5. Read 19 CFR 146.63(c) with the zone operator to confirm whether weekly entry is available for your activity.
6. Check bond requirements for your entries (see [customs bonds](/guides/customs-bonds/)).
