A foreign-trade zone (FTZ) lets you bring foreign goods into a secured US site without paying duty on arrival. Under 19 U.S.C. 81c(a), merchandise in a zone is not subject to US customs laws, except as the statute provides <C id="foreign-trade-zones-11"/>. Once the goods are sent into US customs territory, they become subject to the laws and regulations for imported merchandise <C id="foreign-trade-zones-13"/>. That is when duty is owed. For a seller, the practical gains are cash flow, re-export without paying duty first, and fewer entry filings. The trade-off is that every unit sits inside a supervised, audited inventory system.

## Who's who in a zone

The vocabulary matters, because your contract and your obligations depend on your role.

| Role | What the regulation says |
|---|---|
| Zone | All sites and subzones designated under the sponsorship of a zone grantee <C id="foreign-trade-zones-01"/> |
| Subzone | A site or group of sites established for a specific use <C id="foreign-trade-zones-02"/> |
| Usage-driven site | A site set up for a single operator or user under the alternative site framework <C id="foreign-trade-zones-34"/> |
| Grantee | The corporate recipient of the grant of authority for the zone <C id="foreign-trade-zones-03"/> |
| Operator | Operates within a zone or subzone under an agreement with the zone grantee <C id="foreign-trade-zones-04"/> |
| User | A party using a zone under agreement with a zone operator <C id="foreign-trade-zones-05"/> |

*Source: eCFR, 15 CFR 400.2, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="foreign-trade-zones-01"/>

CBP's own definition of a user is a person or firm using a zone or subzone for storage, handling or processing of merchandise <C id="foreign-trade-zones-33"/>.

## Who can use a zone

Zones are open to the public. Each zone must be operated as a public utility <C id="foreign-trade-zones-06"/>. The grantee must give uniform treatment to all who may apply to use the zone <C id="foreign-trade-zones-07"/>. A small importer has the same right to apply as a large one, though the operator's rates still apply. Those rates and charges must be fair and reasonable <C id="foreign-trade-zones-37"/>.

Two limits shape what you can do inside:

- **No retail.** Retail trade is prohibited in activated areas of zones <C id="foreign-trade-zones-10"/>. CBP's rules say the same, apart from a narrow statutory exception <C id="foreign-trade-zones-31"/>. You cannot sell to consumers from inside the zone. You ship from it.
- **Production needs approval.** Production means substantially transforming a foreign article into a new and different article <C id="foreign-trade-zones-09"/>. It cannot happen in a zone without prior FTZ Board authorization <C id="foreign-trade-zones-08"/>.

Storage and light handling are allowed by statute. Goods may be stored, sold, exhibited, broken up, repacked, assembled, distributed, sorted, graded and cleaned <C id="foreign-trade-zones-12"/>. Breaking bulk and repacking for fulfillment are both on that list.

## The four zone statuses

Every item in a zone has one of four statuses: nonprivileged foreign, privileged foreign, zone restricted or domestic <C id="foreign-trade-zones-35"/>. That status decides how duty is calculated when the goods leave, or whether they can enter US commerce at all.

**Privileged foreign.** You may apply when the goods are admitted, or at any time afterward before they are manipulated or manufactured in the zone in a way that changes their tariff classification <C id="foreign-trade-zones-14"/>. Classification uses the rate in force on the date of filing <C id="foreign-trade-zones-16"/>. The status cannot be abandoned <C id="foreign-trade-zones-15"/>. If you change your mind later, you cannot switch back.

**Nonprivileged foreign.** Foreign merchandise in a zone that lacks privileged foreign status falls here <C id="foreign-trade-zones-36"/>. Classification follows the merchandise's condition at the time the entry or entry summary is filed <C id="foreign-trade-zones-17"/>. If processing turns components into a finished good with a lower duty rate, this status lets the lower rate apply. For production activity, the Board authorization described above still applies <C id="foreign-trade-zones-08"/>.

**Domestic.** US-produced goods, previously imported goods on which duty and tax were paid, and goods previously entered free of duty and tax can have domestic status and later return to customs territory free of quotas, duty or tax <C id="foreign-trade-zones-18"/>.

**Zone-restricted.** This status is for merchandise taken into a zone for the sole purpose of exportation, destruction or storage <C id="foreign-trade-zones-19"/>. Such goods may not be removed for domestic consumption unless the Board finds it in the public interest <C id="foreign-trade-zones-20"/>. The status cannot be abandoned once granted <C id="foreign-trade-zones-21"/>. It does link to drawback: admitting drawback goods to a zone in zone-restricted status can count as exportation <C id="foreign-trade-zones-22"/>.

## Duty deferral: what you defer, and when it ends

Nothing is owed while foreign goods stay in the zone. The obligation starts when goods are sent into customs territory <C id="foreign-trade-zones-13"/>. A seller who brings in a full container but sends 10% a week to fulfillment pays duty on each 10% as it leaves. Goods in zone-restricted status may not be removed to customs territory for domestic consumption except where the Board finds it in the public interest <C id="foreign-trade-zones-20"/>.

### Worked example (hypothetical)

Inputs: a container with $100,000 of dutiable value at a 10% rate is admitted to a zone. You withdraw $25,000 of value to a US fulfillment center each week for four weeks.

- Without a zone: $10,000 in duty is due at entry.
- With a zone: $2,500 is due on each weekly withdrawal, because duty attaches only as goods enter customs territory <C id="foreign-trade-zones-13"/>.
- If $25,000 of that stock is instead exported from the zone, the duty on that portion ($2,500) is not paid, since that portion is never sent into customs territory <C id="foreign-trade-zones-13"/>.

## Weekly entry

Under 19 U.S.C. 1484(i), merchandise withdrawn from a zone during any 7-day period can be covered by a single estimated entry or release <C id="foreign-trade-zones-23"/>. The option belongs to the operator or user of the zone <C id="foreign-trade-zones-24"/>.

The weekly option does not cover merchandise whose entry is prohibited by law <C id="foreign-trade-zones-27"/>. For ordinary (non-weekly) withdrawals filed on CBP Form 3461, the entry summary is due within 10 business days after the time of entry <C id="foreign-trade-zones-29"/>.

## Supervision and records

Zones are supervised sites. Customs officers are assigned to zones as needed to maintain appropriate supervision <C id="foreign-trade-zones-32"/>. The operator must keep records of zone merchandise for 5 years after the merchandise is removed from the zone <C id="foreign-trade-zones-30"/>. Ask the operator what data it needs from you to meet that duty, and get it written into your agreement.

## How to confirm it for your zone

Read 19 CFR Part 146 on the eCFR for zone status, entry and recordkeeping, and 15 CFR Part 400 for who may operate, use or produce in a zone. For the weekly entry statute, read 19 U.S.C. 1484(i) on GovInfo. Then compare the operator's written procedures against those texts.
