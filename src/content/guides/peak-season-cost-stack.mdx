During peak season, several of Amazon's published charges can land on the same unit. On the Multi-Channel Fulfillment (MCF) rate card, a unit shipped in November can carry peak-month storage, a peak fulfillment rate, a 3.5% surcharge on MCF fulfillment fees, and — if it comes back — a return fee. <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-01"/> <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-06"/> <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-08"/> <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-12"/> The worked example below adds them up for one hypothetical unit using Amazon's published MCF rate card. Amazon states that MCF storage and other non-fulfillment fees are the same as FBA's. <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-05"/> FBA's own peak fulfillment amounts are announced in Seller Central; swap them in if you are modeling orders placed on Amazon.

## The layers

Each layer has its own trigger. Knowing what triggers it tells you which ones you can control.

| Layer | Trigger | Published amount |
|---|---|---|
| Peak storage | Units stored in October, November or December | $2.40 per cu ft per month (large standard) |
| Peak fulfillment rate | Order ships October 15, 2026 – January 14, 2027 | $10.97 (large standard, 1–2 lb, "1" column) |
| Fuel and logistics surcharge | MCF fulfillment fees in the US; card does not say whether peak rates include it | 3.5% |
| Lithium battery fee | Item is or contains a lithium battery | $0.11 per unit |
| Remote-area surcharge | Ships to Alaska, Hawaii, Guam, Puerto Rico or the Virgin Islands | 100% (standard-size) |
| Return fee | Item is returned through MCF | $4.70 (large standard, 1–2 lb) |

*Source: Amazon Supply Chain, 2026 MCF rate card, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-01"/> <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-07"/> <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-06"/> <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-08"/> <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-09"/> <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-11"/> <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-12"/>

The calendar behind these triggers — which date starts and ends each one — is on our [2026 peak surcharge schedule](/guides/peak-surcharge-2026-schedule/).

## How storage is charged per unit

Storage is the layer that builds up before the sale. Amazon calculates monthly storage "by cubic foot," based on the average number of units stored per month. <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-02"/> <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-03"/> The rate depends on product type, size tier, time of year and storage utilization ratio. <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-04"/> For large standard, non-dangerous goods, that means $0.78 per cubic foot from January to September and $2.40 from October to December. <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-01"/>

A unit's storage cost is its volume times the monthly rate, for every month it sits in a fulfillment center. Inventory sent in early for the holidays pays peak rates for every peak month it waits.

Storage is counted as a full month for September, October and November. | Storage, November | 0.2 cu ft × $2.40 | $0.48 |

| Fuel and logistics surcharge | 3.5% × $10.97 | $0.38 |
| **Total if kept** | | **$12.47** |

| **Total if returned** | | **$17.17** |

*Rates from Amazon Supply Chain's 2026 MCF rate card, checked Nov 8, 2026; arithmetic is ours and hypothetical (unrounded subtotals).* <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-01"/> <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-06"/> <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-08"/> <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-12"/>

The fulfillment fee and its surcharge make up about 91% of the kept-unit total; peak storage adds just under a dollar. The return fee, if it happens, adds more than a third again. Amazon says MCF return fees include "return logistics transportation, regrading, and restocking." <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-13"/> The rate card does not say whether the 3.5% surcharge applies to return fees, so the example leaves it off.

The remote-area line shows why destination matters in peak season: Amazon calculates that surcharge after adding any lithium battery fee to the base fulfillment fee, and for a standard-size item it is 100%, so it doubles the base. <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-10"/> <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-11"/> The multi-unit lines show the opposite: order size cuts the largest layer roughly in half. <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-06"/> The peak rate table by tier and speed is in our [holiday peak fulfillment fee guide](/guides/holiday-peak-fulfilment-fee/).

## Conditional layers that can join the stack

Two more surcharges can be added in peak season, depending on how much stock you hold and for how long:

- **Aged inventory surcharge**, for inventory stored longer than 181 days. <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-14"/> A unit received in April and still unsold in November would cross that line.
- **Storage utilization surcharge**, when storage utilization surpasses 22 weeks of cover. <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-15"/>

Amazon's public MCF fee policy gives the triggers but not the amounts; check those in Seller Central and add them to the stack only when your account meets the trigger.

## How to confirm the stack for your SKUs

Open the MCF rate card at supplychain.amazon.com/docs/rate-card (linked from supplychain.amazon.com/mcf/pricing) for fulfillment, return and storage rates. <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-07"/> For FBA orders on Amazon, open Seller Central and go to Reports, then Fulfillment, then Fee Preview. <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-19"/> To check the storage layer, the FBA Storage Fees Report shows month_of_charge, storage_rate and utilization_surcharge_rate for each ASIN. <C id="peak-season-cost-stack-18"/> This example uses Amazon's published rates. Your actual charges depend on how Amazon measures and classifies each item, and a dispute over those goes through a Seller Central support case.
