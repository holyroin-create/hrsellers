If you sell on Amazon stores outside the US and want the money in a US bank account, someone has to convert it. Amazon's own option, **Amazon Currency Converter for Sellers**, charges a volume-based percentage that is **1.50% on cross-currency net proceeds under $500,000** a year and drops in steps to **0.75% above $10 million**. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-03"/> <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-04"/> <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-07"/> The choices that matter are which tool converts your money, whether your accounts are linked so volume counts together, and how you record the dollar amount for taxes.

## What the converter does

Amazon describes the converter as automatic: it converts proceeds so you receive funds in 20+ currencies, across 19 Amazon stores. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-01"/> <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-02"/> Setup is a bank account. In Seller Central you go to the gear icon, then Account Info, Payment information and Deposit methods, and add the account. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-13"/> After that, funds generally arrive in your local bank account in 1 to 5 business days. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-12"/>

The exchange rate is not fixed at the time of sale. Amazon says it uses the rate applied by its third-party service provider on the day the transfer to your bank account is initiated. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-11"/> A sale made weeks earlier is converted at the rate on payout day, not the rate when the customer paid. If part of your balance is deferred, as explained in the [DD+7 and payout reserve guide](/guides/dd7-payout-reserve/), that money is converted only when it is eventually paid out.

## The fee tiers

Pricing is based on your total cross-currency net proceeds during the past 12 months. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-03"/> Amazon defines that as total cross-currency net proceeds across all Amazon stores for the past 12 months. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-15"/>

Some currencies don't follow the tiers. Sellers receiving JPY pay a flat 2.0%, and sellers receiving AED, CHF, MXN, KRW or TWD pay a flat 1.5% regardless of tier. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-08"/> <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-09"/> Amazon also notes that volume-based pricing is not available in all stores and currencies, so check the rate shown for your store. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-14"/>

### Linking accounts

Asked whether volume can be combined across stores, Amazon's answer is yes, as long as your global accounts are linked. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-10"/> For a seller with several separate store accounts, linking can move the combined volume into a lower tier.

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: a US seller has $310,000 of 12-month cross-currency net proceeds from one European store account and $250,000 from another, and receives USD. **Assumption (not stated by Amazon):** the tier rate applies to the whole amount converted, not marginally within each tier.

**Unlinked:** each account is under $500,000, so each pays 1.50%: $560,000 × 1.50% = **$8,400**.

**Linked:** combined volume is $560,000, in the $500,000–$1,000,000 tier at 1.25%: $560,000 × 1.25% = **$7,000**.

Difference: **$1,400** on that volume, from the tier change alone. Confirm on Amazon's page how your own accounts are counted before relying on a figure like this.
</Callout>

## Seller Wallet: the other Amazon tool

Seller Wallet solves a different problem. It holds US-store proceeds and lets you transfer them to global bank accounts and convert USD into 20+ currencies. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-16"/> To enroll you should have a US-store seller account in good account health, and Amazon lists further eligibility requirements, including proof of business establishment in a supported country or region. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-17"/> A separate Seller Wallet EUR exists for Germany, France, Spain, Italy, the Netherlands, Ireland and Belgium. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-18"/>

## Seeing the conversion in Amazon's data

Each settlement shows two totals, which is where you can measure what conversion cost you. In Amazon's Finances API, a settlement's original total is stated in the currency of the marketplace where the transactions occurred, while its converted total is in the currency of the marketplace in which the funds were disbursed. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-31"/> <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-32"/> The fund transfer date records when the disbursement was initiated, and it appears only once the settlement is closed. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-33"/> Because the converter's rate is set on the day the transfer is initiated, that date is the one to pair with the exchange rate when you check a payout. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-11"/>

Dividing the converted total by the original total gives the effective rate you received after the fee. Comparing it with a reference rate for the same day, from a bank for example, shows how much the conversion took in practice; the IRS says rates can generally be obtained from banks and US Embassies. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-30"/>

## Recording converted payouts for US taxes

Whatever tool you use, the IRS wants dollars. Amounts on a US tax return must be expressed in US dollars, and your functional currency is generally the dollar unless you are required to use a foreign currency. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-26"/> <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-27"/> With a dollar functional currency, you translate items using the exchange rate prevailing when you receive, pay or accrue them. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-28"/> Where more than one rate exists, the IRS says to use the one that most properly reflects your income, and it notes that rates are generally available from banks and US Embassies. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-29"/> <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-30"/>

This is why the payout amount and the sales amount can differ in your books. The sale is recorded in dollars at the rate when you accrue it, while the payout is converted at the provider's rate on transfer day. <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-28"/> <C id="currency-conversion-payouts-11"/> If you keep accrual books, the [accrual accounting and COGS guide](/guides/accrual-accounting-cogs/) explains how timing differences flow through.

## How to confirm the current fees

Fees and tiers change, so re-check Amazon's [Currency Converter for Sellers page](https://sell.amazon.com/programs/amazon-currency-converter) and [Seller Wallet page](https://sell.amazon.com/tools/seller-wallet) before comparing options; the full terms sit in Seller Central help. For translating amounts on your return, the IRS page on [foreign currency and exchange rates](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-currency-and-currency-exchange-rates) is the reference. Whether you have a non-dollar functional currency is a question for a tax professional, not something this page settles.
