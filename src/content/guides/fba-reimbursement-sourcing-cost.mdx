When Amazon loses or damages your FBA inventory before it is sold, the sourcing-cost text of its reimbursement policy pays the item's sourcing cost — what you paid for the product, not what it cost to land it in the US. <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-03"/> <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-02"/> The decision for you is what figure to put on file for each SKU, how to support it, and whether a reimbursement you received is worth disputing.

This page is built on Amazon's FBA inventory reimbursement policy as published in a marked-up ("redlined") PDF on Amazon's own media server. A redline shows old and new wording together, and the PDF also contains passages that value shipment-to-Amazon, removals and unsellable-unit claims on estimated sale proceeds, which the document does not reconcile with the sourcing-cost text. Only the sourcing-cost rules are summarized here, as the PDF states them. The live policy is published inside Seller Central and should be checked there before you rely on any figure.

## What "sourcing cost" means

Amazon defines sourcing cost as your cost to source a product from a manufacturer, wholesaler or reseller — or, if you make it yourself, your cost to produce it. <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-01"/> It excludes shipping, handling, customs duties and other costs. <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-02"/>

That exclusion matters for importers. If your books carry a landed cost that includes freight and duty (see [accrual accounting and COGS](/guides/accrual-accounting-cogs/)), the sourcing-cost figure covers only the product-cost part of it.

| Cost component | In Amazon's sourcing cost? |
|---|---|
| Cost to source from a manufacturer, wholesaler or reseller | Yes |
| Your cost to produce the item, if you are the manufacturer | Yes |
| Shipping and handling | No |
| Customs duties | No |
| Other costs | No |

*Source: Amazon, FBA inventory reimbursement policy, redlined PDF on m.media-amazon.com, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-01"/> <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-02"/>

Two exceptions change the number. In the pre-order section, units that were already unsellable when they were lost, damaged or removed are paid at a reduced estimated cost, and Amazon may ask for documentation. <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-13"/> And when Amazon refunded or replaced an item on your FBA order, a customer return claim is valued on the refund amount or the replacement's sales price on the original order, minus applicable fees, rather than on your sourcing cost. <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-14"/> So a sourcing cost on file matters most for inbound shortfalls, removals and warehouse losses — the events that happen before a customer is involved.

The item also has to be registered in FBA at the time it is lost or damaged, among the policy's other eligibility conditions. <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-19"/> For the inbound side of those conditions, see [shipment discrepancy claims](/guides/shipment-discrepancy-claims/).

The policy points to a Seller Central help page for accepted proof-of-value documentation; check that list there. <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-20"/> Whatever you submit, a supplier invoice that shows the SKU's unit price separately from freight or duty lines matches the definition most directly.

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: factory price $6.40 per unit; ocean freight allocated $1.10 per unit; customs duty $0.60 per unit; landed cost therefore $8.10. A fulfillment center loses 12 units, and your $6.40 sourcing cost is on file and accepted.

- Reimbursement at sourcing cost: 12 × $6.40 = $76.80.
- Landed cost of the same units: 12 × $8.10 = $97.20.
- Unrecovered: $97.20 − $76.80 = $20.40, all of it freight and duty, which the definition excludes.

If no figure were on file, the payout would rest on Amazon's own cost estimate instead.
</Callout>

## Keeping the figure current

A sourcing cost is not a one-time entry. Supplier prices move between production runs, and the policy's fallback works against you when they rise: if Amazon declines a new figure, it pays on the previously approved amount. <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-11"/> Amazon also said that after March 31, 2025 it would refresh its own cost estimates periodically, so a SKU without a figure on file can be valued differently over time. <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-12"/> <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-06"/>

A simple record per SKU keeps updates defensible:

- the supplier invoice number and date for the current production run;
- the unit price on that invoice, with freight, duty and handling kept on separate lines;
- the date you entered or changed the figure in Seller Central and the outcome;
- for your own manufacturing, the cost build-up behind the per-unit figure.

Updating the figure each time the invoice price changes, rather than in a batch later, keeps each submission tied to a single document — and avoids repeat requests inside the 30-day window. <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-10"/>

## Disputing an amount

If you don't agree with Amazon's valuation of a unit, the policy says you can file a claim through Contact Us in Seller Central within 60 days after the reimbursement was issued; in the redlined PDF this sentence sits in the post-order section, so confirm the channel for your claim type in Seller Central. <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-15"/> Having already received a decision on a cost update request or amount dispute for the same item within the last 30 days, with no new information, is one of the listed reasons to decline, so a second attempt should add a document the first one lacked. <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-10"/>

To find what was paid and why, the FBA Reimbursements Report lists itemized reimbursements with the reason and is updated daily. <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-18"/> In Amazon's financial event data, these payments carry the adjustment type FBAInventoryReimbursement. <C id="fba-reimbursement-sourcing-cost-17"/> Matching the two each month, and noting the 60-day date for each line, helps you spot values to question in time.

## How to confirm

This page is not accounting or legal advice; how a reimbursement is booked is a question for your accountant, and the amount itself is settled only through Amazon's dispute process. Re-check:

- The live FBA inventory reimbursement policy and the "Manage Your Sourcing cost" page in Seller Central.
- Amazon's policy PDF on m.media-amazon.com (file Redlined_FBA_Reimbursement_policy_US.pdf) for the sourcing-cost wording quoted here.
- SP-API docs on developer-docs.amazon: "Report type values – FBA" (Reimbursements Report) and the listFinancialEvents reference (FBAInventoryReimbursement).
