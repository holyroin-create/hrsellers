Selling an Amazon FBA business is, for tax purposes, a sale of many assets at once. The IRS says the sale of a business is usually not a sale of one asset, and that a lump-sum sale is treated as a sale of each individual asset. <C id="selling-your-fba-business-01"/> <C id="selling-your-fba-business-02"/> The decisions that follow from that are how to split the price between inventory, other assets and goodwill, how to write that split into the contract, and what to file and close once the deal is done.

One part is outside IRS and SBA rules: whether a Seller Central account, its listings and its history can be transferred to a buyer is governed by Amazon's own policies, which are published inside Seller Central and should be checked there before you agree how the business will be handed over.

## Why the price allocation matters

Because each asset is treated as sold separately, each piece can be taxed differently. Inventory is the clearest case for a product seller: the IRS says the sale of inventory results in ordinary income or loss. <C id="selling-your-fba-business-05"/> If the business is held in a partnership and you sell your partnership interest instead of the assets, the IRS treats that interest as a capital asset when sold, but the part of any gain or loss from unrealized receivables or inventory items is treated as ordinary gain or loss. <C id="selling-your-fba-business-06"/> How the deal is structured therefore changes the tax result.

The IRS fixes the method. Except for assets exchanged under nontaxable exchange rules, both the buyer and seller of a business must use the residual method to allocate the price. <C id="selling-your-fba-business-03"/> The parties can agree the allocation in writing, and that agreement is binding on both unless the IRS determines the amounts are not appropriate. <C id="selling-your-fba-business-04"/>

## The seven asset classes on Form 8594

Form 8594 is required when goodwill or going concern value attaches, or could attach, to the assets sold. <C id="selling-your-fba-business-07"/> The price is allocated through seven classes in order, with whatever is left landing in the last.

| Class | What it holds (IRS definition, shortened) | Typical FBA example |
|---|---|---|
| I | Cash and general deposit accounts | Cash left in the business account |
| II–III | Actively traded personal property; assets marked to market at least annually | — |
| IV | Stock in trade or property properly included in inventory | Units in FBA and at the warehouse |
| V | All assets not in another class | Equipment, furniture |
| VI | Section 197 intangibles other than goodwill and going concern value | A covenant not to compete, other identified intangibles |
| VII | Goodwill and going concern value | The residual |

*Source: IRS — Instructions for Form 8594, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="selling-your-fba-business-10"/> <C id="selling-your-fba-business-31"/> <C id="selling-your-fba-business-32"/> <C id="selling-your-fba-business-11"/> <C id="selling-your-fba-business-15"/> <C id="selling-your-fba-business-12"/> <C id="selling-your-fba-business-14"/>

If your deal includes a non-compete, note where it goes. The instructions list a covenant not to compete entered into in connection with acquiring an interest in a trade or business among the section 197 intangibles in Class VI. <C id="selling-your-fba-business-13"/> For trademarks and brand assets, see the [Brand Registry and trademark guide](/guides/brand-registry-trademark/).

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: agreed price $400,000. Cash transferred $10,000 (Class I). Inventory valued at $120,000 (Class IV). Equipment $5,000 (Class V). Non-compete and other identified intangibles $25,000 (Class VI).

Residual method: $400,000 − $10,000 − $120,000 − $5,000 − $25,000 = **$240,000** to Class VII goodwill and going concern value.

The sale of the $120,000 of inventory produces ordinary income or loss for the seller. If buyer and seller agree this allocation in writing, the agreement is binding on both unless the IRS determines the amounts are not appropriate.
</Callout>

## Filing after the sale

Generally both the purchaser and the seller must file Form 8594 and attach it to their income tax returns, for the year in which the sale date occurred. <C id="selling-your-fba-business-08"/> <C id="selling-your-fba-business-09"/> If part of the price is adjusted later, for example under an earn-out, and the consideration changes in a later year, a new Form 8594 with Part III is filed for each year an increase or decrease occurs. <C id="selling-your-fba-business-16"/> Not filing a correct form by the return due date can bring penalties unless there is reasonable cause. <C id="selling-your-fba-business-17"/>

The IRS's closing checklist lists Form 8594 if you sell your business, and Form 4797 for each year you sell or exchange property used in the business. <C id="selling-your-fba-business-18"/> <C id="selling-your-fba-business-19"/> A sole proprietor also files Schedule C for the final year's profit or loss. <C id="selling-your-fba-business-30"/> If you kept the books on an accrual basis, the [accrual accounting and COGS guide](/guides/accrual-accounting-cogs/) shows how inventory carrying values feed that final return.

## Closing out the old business

After the sale, the seller's side has its own clean-up. SBA lists cancelling registrations, permits, licenses and business names; handling final income tax and sales tax returns; and cancelling the EIN while notifying federal and state tax agencies. <C id="selling-your-fba-business-26"/> <C id="selling-your-fba-business-27"/> <C id="selling-your-fba-business-28"/> The IRS will not close the business account until all necessary returns are filed and taxes paid. <C id="selling-your-fba-business-20"/> The sales tax part matters for marketplace sellers registered in several states; the [sales tax nexus guide](/guides/sales-tax-nexus/) explains where those registrations come from.

## How to confirm the rules for your sale

The IRS sources are [Publication 544](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p544) (Sale of a Business), the [Form 8594 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8594) and the [Closing a business](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/closing-a-business) page; SBA's [Close or sell your business](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/manage-your-business/close-or-sell-your-business) page covers the process. Amazon's account transfer rules are in Seller Central. The allocation and deal structure are decided with your attorney and tax adviser; this page explains the framework they will use.
