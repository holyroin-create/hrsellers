Selling items together on Amazon takes one of three shapes, and each has different rules: a physical bundle that you pack as a single unit under its own ASIN, a Virtual Bundle that links products you already stock, and the federal labeling that applies the moment several units share one retail package. <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-07"/> <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-08"/> <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-01"/> Choose the shape first; the listing, the barcode and the label follow from it.

## Three ways to sell items together

Amazon describes product bundling as a strategy where you group and sell multiple complementary products together. <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-06"/>

Amazon's full bundling policy for physical bundles, including what can be combined and how a bundle must be described, is published inside Seller Central; check it there before creating a bundle ASIN.

## Virtual Bundles: the Brand Registry route

Brand Registry describes Virtual Bundles as a way to group and sell multiple products in one listing so customers can buy more from your brand. <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-01"/> They are a benefit for brands enrolled in Brand Registry and FBA, <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-02"/> and Amazon lists these conditions:

- Bundles must include 2 to 5 different products. <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-03"/>
- All products in the bundle must be in new condition. <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-04"/>
- The total cost must be equal to or less than the combined price of each item bought individually. <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-05"/>
- Some categories, such as gift cards, digital items and subscription products, are not allowed. <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-09"/>

Brand Registry itself is free, but it requires an active registered trademark or a pending trademark application for your brand name or logo. <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-10"/> <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-22"/> See the [Brand Registry guide](/guides/brand-registry-trademark/) for that step. Because a Virtual Bundle does not require packing the products together as one unit, it can test a combination before you commit to a combined box.

## Labeling a physical multipack or bundle

Once units share one retail package, the Fair Packaging and Labeling Act rules in 16 CFR Part 500 set how the net quantity is declared. The regulation distinguishes three package types:

| Package type | Definition | Net quantity statement |
|---|---|---|
| Multiunit (500.27) | Two or more individually packaged or labeled units of an identical commodity in the same quantity | Number of units, quantity of each unit, and total quantity |
| Variety (500.28) | Two or more units of similar but not identical commodities | Units of each identical commodity with its weight or measure; total quantity last |
| Combination (500.29) | Two or more units of dissimilar commodities | Weight, measure or count suited to each package or unit |

*Source: eCFR, 16 CFR 500.27–500.29, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-11"/> <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-12"/> <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-15"/> <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-16"/> <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-17"/> <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-18"/>

The regulation's own multiunit example reads: "6 Bars, Net Wt. 3.4 ozs. (96.3 g) each, Total Net Wt. 1 lb. 4.4 oz. (578 g)." <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-13"/> If the inner units are unlabeled and not meant for separate sale, the declaration may give the total quantity of the multipack without regard to inner packaging. <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-14"/>

A three-pack of the same 8 oz lotion is a multiunit package. A lighter-fluid-and-flints set is the regulation's own combination example. <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-18"/> The [packaging design guide](/guides/packaging-design-fba/) covers where the net quantity sits on the panel.

## Barcodes: when a combination needs a new GTIN

GS1's GTIN Management Standard treats a fixed set of products sold together as a predefined assortment: a fixed composition of two or more trade items combined and sold as a single physical trade item. <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-19"/> Changing, adding or replacing any item in that assortment requires a new GTIN. <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-19"/> At the case level, changing the number of trade items in a case also requires a new GTIN; GS1's example is a case moving from 9 trade items to 12. <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-20"/>

In practice, swapping one item in a fixed gift set means a new GTIN. Costs and options for licensing GTINs are covered in the [GS1 barcodes guide](/guides/gs1-barcodes-fnsku/).

## The fee side: why packs change the per-unit math

Amazon charges referral fees as a percentage of the total price or a minimum amount, whichever is greater; <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-23"/> in Home and Kitchen, for example, the rate is 15% with a $0.30 minimum. <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-21"/> FBA fulfillment costs are based on the product's weights and dimensions. <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-24"/> A physical multipack is one unit with its own weight and dimensions, so it carries one fulfillment fee instead of several.

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): three singles vs one 3-pack">
Inputs: a Home and Kitchen item sold at $6.00 as a single, or as a 3-pack at $16.00; referral fee 15%, $0.30 minimum. <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-21"/>

- Three singles: referral 15% × $6.00 = $0.90 each, so $2.70 for three orders, plus three separate fulfillment fees.
- One 3-pack: referral 15% × $16.00 = $2.40, plus one fulfillment fee for a heavier, larger unit.

The referral saving is $0.30. The fulfillment fee can make a larger difference: compare one fee for the pack against three fees for singles in the Revenue Calculator. The [fixed per-unit fee guide](/guides/fbt-fixed-dollar-problem/) works through why this matters most for low-priced items.
</Callout>

If the pack will ship in its own packaging under Amazon's SIPP program, note that products grouped for one SIPP test must have the same number of units in a pack, <C id="bundles-and-multipacks-25"/> so a single and a 3-pack are certified separately. Details are in the [SIPP guide](/guides/sipp-packaging-program/).
