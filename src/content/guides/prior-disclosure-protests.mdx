The right fix depends on two questions. Did the mistake cost the government money, or cost you money? And has the entry liquidated yet? If you overpaid, the tool is usually a protest. It must be filed within 180 days of the liquidation or other decision <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-17"/>. If you underpaid or filed false information, the tool is a prior disclosure. It is made before, or without knowledge of, a formal investigation <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-25"/>, and it can reduce a negligence penalty to interest on the unpaid duty <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-38"/>. The protest clock usually starts at liquidation, so start there.

## Step one: know where your entry is in the liquidation cycle

Liquidation is the final computation or ascertainment of duties on an entry <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-01"/>. The official record is CBP's electronic posting on its website, which is deemed the legal evidence of liquidation <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-02"/>. A courtesy notice from CBP is not a formal notice of liquidation <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-03"/>. Do not wait for a courtesy notice to start counting.

Unless the period is extended, an entry not liquidated within one year from the date of entry is deemed liquidated by operation of law <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-04"/>. CBP can extend that period. Each extension may run up to 1 year <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-05"/>, and total extensions may not exceed 3 years <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-06"/>. Unless liquidation is suspended, an entry not liquidated within four years of entry is deemed liquidated <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-07"/>. You can ask for an extension yourself, in writing and before the period expires, if you show good cause <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-08"/>. This can help if you need time to gather facts before liquidation.

## Protests: getting money back after liquidation

### What you can protest

Protestable decisions include the classification and the rate and amount of duties <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-13"/>. They also include the liquidation or reliquidation of an entry <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-14"/>. A wrong HTS code that inflated duty is a classification issue you can raise against the liquidation.

### Who files, how, and when

The importer or consignee on the entry papers, or their surety, may file, along with certain others <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-15"/>. A written protest goes on CBP Form 19, or electronically through a CBP-authorized system <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-16"/>. It must include the entry number and date <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-18"/>. You can amend it until the filing period expires <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-19"/>. File early and add detail later if you need to.

A protest is not your only option in the first three months. CBP may voluntarily reliquidate an entry within 90 days from the date notice of liquidation, or deemed liquidation, is given <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-10"/>. Voluntary reliquidation is at CBP's discretion, so file the protest anyway while the 180 days are running.

A denied protest is not the end. You may contest the denial in the United States Court of International Trade <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-23"/>. The deadline is 180 days after the denial notice is mailed or the protest is deemed denied <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-24"/>.

<Callout type="warning" title="The clerical-error trap">
For entries made on or after December 18, 2004, CBP has no authority to reliquidate an entry to correct a clerical error, mistake of fact or other inadvertence unless a valid protest was filed <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-12"/>. A typo that cost you duty has to be protested inside the 180 days.
</Callout>

## Prior disclosure: limiting penalties when you underpaid

Errors in the government's favor carry penalty exposure under 19 CFR 162.73. The table compares the regulatory maximums with and without a valid prior disclosure.

| Culpability | Maximum without prior disclosure | Maximum with prior disclosure |
|---|---|---|
| Negligence | Lesser of domestic value or 2× the lost duties <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-33"/>; 20% of dutiable value if no loss <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-34"/> | Interest on the lost duties, from the liquidation date <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-38"/> |
| Gross negligence | Lesser of domestic value or 4× the lost duties <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-35"/> | Interest on the lost duties, from the liquidation date <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-38"/> |
| Fraud | Domestic value of the merchandise <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-36"/> | 1× the lost duties <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-37"/> |

*Source: eCFR, 19 CFR 162.73, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-33"/>

### What a valid disclosure requires

- **Timing.** You must disclose to a Customs officer before, or without knowledge of, the start of a formal investigation <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-25"/>. If you claim you did not know an investigation had started, you have the burden to prove it <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-32"/>.
- **Form.** A written disclosure should be addressed to the Commissioner of Customs <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-31"/>. If you disclose orally, you must confirm it in writing within 10 days <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-26"/>. A mailed disclosure counts as made at the time of mailing <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-30"/>, which matters when you are racing an inquiry.
- **Missing facts.** You can disclose before you have every number. Information unknown at the time of disclosure can be supplied within 30 days, and you can request extensions <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-27"/>.
- **Money.** You must tender the actual loss of duties, taxes and fees <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-28"/>. You can pay at the time of disclosure or within 30 days after CBP notifies you in writing of its calculation <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-29"/>.

### Worked example (hypothetical)

Inputs: over 18 months you declared a product under an HTS code at 3.9% when the correct rate is 7.5%. Total entered value was $200,000, so you underpaid $7,200 in duty (3.6% × $200,000). The error was negligent, and CBP has not contacted you.

- Without disclosure, the regulatory cap is the lesser of domestic value or 2 × $7,200 = $14,400 <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-33"/>.
- With a valid prior disclosure and tender, the penalty is limited to interest on the $7,200, computed from each entry's liquidation date <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-38"/>.
- A valid disclosure also requires you to tender the $7,200 actual loss of duties <C id="prior-disclosure-protests-28"/>.

## How to confirm the deadlines for your entry

Read the texts on the eCFR: 19 CFR Part 159, Subpart A, for liquidation; 19 CFR Part 174 for protests; 19 CFR 162.73–162.74 for penalties and prior disclosure; and 19 CFR Part 173 for reliquidation and refunds before liquidation. Check your entry's liquidation date against CBP's electronic posting before you count any deadline.
