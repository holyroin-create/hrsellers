A seller who buys goods for resale deducts their cost through cost of goods sold (COGS), not as an ordinary expense, and figures it on Schedule C, lines 35 through 42. <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-11" /> <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-12" /> Whether you must keep a formal inventory, and therefore use an accrual method for purchases and sales, depends on the small business taxpayer test: for 2025 returns, IRS Publication 334 sets it at average annual gross receipts of $31 million or less for the 3 prior tax years, and you must not be a tax shelter. <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-01" /> <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-05" />

If you meet that test, you can choose not to keep an inventory, but you still need a method that clearly reflects income: either treat inventory as non-incidental materials and supplies, or follow your financial accounting treatment. <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-06" /> This page walks through those choices and the COGS lines, using the current revisions of Publication 334 (2025) and Publication 538.

<Callout type="note" title="Scope of this page">
This page summarizes IRS Publication 334 (2025) and Publication 538 (Rev. January 2022). It is not tax advice; for your return, a CPA or enrolled agent is the route. Publication 334 is written for individuals who file Schedule C.
</Callout>

## Which publications and which year

Publication 334 is titled "Tax Guide for Small Business (For Individuals Who Use Schedule C)," and the current revision is "for use in preparing 2025 Returns." <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-01" /> Publication 538, Accounting Periods and Methods, is still the January 2022 revision; its gross receipts test text gives "$26 million or less (indexed for inflation)," a figure frozen at that revision date. It is not the 2025 amount. <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-20" /> For tax year 2025, use the Publication 334 figure.

## Cash, accrual, and the inventory rule

Under the cash method you report income you "actually or constructively receive during your tax year." <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-02" /> "Under an accrual method of accounting, you generally report income in the year earned." <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-03" /> When you are required to account for inventories, "you must generally use an accrual method of accounting for your purchases and sales." <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-04" />

The exception is for small business taxpayers. Qualification requires "average annual gross receipts of $31 million or less for the 3 prior tax years (indexed for inflation)" and not being a tax shelter as defined in section 448(d)(3). <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-05" />

### The two options if you qualify

  {t: "Option A", d: "Treat inventory as non-incidental materials and supplies"},
  {t: "Option B", d: "Follow your financial accounting treatment of inventories"},
  {t: "Otherwise", d: "Keep inventories; accrual method for purchases and sales"}

- **Non-incidental materials and supplies.** Inventory treated this way "is used or consumed in your business in the year you provide" it to customers. <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-07" /> For a reseller, that ties the cost to the year the goods go to customers, not the year of purchase.
- **Financial accounting treatment.** Your treatment "is determined with regard to the method of accounting you use" in your applicable financial statement, or, without one, in your books and records under your own accounting procedures. <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-08" />

## What goes into cost of goods sold

"If you make or buy goods to sell, you can deduct the cost of goods sold from your gross receipts." <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-11" /> Schedule C lays out the calculation:

| Schedule C line | Item |
|---|---|
| 35 | Inventory at beginning of year |
| 36 | Purchases less cost of items withdrawn for personal use |
| 37 | Cost of labor |
| 38 | Materials and supplies |
| 39 | Other costs |
| 40 | Add lines 35 through 39 |
| 41 | Inventory at end of year |
| 42 | Cost of goods sold |

*Source: IRS, Publication 334 (2025), chapter 6, "Figuring Cost of Goods Sold on Schedule C, Lines 35 Through 42," checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-12" />

Line-by-line points from Publication 334 that matter for importers and resellers:

- **Line 35.** Beginning inventory is "usually identical" to the prior year's closing inventory; a difference must be explained in a schedule attached to the return. <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-13" />
- **Line 36.** "If you are a merchant, use the cost of all merchandise you bought for sale." You use the prices you pay, not stated prices, and deduct all returns and allowances from total purchases. <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-14" /> Merchandise withdrawn for personal or family use must be excluded. <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-15" />
- **Line 37.** "Labor costs are usually an element of cost of goods sold only in a manufacturing or mining business." <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-16" />
- **Line 39.** Freight-in, express-in and cartage-in on merchandise bought for sale are part of cost of goods sold, as are containers and packages that are an integral part of a manufactured product. <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-17" /> Overhead includes items "such as rent, heat, light, power, insurance" when they belong in COGS. <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-18" />

Inbound freight on merchandise bought for sale is therefore part of cost of goods sold rather than a separate shipping expense. Duties and tariffs on imported goods are covered on [the 2026 tariff stack page](/guides/tariff-stack-2026/); how they are classified for tax purposes is a question for your preparer.

### Uniform capitalization

Under the uniform capitalization rules, "you must capitalize the direct costs and part of the indirect costs for production or resale activities," and small business taxpayers are listed among the exceptions. <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-19" />

## Valuing and identifying inventory

If you keep inventories, "you must value your inventory at the beginning and end of each tax year," <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-09" /> and your method "must conform to generally accepted accounting principles for similar businesses". <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-10" /> Inventory includes "merchandise or stock in trade," along with raw materials, work in process, finished products and supplies that physically become part of the item for sale. <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-21" />

Publication 538 sets out how to identify which costs are in ending inventory:

| Method | What it assumes or requires (Pub. 538) |
|---|---|
| Specific identification | Used when you can identify and match the actual cost to the items in inventory |
| FIFO (first-in first-out) | Items purchased or produced first are the first items sold |
| LIFO (last-in first-out) | Items purchased or produced last are sold first; Form 970 or a statement filed with the return for the first LIFO year |

*Source: IRS, Publication 538 (Rev. January 2022), Inventories, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-22" />

The valuation methods generally available are cost, lower of cost or market, and retail; goods accounted for under LIFO are excluded from lower of cost or market and "must be inventoried at cost." <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-23" /> "You must use the same accounting method from year to year," and changing your inventory accounting requires Form 3115. <C id="accrual-accounting-cogs-24" />

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical inputs)">
A seller starts the year with $40,000 of inventory (line 35), buys $150,000 of merchandise and takes $2,000 of it for personal use (line 36: $148,000), pays $6,000 of freight-in (line 39), and counts $55,000 of inventory at year end (line 41). Line 40 is $40,000 + $148,000 + $6,000 = $194,000. Cost of goods sold on line 42 is $194,000 − $55,000 = $139,000.
</Callout>

## What to check yourself

1. Open Publication 334 on irs.gov, confirm the revision year on the title, and read chapter 2 (Accounting Methods, Inventories) and chapter 6 (How To Figure Cost of Goods Sold).
2. Check the small business taxpayer gross receipts figure stated for the tax year you are filing; it is indexed for inflation.
3. Read Publication 538, Inventories, for identification and valuation methods, and the section on changing accounting methods.
4. Compare your Schedule C lines 35 through 42 with your inventory records before filing.
