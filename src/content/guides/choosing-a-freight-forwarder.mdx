Choose a freight forwarder by checking three things the law makes checkable: whether the company holds the Federal Maritime Commission license or registration its role requires, whether its financial responsibility is on file, and whether its invoicing follows the federal rules you can enforce. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-04"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-16"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-20"/> The FMC regime covers ocean freight; customs entry is a separate licensed activity, and air freight forwarders are licensed by IATA, so this page sorts out which credential answers which question.

## Who is who: forwarder, NVOCC, customs broker

Federal rules group ocean middlemen under one label. An ocean transportation intermediary (OTI) is either an ocean freight forwarder or a non-vessel-operating common carrier (NVOCC). <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-01"/> The two do different jobs:

Two details matter for importers. First, the forwarder definition is written around dispatching shipments from the United States, while the NVOCC is a carrier in its own right that does not run ships. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-02"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-03"/> Second, nothing in the FMC rules makes an ocean intermediary your customs broker. Customs business includes transactions with CBP concerning the entry and admissibility of merchandise, its classification and valuation, and the payment of duties, and doing it for others requires a broker license under 19 CFR Part 111. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-25"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-26"/> An importer can transact customs business solely on its own account. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-27"/> Who is the importer in the first place is a contract question covered in [Incoterms: who is the importer](/guides/incoterms-who-is-importer/).

For air freight, trade.gov notes that freight forwarders are licensed by the International Air Transport Association (IATA). <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-28"/> How the modes compare on filing deadlines is in [Freight modes](/guides/freight-modes/).

## The license and the money behind it

In the United States, no one may advertise, hold itself out or act as an OTI without a valid FMC license, and the FMC states that U.S.-based forwarders and NVOCCs must obtain one. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-04"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-05"/> Foreign-based NVOCCs that do not take a license register instead, by filing a current Form FMC-65, and those registrations last three years. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-12"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-13"/>

Each license comes with a required amount of financial responsibility — a bond, insurance or other surety.

| OTI type | Financial responsibility required (46 CFR 515.21) |
|---|---|
| Ocean freight forwarder | $50,000 |
| Licensed NVOCC | $75,000 |
| Registered (foreign-based, unlicensed) NVOCC | $150,000 |

*Source: eCFR — 46 CFR 515.21, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-09"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-10"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-11"/>

That money exists for you. Shippers, common carriers and other affected persons may seek payment from the bond, insurance or other surety for damages arising from the OTI's transportation-related activities. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-14"/> One route to payment opens when the OTI fails to respond within 45 days of the notice of claim and the financial responsibility provider deems the claim valid. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-15"/>

## Checking the FMC list

The FMC publishes a list of OTIs that have met its requirements. An NVOCC appears on it only after obtaining a license and submitting proof of financial responsibility, and NVOCCs without an active Form FMC-1 on file are treated as out of compliance and left off. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-16"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-17"/> A company licensed in both roles appears on both the NVOCC and forwarder lists. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-18"/> The FMC also states that NVOCC tariffs must be open for public inspection and show all rates, charges, classifications, rules and practices. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-19"/>

## What a licensed forwarder owes you

The rules give you leverage on price transparency and honesty. On request, a licensed forwarder must provide a complete breakout of its charges and a true copy of any underlying document or bill of charges, and its invoices must say so. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-20"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-21"/> It may not share, directly or indirectly, its compensation or forwarding fee with a shipper, consignee, seller or purchaser (or their agents, affiliates or employees), with anyone advancing the purchase price or guaranteeing payment, or with anyone holding a beneficial interest in the shipment. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-22"/> No OTI may prepare or file a document it has reason to believe is false or fraudulent, and an OTI that suspects noncompliance, an error, misrepresentation or omission must advise its principal or shipper promptly. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-23"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-24"/>

Freight forwarding services are defined as dispatching shipments on behalf of others, and the listed services include arranging cargo insurance — so ask whether insurance is included or offered, rather than assume. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-07"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-08"/>

## Demurrage and detention bills

46 CFR Part 541 covers demurrage and detention invoices issued by ocean common carriers, marine terminal operators and NVOCCs. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-37"/> The invoice must be issued within 30 calendar days of the date the charge was last incurred; if it is not, the billed party is not required to pay. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-38"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-39"/> An invoice missing required information — which includes the allowed free time in days — likewise eliminates the obligation to pay. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-40"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-42"/> You must be given at least 30 calendar days from the issuance date to request mitigation, refund or waiver. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-41"/>

## Where Amazon's own program fits

If you sell through FBA, Amazon offers a door-to-door ocean and air freight program, Amazon Global Logistics, which currently ships from mainland China, Hong Kong and Vietnam. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-31"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-32"/> It requires FBA enrollment, provides customs brokers for clearance, and asks you to supply importer of record details during onboarding. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-33"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-35"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-34"/> The vetting questions below apply to any provider you compare it with.

## Vetting checklist

trade.gov suggests comparing costs and services across a few providers and notes that many forwarders specialize in certain types of shipments. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-30"/> <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-29"/> Turn that into questions with checkable answers:

<Checklist title="Before you book an ocean shipment" items={[
  "Name on the FMC OTI list (NVOCC, forwarder, or both) — or a current FMC-65 registration for a foreign NVOCC",
  "Which party issues the bill of lading, and in what role",
  "Who files the Importer Security Filing, and who provides the data in time",
  "Who your customs broker is, and whether you or they hold the power of attorney",
  "A written breakout of every charge, including demurrage and detention free time",
  "Whether cargo insurance is arranged, by whom, and on what value",
  "Your role as importer of record, in writing"
]} />

The ISF item is a timing question: most Importer Security Filing elements are due no later than 24 hours before the cargo is laden aboard the vessel at the foreign port, so supplier data has to reach the filer before loading. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-36"/> Bond questions are covered in [Customs bonds](/guides/customs-bonds/).

## How to confirm a forwarder's status

Search the company on the FMC's OTI list at www2.fmc.gov/oti and read 46 CFR Parts 515 and 541 on eCFR for the license, bond and billing rules quoted here. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-16"/> For customs-broker status, the governing text is 19 CFR Part 111. <C id="choosing-a-freight-forwarder-26"/> This page explains the federal licensing framework; a dispute over a specific claim or invoice is settled through the bond claim process or the FMC, and a maritime attorney can advise on your facts.
