Sellers pick a forwarder the way they pick a supplier: by quoting three and taking the cheapest number. The number they compare is the one that matters least.

## What the invoice actually contains

One reported component breakdown for a 1 CBM LCL shipment from Shenzhen to a US fulfilment centre:

| Line | Reported |
| --- | --- |
| Ocean freight | $80–$200 |
| Origin handling | $60–$120 |
| Export customs (China) | $30–$60 |
| Destination terminal handling | $80–$150 |
| Customs brokerage | $80–$200 |
| ISF filing | $25–$50 |
| Drayage to the fulfilment centre | $80–$200 |
| Forwarder service fee | $50–$150 |
| **Total** | **$485–$1,130** |

Plus import duties, and plus any examination — see the [customs holds guide](/guides/customs-holds-and-exams/), where an intensive exam is reported at $500 to $2,500 and beyond, with the importer paying.

**Ocean freight is roughly a fifth of the total, and it is the line every quote leads with.**

For air, reported forwarder fees are typically bundled into an all-in per-kg rate of $5 to $12. For FCL, reported flat handling fees of $200 to $500 plus freight. **Ask which structure you are being quoted in**, because a per-kg air rate and an ocean freight line are not comparable numbers.

## DDP, and the question underneath it

Reported DDP pricing: $800 to $1,800 per CBM by sea, or $6 to $12 per kg by air, all-inclusive. The forwarder handles freight, customs, duties and last-mile delivery, and you pay one price.

**The appeal is real** — one price, one counterparty, no separate brokerage relationship, and reporting recommends it for sellers who do not want to manage customs.

**The question it hides** is the one from the [Incoterms guide](/guides/incoterms-who-is-importer/): whose name appears as importer of record on the CBP entry. DDP shifts cost and arrangement; whether it shifts the legal position depends on who is actually declared. **Ask, in writing, before the first shipment.** Your [classification liability](/guides/hts-classification-basics/) follows the declaration, not the invoice.

**And a second question:** what happens if duty rates move between quote and arrival. A DDP price contains an estimate of duty, and after two years of tariff changes that estimate has been wrong repeatedly.

## What a good forwarder does that a cheap one does not

**Origin prep.** Since Amazon [ended US prep services](/guides/amazon-stopped-prepping/) on 1 January 2026, this changed from a convenience to a cost line. Reported origin services include FNSKU labelling, shipping labels and carton marks to Amazon specification, poly-bagging for suffocation warnings, product inspection before goods leave China, and consolidation from multiple suppliers into one shipment.

**Do the arithmetic:** origin prep at a reported rate against a domestic prep centre at $0.35 to $2.00 per unit. For simple prep on a full container the origin option is usually cheaper; for complex prep or where you want to inspect the work, it is not.

**Correct FBA routing.** Reported middle-mile services check inventory into Amazon within 2 to 4 days. A forwarder unfamiliar with Amazon's appointment and routing requirements produces delays that appear on no quote.

**Consolidation.** Combining goods from several suppliers into one shipment is what makes LCL economics work for a seller buying from multiple factories.

**Compliance filing.** ISF on ocean, and — since 8 July 2026 — [CPSC certificate eFiling](/guides/us-product-compliance/) where applicable. Ask whether they handle it.

## The questions to ask

**Quote structure**
- Is this all-in or freight only? List every additional charge.
- What is not included? Duties, exam fees, demurrage, storage.
- How long is the quote valid? Rates move weekly.

**Customs**
- Whose name is on the entry as importer of record?
- Do you file ISF, and what happens if it is late?
- Do you handle PGA filings — CPSC, FDA — where my products need them?

**Amazon specifics**
- Do you prep at origin, and at what per-unit rate?
- Have you delivered to Amazon fulfilment centres before? Which?
- Who books the delivery appointment?
- What happens if Amazon refuses the shipment?

**Failure handling**
- What happens on a customs hold, and who pays?
- What is your liability for damage or loss?
- Do you carry cargo insurance, or do I arrange it?

**That last group is the one that separates forwarders**, and it is the one nobody asks until it matters.

## The peak season caveat

Reporting is direct: build buffer time into restocks, especially in Q3 and Q4 when delays are described as common.

Against the [peak season guide](/guides/peak-season-cost-stack/) and Amazon's inbound deadlines, a two-week freight delay in October is not a freight problem — it is a stockout in the highest-demand weeks, at peak fulfilment rates, with capacity you may not be able to replace. **Book earlier than the arithmetic says.**

## What to do

**Quote all-in, not freight only**, and require the component list.

**Get quotes dated this week** and treat validity as short.

**Ask the importer-of-record question in writing** on any DDP arrangement.

**Price origin prep against domestic prep** per SKU, not per shipment.

**Check they have delivered to Amazon before.** It is a routing and appointment competence, not a general freight one.

**Ask what happens when it goes wrong** before you need to know.

**Book Q4 freight earlier than you think you need to.**
