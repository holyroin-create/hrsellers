Amazon sellers who need money for inventory have two official places to start: **Amazon Lending**, where eligible US businesses receive financing invitations from third-party providers inside Seller Central, and **SBA-backed loans**, which come through banks and other lenders. <C id="seller-financing-02"/> <C id="seller-financing-03"/> <C id="seller-financing-18"/> The decision is mostly about repayment pattern and cost: a loan with fixed monthly payments, an advance that takes a share of your sales, or a credit line you draw on as needed.

## How Amazon Lending works

Amazon Lending offers business financing to eligible US small and medium-sized businesses, and the money comes from third-party financing providers. <C id="seller-financing-01"/> <C id="seller-financing-02"/> You don't apply cold. You check for financing invitations in Seller Central. <C id="seller-financing-03"/>

Amazon says applying does not impact your personal credit. <C id="seller-financing-06"/> As for who gets invited, providers look for a proven track record of growing sales and high customer satisfaction. <C id="seller-financing-15"/>

### What providers weigh

Amazon's October 31, 2025 financing guide adds detail on how offers are decided. Eligibility might be based on criteria such as your credit profile, sales history or other business performance factors, and in many cases offers are based on criteria other than credit scores. <C id="seller-financing-33"/> <C id="seller-financing-34"/> Applying also means agreeing to share your Amazon selling data with the provider. <C id="seller-financing-04"/> The paperwork is not standardized either; the provider decides what information it needs to process the application. <C id="seller-financing-35"/>

The same guide describes a business line of credit as revolving funding up to a set limit. <C id="seller-financing-37"/> Its practical advice is to have a plan or budget for how you will use and repay any funding before you accept it. <C id="seller-financing-36"/> For a seller, that plan is mostly an inventory plan: which units the money buys, when they arrive, how fast they sell through, and which payouts will cover each repayment.

### The products on Amazon's page

Amazon's FAQ says term loans and merchant cash advances are available to eligible US-based businesses; the same page also describes an SBA 7(a) option and a line of credit. <C id="seller-financing-14"/>

The cash advance is the one that behaves differently. It ties payment to a portion of your future sales for a fixed capital fee, and Amazon's page says no additional fees or interest are charged. <C id="seller-financing-09"/> <C id="seller-financing-10"/> The line of credit has no origination or maintenance fees. <C id="seller-financing-13"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical)">
Inputs: a $20,000 cash advance with a $2,000 flat fee, so $22,000 to repay, and a Payment Rate of 10% of sales.

At $25,000 of sales a month, repayment is $2,500 a month, about **8.8 months** to clear $22,000. If sales drop to $15,000 a month, repayment falls to $1,500 a month and stretches to about **14.7 months**. The fee is the same either way, so the effective annual cost is higher when you repay faster.

Compare a $20,000 term loan: payments are equal every month regardless of sales, which is easier to plan but doesn't ease off in a slow month.
</Callout>

## SBA-backed loans

The SBA describes its loans as making it easier for small businesses to access affordable funding. <C id="seller-financing-18"/> 7(a) loans are SBA's primary program for long-term financing and are delivered by SBA 7(a) lenders. <C id="seller-financing-19"/> SBA's Lender Match tool connects you with an SBA-approved lender. <C id="seller-financing-31"/>

Normally a business must meet SBA size standards, be able to repay and have a sound business purpose. <C id="seller-financing-32"/> For 7(a) loans specifically, the business must operate for profit, be small under SBA size requirements and be unable to get the desired credit on reasonable terms from non-federal, non-state and non-local government sources. <C id="seller-financing-22"/>

| Program | Maximum | Uses relevant to sellers | Notable terms |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7(a) | $5 million | Working capital, refinancing current business debt, changes of ownership | Delivered by SBA 7(a) lenders |
| 504 | Not stated on the overview page | Not detailed on the overview page | Fixed rate, through Certified Development Companies |
| Microloan | $50,000 (average about $13,000) | Working capital, inventory, supplies, equipment | Up to six years; rates generally 8%–13% |

*Source: U.S. Small Business Administration — loan program pages, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="seller-financing-20"/> <C id="seller-financing-21"/> <C id="seller-financing-23"/> <C id="seller-financing-24"/> <C id="seller-financing-25"/> <C id="seller-financing-26"/> <C id="seller-financing-28"/> <C id="seller-financing-29"/>

Microloans list inventory among their permitted uses. <C id="seller-financing-26"/> Proceeds can't be used to pay existing debts or buy real estate, and intermediaries generally require collateral plus the owner's personal guarantee. <C id="seller-financing-27"/> <C id="seller-financing-30"/> The 7(a) "changes of ownership" use matters if you are buying or selling a business; see the [guide to selling your FBA business](/guides/selling-your-fba-business/). <C id="seller-financing-21"/>

## How to confirm the terms

Re-check Amazon's [Amazon Lending page](https://sell.amazon.com/programs/amazon-lending) and your actual offer in Seller Central, where the binding rate, fee and Payment Rate are shown. For SBA products, use the SBA's [loan overview](https://www.sba.gov/funding-programs/loans), [7(a) page](https://www.sba.gov/funding-programs/loans/7a-loans) and [microloan page](https://www.sba.gov/funding-programs/loans/microloans), then get terms in writing from the lender. This page compares structures; whether a given loan is affordable for your business is a judgment for you and your accountant.
