If you import a consumer product that is subject to a Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) rule, ban, standard or regulation, you, as importer, issue the certificate of compliance, and since July 8, 2026 the certificate data has to be filed electronically with the customs entry for most such products. <C id="us-product-compliance-06" /> <C id="us-product-compliance-03" /> The certificate is a General Certificate of Conformity (GCC) for general-use products or a Children's Product Certificate (CPC) for children's products, and a CPC must rest on testing by a CPSC-accepted third-party laboratory. <C id="us-product-compliance-18" />

This page covers the certificate rule in 16 CFR Part 1110 as revised by the CPSC's January 2025 final rule, and the children's product testing rule in 16 CFR Part 1107. Which product safety rules apply to your item is a separate question; the certificate is where you list them.

<Callout type="note" title="Scope of this page">
This page summarizes CPSC certificate and testing rules. Whether a specific rule applies to your product is a determination for you and, where needed, a testing lab or compliance attorney.
</Callout>

## The 2025 final rule and its dates

In January 2025 the CPSC, in consultation with US Customs and Border Protection, issued a final rule revising its Certificates of Compliance regulation, aligning it with other CPSC testing and certification rules and implementing "electronic filing of certificates (eFiling) with CBP." <C id="us-product-compliance-01" /> A September 2025 correction states that the effective date of the rule published at 90 FR 1800 "is July 8, 2026." <C id="us-product-compliance-02" />

The rule applies on July 8, 2026 to regulated products and substances, except those imported into a foreign trade zone and later entered for consumption or warehousing, for which it applies on January 8, 2027. <C id="us-product-compliance-03" />

## GCC or CPC

16 CFR 1110 defines a General Certificate of Conformity as "a certificate of compliance for a finished product issued pursuant to section 14(a)(1) of the CPSA." <C id="us-product-compliance-04" /> A Children's Product Certificate is "a certificate of compliance for a finished product issued pursuant to section 14(a)(2) of the CPSA." <C id="us-product-compliance-05" /> The practical difference is the testing basis: a CPC relies on third-party testing under Part 1107, described below.

## Who issues the certificate

- **Imported products.** Under 1110.7(a), the importer "is the finished product certifier that must issue a finished product certificate." <C id="us-product-compliance-06" />
- **Products made in the US.** Under 1110.7(b), the manufacturer certifies; for a privately labeled product, the private labeler certifies unless the manufacturer issues the certificate. <C id="us-product-compliance-07" />

A seller who imports under its own importer of record number is therefore the certifier, even if the factory provides test reports. The final rule preamble states that certifiers may rely on others' test reports, but the finished product certifier still issues its own certificate. <C id="us-product-compliance-14" />

## What the certificate must contain

| Element (16 CFR 1110.11) | Practical note |
|---|---|
| The covered product, with at least one listed unique identifier and a description sufficient to match it to the certificate | Model, SKU or GTIN plus a plain description |
| Each applicable rule, ban, standard or regulation, listed separately | One line per rule |
| The certifier's name and contact information | Importer or domestic manufacturer |
| The individual maintaining records, with contact information | Name a person, not only a company |
| Date and place of manufacture | Factory city and country, month and year |
| Date and place of the most recent testing | Lab and test date |
| The certifier's attestation | Set wording for paper and electronic certificates; for eFiled certificates the attestation is in the Product Registry |

*Source: Office of the Federal Register (eCFR), 16 CFR 1110.11 Certificate content, checked Nov 8, 2026. Right-hand column is explanatory.* <C id="us-product-compliance-08" />

## eFiling with the entry

For finished products manufactured outside the United States and offered for importation for consumption or warehousing, the certifier "must eFile the finished product certificate data elements required in § 1110.11" in CBP's Automated Commercial Environment (ACE) at the time of filing the entry. <C id="us-product-compliance-09" /> An eFiled certificate "must be in the English language." <C id="us-product-compliance-10" />

The final rule describes two routes. With the Full PGA Message Set, "the importer submits all certificate data elements via ACE." With the Reference PGA Message Set, the importer enters all certificate data elements into CPSC's Product Registry "prior to filing entry with CBP" and "submits a unique reference identifier (ID) via ACE as part of their entry filing." <C id="us-product-compliance-11" /> For mail shipments, the importer "must enter certificate data into the Product Registry before arrival of the shipment in the United States." <C id="us-product-compliance-12" /> The preamble also states: "The CPSA does not provide a *de minimis* exemption for certificates." <C id="us-product-compliance-13" />

Once issued, the certifier "must furnish the required finished product certificate to each distributor or retailer" of the product. <C id="us-product-compliance-15" /> Certificates and supporting records must be kept "for at least five years from the certificate creation date." <C id="us-product-compliance-16" />

## Children's products: third-party testing under 16 CFR Part 1107

Part 1107 "establishes the protocols and standards for ensuring continued testing of children's products." <C id="us-product-compliance-17" /> Manufacturers must submit samples to a third party conformity assessment body, defined as "a testing laboratory whose accreditation has been accepted by the CPSC." <C id="us-product-compliance-18" />

After the initial certification, periodic testing continues at intervals that depend on the controls in place:

| Situation (16 CFR 1107.21) | Minimum periodic testing |
|---|---|
| Default | At least once a year |
| Production testing plan in place | At least once every two years |
| Testing by an ISO/IEC 17025-accredited lab | At least once every three years |

*Source: Office of the Federal Register (eCFR), 16 CFR 1107.21 Periodic testing, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="us-product-compliance-19" />

A material change triggers new testing. Section 1107.23 covers changes in "product design or manufacturing process, including the sourcing of component parts," which require testing of new samples and a new CPC. <C id="us-product-compliance-20" /> Testing records must be kept for five years under 1107.26. <C id="us-product-compliance-21" /> A switch of fabric supplier or paint vendor on a children's product is a sourcing change to evaluate under that section.

## Decision table: what the certificate rule requires for your setup

The sections above follow the regulation's order. The table below reorders the same rules by seller situation.

| If your situation is… | What applies | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| You import a finished product for consumption or warehousing | You are the certifier, and you eFile the 1110.11 data elements in ACE when the entry is filed | 16 CFR 1110.7(a), 1110.13(a)(1) <C id="us-product-compliance-06" /> <C id="us-product-compliance-09" /> |
| Your goods go into a foreign trade zone before entry | The rule applies January 8, 2027 rather than July 8, 2026 | Final rule <C id="us-product-compliance-03" /> |
| You ship individual units by mail | No de minimis exemption for certificates; certificate data in the Product Registry before arrival | Final rule <C id="us-product-compliance-13" /> <C id="us-product-compliance-12" /> |
| The product is a children's product | Children's Product Certificate, with samples sent to a CPSC-accepted third-party lab | 16 CFR 1110, Part 1107 <C id="us-product-compliance-05" /> <C id="us-product-compliance-18" /> |
| You switch a component supplier on a children's product | New testing and a new CPC | 16 CFR 1107.23 <C id="us-product-compliance-20" /> |
| Your factory sends its own test reports | You may rely on them, but you still issue your own certificate | Final rule <C id="us-product-compliance-14" /> |
| You private-label a product made in the U.S. | The private labeler certifies unless the manufacturer does | 16 CFR 1110.7(b) <C id="us-product-compliance-07" /> |
| You sell through distributors or retailers | Furnish the certificate to each one | 16 CFR 1110.13(b) <C id="us-product-compliance-15" /> |

*Source: Office of the Federal Register / National Archives (eCFR), 16 CFR Parts 1107 and 1110, and CPSC correction notice (Federal Register, Sept. 2025), checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="us-product-compliance-03" /> <C id="us-product-compliance-06" /> <C id="us-product-compliance-09" /> <C id="us-product-compliance-20" />

Two rows change a sourcing decision rather than a filing step. Switching a component supplier on a children's product is a design or manufacturing change that triggers new testing and a new CPC, <C id="us-product-compliance-20" /> so a cheaper component carries a test cost. Routing goods through a foreign trade zone moves the start date for those goods to January 8, 2027. <C id="us-product-compliance-03" />

## What to check yourself

1. Read 16 CFR Part 1110 on eCFR, especially 1110.7, 1110.11, 1110.13 and 1110.17.
2. Open the Federal Register final rule "Certificates of Compliance" (90 FR 1800, January 8, 2025) and the September 24, 2025 correction for the dates.
3. For children's products, read 16 CFR Part 1107, subpart C, on periodic testing and material change.
4. Confirm with your customs broker which PGA Message Set route they will use for your entries.
