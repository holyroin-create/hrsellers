Everyone covers the Q4 storage spike. Almost nobody covers this one. It's charged on every unit you actually sell, not on the ones that sit, and if your peak goes well that makes it the larger of the two.

## What Amazon announced

The holiday announcement was posted to Seller Central on July 7, 2026. The terms:

| Item | 2026 holiday terms |
| --- | --- |
| Window | October 15, 2026 to January 14, 2027; non-peak rates resume January 15 |
| Programs | FBA, Remote Fulfillment with FBA, Multi-Channel Fulfillment, Buy with Prime |
| Uplift | Same per-unit increase over non-peak as last year; average $0.32 per unit (US), CAD $0.39 (Canada) |
| Surcharge | 3.5% fuel and logistics surcharge applies on top of peak fees |
| Basis | Charged by ship date, on the size tier and weight of each unit |
| Tools | Peak rates loaded into the Revenue Calculator, Profit Analytics and Fee and Economics Preview |
| AWD | Off-peak storage pricing extended to October 31 for AWD inventory auto-replenished into FBA |

The average conceals a per-tier range. Amazon's help page gives per-tier example uplifts running from roughly $0.19 for the lightest small-standard units to about $2.81 for large oversize; your figure is on the Fee Preview report.

## Same headline, bigger bill

The uplift is unchanged from last year. The unit cost is not, and the reason is what sits underneath it:

```
2025 peak unit cost = 2025 base fee + peak uplift
2026 peak unit cost = (2026 base fee + peak uplift) × 1.035
```

Take a large standard unit with a $6.00 base fee in 2025 that rose to $6.10 in January. Last peak: $6.00 + $0.40 uplift = **$6.40**. This peak: ($6.10 + $0.40) × 1.035 = **$6.73**. A seller who filed the July announcement under "no change" is short 33 cents on every unit, before storage. Illustrative fees; the shape is the point.

## Why the dates matter more than the amount

**It opens October 15**, before Prime Big Deal Days and the Black Friday block, and two weeks before most sellers think of peak as starting.

**It closes January 14.** Everything you sell in the first fortnight of January — exchanges, and the post-Christmas clearance most sellers run to shift peak overstock — is fulfilled at peak rates. That interacts badly with the [removal decision](/guides/removal-vs-hold-decision/): a seller clearing unsold peak inventory through January promotions is discounting **and** paying peak fulfillment on every unit that moves.

**It is charged on ship date, not order date.** An order placed on October 14 that ships on the 15th pays peak.

## The full peak cost picture

Four charges rise across the same period and none replaces another:

| Charge | Window | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Peak fulfillment fee | 15 Oct – 14 Jan | +$0.32 per unit average, by tier |
| Fuel and logistics surcharge | Year-round, no end date | 3.5% of the fulfillment fee including the peak uplift |
| Peak storage | 1 Oct – 31 Dec | $0.78 → $2.40 per cubic foot standard; $0.56 → $1.40 oversize |
| Advertising | Prime Day and Q4 | CPCs materially higher; see the PPC benchmarks guide |

Plus deal and coupon fees where you participate — see the [deals guide](/guides/deal-and-coupon-fees/) — and the cash timing covered in the [peak season cost stack](/guides/peak-season-cost-stack/). A seller modeling Q4 on storage alone is modeling one of four.

## The inbound deadlines

The same announcement moved the calendar earlier. Amazon's fulfillment centers prioritize receiving in September and October and switch to order processing in November; inventory arriving late loses the Prime badge for the events it was meant to serve.

| Date | Deadline |
| --- | --- |
| October 20, 2026 | Black Friday Week and Cyber Monday deal submission closes |
| October 21, 2026 | FBA inbound deadline for minimal-split shipments (Black Friday / Cyber Monday) |
| October 28, 2026 | Last of the published inbound windows (AWD and optimized splits run later than minimal splits) |

Confirm the dates for your account under `Seller Central > Inventory > Shipments > Send to Amazon`, where the holiday cut-off is shown against each placement option. The practical consequence: the [inbound placement decision](/guides/inbound-placement-split-arithmetic/) has a deadline in Q4, and missing the minimal-split cut-off forces you into whichever option is still open rather than the one your arithmetic chose.

## What to do

- **Add the peak uplift and the surcharge to Q4 unit economics**, in contribution margin per unit sold — not in the storage model. Different line.
- **Check MCF volume.** If you fulfil Shopify or other channels through Amazon, the peak fee and the surcharge land there too, and neither shows up in Amazon marketplace reporting.
- **Reconsider January clearance.** Either clear before Christmas or wait until January 15. The fortnight in between is the worst of both.
- **Diarize October 15, not November 1.** Anything you want fulfilled at standard rates ships before the fifteenth.
- **Book the minimal-split deadline of October 21 now** if that is the placement option your arithmetic prefers.

**Run it on your own figures.** The [margin and break-even calculator](/tools/margin-calculator/) takes price, cost of goods, referral rate, fulfillment fee, returns and ad spend and returns net profit per unit, net margin and the maximum ACOS the product can carry. Nothing is stored and nothing leaves your browser.
