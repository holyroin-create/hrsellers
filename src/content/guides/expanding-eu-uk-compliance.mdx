Before a US seller lists a single unit in Germany or the UK, three questions must have answers: who in the EU is legally responsible for the product, what safety and identity data the listing and packaging must carry, and who accounts for VAT. In the EU the first answer is not optional. Since 13 December 2024, Regulation (EU) 2023/988 says a consumer product "shall not be placed on the market unless there is an economic operator established in the Union" responsible for it. <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-01"/> <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-03"/> Great Britain runs a separate regime, so the UK needs its own check.

## How Amazon structures Europe and the UK

Amazon says a Europe account reaches "Amazon customers in 28 countries," and that "Product requirements vary by country." <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-23"/> <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-24"/> For FBA, "Pan-European FBA is available in France, Germany, Italy, Spain, and Poland." <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-27"/> Joining it means listing in the France, Germany, Italy and Spain stores "using the same SKUs" and enabling at least two of France, Germany, Italy, Spain or Poland for FBA storage. <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-28"/> Amazon's instruction is to "Start by registering for VAT in each country," then switch it on in FBA settings. <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-29"/>

The UK sits outside that network: "As of January 1, 2021, the UK is no longer part of Pan-European FBA," so to sell with FBA there "you need to export inventory to fulfillment centers in the UK." <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-25"/> <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-26"/>

## EU product safety: the GPSR obligations that reach a US seller

The regulation "lays down essential rules on the safety of consumer products placed or made available on the market." <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-02"/> For a US brand owner selling into the EU, the provisions that change day-to-day work are these:

| Obligation | Where in Regulation (EU) 2023/988 |
|---|---|
| An EU-established economic operator responsible for the product | Art. 16(1) |
| That operator's contact details on the product, packaging, parcel or an accompanying document | Art. 16(3) |
| Technical documentation, kept 10 years after placing on the market | Art. 9(2)–(3) |
| A type, batch or serial number or other identifying element | Art. 9(5) |
| Manufacturer's name, registered trade name or registered trade mark, and postal and electronic address | Art. 9(6) |
| Instructions and safety information in a language consumers easily understand | Art. 9(7) |
| Required information in online listings | Art. 19 |

*Source: EUR-Lex, Regulation (EU) 2023/988, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-03"/> <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-04"/> <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-05"/> <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-06"/> <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-07"/> <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-08"/> <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-10"/>

The EU responsible person is not a mailbox. Article 16(2) requires that operator to "regularly check" that the product matches the technical documentation and the identification, contact and language requirements, and to provide "documented evidence of the checks performed" when market surveillance authorities ask. <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-05"/>

### What the listing itself must show

Article 19 says distance-sale offers must "clearly and visibly indicate at least":

Marketplaces carry their own duties. Article 22 requires them to designate a single point of contact for authorities and to process product safety notices "within three working days from the receipt of the notice." <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-11"/>

Stock already on the market before the cutover has a transition rule: Article 51 says Member States "shall not impede the making available on the market" of products covered by the old directive that were placed on the market before 13 December 2024. <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-12"/>

## EU VAT: when the marketplace is treated as the seller

Directive (EU) 2017/2455 inserted Article 14a into the EU VAT Directive. Where a marketplace facilitates distance sales of goods imported "in consignments of an intrinsic value not exceeding EUR 150," it "shall be deemed to have received and supplied those goods himself." <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-30"/> The same deemed-supplier rule covers the supply of goods within the EU "by a taxable person not established within the Community" to a non-taxable person. <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-31"/> Separately, Amazon's Pan-European FBA instructions still start with registering for VAT in each country. <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-29"/>

## Great Britain and Northern Ireland: product safety

In Great Britain the General Product Safety Regulations 2005 still apply. GOV.UK says "The regulations require all consumer products to be safe in their normal or reasonably foreseeable usage," and the guidance "Applies to England, Scotland and Wales." <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-13"/> Northern Ireland differs: there the 2005 regulations "were superseded on 13 December 2024" by the EU regulation. <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-16"/>

**UKCA or CE?** GOV.UK states that "Conformity marking is only necessary if required under sector-specific regulations." <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-14"/> Where it is required, "the UK continues to recognise the CE (European Conformity) marking" under the Product Safety and Metrology (Amendment) Regulations 2024, where product rules meet UK needs. <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-15"/> As importer, you indicate "identification details on the product (name, trade name or trademark, and postal address)." <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-17"/> For goods imported from the EEA, the importer's details can be set out elsewhere instead, under special rules that apply "before 11pm on 31 December 2027." <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-32"/>

## UK VAT for an overseas seller

For goods outside the UK in consignments of £135 or less sold to customers in Great Britain, "Where those goods are sold through an online marketplace, the online marketplace will be liable for the VAT." <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-18"/> For goods already in the UK when sold by a seller not established in the UK, the marketplace also accounts for the VAT on sales made through it. <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-20"/> But the seller is not off the hook: "The overseas seller will remain liable for any import VAT and Customs Duty when the goods are first imported into the UK." <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-19"/> HMRC's manual adds that non-established taxable persons "must notify HMRC of their liability to be registered regardless of the value of the taxable supply." <C id="expanding-eu-uk-compliance-21"/>

## How to confirm it for your products and countries

Read the regulation on [EUR-Lex (Regulation (EU) 2023/988)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R0988), the GOV.UK guidance on [using the UKCA marking](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/using-the-ukca-marking) and [general product safety in Great Britain](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/general-product-safety-regulations-2005), and HMRC's [VAT guidance for overseas goods sold through online marketplaces](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-and-overseas-goods-sold-to-customers-in-the-uk-using-online-marketplaces). Amazon's [FBA international page](https://sell.amazon.com/fulfillment-by-amazon/fba-international) covers Pan-European FBA and UK set-up. Whether a specific product needs a UKCA or CE mark depends on the sector regulation that covers it, and national tax authorities decide VAT registration questions.
