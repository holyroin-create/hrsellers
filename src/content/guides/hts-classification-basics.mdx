You find an HTS code by reading the tariff schedule's headings and notes in a fixed order set by the General Rules of Interpretation, and you defend it by keeping the reasoning, the product facts and, where the stakes justify it, a binding ruling from CBP. <C id="hts-classification-basics-05"/> <C id="hts-classification-basics-08"/> The schedule is maintained so that the legal text ends at 8 digits, while your entry uses the full 10-digit number. <C id="hts-classification-basics-02"/> <C id="hts-classification-basics-03"/> The responsibility for getting it right sits with you: USITC states that importers are ultimately responsible for knowing CBP requirements. <C id="hts-classification-basics-07"/>

## How an HTS number is built

USITC states that goods are classified in the provisions of chapters 1 through 97. <C id="hts-classification-basics-04"/> Inside a chapter, the digits narrow down step by step.

| Digits | What USITC calls it | Legal status |
|---|---|---|
| First 4 | Heading | Legal text |
| 6 and 8 | Subheadings | Legal text ends at 8 digits |
| 9–10 | Statistical suffix | Used on entries with the 8-digit subheading, beyond the legal text |

*Source: U.S. International Trade Commission, HTS FAQ, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="hts-classification-basics-01"/> <C id="hts-classification-basics-02"/> <C id="hts-classification-basics-03"/>

Two practical consequences follow. First, a code printed on a supplier's invoice is only a starting point: you still have to confirm the U.S. 8-digit subheading and the 10-digit statistical line. Second, export codes come from a different schedule: USITC notes that exports are classified using Schedule B from the Census Bureau. <C id="hts-classification-basics-09"/>

The schedule also moves. USITC announced that 2026 HTS Revision 22 was published on October 30, 2026, so a code you looked up in spring should be rechecked against the current revision before you rely on it. <C id="hts-classification-basics-10"/>

## The General Rules of Interpretation, in order

The GRIs are applied in sequence; you only move to the next rule when the previous one does not settle the question. **Edition note:** the rule wording summarized below was checked against the 2004 edition of the HTSUS General Rules of Interpretation in USITC's archive, because the current edition's text could not be retrieved for this check. Read the General Rules in the current edition on hts.usitc.gov before relying on any wording here.

One U.S. addition matters too: where a heading depends on use, Additional U.S. Rule of Interpretation 1(a) (2004 edition) says that, for a classification controlled by use other than actual use, the controlling use is the principal use. <C id="hts-classification-basics-20"/> A classification ruling request likewise asks for the article's chief use in the United States. <C id="hts-classification-basics-25"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): a bundled set">
Inputs: a gift set of a stainless-steel tumbler, a silicone sleeve and a cleaning brush, put up together in one retail box. GRI 1 first: check whether any heading or chapter note covers the set as a whole. If none does, the set is a candidate for GRI 3(b), and the question becomes which component gives it its essential character — here, plausibly the tumbler, with the sleeve and brush as accessories. If you cannot defend one component as essential, GRI 3(c) sends you to the heading last in numerical order. Write down which rule decided it and why; that note is the core of your reasonable-care file.
</Callout>

## What "reasonable care" looks like on paper

The penalty standard is concrete. CBP's guidelines define negligence as failure to exercise the degree of reasonable care and competence expected from a person in the same circumstances. <C id="hts-classification-basics-33"/> They state plainly that an unreasonable classification will be considered a lack of reasonable care, and that failing to follow a binding Customs ruling may be treated the same way. <C id="hts-classification-basics-34"/> <C id="hts-classification-basics-35"/>

<Stat value="2×" label="Guideline maximum for negligence with a duty loss: two times the loss of duty, capped at the goods' domestic value" claim="hts-classification-basics-36" />

The code also has to appear on your paperwork: 19 CFR 141.90(b) requires the importer or customs broker to include the appropriate subheading on the invoice or with the invoice data. <C id="hts-classification-basics-21"/> And whatever you rely on must still exist later — records relating to an entry are kept for 5 years from the date of entry. <C id="hts-classification-basics-40"/>

## When to ask CBP for a binding ruling

A ruling is the strongest defense you can get, with limits you need to understand before relying on one.

Anyone with a direct and demonstrable interest in the question may ask, and the request should be in the form of a letter. <C id="hts-classification-basics-23"/> <C id="hts-classification-basics-24"/> For classification, include a full and complete description of the article, its chief use in the United States and its commercial, common or technical designation; for multi-material goods, the relative quantity by weight and by volume and the value of each material. <C id="hts-classification-basics-25"/> <C id="hts-classification-basics-26"/> Add photographs, drawings or other pictorial representations. <C id="hts-classification-basics-27"/> Within 90 days after issuing an interpretive decision, CBP publishes it in the Customs Bulletin or otherwise makes it available for public inspection. <C id="hts-classification-basics-31"/>

## If you find an error after entry

Two routes exist in the regulations. A valid prior disclosure of a negligent violation with actual duty loss reduces the penalty to the interest on the lost duty. <C id="hts-classification-basics-37"/> After liquidation, the classification and the rate and amount of duties are matters you can protest, generally within 180 days of the decision such as the notice of liquidation. <C id="hts-classification-basics-38"/> <C id="hts-classification-basics-39"/> Which route fits depends on facts a licensed customs broker or trade attorney should review; this page explains the framework, not the outcome for your product.

## How to confirm it for your goods

Start in the current HTS at hts.usitc.gov and read the section and chapter notes before the headings. For informal help, USITC offers classification guidance, but says it is not binding advice. <C id="hts-classification-basics-06"/> For a decision CBP must follow, request a binding ruling under 19 CFR Part 177, whose text is on eCFR. <C id="hts-classification-basics-08"/> <C id="hts-classification-basics-28"/>
