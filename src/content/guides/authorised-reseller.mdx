On Amazon, "authorized reseller" can mean two different things, and they unlock different rights. The first is approval to sell a restricted brand, which lets you add your offer to the brand's existing listing; Amazon asks for a purchase invoice from a manufacturer or distributor, or a brand authorization letter. <C id="authorised-reseller-04"/> <C id="authorised-reseller-09"/> <C id="authorised-reseller-10"/> The second is the Reseller role in Brand Registry, which only the brand can assign and which adds privileges such as creating new product detail pages. <C id="authorised-reseller-17"/> <C id="authorised-reseller-18"/> Decide which one you need before you ask a brand for paperwork.

For a brand enrolled in Brand Registry, matching an existing listing does not give you the ability to create new product detail pages. <C id="authorised-reseller-16"/> The Reseller role does, as part of a limited set of brand selling benefits. <C id="authorised-reseller-18"/> <C id="authorised-reseller-20"/> Amazon's A+ Content and Vine pages both list the Reseller role as qualifying, <C id="authorised-reseller-28"/> <C id="authorised-reseller-29"/> while Brand Analytics stays closed to it. <C id="authorised-reseller-21"/> The [A+ and brand tools guide](/guides/aplus-content-brand-tools/) covers what each tool does.

## Level 1: getting approved to sell a brand

Amazon's store allows a wide range of branded and generic products, but in some cases you need permission from the brand or from Amazon first. <C id="authorised-reseller-01"/> Before buying stock, Amazon's advice is to get it from a legitimate source, such as a wholesale supplier or manufacturer, <C id="authorised-reseller-02"/> and to check that the product is safe and complies with the laws of the countries where you sell. <C id="authorised-reseller-03"/>

### The brand authorization letter

A brand authorization letter is a letter directly from the brand that authorizes you to sell its products. <C id="authorised-reseller-11"/>

If your company trades under a name other than the one on your selling account, the letter still has to match the name and address in the account. <C id="authorised-reseller-13"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): is the letter still fresh?">
Inputs: a brand signs your authorization letter on June 1, 2026; you plan to apply on November 8, 2026.

- June 1 to November 8 is 160 days, inside the 180-day limit. <C id="authorised-reseller-12"/>
- Day 180 falls on November 28, 2026. An application after that date needs a newly dated letter.

If you resell several brands, track each letter's day-180 date, and ask for renewals before you need them.

## Level 2: the Brand Registry Reseller role

The Reseller role is for external, third-party sellers authorized to sell a brand's goods. <C id="authorised-reseller-19"/> It is different from the Brand Representative role, which is for first-party sellers directly employed by the brand. <C id="authorised-reseller-27"/> Amazon's roles guide says you can have only one role per selling account: Reseller or Brand Representative. <C id="authorised-reseller-22"/>

If you have an agreement with a brand, you ask the brand to assign the role. <C id="authorised-reseller-17"/> The mechanics, from Amazon's guide to roles:

- The brand's Administrator can invite as many selling accounts as it likes. <C id="authorised-reseller-25"/>
- To connect a seller's account that is not its own, the brand provides that seller's merchant token, a unique identifier you find in your selling account. <C id="authorised-reseller-23"/>
- The invitation arrives by email, <C id="authorised-reseller-34"/> and you must already have a Brand Registry account to receive it. <C id="authorised-reseller-24"/>

Amazon advises brands to give protection roles, which enable protection benefits, only to employee or other internal accounts. <C id="authorised-reseller-26"/> So as a reseller, expect a selling role, not a protection role.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What Amazon's pages say |
|---|---|
| Buying stock before checking for Apply to sell | Restricted products show the button at the Add Products step |
| Sending a letter older than 180 days | The letter must be dated within 180 days |
| Letter addressed to a trading name | Your name and address must match the selling account |
| Expecting approval to let you build a new listing | Matching does not allow new detail pages; the Reseller role does |
| Asking the brand for a role before opening Brand Registry | You need a Brand Registry account to receive the invitation |
| Expecting Brand Analytics as a reseller | The Reseller role does not grant it |

## What Transparency changes for resellers

Transparency assigns a unique code to every unit of an enrolled product. <C id="authorised-reseller-30"/> Once a brand's products are fully protected, they cannot be listed in Transparency-supported stores without valid codes that match the enrolled product, <C id="authorised-reseller-31"/> and all units need a valid code, Amazon-issued or an existing serial code connected to Transparency, or they fail the Transparency check. <C id="authorised-reseller-33"/> Amazon presents the codes as a way to verify that only authentic items reach customers, whether orders are fulfilled by Amazon or shipped directly by the brand. <C id="authorised-reseller-32"/> For a reseller, uncoded units of a fully protected product cannot be listed, so ask your supplier whether a product is Transparency-enrolled before you order.

<Callout type="note" title="Scope">
Amazon's approval and roles decide what you can do on Amazon. Whether a brand can lawfully restrict who resells its goods is a separate question of contract and trademark law; a distribution agreement and a lawyer settle that, not a Seller Central application. If a brand files an IP complaint against your listing, see the [IP complaints guide](/guides/ip-complaints-retractions/).
</Callout>
