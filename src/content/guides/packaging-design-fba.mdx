Good FBA packaging has to do three jobs at once: carry the label information federal rules require, stay as small and light as the product allows because Amazon prices fulfillment on weight and dimensions, and survive shipping well enough to go out without an Amazon overbox. <C id="packaging-design-fba-01"/> Settle the label panel and the structure before you order printing.

## Start with the dimensions, because fees follow them

Amazon's FBA fulfillment cost per unit covers picking and packing, shipping and handling, customer service and product returns, <C id="packaging-design-fba-02"/> and it is based on the product's weights and dimensions. <C id="packaging-design-fba-01"/> Storage is charged monthly based on your daily average volume, measured in cubic feet. <C id="packaging-design-fba-03"/> Size tiers use the greater of unit weight or dimensional weight, <C id="packaging-design-fba-24"/> so an oversized, airy box can cost you on both lines even when the product itself is light.

Amazon's own 2026 fee announcement lists updating your product packaging among the ways to reach lower fees. <C id="packaging-design-fba-04"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): trimming a box">
Inputs: a product currently packed in a 12 × 8 × 4 inch box; a redesigned box of 10 × 7 × 3 inches holds it safely.

- Current volume: 12 × 8 × 4 = 384 cubic inches = 384 ÷ 1,728 ≈ 0.22 cubic feet.
- New volume: 10 × 7 × 3 = 210 cubic inches ≈ 0.12 cubic feet.
- Reduction: (384 − 210) ÷ 384 ≈ 45% less space per unit.

Because storage is charged on cubic feet, the storage line on this item falls in proportion. <C id="packaging-design-fba-03"/> Whether the fulfillment fee also drops depends on whether the smaller box moves the item into a lower size or weight tier, which Amazon's Revenue Calculator estimates. <C id="packaging-design-fba-26"/>
</Callout>

## What the federal label rules require

The Fair Packaging and Labeling Act rules in 16 CFR Part 500 apply to consumer commodities: products customarily distributed for retail sale for personal care or for services ordinarily performed in the household. <C id="packaging-design-fba-06"/> Part 500 builds on one concept, the principal display panel: the part of a label most likely to be displayed or examined under normal retail display. <C id="packaging-design-fba-07"/>

| Element | What 16 CFR Part 500 requires |
|---|---|
| Identity of the commodity | On the principal display panel, as a principal feature, easily read, in lines generally parallel to the base |
| Net quantity of contents | Separately and accurately stated on the principal display panel, in the bottom 30% of the panel, parallel to the base |
| Units | Fluid measure for liquids; weight or mass for solid, semi-solid or viscous products; otherwise weight, measure or count |
| Name and place of business | The manufacturer, packer or distributor, stated conspicuously |
| Qualifying phrase | Required when the named party is not the manufacturer, e.g. "Manufactured for" or "Distributed by" |

*Source: eCFR, 16 CFR 500.2–500.7, checked Nov 8, 2026.* <C id="packaging-design-fba-08"/> <C id="packaging-design-fba-09"/> <C id="packaging-design-fba-12"/> <C id="packaging-design-fba-13"/> <C id="packaging-design-fba-14"/> <C id="packaging-design-fba-10"/> <C id="packaging-design-fba-11"/>

### The bottom-30% rule in practice

The net quantity has to sit within the bottom 30 percent of the area of the label panel, in lines generally parallel to the base on which the package rests as designed to be displayed. <C id="packaging-design-fba-13"/>

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): sizing the net quantity band">
Inputs: a front panel 5 inches tall and 4 inches wide, standing on its 4-inch edge.

Panel area: 5 × 4 = 20 square inches. Bottom 30%: 0.30 × 20 = 6 square inches, a band 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5 inches tall across the bottom of the panel. <C id="packaging-design-fba-13"/>
</Callout>

Decide which face is the display face before the artwork is drawn, because "bottom" follows the way the package is designed to stand.

### Minimum type size and clear space for the net quantity

Type height for the net quantity depends on the area of the principal display panel. For a rectangular package that area is the height times the width of the side bearing the panel; for a cylindrical or nearly cylindrical container it is 40 percent of the height times the circumference. <C id="packaging-design-fba-39"/>

| Principal display panel area | Minimum height of net quantity type |
|---|---|
| 5 sq in or less | 1/16 inch (1.5 mm) |
| More than 5, up to 25 sq in | 1/8 inch (3.1 mm) |
| More than 25, up to 100 sq in | 3/16 inch (4.7 mm) |
| More than 100, up to 400 sq in | 1/4 inch (6.35 mm) |
| More than 400 sq in | 1/2 inch (12.7 mm) |

*Source: eCFR, 16 CFR 500.21(b), checked Nov 9, 2026.* <C id="packaging-design-fba-40"/>

The declaration also needs clear space: above and below, a gap at least equal to the height of its lettering; to the left and right, at least twice the width of the letter "N" in that type. <C id="packaging-design-fba-41"/> Leave that space free when you place icons or barcodes in the bottom band.

<Callout type="example" title="Worked example (hypothetical): type size for two pack shapes">
Inputs: the 5 × 4 inch front panel above, and a 6-inch-tall bottle with a 7-inch circumference.

- Box: 20 sq in falls in the "more than 5, up to 25" row, so the net quantity type must be at least 1/8 inch tall, and the clear space above and below it at least 1/8 inch. <C id="packaging-design-fba-40"/> <C id="packaging-design-fba-41"/>
- Bottle: 0.40 × 6 × 7 = 16.8 sq in, the same row, so also at least 1/8 inch. <C id="packaging-design-fba-39"/>
</Callout>

### Private label: the "Distributed by" line

If a factory makes your product and your company sells it, your company can be the named party, but the name must be qualified by a phrase that reveals your connection, such as "Manufactured for" or "Distributed by." <C id="packaging-design-fba-11"/> The street address may be left off only if it appears in a readily accessible, widely published and publicly available resource. <C id="packaging-design-fba-25"/>

Two other marks belong on the same artwork plan: the country-of-origin marking for imported goods, covered in [origin marking requirements](/guides/origin-marking-requirements/), and the GTIN barcode, covered in [GS1 barcodes](/guides/gs1-barcodes-fnsku/).

## Choose a structure that can ship as-is

If you want the box to travel without an Amazon overbox, design to the Ships in Product Packaging (SIPP) specs from the start. Product and packaging must be at least 6 × 4 × 0.375 inches regardless of packaging structure. <C id="packaging-design-fba-17"/>

Each inbound unit of a SIPP-certified product needs an exterior scannable label, <C id="packaging-design-fba-18"/> so leave a flat, unprinted area on the outer face where the label can go without covering the required panel text. The certification steps and per-unit discounts are covered in the [SIPP guide](/guides/sipp-packaging-program/); the design point here is that a structure meeting these specs earns lower FBA fulfillment fees once enrolled. <C id="packaging-design-fba-21"/>

The stakes rose for bulky items: new packaging fees took effect for small bulky and large bulky products in the US starting January 15, 2026, <C id="packaging-design-fba-20"/> while products already enrolled in SIPP remain unaffected. <C id="packaging-design-fba-27"/>

## Design out Amazon's prep work

Several of the prep categories and prep instructions in Amazon's Fulfillment Inbound API (version 2024-03-20) describe packaging features you settle at the design stage. The definitions below come from that API; the detailed prep instructions behind them are in Seller Central's FBA prep pages.

| Prep category or instruction | What triggers it | Design response |
|---|---|---|
| BABY | Made for a child aged three or younger; packaging with cutouts greater than one square inch <C id="packaging-design-fba-28"/> | Avoid open windows over 1 sq in on baby products |
| SMALL | Longest side under 2 1/8 inches, the width of a credit card <C id="packaging-design-fba-29"/> | A unit whose longest side is 2 1/8 in or more is outside this category |
| PERFORATED | Packaging with a perforated opening <C id="packaging-design-fba-30"/> | Use a tear strip only on the outer shipper, not the retail unit |
| LIQUID | Liquid or viscous without a double seal <C id="packaging-design-fba-31"/> | Specify a secondary seal: induction seal, safety ring, clips, heat-shrink band or boxing <C id="packaging-design-fba-35"/> |
| FRAGILE | Glass or otherwise fragile, or a liquid in glass <C id="packaging-design-fba-32"/> | Overboxing may be required for fragile items; for items over 4.5 kg, double-wall corrugated boxes <C id="packaging-design-fba-34"/> |
| Suffocation warning | Poly bag opening of 12 cm or more, measured flat <C id="packaging-design-fba-33"/> | The warning must be printed on the bag or attached as a label |
| Blank sticker | A bad barcode that cannot be covered by another sticker <C id="packaging-design-fba-36"/> | Place any printed barcode where an FNSKU label can cover it, or leave it off |

*Source: Amazon Selling Partner API models, Fulfillment Inbound v2024-03-20, checked Nov 9, 2026.*

Two practical consequences follow. First, in the US marketplace the inbound API does not accept Amazon as the label owner or the prep owner, which leaves SELLER or NONE as the accepted values there. <C id="packaging-design-fba-37"/> Second, you can check the prep details on file for your SKUs before you print a new run: the `listPrepDetails` operation returns the preparation details for a list of seller SKUs in a marketplace. <C id="packaging-design-fba-38"/>

## Branding without breaking the rules

Shipping in your own packaging is also a branding surface. Amazon describes SIPP packaging as a chance to display your brand and product messages and improve the unboxing experience. <C id="packaging-design-fba-19"/> Customers can still choose Amazon packaging at no additional cost, <C id="packaging-design-fba-22"/> so the box should work both as the shipper and as a retail pack inside an Amazon carton. Messages inside the box carry their own rules, covered in [product inserts](/guides/product-inserts/).
