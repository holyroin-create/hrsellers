Sellers treat packaging as branding with a protective function. Amazon treats it as the thing it measures, weighs, stores, labels and ships — and prices accordingly.

This is the assembled version of a decision that appears in eight other guides on this site.

## The six things it decides

**1. Cubic feet, and therefore every storage layer.**

```
cubic feet = (L × W × H in inches) ÷ 1,728
```

A 12 × 10 × 8 box is **0.556 cu ft**. The same product in an 8 × 8 × 6 box is **0.222** — about 60% less, applied to the base rate, the Q4 multiplier, the aged-inventory surcharge and the utilisation surcharge. See the [storage guide](/guides/fba-storage-four-layers/).

**2. Size tier, and therefore the fulfilment fee.** Dimensions and weight together. Reporting is explicit that a product looking profitable in one size tier can lose margin when the box pushes it into a higher one — and names low-priced products, bundles, home goods, toys, fragile items and **anything with air-filled packaging** as most exposed.

**3. Weight thresholds.** The [bundling guide](/guides/bundles-and-multipacks/) covers a published example where a bundle stays economic only under 1.5 lb. Packaging weight counts toward that.

**4. SIPP eligibility.** Reporting ties bulky base fee decreases in 2026 to SIPP enrolment, which requires packaging passing ISTA-6 transit testing — see the [SIPP guide](/guides/sipp-packaging-program/). Packaging designed only for a shelf generally does not pass.

**5. Prep cost.** Since Amazon [ended US prep services](/guides/amazon-stopped-prepping/) on 1 January 2026, poly-bagging, over-boxing and assembly are billed at a reported $0.35 to $2.00 per unit. Packaging that arrives FBA-ready costs nothing to prep; packaging that needs work costs it on every unit forever.

**6. Compliance.** Origin marking, suffocation warnings, and — under SIPP — the fact that the customer sees your packaging directly.

## The specification to give a supplier

Most packaging conversations happen in terms of appearance. This is the list that decides cost:

**Dimensions, in the condition Amazon receives it.** Not the product — the packed unit, measured at its widest points. Ask for the actual measured figure on a sample, not the drawing.

**Weight, packed.** Including any insert, manual or accessory.

**Durability.** If SIPP is a target, ISTA-6 is the standard to specify at design stage, not test for afterwards.

**Poly bag specification** where required — reporting names a 1.5 mil minimum with suffocation warnings.

**Origin marking**, permanent and visible to the end purchaser — see the [marking guide](/guides/origin-marking-requirements/).

**Brand marking**, permanent, if Brand Registry is a target. Reporting names stickers as the most common Brand Registry rejection cause.

**Barcode placement**, with space for an FNSKU label to cover the manufacturer barcode entirely where required.

**Every one of these is cheaper to specify than to fix.** A packaging change after tooling means another production run.

## The three habits that cost most

**Air-filled packaging.** Void space is cubic feet you pay for monthly and dimensional weight you pay for per shipment. Reporting names it among the most exposed categories after the 2026 size-tier changes.

**Designing for a retail shelf.** Display cartons are wide, thin and structurally weak — the opposite of what survives a conveyor and fails ISTA-6.

**Deciding packaging after the product.** By then tooling is committed and the box is designed around what exists rather than around the tier boundary you are trying to stay inside.

## The tier-boundary check

The single highest-return exercise, and it takes an afternoon:

1. **Get the current fulfilment fee** for your packed dimensions from the [Fee Preview report](/guides/seller-central-reports/).
2. **Find the boundary above and below you.** Size tier definitions are published.
3. **Measure the gap.** If you are within a centimetre or a few ounces of a lower tier, a packaging revision pays for itself on every future unit.
4. **Check the cubic feet at the same time.** The tier decides the fulfilment fee; the cubic feet decide the storage bill. They move together but not identically.

**Run it before the first order, and again whenever Amazon revises tier definitions** — reporting notes Amazon periodically updates tier definitions using actual dimensions, weight and dimensional weight.

## Where it interacts with everything else

**Freight.** Air freight prices on chargeable weight — the greater of actual and volumetric — so bulky light packaging is penalised twice: once at the border and monthly at the warehouse. See the [freight guide](/guides/freight-modes/).

**Returns.** Packaging that fails in transit produces damage returns, which feed the [returns processing fee and the Frequently Returned Item badge](/guides/returns-cost-and-badge/).

**Inspection.** Packed dimensions and weight belong on the [pre-shipment inspection](/guides/qc-inspections/) checklist, measured rather than assumed.

**Product research.** The [research guide](/guides/product-research-cost-errors/) names measuring the product instead of the box as one of six priced mistakes. This is that mistake in reverse.

## What to do

**Ask for packed dimensions and weight before you model anything.** Not the product's — the shipping unit's.

**Check the tier boundary** before tooling is committed.

**Specify durability at design stage** if SIPP is a target.

**Eliminate void space.** It is the cheapest saving available and it compounds monthly.

**Specify marking and branding as permanent**, in the same document.

**Measure a sample rather than trusting the drawing.** Suppliers quote design dimensions; Amazon charges on actual ones.

**Re-check when Amazon revises size tiers**, and when you change anything about the product.

**Check it on your own package.** The [dimensional weight calculator](/tools/dimensional-weight/) takes your packed dimensions and weight and returns billable weight and cubic feet — the two figures that drive freight and storage. Nothing is stored and nothing leaves your browser.
