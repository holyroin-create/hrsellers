Amazon put a number on its biggest listing change in years: per the company's **13 August FAQ, more than 984 million titles** have been updated since the 10 June announcement. The 75-character cap took effect **27 July**; what remains over-limit is being rewritten by Amazon's AI, gradually and without per-listing ceremony.

## The mechanics that decide who keeps control

Three details separate sellers who manage this from sellers it manages: the cap is 75 characters **including spaces** (media exempt); the new **Item Highlights** field carries up to 125 searchable characters, so total indexable space stays near 200 — split across two fields with different jobs; and the review window is unequal — **brand-registered sellers reportedly get 14 days** to review AI rewrites, unregistered sellers get the change applied directly. Listings stay active throughout, which is precisely why the quiet rewrite is easy to miss until conversion moves.

The audit order: highest-revenue ASINs first. Keep the primary converting keyword inside the 75; move materials, use cases and secondary terms into Highlights — the same noun-phrase discipline our [listing guide](/guides/listing-optimisation-a10/) applies. A title you wrote will beat a title a model summarised, and after 984 million rewrites, "I did not get to it" is a choice.
